## Reliability Improvement of Resistance Switching  Devices by Insertion of a Novel 2D Material MXene  

Nan He 1, Xinwei Liu 1, Fei Gao 1, Yu Wang 1, Miaocheng Zhang 1, Xinyi Shen 1, Ertao Hu 1, Xiang Wan 1,  Xiao Gong 2, Lin He  $^{1*}$  , Xiaojuan Lian 1, and Yi Tong 1\*    1 Department of Electronic and Optical Engineering, Department of Microelectronics, Nanjing University of Posts and  Telecommunications, 9 Wenyuan Road, Nanjing, 210023, China.    2 Electrical and Computer Engineering, National University of Singapore, 119077, Singapore.  \* Phone: +86-25-85866131   Fax: +86-25-85866131   Email:  tongyi@njupt.edu.cn

Abstract-   High-density and reliable electronic synapses with  bipolar resistive switching behavior are the building blocks for the  hardware implementation of artificial neural network. However,  the power consumption and reliability of resistance states are  major challenges for commercialization of electronic synapses.  Here,   a   fully   complementary   metal-oxide   semiconductor   (CMOS)-compatible resistance switching device and its crossbar  arrays with the introduction of a novel two-dimensional transition  metal carbides MXene   $\mathrm{(Ti_{3}C_{2})}$   has been investigated. Comparing  with   $\mathbf{TiN}/\mathbf{Cu}/\mathbf{SiO}_{2}/\mathbf{TiN}$   device, low operation voltage has been  achieved as well as reliable resistance states in MXene/SiO 2   structure. In addition, long retention exceeding   ${\bf3.5\times10^{3}}$   seconds  has been measured. Furthermore, the fitting results demonstrate  that the conduction mechanisms of HRS and LRS show space  charge limited conduction (SCLC) and ohmic characteristics,  respectively. The controllability of conductive filament growth is  proposed to be responsible for the performance improvement of  reliability.

Keywords  –  Resistance switching devices, Crossbar arrays,  T wo-dimensional material, Conduction mechanism.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Resistance switching (RS) devices are now becoming of  interest for next-generation scalable nonvolatile memory as  well as artificial synapses due to their fast switching speed,  excellent   endurance,   superior   scalability,   and   CMOS  compatibility [1] - [4]. The binary transition metal oxides, such  as  $\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$  ,  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$  , NiO,  $\mathrm{TaO_{x}}$  , and   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   have been reported to have  RS effects and among them   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  -based memristors have been  extensively studied due to their CMOS-compatibility [5] – [8].  Crossbar arrays based on two-terminal resistive switches have  been proposed as a leading candidate for future high-density  memories and logic applications [9] – [12]. Because synapses  are bridges between many neurons and the analog function in  the storage of synaptic weights is inherent in two terminal

nodes, there are some similarities between neural networks and  crossbar arrays, in which RS devices are arranged at each  cross-point [13]. However, stabilization of resistance states is  hard to be achieved for crossbar structure of RS devices. Many  different kinds of two-dimensional (2D) materials has offered a  new pathway for achieving high performance RS devices,  including black phosphorous, graphene oxide   and   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   [14] –  [16]. In 2011, Naguib et al. used hydrofluoric acid etching to  remove the Al layer in   $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{AlC}_{2}$  , thus forming a new 2D metal  carbide   $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$    [17]. In order to emphasize that the morphology  of this new material is similar to that of graphene, and that this  new material is prepared in the MAX phase, this new material is  named MXene [18].   Recently, 2D material MXene has attracted  extensive research interest in academia for its good  conductivity, high stacking density, and hydrophilicity [19] –  [22]. However, the application of MXene in brain-inspired  devices has been rarely investigated. Therefore, it is worth  exploring the MXene-based RS devices and its influence on  reliability improvement.

In this work, we successfully designed and fabricated RS  devices and crossbar arrays with TiN/Cu/SiO 2 /TiN and  TiN/Cu/MXene/SiO 2 /TiN structures. These two structures have  been compared to explore the impact of MXene on the  reliability of RS devices. In addition, the retention, analog  behavior, and the conduction mechanism   of MXene/SiO 2   structure were investigated in details. We found that the  introduction of MXene may provide a useful guideline for  designing reliable RS devices as electronic synapses for  brain-inspired systems.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

The studied resistance switching (RS) devices (Fig. 1(a)) of  TiN/Cu/MXene/SiO 2 /TiN has been fabricated on Si wafer. As  for the fabrication, first, a  100 nm-thick carbon nitride (TiN)  layer was deposited on the substrates by RF magnetic sputtering  as the bottom electrode. Then, an 80 nm-thick   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer  serving as an insulating layer was sputtered from   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   target  with an Ar gas with a flow rate of 10sccm and a pressure of  1morr.   Subsequently, the mixture of MXene powders and  dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was obtained (  $0.3\ \mathrm{~g~}$   in 6 ml  dimethyl sulfoxide), which was swirled on   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   using the spin  coater. Next, 25 nm-thick Metallic Cu layer was deposited by  DC sputter-deposited at ambient temperature as the top  electrode. Finally, 100 nm-thick TiN was sputtered as a  protective layer for the top electrode.   In the comparative   $\mathrm{TiN}/\mathrm{Cu}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{TiN}$   device, the thickness of the film layer and the  preparation method were the same as described above.

In order to increase the density of RS devices, we designed  three-layer mask to fabricate devices with crossbar structure.  Fig. 1(b) shows the schematic diagrams of crossbar arrays with  three-dimensional structure and Fig. 1(c) shows the planar  structure.

Synapses can be viewed as two-terminal devices, and  synaptic weights can be dynamically modified and stored using  consecutive spikes [25]. When consecutive positive (0 to 1.8 V)  voltage sweep is applied to the device, its conductivity  continuously increases with the voltage sweep [26]. The analog  behavior of TiN/Cu/MXene/SiO 2 /TiN devices under DC  programming conditions is shown in Fig. 4(a), which is similar  to inhibition in synapses.

To further clarify the switching mechanisms of the  TiN/Cu/MXene/SiO 2 /TiN device, the positive part of  I-V  curve  in Fig. 3(a) was re-plotted on a log-log scale, as shown in Fig.  4(b) [27]. The double logarithm plot for LRS exhibits a linearly  ohmic behavior with a slope of 1.01. For the HRS, charge  transport behavior are different with LRS and can be classified  into three regions: the Ohmic region   $\mathrm{(I^{\infty}V)}$  , the Child’s law  region   $(\mathrm{I}^{\infty}\mathrm{V}^{2})$  , and the steep current increase region [28, 29].  This indicates that the carrier transport mechanism of LRS is  dominated by ohmic conduction whereas HRS is found to be  the SCLC. A typical conductive filaments model can also be  used to explain conduction behavior, in other words, the  conduction mechanism is due to the formation and rupture of  conductive filaments in the   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  -based switching layer.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In conclusion, RS devices in the form of crossbar arrays with  and without 2D material MXene were fabricated   successfully  using the D.C. sputtering technique for non-volatile resistive  switching memory applications, and the performance of RS  devices has been studied in details. The conclusion is that the  adoption of MXene in RS layer exhibits  excellent performance,  including the low operation voltage and reliable OFF resistance  states. It is anticipated that this research will open the door for  the use of MXene in performance improvement and will have a  critical impact on the further advancement of memory and  neuromorphic applications.
