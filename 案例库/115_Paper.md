## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Copper (Cu) wire is a good alternative to expensive gold (Au)  wire for wire bonding interconnects in many semiconductor and  microelectronic applications [1-2]. However, Cu wire does  pose some challenges, like it is harder than Au wire and it can  easily oxidize in air especially with moisture. Cu oxide layer is  not a passivation layer and it will continue to grow in thickness.  The Cu oxide will hinder the adhesion between wire and pad  and/or   lead,   causing   non-stick-on-pad   (NSOP)   and  non-stick-on-lead (NSOL). Therefore, the shelf life and floor  life of Cu wire are much shorter than that of Au wire. Special  package and storage conditions are needed for Cu wire [3].  Normally, Cu wire shelf life is 6 months in vacuum package  condition. Its floor life is 7 days after opening the vacuum  package and kept in the clean room. In this work, an abnormal  Cu wire oxidation issue was encountered in an assembly

production line. A batch of Cu wires were opened from the  vacuum package and were hung in the wire bonder in clean  room. The initial wire color is normal Cu color. However, after  1-3 days, the Cu spools which were hung on the wire bonder  gradually become dark red color, and several NSOP and NSOL  occurred.

The problematic spools were sent for TOF-SIMS surface  analysis. TOF-SIMS results showed that the corrosion products  are CuCl and CuO. It is suspected that the chloride (Cl) may  come from the Cl-contained floor detergent which is used in the  production clean room. Simulation test was conducted to verify  the contamination source in the production clean room, and the  simulation results matched well with the actual result. It is  suggested that the assembly houses should use Cl-free detergent  in the production floor to prevent the Cl contamination.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

The Cu spools were analyzed using surface analysis  equipment Time-of-Flight Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry  (TOF-SIMS) and X-Ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS).  The TOF-SIMS used is a model of TOFSIMS5 from IonTof.  TOFSIMS is a highly sensitive surface analytical technique,  using a pulsed and focused ion beam and time-of-flight  analyzer to produce positive and negative mass spectra from the  outer 1 to 2nm of materials. It is capable of providing detailed  information about the molecular structure of surfaces. Ion  intensity was normalized to total ion intensity, then x1,000,000.

XPS was conducted with Ulvac PHI Quantera II. X-ray  source used is from 1486eV Al Kα. The analysis beam size is at  10um in diameter. The sputter gun used is from 2keV Argon ion  withan area of   $1.5\mathrm{mm}x1.5\mathrm{mm}$  . The depth is calibrated with  standard   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  .

After analysis on the problematic spool, simulation test was  conducted to verify the hypothesis. The whole experiment  process flow is shown in Fig. 1.

From the TOF-SIMS analysis, it is found that the corrosion  product of the problematic spools is mainly   $\mathrm{CuCl}_{2}$  . It is  suggested that the Cl may come from HClO. Cu and HClO react  and the formula equation like below:

Cu + HClO = CuO + HCl                           (1)
2HCl+CuO=CuCl

$$\scriptstyle2\mathrm{HCl+CuO=CuCl_{2}+H_{2}O}$$
It is suspected that the Cl highly probably came from the  Cl-contained floor detergent which was used in the production  clean room. After the operator wipe the floor, the detergent  remains will evaporate and contaminated the spools which was  hanging on the wire bonder. With the attack of Cl, moisture and  oxygen, the Cu spools surface were oxidized and show the dark  red color.

To verify this hypothesis, simulation test was conducted to  reproduce the issue. Simulation test was performed as this:  Cl-contained detergent was diluted with water and was loaded  into a plastic container. The Cu spools were hung over the  container, beneath which there was Cl-contained detergent, as  shown in Fig. 4. The spools were monitored daily to see how  the Cu wire surface changes.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

A batch of Cu wires were found to be oxidized when they  were hung in the wire bonders in production clean room after  2-3 days. From the TOF-SIMS analysis, it is found that the  corrosion products of the problematic spools are mainly  $\mathrm{CuCl}_{2}$  .  Simulation test verified that the contamination probably come  from the Cl-contained floor detergent. It is suggested that the  assembly houses should use Cl-free detergent in the production  room to prevent the Cl contamination.
