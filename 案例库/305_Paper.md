## Methodology to Investigate the Root Cause of  Threshold Voltage Drift of Transistor Devices using  Capacitance Voltage Measurements  

Ang Chung Keow 1 \* , and Muhammad Adi Negara 2     1 Quality Management (FA Lab) &  2 Technologies Dept. of Infineon Technologies (Kulim) Sdn Bhd, Lot 10&11, Jalan Hi-Tech 7,  Industrial Zone Phase II, Kulim Hi-Tech Park, 0900 Kulim, Kedah, Malaysia.  \* Phone: +60-4-4278744   Fax: +60-4-4278459   Email:  chungkeow.ang@infineon.com

Abstract-   An accurate methodology is needed during failure  analysis of non-visual defects leading to threshold voltage (V T )  drift such as charge trapped or slightly doping drifted in  transistor devices. A method using capacitance measurement was  investigated to find the root cause of V T  drift of transistor devices  during   front-end-of-line   (FEOL)   processing   using  capacitance-voltage (CV) measurement.

Keywords  –  Capacitance-voltage (CV) measurement, Threshold  voltage   $\langle V_{\mathcal{D}}\rangle$   drift, Hauser CVC software, Oxide charge, Doping,  Transistor .

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

The CV characterization method used for the study of MOS  transistor devices is total capacitance measurement, Cgg.  Measurement setup of Cgg is drain, source and substrate (body)  are grounded and a voltage is applied to the poly-silicon gate  (VG) as illustrated in Fig. 1.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

$\mathrm{V_{T}}$   is defined as the minimum gate electrode bias required to  strongly invert the surface of semiconductor under the transistor  gate and form a conducting channel between drain and source  regions. The value of  $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   is determined during wafer fabrication  process. Shift of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   value can be caused by numerous  parameters and normally it can be detected at process control  monitoring (PCM) process step.   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   consists of several  contributions from oxide charges (flat band voltage   $\mathrm{V_{FB}}$  ), oxide  thickness   or   capacitance    $\mathrm{(T_{ox}/C_{ox})}$     and   doping   (N A )  components. Any variation of these parameters will result in the  drift of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   value as described in equation below for n-type  transistor device [1]:

Oxide thickness, oxide charge or doping related issues are  highly suspected as failure mechanism of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift and can be  quantified easily using CV measurement. It is difficult to  investigate the cause of  $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   shift using standard current-voltage  (IV) measurement which is mainly used to extract the  $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   value.  V FB , N A , and T OX  values are needed to analyze further the root  cause of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift. Therefore, this paper outlines a scientific  approach to investigate   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift using CV measurement  analysis.

The capacitance depends on the voltage that is applied to the  gate (with respect to the body). The dependence is shown in  Fig. 2 which describes a P-type MOS capacitor in three  operating regions separated by two voltages. In accumulation  region, which carriers of the same type as the body accumulates  at the surface. In depletion region, the surface is devoid of any  carriers leaving only a space charge or depletion layer, and in  inversion region which carriers of the opposite type from the  body aggregate at the surface to “invert” the conductivity type  and create a P-type channel. The two voltages that demarcate  the three regions are Flat band Voltage   $\left({{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{FB}}}\right)$   which separates  the accumulation region from the depletion regions and the  Threshold Voltage   $\left({{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{T}}}\right)$   which demarcates the depletion region  from the inversion region.

Based on Hauser CVC simulation results as shown in Fig.  3-5, the maximum capacitance (Cmax) is observed varying to  lower value if T OX  is thicker. The minimum capacitance (Cmin)  is observed varying to lower value if doping concentration (N A )  is lesser. For   $\mathrm{V_{FB}}$   variation input data, the simulation results  showed the overall Cgg curve is shifted to left-side if the V FB  is  higher value. Thus, the CV characteristic of MOS device can be  easily simulated by using Hauser CVC software to determine  the variation of device structure parameters.

For CV characterization, an Agilent 4284A Precision LCR  Meter is used for Cgg measurement. An AC impedance meter,  sometimes   called   an   LCR   meter   (for   inductance-L,  capacitance-C   and   resistance-R),   measures   complex  impedances with an auto balanced bridge maintaining AC  virtual ground at the sense side of the capacitor. The operation  of LCR meter is relatively simple as shown in Fig. 6. It  measures AC impedance by supplying an AC voltage out of the  high current terminal (HCUR). The current through the device  is measured by the low current terminal (LCUR), and the  voltage across the device is measured by the high and low  potential terminals (HPOT and LPOT) [5]. For my case study,  HCUR and HPOT are connected to high side of my test setup  however LCUR and LPOT are connected to low side of my test  setup. Test frequency of   $100\ \mathrm{{kHz}}$   and parallel AC impedance  model are being used for the case study as well.

By matching the measured CV curves to the CV simulation  results illustrated in Fig.7-10, the root cause of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift can be  identified either due to   $\mathrm{T_{ON}}$   issue based on their Cmax values,  doping related issue based on their Cmin values or oxide charge  issue based on their   $\mathrm{V_{FB}}$   values. A different Cmax, Cmin and   $\mathrm{V_{FB}}$   values represents a variation in T OX , doping level and oxide  charges number respectively [1-2]. Fig.7-9 shows an example  of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift case due to a thicker oxide thickness with lower  Cmax value signature and due to higher doping level with  higher Cmin value signature respectively. A parallel CV shift to  the left with similar Cmax and Cmin can be seen in Fig.10 for  the case of positive oxide charges leading to   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$  drift to lower  side. Based on the  $\mathrm{V_{FB,}}$  , doping, and T OX  values of measured CV  curve extracted using Hauser CVC software, the root cause of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift can be confirmed simultaneously from Fig.7-10.

Non visual defect such as charge trapped or slightly doping  drifted in the transistor device is a difficult analysis to be  convinced. The root cause of   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   drift due to various T OX ,  doping level and oxide charge number has been proven easily  using CV measurement analysis in this paper. It is imperative to  have in depth understanding of device behavior and  measurement knowledge to subject the device with a right  analysis methodology and process flow.
