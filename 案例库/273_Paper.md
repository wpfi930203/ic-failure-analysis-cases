## Poly-Si Unetch Failure Due to Etching Rate  Dependence of Si Orientation  

Dahyun Nam\*, Sukchan Song, Eunbee Go, Bongsu Chae, Ara Kim, BoMi Kim, Haeran Kim, Ji-Sun Yang, SoYeon  Han, Hyunjun Ryu, Shinyoung Chung, Brandon Lee  Foundry Business, Samsung Electronics, 1-1 Samsungjeonja-ro, Hwaseong-si, Gyeonggi-do, 18448, Republic of Korea  \* Phone: +82 10 5175 1536 Email: dahyun.nam@samsung.com

Abstract-   The origin of poly-Si unetch failure in FinField-effect  transistor (FinFET) was defined as an etch rate dependence of a  specific Si grain in Si poly crystalline. We analyzed a failure in a  FinFET based static random access memory (SRAM). Abnormal  point in a passive voltage contrast result was measured by  nano-probing and fast  $\mathbf{V_{th}}$   shift was detected at a pull down node  in SRAM. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was applied  to find out the cause of the failure, finding that poly-Si in dummy  gate was not successfully etched. High-resolution TEM and fast  Fourier transformation analysis showed that there was a (111) Si  grain. The etch rate of (111) Si is   $\mathbf{1-}2\%$   of (110) Si, which leads  poly-Si unetch failure in gate in a FinFET structure.

Keywords  –  Si   unetch, Si orientation, HR-TEM, Fast Fourier  transform, Diffraction pattern .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Increasing demands of smaller and lighter electronic devices  encourage scaling down of integrated circuits (IC). Static  random access memory (SRAM) is one of the most important  elements in ICs. To make smaller sized SRAM, FinField-effect  transistor (FinFET) is introduced [1,2,3]. FinFET is built on a  substrate where the gate wrapped around the channel, forming a  double gate structure. To make this FinFET structure, the  process includes dummy gate deposition, composed of poly-Si,  and then the poly-Si in dummy gate is replaced by gate  materials to match designed threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{th})}$   [4,5]. To  remove poly-Si in dummy gate, wet etching is commonly used.  If this etching process is not effectively done, poly-Si will not  be removed, and followed gate material deposition will not be  evenly applied which leads malfunction of a gate. Si is known  to have a high etch selectivity depends on its orientation [6,7].  Therefore, it is essential to control Si orientation during  fabrication.

In this work, we found a gate malfunction due to poor  etching of poly-Si in gate. Remained poly-Si blocked gate  material to deposit and it induced leak current, higher   $\mathrm{I_{d,off}}.$   By  examining   unetched   poly-Si   region   using   high  resolution-transmission electron microscope (HR-TEM), a  large sized Si grain, \~5 nm in diameter, was found. The crystal  orientation of the Si grain to etching direction was calculated  perpendicular to (111) with a difference of   ${\sim}8^{\circ}$  . It is assumed  that this (111) oriented Si grain decreased etching rate and the  etching was not properly done.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

For passive voltage contrast (PVC) test, scanning electron  microscope was used. Nanoprobing at the contacts to  source/gate/drain was carried out using Thermo Fisher  Scientific’s Hyperion atomic force probe (AFP) system. With  AFP results, the exact point of the failure is predicted. Then,  Ga-beam focused ion beam (FIB) was used to prepare  plan-view and vertical-view TEM and scanning transmission  electron microscope (STEM). FEI Talos with an acceleration  voltage of   $200\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{\kV}}$   was used to measure TEM, STEM, and  HR-TEM. Fast Fourier transformation (FFT) results were  analyzed by comparing diffraction pattern simulation results  from Single Crystal.

In order to find the reason of the leakage, plan STEM is  measured. Fig. 2(a) shows plan STEM image and arrowed  point, an edge of a gate, is not well defined. There are two  possible ways on a gate  failure. One is that the gate structure  itself is not well defined and the other is that the gate structure is  well defined but the gate material is poorly filled. To see which  case it is, the vertical TEM specimen was fabricated for the  dotted region and the result is shown in Fig. 2(b). It clearly  shows that the arrowed part of the gate is not well defined,  which causes abnormal function of the gate and hence generates  leakage current.

Dummy gate is fabricated and etching process follows to  define gate structure. It seems that there had been an obstacle  during etch process. HR-TEM is applied to the unetch area,  solid square area in Fig 2(b), and periodic structure is shown for  whole fin area. On the other hand, periodic structure is shown

only for the top part of the gate area as in Fig. 3(a), marked as a  square. It seems that there is a Si grain with a diameter of  ${\sim}5\;\mathrm{nm}$    at top of the unetched gate area. To define crystal orientation of  this grain, FFT is applied for the solid square area, and the result  is shown in Fig. 3(c). The lattice vector of the Si particle is  [-1.18, 1.18, 0.47] in z-y plane, which is   ${\sim}82^{\circ}$   apart from the  (111) face. To check the commonality of this analysis, FFT was  also applied for the fin area and the result is shown in Fig. 3(b).  The FFT pattern shows that the fin is (010) oriented in z-y plane  and it is perpendicular to (100), which is the growth direction of  the wafer.

(111) face of Si is known to have high etching selectivity in  KOH and tetramethyl-ammonium-hydroxide [6,7]. Etch rate of  (111) oriented Si is in between  $1\%$   and   $2\%$   compared to that of  (110) oriented Si. It is implicated that the (111) oriented Si  grain has been formed in dummy gate and the etching was not  sufficiently done because (111) Si grain blocked the etchant  passage.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Gate failure in SRAM is defined using PVC test, nano  probing, and TEM. HR-TEM and FFT located (111) oriented Si  grain in the gate structure. The poly-Si in dummy gate was not  sufficiently peeled off during the etching process due to this  (111) Si grain for the etch rate of (111) Si is below  $2\%$   of (110)  Si. This failure caused malfunction of the gate and hence faster   $\mathrm{V_{th}s}$   and higher I D.off .
