## Electrical Performances and Physics Based Analysis  of 650V E-mode GaN Devices at High Temperatures  

Chi Zhang, Sheng Li, Siyang Liu, Jiaxing Wei, Wangran Wu, Weifeng Sun\*  National ASIC System Engineering Research Center, Southeast University, Nanjing, China.  \* Phone: +86 25 83795811   Fax: +86 25 83795077   Email: swffrog@seu.edu.cn

Abstract-   High temperature performances of commercial cascode  GaN FET and p-GaN HEMT are investigated and compared in  this paper. The differences of device structures lead to quite  different high temperatures performances. The p-GaN device  shows smaller shifts in threshold voltage but larger increase in gate  leakage current at high temperatures than cascode device.  Moreover, high temperatures also lead to different variations in  dynamic electrical parameters. By using analytical models, it is  found that the decrease of electron mobility dominates the  variations in switching parameters for p-GaN device, however, the  decrease of   $\mathbf{V_{th}}$   dominates the variations for cascode device.

Keywords  –  E-mode,   HEMT,   $\pmb{p}$  -GaN gate, cascode, high  temperature, electron mobility, dynamic characteristics, analytical  models.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

GaN-based power devices become expected to renovate the  traditional power systems to improve the conversion efficiency  [1]-[3]. The two types of state-of-art commercial E-mode GaN  devices in the power systems, cascode GaN field effect  transistors (FETs) [4] and p-GaN high electron mobility  transistors (HEMTs) [5], usually work at elevated ambient  temperatures, which may lead the reduction of the conversion  efficiency, even the failure of system function. Some published  literatures showed the shifts of electrical parameters for power  GaN devices at high temperatures, but not comprehensive [6]- [7]. It is necessary to consider the electrical parameter variations  of these two kinds of E-mode devices at high ambient  temperatures when modeling the device behaviors and  designing power electronics systems, but little high temperature  information of the commercial GaN devices can be acquired  from the datasheet released by the companies or from the  articles [8]-[9]. Thereby, comprehensive understandings of high  temperature performances of GaN devices are of great  significance.

In this paper, comprehensive high temperature performances  of the two kinds of commercial available E-mode GaN power  devices are investigated and compared, and the related physics  based mechanisms of the differences are discussed.

Fig. 3 shows the variations of threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{th})}$   and  drain to source on-sate resistance   $\mathrm{(R_{on})}$   of the studied devices  with temperature. To be noted,  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   is extracted at drain to source  current   $\left(\operatorname{I_{ds}}\right)$   equaling to   $500\upmu\mathrm{A}$  ,   $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   is extracted at   $\mathrm{I_{ds}}$   equaling  to 5A, gate to source voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{gs})}$   equaling to 6V and 8V for pGaN device and cascode device, respectively. It can be seen that

the decreases in   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   of cascode device are much severe than  those of p-GaN device. Mg doping is not fully ionized under  zero bias condition for p-GaN device, nonetheless, incomplete  ionization does not affect the Schottky barrier and depletion  region, both at zero bias and when the gate is biased even at   $25\mathrm{^\circC}$  , an activation of dopants occurs due to the presence of a  large electric field. Due to the high doping concentration in pGaN cap, the extension of Schottky depletion region near gate  contact is small at high temperature, resulting in a stable   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   of  p-GaN device [10]. For Cascode HEMTs, most of the electrical  parameters are determined by the performances of internally  integrated Si-based metal-oxide-semiconductor field effect  transistor (MOSFET), the physics based mechanisms behind  the behaviors of the whole device at high temperatures can be  understood based on the MOSFET, which are relatively mature.  The mechanisms behind the variations for cascode device are  similar to Si/SiC based devices [11].

Both the two devices show increase in  $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   with the increasing  temperature. For p-GaN devices, the electron mobility   $\left(\upmu_{\mathrm{eff}}\right)$    decreases monotonically with increasing temperature due to the  enhanced scattering rate, which has been reported in the  literature [12]. It is acknowledged that the increase of  $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   results  from the reduction in electron mobility at high temperatures for  traditional devices. It can be seen from the basic I-V model [13]  that the same conclusions can be made for p-GaN device as the  following:

(1)

$$\begin{array}{r l r}{\lefteqn{I_{d s}=\frac{W_{g}\mu_{e f f}q K_{p i}\left(V_{g s}-V_{t h}-\frac{V_{d s}}{2}\right)V_{d s}}{\left(d_{A l G a N}+d_{G a N}\right)*l_{g}}}}\\ {\lefteqn{g_{m}=\frac{d I_{d s}}{d V_{g s}}=\frac{W_{g}\mu_{e f f}q K_{p i}V_{d s}}{\left(d_{A l G a N}+d_{G a N}\right)*l_{g}}\left(1-\frac{d V_{t h}}{d V_{g s}}\right)}}\end{array}$$
(2)

Where   $\mathrm{W_{g}}$  is the width of the gate contact,   $\upmu_{\mathrm{eff}}$  is the effect  electron mobility in channel,   $\mathrm{l_{g}}$   is the gate length of the device,   $\mathsf{d}_{\mathrm{AlGaN}}$   is the depth of the AlGaN barrier,  $\mathsf{d}_{\mathrm{GaN}}$   is the depth of the  p-GaN cap,   $\mathrm{K_{\mathrm{pi}}}$   is the transconductance parameter, which is  constant.

Though the hole concentration in the p-GaN cap is increased  at high temperatures, the extension of the Schottky depletion  cannot be influenced significantly at the same   $\mathrm{V_{gs}}$   since the  doping concentration is high and the impurities in the depletion

region are fully ionized, leading to the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   stability of p-GaN  device [14].

Fig. 4 shows the reverse conduction characteristics of the two  devices at high temperatures. It is known that the reverse  voltage drop on the p-GaN device can be modulated by the gate  bias, but that of cascode device cannot. The reverse conduction  characteristics are measured at   $\mathrm{V_{gs}=-3V}$   here. From the  measurement results, high temperatures lead even higher  reverse voltage drop on the p-GaN device. The increase of  reverse   $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   is also because that the   $\upmu_{\mathrm{eff}}$  decreases at high  temperatures.

Fig. 5 shows the gate leakage current   $\left(\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{gss}}\right)$   at drain to source  voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{ds})}$   equaling to 0V. The lack of gate oxide layer for pGaN device leads to large   $\mathrm{I_{gss}}$  . Though the Schottky diode  formed by gate metal and p-GaN cap is reverse biased, the  leakage current is large due to the tunneling process.

The capacitance characteristics determine the switching  performances of the power devices to a certain extent [15].  Therefore, it is significant to investigate the capacitance

characteristics at high temperatures. Fig. 6 shows the terminal  capacitances of the two devices. The input capacitance   $\mathrm{(C_{iss})}$  ,  the reverse capacitance   $(\mathrm{C_{rss}})$   and the output capacitance   $(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{loss}})$  are measured at 1MHz. Similar to the traditional devices, since  the extension of depletion region changes little at high  temperatures, the terminal capacitances of both the two devices  show negligible changes. Thereby, the high temperature  influences upon the capacitances are neglected when analyzing  the switching characteristics [16].

Fig. 7 and Fig. 8 present the measured switching waveforms  of the devices at different temperatures. It can be seen that the  switching characteristics at high temperatures are quite different  between the two devices. For p-GaN device, obvious increases  of plateau voltage (V GP ) are observed with the increasing  temperature. Furthermore, the increases of   $\mathrm{V_{GP}}$   induce slower  turn-on speed and faster turn-off speed at high temperatures  during the turn-on and turn-off processes, which are quite  different from those of SiC based MOSFET [17] .

Form previous studies, the decreases of   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   dominate the  variations of switching parameters for SiC devices [17]. The  same conclusions can be made for cascode device [4]. However,  the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$    of p-GaN device is stable but the   $\upmu_{\mathrm{eff}}$  decreases a lot at  high temperatures, resulting in reductions in transconductance   $\left(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{m}}\right)$   according to the previous I-V model. From the dynamic  parameter models, the decrease in  $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   at high temperatures leads  the increases of plateau voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{GP}})$  , the increase of current  rise time   $(\mathrm{{t}_{i,r})}$  , and decrease of voltage rise time   $\mathrm{(t_{v,r})}$   for p-GaN  device, which are consistent with the measured results. Finally,  the calculated total switching energy loss   $\mathrm{(E_{total})}$   decreases with  temperatures. For turn-off process, the decrease of   $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   also leads  to obvious decreases of   $\mathbf{t}_{\mathrm{v,r}}$  . As shown in Fig. 8, the   $\mathbf{t}_{\mathrm{i,r}}$  and   $\mathrm{{t_{v,r}}}$  dominate the switching time, so the total switching time is  reduced. It can be concluded it is the   $\upmu_{\mathrm{eff}}$  that dominates the  variations of dynamic parameters at high temperatures.

IV. C ONCLUSION

The high temperature static and dynamic characteristics of  commercial E-mode GaN power devices are compared and the  related physics-based mechanisms of the differences are  discussed in this paper. For static characteristics, high  temperatures mainly induce worse   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   instability for cascode  device and larger gate leakage current for p-GaN device. For  dynamic characteristics, it is the   $\upmu_{\mathrm{eff}}$  that dominates the

variations in switching parameters for p-GaN device, however,  the decrease of   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   dominates the variations for cascode device.
