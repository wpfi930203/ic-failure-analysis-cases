## Advanced TEM   Application in 10nm below  Technology Node Device Analysis  

Hong Yang 1,2 , Yongliang  $\mathrm{Li}^{2}$  , Huilong Zhu 2 , Xiaogen Yin 1,2  and Anyan   $\mathrm{D}\mathbf{u}^{2^{*}}$    1 Microelectronics Institute, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China  2 Key Laboratory of Microelectronics Devices & Integrated Technology, Institute of Microelectronics, Chinese Academy of  Sciences, Beijing 100029, China  Phone: +86 82995894 Fax: +86 82995894 \*Email: duanyan@ime.ac.cn

Abstract-   The probe corrector STEM can provide the large field of  view strain analysis and sub-nano chemical information analysis  capability, which is very useful analysis tool for 10nm and below  node device analysis. This paper will demonstrate some  application case.

Keywords  – FINFET, SMF, Strain measurement, EELS

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Since 90nm technology, IBM first introduced embedded  SiGe in S/D to improve PMOS perform. Strain engineering is  widely used in MOS to improve the device perform.  The  channel strain measurement is the key for technology  developing (TD) team to set-up the S/D epitaxy grown process  flow for   $65\mathrm{nm}$   and below node.  For channel strain  measurement, the NBD (Nano-Beam Diffraction) and HRTEM  GPA are developed and used for strain analysis in 65nm and  below technology node PMOS device and provide good results.  Recently, the Precession Electron Diffraction (PED) and  scanning Moiré Fringes (SMF) are also developed for strain  analysis. Comparing with NBD, the PED can provide more  tolerance of specimen tilted. And SMF can provide more field  of view of strain analysis comparing HRTEM.

Comparing with NBD, there are very big data spaces and  long data collection time, the HRTEM needs to focus the  E-beam to a small area to get good quality images for late GPA  analysis. The high-density E-beam and long time expose makes  it easy to cause Si and low-k dielectric spacer to be damaged,  which will affect the quality of images. HRSTEM, with very  small beam current and short stop time on each spot, will  provide high quality atomic resolution images and little E-bam  damage. Thus, the HRSTEM is good replacement for HRTEM  in strain analysis for FINFET structure devices.

But the SMF (scanning Moiré Fringes) is a new developing  skill used in semiconductor devices by Samsung TEM team  [1\~4]. The strain in SiGe channel is high when used in 5nm  devices. In this paper, we present SMF study the strain and  crystal defects in the strain SiGe.

For 20nm and above node planar devices, the channel strain  analysis can be performed in a full process chip. But the MOS  device structure changes from planar to 3D as FINFET  structure used in the Intel 22nm and Foundry   $16/14\mathrm{nm}$  . There  are many process flow and channel strain measurement changes.

It is not possible to perform channel strain measurement in full  process chip in FINFET chip. The challenge of doing strain  measurement in FINFET device will be discussed in this paper.

Another problem in 10nm and below devices failure analysis  is the chemical analysis for peak overlapping element in X-ray  element analysis.  There are more and more elements used in  FEOL and MEOL process. The sub-nano chemical analysis is  the key capability for the HK/MG FEOL and MEOL/BEOL  process or MRAM devices.  In this paper, we demonstrate the  STEM/EELS   sub-nano   chemical   analysis   with   clear  identification of the different elements with EELS analysis.

## II.   E XPERIMENTAL 

For 7nm and below technology node, FIN CD shrinking  results in a reduction in Si mobility. so that the SiGe FIN would  be used to replace Si in PMOS at 5nm or below node. The IBM  team has proposed the SiGe buffer layer then strain SiGe for  PMOS and strain Si for NMOS. There are two typical methods  to form SiGe FIN as FIN first (Si FIN form the first, STI Oxide  filling/CMP, then etch Si FIN and epitaxial grown SiGe) or FIN  last (Etch P area, SiGe Epitaxial grown, then FIN pattern/Etch,  STI oxide filling/CMP). In this paper, the   $50\%$   SiGe strain layer  was grown on SiGe buffer layer/Si. This sample is used to  confirm the SMF capability, which is the same bulk sample as  many papers report as a tool of strain and defect detecting. FIN  first sample is used to simulate SMF in FIN structure.

Compared to other researcher work in SMF, which need to  buy the software to precession control the scan step in SMF  experiments, this work is done with normal Gatan digitscan  software in FEI Themis STEM. The SMF strain study the  sample is 50nmSiGe (  $50\%$   Ge) grown on about 3um SiGe  buffer layer (1um SiGe(30%Ge)/1um SiGe(20%Ge) / 1um  SiGe   $(10\%\mathrm{Ge})/\mathrm{Si})$  ).  The TEM image of Fig1 shows that there is  dislocation missing in the very thin specimen  $\mathord{\left(<}50\mathsf{n m}\right)$   due to  image force effect in bulk samples. The TEM specimen  ${\sim}0.2\mathrm{up}$  thicknesses (fig 1b) is needed for keeping dislocations and  reduce the specimen thickness resulting in the strain relax effect  in pure Si crystal structure.  Comparing HRSTEM max field of  view   $42\!\times\!42\mathrm{nm}$  , the SMF can provide a large field of  view  $(275\!\times\!275\mathrm{nm})$  ) of strain analysis and is easier to study the  dislocation in sample. Figure 2 is the SMF and HRSTEM image  of  $50\mathrm{nm}\;\mathrm{SiGe}(50\%)$   on top of SiGe buffer. It is easy to observe  the crystal defect(dislocations) at the interface in SMF

As shown in  figure 4c, the FIN channel is cover with HK/MG poly-crystal  materials, which block the imaging and diffraction base  TEM/STEM strain analysis in full process wafer. In order to  analyze the Si FIN channel with full process chips, TEM  specimen thickness needs thinning down to the blue box in  figure 4B to remove the both HK and MG in both sides of TEM  specimen. However, there are two problems when preparing  this kind TEM specimen. The first problem is difficulty in  stopping FIB at exactly the interface of HK/Si at FINFET in  both sides. The second problem is the strain relaxation, as  shown in figure 1, the thin TEM specimen stop at crystal  structure, there are strain relaxations and misfit dislocations  disappearing due to surface mirror force effect.

In figure 3, it is the SMF of   $10\%\mathrm{SiGe}/\mathrm{Si}$   interface. It is easy  to see the difference of dSMF in SiGe between dSMF in Si, due  to strain relaxation in SiGe. The SMF results  show that the strain in   $10\%$   SiGe/SI is about  $2\%$  .

There are three different SRAM cell structure at FIN edge,  the typical FINFET structure is schematic shown in figure 7.  The dummy gate location will affect the eSiGe profile, which  may results the channel strain difference in gate 1 compared to  gate 2 or 3 as marked in figure 7. The HRSTEM can only  provide the one channel per image, but with the large field of  view from SMF, it is possible to resolve gate 1\~3 gate channel  in one image. So that it is easier to find the difference of gate 1  to comparing gate 2 or 3 to reduce the measurement error effect  in strain measurement.

The STEM/EELS is used to study the interface of HK/MG  etch back structure [5]. It is important to identify the etch and  remove the W and TiN without damaging the HfOx layer for  process set-up. In normal EDX analysis, there is an overlap  peak of Si with W at 1.8KV. But there are the different in Si and  W in EELS spectra due to high resolution of EELS. As a result,

it is possible to differentiate the Si with W in EELS spectra and  perform mapping analysis without overlapping effect.  Figure 8  is the This method can apply to different Ti with O for the high  resistance contact bottom FA. The STEM/EELS mapping clear  show that the results are about sub-nano resolution and there is  no Si/W overlapping effect. It is the TiN top layer oxide during  the dry etch.  The results clearly show that the STEM/EELS  mapping is very powerful tool for 10nm and below device PFA  analysis.

The Precession Electron Diffraction (PED) is set-up as the  image 9a. at each point, the E-beam is with rocking angle  $\upalpha$   and  very fast rotation 360 degree to get diffraction patterns. The  beam rotation results the poor spatial resolution for PED about  2-3nm comparing with NBD 1nm. But PED can perform strain  measurement with large tolerance of specimen tilted angle,  which is key for 7nm and below technology node due to higher  and narrow FIN structure.

The basic theory of SMF is schemes shown in figure 9b.  The  contrast of STEM in HAADF detector is mainly due to atomic  Z contrast. It is bright when the E-beam scanning on atomic  rows, and it is dark when it is scanning at channel of the Si  crystal structure.  So that there is scanning Moiré Fringes form.

## III. C ONCLUSION 

The advance STEM/EELS is very power tool for 10nm and  below technology node of FINFET strain analysis and chemical  analysis.
