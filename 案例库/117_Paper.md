## Silicon Precipitates Counts and Size Study in  Aluminium Bond Pads   

Lim Wei Lee\*; Mohamad Esa Azlin MohdNoor; Marks Michael Raj  Infineon Technologies (Kulim) Sdn Bhd. Lot 10 &11, Jalan Hi-Tech 7, Industrial Zone Phase 2, Kulim Hi-Tech Park. 09090 Kulim,  Kedah. Malaysia  \* Phone: +60-4-427-9642   Fax: +60-4-24-8442   Email:  weilee.lim@infineon.com

Aluminium (Al) based metallization has been widely used in  semiconductor due to the process ability and the material behavior.  However, pure Al based material cannot be used due to the hillock  issue, void formation and also Al spiking caused by interaction with  silicon. Thus, small amount of Copper (Cu) and Silicon (Si)  precipitates were introduced into the Al layer to resolve these issues.  Nowadays the bond pads metallization has becoming thinner owing  to the recent semiconductor needs thus the presence of big Si  precipitates will have risk leading to cratering issue in wire-bonding  process. Here in, we report for the first time on how to quantify the Si  precipitates counts and Si precipitates size in Al based metallization  through failure analysis methods.

Keywords  –  Aluminium bond pads, silicon precipitates, copper  wires, cratering

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Semiconductor device technology has been growing in  complexity and function since it has been introduced.  Currently, there are 3 major forms of interconnect for integrated  circuits which is wire-bonding, flip chip and tape automated  bonding. Out of these, wire bonding is the favorable  interconnect method due to it is least expensive and stable  process. There are a number of wire-bonding methods which  are widely used in semiconductor industry, for instance  thermocompression   bonding,   ultrasonic   bonding   and  thermosonic bonding. Main objective of wire-bonding is to  create the main “communication/contact” between the chips to  outside world.

In the field of semiconductor, Al is one of the metals which  have been extensively used in the process. One of the reasons  Al based metallization is preferable metallization selection  because it is third earth-abundance metals which can form a  very stable, refractory, adherent oxide on the surface. Other  than that, Al based metallization can be easily processed by  current wafer fab processing tool. There were however Al  metallization alone is not suitable to be used for bond pad metal  because electromigration can occur at high current densities  which will then affect the reliability of the semiconductor  devices. The occurrence of electromigration in Al leads to two  distinct wear-out failure modes which are void formation and  Al spiking effect. Void formation is caused by condensation of  vacancies in the Al which eventually leading to open circuit. Al  spiking effect is caused by growth of etch pits into silicon to a  depth which sufficient to short out an underlying junction [1].

One of the solutions to overcome the electromigration issues in  Al, Silicon (Si) and Copper (Cu) precipitates were introduced  into the Al based bond pads to improve the resistance of Al  electromigration effect. Si and Cu precipitates in Al retard the  grain boundary migration of Al ions, leading to an enhanced  lifetime [2]. Nevertheless, the presence of Cu precipitates in Al  might lead to galvanic corrosion if the chip subjected to  uncontrolled moisture environment. Hard Si precipitates might  initiate cratering due to the interaction with wire-bonding  process. Current technologies required thinner Al bond pads,  thus cratering caused by Si precipitates has become more  prominent.

Gold (Au) wires have been used as bonding wires since 1950s.  Due to increase price of gold in the world market, Cu wires  have been chosen as an alternative material to replace gold  wires for wire bonding. According to Breach, Cu wires is  generally cheaper as compared to gold wires, has better  electrical conductivity and more reliable due to slow  intermetallic formation between Cu-Al [3-7]. Although Cu  wires exhibited better electrical conductivity and more reliable,  Cu wires has a higher stiffness in comparison to Au wires.  Apart from this, Cu wire-bonding also needs more ultrasonic  energy and higher bonding force which potentially leading to  cratering issue. This scenario will be worsen when there is  presence of big and hard Si precipitates in the Al bond pads.  The schematic diagrams in Figure. 1 illustrate how the presence  of big Si precipitates can lead to cratering effect.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

First, the wafers were cleaved to a size of \~3.0 cm x 3.0 cm for  easy handling. The cleaved samples were immersed into a  beaker contained of  $36\%$   hydrochloric acid (HCl) for a duration  of 40 minutes at room temperature until all the Al metal bond  pads were fully dissolved. After etching with acid, the samples  were subjected to water rinsed in a beaker follow by cleaning  using acetone as shown in Figure 3 (a) and Figure 3 (b),  respectively. Lastly, the samples were dried in oven at  $80\mathrm{{}^{o}C}$   for  5-10 minutes. For Si precipitates count and size measurement,  the etched samples will later characterize by using SEM  (Hitachi S-4800) at a fixed magnification of 3k. The obtained  SEM images will be transferred to optical microscope software  (Stream) to analyze the Si precipitates sizes and distributions

In order to study the effectiveness and consistent of this  method, wafers produced from two different tool types (Tool X  and Tool Y) were selected for the study. Tool Y is known to  have the hot and cold deposition capability during the plasma  vaporize deposition (PVD) process which capable to produce a  smaller distribution of Si precipitates size and Si precipitates  count.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

Figure 4 (a) depicts the SEM image of Al bond pad which had  been subjected to chemical treatment by HCl acid to remove Al  metal. It can clearly be seen that the Si precipitates were  original in the Al based bond pad was remained on the surface  of bond pad area and not been introduced during the wafer  sawing process. The calculation of the Si precipitates count and  also the biggest size of Si precipitates will be automatically  calculated by software as presented in Figure 4 (b). The biggest  size of the Si precipitates will be taken into account as it  possesses higher risk of inducing Al bond pads cratering after  wire bonding process.

The study of Si precipitates count and Si precipitates size was  also carried out for samples deposited with different PVD tool.  It is well known that the PVD process conditions such as

temperature, pressure and deposition power play a role in  producing a different number of Si precipitates as well as Si  precipitates sizes. Wafers deposited using tool X was prepared  at high PVD temperature which will results in producing a  higher Si precipitates count. Tool Y is capable of producing  wafers in hot cold condition which able to produce a lower Si   precipitates count and smaller Si precipitates size. A total of 3  months data were being generated and analyzed.

JMP results at Figure 5 presents the comparison of Si  precipitates count for the Al bond pad processed using tool X  and tool Y. The 3 months data clearly shown that a much higher  Si precipitates count was observed for the wafers produced by  tool X as compared to that from tool Y. This data clearly shows  this failure analysis methodology capable and effectively  performing measurement counts on Si precipitates. On the other  hand, this data is important to define the next generation of tool  needed for the production to reduce the Si precipitates and  reduce the risk of cratering.

Apart from the Si precipitates count, the sizes of Si precipitates  in Al bond pads produced by tool X and tool Y were also  collected, as presented in Figure 6. The result indicates that tool  X has a much bigger Si precipitates size as compared to tool Y.  Based on the distribution chart, the Si precipitates in the Al  bond pad was in the range of   $0.57~\ensuremath{\upmu\mathrm{m}}\textrm{-}2.0~\ensuremath{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   with a high  population range falls below   $1.76~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . On the contrary, the Si  precipitates size obtained from wafer produced using tool Y is  in the range of   $0.51\upmu\mathrm{m}\cdot1.64\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$   with the high population falls  below   $1.25~\ensuremath{\,\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  . It is clearly demonstrated that different tool  types will also contribute to the different Si precipitates counts  and sizes. Based on this study, tool X is clearly not a suitable  candidate for thin Al bond pads metallization as since it creates  big Si precipitates inside Al bond pads which possess higher  risk of cratering during the wire bonding process.

Wire bonding evaluation was done comparing tool X and tool Y.  Same wire bonding parameter was used on tool X and tool Y in  order to compare the risk of the cratering effect. Wire bonding  result showed tool X generated 1/54 sample of cratering effect  whereas 0/54 units generated 0/54 sample of cratering. ## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In conclusion, a systematic physical failure analysis  approach to investigate the number of Si precipitates, sizes and  distributions in Al bond pads. This case study helps in  developing a robust Al based bond pads as well as selecting the  right tool for future development. The failure analysis approach  also helps the team to further characterize the wire bonding  parameter by using Cu wires to ensure no pad cratering risk.
