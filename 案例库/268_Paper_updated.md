## Sparse Denoising Method for Chip Ultrasonic  Signals Based on Dual Memory-Enhanced Deep  Unfolding Network  

Yi Zhu 1 , Xinfang Duan 1 , Lei  $\mathrm{{Nu}^{1}}$  , Ke Li 1 , Jiefei  $\mathrm{{G}u^{l}}$  , Xinwei Zhao 1 , Michael Pecht 2  1 School of Intelligent Manufacturing, Jiangnan University, 214122 Wuxi, China  2 Center for Advanced Life Cycle Engineering, University of Maryland, College Park, 20742 Maryland, USA  Phone : (0086)   18861807687, Email : lei_su2015@jiangnan.edu.cn

Abstract —This article proposes a sparse denoising method  for ultrasonic signals based on the dual memory-enhanced deep  unfolding network (DME-DUN), which aims to accurately  reconstruct high-frequency ultrasonic signals in flip chip  detection. To address information loss during the iterative  process in deep unfolding networks, two memory enhancement  mechanisms are designed, i.e., adjacent-stage and cross-stage.  For adjacent-stage, the high-throughput information from the  previous stage is introduced into the current stage, thus  facilitating the transmission of information between adjacent  stages. Additionally, the cross-stage memory enhancement  mechanism is applied to promote information fusion between  separated stages. This mechanism is based on Richardson  extrapolation and calculates the output error between every two  adjacent stages to update the sparse coefficient. Extensive  experiments verify that the proposed method effectively  captures weak echo features while removing noise.

Keywords—high   frequency   ultrasonic   detection,   deep  unfolding network, sparse representation, flip chip

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Flip chip technology has become a critical component of  advanced integrated circuit packaging, owing to its superior  performance in electronic packaging. However, as flip chips  progress towards high-density and ultra-fine spacing,  microdefects tend to occur at the chip bond, e.g., cracks, voids  and missing balls [1]. These defects seriously affect the  reliability of chip packaging, making it of great practical  significance to detect flip chips. Acoustic micro imaging  (AMI) is an efficient non-destructive testing technique and has  been widely used in the field of chip defect detection.  However, during the inspection process, in addition to the  inherent noise of the detection system, the ultrasonic signals  are affected by the scattering effect of grains in the highly  scattering material. The scattering effect makes the signals  received from defects more complex, resulting in the  phenomenon of echo aliasing. For microdefects in chips, the  noise interference can significantly affect the signal  characteristics and reduce the identification accuracy of the  defects in solder balls [2]. Therefore, it is necessary to denoise  the chip ultrasonic signal in order to better analyze and  evaluate the internal structure and defects of the chip.

Sparse representation (SR) is an efficient method for  signal denoising due to the sparsity of chip ultrasonic signals.  The solution of sparse representation can be divided into two  categories, i.e., greedy methods and convex relaxation based  approaches. Greedy methods are used to solve the   $l_{0}$  norm  minimization problem, which includes orthogonal matching  pursuit (OMP) [3], multipath matching pursuit (MMP) [4],  stagewise weak orthogonal matching pursuit (SWOMP) [5],  etc. Convex relaxation based approaches transform a non

convex optimization problem into a convex optimization  problem that is easier to solve.  l 1  norm is often used to replace  l 0  norm, thus it can be solved by least absolute shrinkage and  selection   operator   (LASSO)   [6],   iterative   shrinkage  thresholding algorithm (ISTA) [7] and least angle regression  (LAR) algorithm [8]. Nevertheless, these traditional  approaches often necessitate manual feature design, which is  constrained by human limitations and may not fully capture  the intricacies of the data.

With the rapid development of deep learning, its  application in signal processing has become widespread [9].  Fraunhofer-Institut   für   Kommunikation,  Informationsverarbeitung und Ergonomie  (FKIE) developed  an acoustic system which combined artificial intelligence  methods, such as deep learning using spectro-temporal  features [10]. Peng et al. [11] transformed the noisy audio into  image and developed an acoustic denoising neural networks  to extract noisy feature, which is superior to over traditional  methods. Zhou et al. [12] proposed a noise-aware deep  learning model with fullband–subband attention network  (NAFSA-Net), which is effective for acoustic signal denoising.  Deep learning has made many achievements in extracting  complex features hidden in data [13], however, the majority  of deep learning architectures are considered as a black box,  which lacks interpretability.

Recently, a category of techniques known as deep  unfolding networks (DUNs) has emerged. The basic idea of  DUNs is unfolding iterative steps of classical optimization  algorithms into network modules with multiple unfolding  stages. DUNs have been applied to inverse problems in  various fields, e.g., denoising, deblurring and super-resolution  reconstruction. Inspired by ISTA, Zhang et al. [14] proposed  a network called ISTA-Net to optimize the general   $l_{1}$   norm  model, which has good interpretability while realizing fast and  accurate reconstruction. Yang et al. [15] unfolded the  alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) into a  depth architecture and applied it to compressed sensing,  improving the reconstruction accuracy and speed of  compressed sensing imaging. Mou et al. [16] proposed a deep  generalized unfolding network (DGUNet) for image  restoration, forming a model that can be trained end-to-end  and maintain interpretability. Sheng et al. [17] proposed a  deep network called SRNet, which expanded the iterative  optimization process of traditional sparse coding, thus  improving the denoising performance of traditional sparse  models. Gkillas et al. [18] proposed a new sparse coding  optimization problem, which used learnable regularization  terms for interested signal characteristics and utilized neural  networks as priori to capture potential correlations of signals.  However, details and features captured by each stage may not  be fully preserved in these network architectures when

information is transmitted between stages. The loss of certain  information significantly restricts the network performance.

To address the above issues, we propose a dual memoryenhanced deep unfolding network (DME-DUN) for chip  ultrasonic   signals   denoising,   which   combines   the  interpretability of iterative algorithms with the superior  performance of deep learning. Our method divides the  problem of information loss into two aspects, i.e., adjacentstage and cross-stage information loss, and then designs two  memory enhancement mechanisms. The major contributions  of this work are as follows.

Our DME-DUN maps the iterative steps of the  proximal gradient descent algorithm to multiple stages  of the network. The network naturally inherits the  interpretability of the iterative process of classical  optimization algorithms.       We design an adjacent-stage memory enhancement  mechanism to select intermediate features from  different layers within each stage for integration. Thus,  high-throughput information can be transmitted to the  next stage, promoting information exchange between  adjacent stages.       We design a cross-stage memory enhancement  mechanism based on Richardson extrapolation, which  updates the current sparse coefficient by calculating  the output error between every two adjacent stages.  Therefore, information from earlier stages can be  effectively integrated.       Extensive experiments show that our DME-DUN can  accurately reconstruct the weak echo features in the  presence of noise, which effectively improves the  reconstruction quality.

## II.   B ACKGROUND 

When detecting ultrasonic echo signals, it is usually  believed that the received echo signals are linear superposition  of reflected signals from different interfaces. Considering the  scattered noise, the ultrasonic signal of the chip is expressed  as

$$\pmb{y}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\pmb{\phi}_{i}(t)+\pmb{\varepsilon}(t)$$
where   $y(t)$   is the received echo signal,   $c_{i}$   is the reflection  function of the  $i$  -th interface,   $\mathbf{\nabla}\phi(t)$   is the reflected signal of the  i -th interface, and   $n$   is the total number of echoes. Since there  are only a limited number of reflecting interfaces and defects  in the chip that generate echo signals, SR methods can be used  for chip ultrasonic signals.

$$\pmb{y}=\pmb{D}\pmb{x}+\pmb{\varepsilon}$$
where   $\pmb{D}$   is an overcomplete dictionary, and   $_x$  is the sparse  coefficient. Given the input signal  $\pmb{y}$  , the sparse representation  is transformed into the following optimization problem.

$$\arg\operatorname*{min}_{x}\frac{1}{2}\,||\,D x-y\,||_{2}^{2}\,+\lambda R(x)$$
The first term is used to limit the approximation between the  input signal and the reconstructed signal. The second term  represents the regularization term related to prior information,  where   is the regularization parameter, and   $R(x)$   is the  regularization function.

## III. PROPOSED APPROACH

## A.   DME-DUN Model  

The proximal gradient descent (PGD) algorithm is used to  solve (3), which is solved iteratively by the following two  steps.

$$\pmb{r}^{(k)}=\pmb{x}^{(k-1)}-\rho\pmb{D}^{\mathrm{T}}(\pmb{D}\pmb{x}^{(k-1)}-\pmb{y})$$
$$\mathbf{\boldsymbol{x}}^{(k)}=\mathbf{\boldsymbol{p}}\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda,R}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{r}}^{(k)})$$
Equation (4) performs the gradient descent process, where  $k$   is  the number of iterations and  $\rho$   is the step size. Equation (5)  represents the proximal mapping, where   $x$   is solved by the  proximal operator.

Specifically, the iterative solving process is unfolded to  form a deep learning architecture, as shown in Fig. 1. In  existing DUNs, most gradient descent modules (GDMs)  operate with single-channel input and output. However, the

efficiency of single-channel information transmission is  limited, and thus, we design a multi-channel GDM. By  splitting (4),   $\pmb{r}^{(k)}$   actually corresponds to three components,  with the first two related to   ${\pmb x}^{(k-1)}$    and the last related to  y . In  the proposed multi-channel GDM, these three components are  converted to the multi-channel feature domain.

The proximal mapping module (PMM) is composed of the  SCConv-ReLU-SCConv-Soft-SCConv-ReLU-SCConv  structure, where SCConv in [19] is the self-calibration  convolution. SCConv expands the receptive field through  internal communication and thus enhancing the feature  extraction capability. Soft represents the soft threshold  operation, which suppresses small sparse coefficients and thus  improving the sparsity of the inversion results. To address the  issue of information loss during the iterative solution, we  design two memory enhancement mechanisms in the proximal  mapping module. The details are provided in the following  two sub-sections.

## B.   Adjacent-Stage Memory Enhancement  

Due to the fact that each stage in DUNs can only capture  local information within that stage, the important information  from previous stages may be lost as the stages increase. To  alleviate the loss of useful information between adjacent  stages, We design a high-throughput adjacent-stage memory  mechanism in PMM, aiming to ensure optimal information  flow and further improve the overall performance of the  model.

To facilitate the exchange of information between iteration  stages, we select the intermediate features of different layers  in each stage. These features are then inserted into the next  stage for adjacent-stage information interaction. Specifically,  we extract the output of the current stage after Soft in PMM,  and then it is transmitted to PMM of the next stage together  with the output of GDM. Next, the output of GDM   $\pmb{r}^{(k)}\in\mathrm{~\emph~{~}~}^{N_{f}\times W\times H}$    is fed into PMM of each stage as the initial  input, which is then concatenated with   $\pmb{r}^{(k-1)}\in\mathsf{\Omega}^{N_{f}\times W\times H}$   and   $\pmb{z}^{(k-1)}\in\quad^{N_{f}\times W\times H}$   from the previous stage. Then, a   $1\!\times\!1$   Conv is  used for multi-channel fusion to obtain the final input. The  calculation process can be expressed as

$$\begin{array}{r l}&{\pmb{r}^{(k c)}=\mathrm{Concat}(\pmb{r}^{(k-1)},\pmb{r}^{(k)},\pmb{z}^{(k-1)})}\\ &{\pmb{r}^{(i n)}=\mathrm{Conv}(\pmb{r}^{(k c)})}\end{array}$$
where   $\pmb{r}^{(k c)}\in\ \ \ ^{3N_{f}\times W\times H}$   denotes the fused information in the  current stage, and   $\pmb{r}^{(i n)}\in\quad^{N_{f}\times W\times H}$   denotes the final input of  PMM.

Through the concatenation of   $r^{(k-1)}$   and   $\pmb{z}^{(k-1)}$  , the highthroughput information of the previous stage is introduced  into the current stage. The information is then transmitted to  the next stage, prompting the network to learn the data features  with greater accuracy. In this way, the richness of features is  preserved, thus enhancing the memory between adjacent  stages.

## C.   Cross-Stage Memory Enhancement  

With the transmission of information, the information  obtained in the earlier stages tends to be forgotten. However,  the output of each stage contains some useful information and  its loss may lead to the omission of certain details in signal

reconstruction. To address this problem, we design a crossstage memory enhancement mechanism, aiming to integrate  the information from previous stages into the current iteration  and obtain a more accurate reconstruction result.

Richardson extrapolation is an effective numerical  analysis method, whose basic idea is to estimate a more  accurate approximation by calculating the differences  between multiple approximations. Inspired by Richardson  extrapolation, we incorporate a memory retrieval step before   $\pmb{x}^{(k-1)}$   in each stage is input into GDM. The updated formula  is designed as follows.

$$\begin{array}{c}{{{\pmb x}^{(k-1)!}={\pmb x}^{(k-1)}+[({\pmb x}^{(k-1)}-{\pmb x}^{(k-2)})-({\pmb x}^{(k-2)}-{\pmb x}^{(k-3)})}}\\ {{-\cdots-({\pmb x}^{(k-p)}-{\pmb x}^{(k-p-1)})]/\,(2^{\,p}-1),}}\end{array}$$
Where   $p$   is the order and denotes the number of previous  approximation solutions used in calculating the new value.  The current sparse coefficient is updated by selecting the  appropriate order and calculating the error between the sparse  coefficients of each two adjacent stages. This mechanism  facilitates effective information fusion across different stages.

The accuracy of the calculation results increases with the  larger order, however, this also leads to an increase in  computational complexity. To strike a balance between  accuracy and calculation cost, we set the order p to 3, i.e., we  use the results of the earlier three stages in (7).

Therefore, when  $k$   satisfies  $k\!\ge\!4$  , the results of the previous  stages will be utilized at each stage in order to obtain a more  accurate sparse coefficient as the input. The new output   ${\pmb x}^{(k-1)^{*}}$  through memory backtracking incorporates the information  accumulated in the previous three stages, effectively  improving the result accuracy.

## V.   C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we propose a denoising method based on  DME-DUN to achieve accurate reconstruction of chip  ultrasonic signals. To address the issue of valuable  information loss during the transmission process, we design  two memory enhancement mechanisms of adjacent stages and  separated stages. For adjacent-stage information loss, we  select the intermediate features of different layers in each  stage for integration, and these high-throughput information is  transmitted to the next stage, thus increasing the information  interaction between adjacent stages. For cross-stage  information loss, we fuse the accumulated information of  previous stages into the current stage by calculating the output  error between each two adjacent stages, thus obtaining a more  accurate sparse coefficient. Simulation and experimental  results show that the proposed method achieves the optimal  denoising performance while improving the interpretability of  deep learning.
