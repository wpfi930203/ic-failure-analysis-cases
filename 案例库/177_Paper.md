## PBTI Study in Native High Voltage Device  

B. A. Tsai\* 1 , Z. A. Peng 1 , H. W. Chuang 2 ,   C. F. Chen 2 , C. Y. Hsu 1     1 Technology Development Division IV, Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation    2 Reliability Engineering Department, Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation  \* Phone: +886-3-5795000-4923  Fax: +886-3-5792164   Email: batsaia@psc.com.tw

Abstract -  The native HV device (w/o Vt implantation) and regular  HV device (w/i Vt implantation) of PBTI Vt shift behaviors are  investigated in this paper, and an envisioned mechanism is  provided. Native HV device showed a high Vt shift at the beginning  of  $\mathbf{Vg}$   stress, the  $\mathbf{Ig-Vg}$   curve measured supported this observation;  also, the mismatch characterizations were also measured and  compared in both native and regular HV devices.

Keywords  –  native, high voltage, HV, mismatch, PBTI, BTI,  device reliability .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Accurate circuit design requires availability of confident  transistor mismatch. Random device fluctuations of MOSFETs  limits performance for accurate analog complementary  metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) circuits, some literatures  on MOSFET mismatch show that it is  primarily impacted by   random dopant fluctuations (RDF), poly-silicon granularity, line  edge roughness and fixed oxide charges [1]-[5].

RDF comes from both the randomness of the location and the  number of dopant atoms in the active MOSFET channel. The  variation of Vt is between two identically MOSFETs devices,  which is referred to as transistor mismatch [6]–[9]. Transistor  mismatch result is proportional to the inverse square root of the  active and gate overlap area [10]–[12].

Since long lifetime is needed, the device characterization shift  will reflect its degradation and mismatch of transistor  performance. Therefore, device characterization shift needs to  be minimized. Positive bias-temperature instability (PBTI) is an  important reliability consideration for MOSFETs. PBTI  reliability is tested at high temperature - a bias condition setup  with a positive bias at the gate and the other nodes of the device  are connected to the ground. Stress and measurement are  conducted at high temperature. The voltage stress in high  temperature causes an increase to the interface state density and  the electrons will be trapped in the dielectric, resulting in a drive  current decrease and a higher Vt [13].

High voltage devices have been the mainstream technology  behind aerospace-defense and other RF power applications  [14]-[15]. In this paper, we investigated the PBTI of high  voltage device with regular HV device and native HV device,  and then envisage the physical mechanism to explain the  experimental results.

## III.   R ESULTS  &   D ISCUSSIONS   

The transistor Vt was measured in the operating region. A pair  of identically HV devices placed in the same AA was used in the  Vt measurements, HV devices have a common source, bulk and  gate terminals, each HV device separates at the drain terminal,  the current flow is in the same lattice direction from source to  drain to eliminate mobility factor by a same channel lattice  direction. The data retrieved from wafer mapping, difference of  width and length dimension are designed to characterize.  Standard deviation of Vt difference σ (ΔVt) is calculated from  the measurement between two adjacent identical devices. Fig. 2  shows the HV structure used to characterize the Vt mismatch for  wi/wo well and channel implantations (Vt implantation), the  difference of two Vt values ΔVt is related to RDF. The σ (ΔVt)  of the native HV device is lower than for the regular HV device,

as shown in Fig. 3,  the slope in native HV device is also lower,  which means a smaller Vt variation in a small dimension area  because RDF factor is reduced by taking off Vt implantation.

PBTI stress was performed at gate side with low, middle and  high voltages. During stress, threshold voltage was measured  with the constant current method,  the gate voltage at which the  drain current reaches a pre-defined fixed value, and  Vds=operation voltage is used to define the Vt. Stress time  related with Vt shift is recorded.

Vt shifts of native HV device with low, middle and high  voltages are compared and shown in Fig. 4 (a). For low   $\mathrm{Vg}$  stress, a smaller Vt shift was at the beginning of   $\mathrm{Vg}$  stress,  following the  $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress time increasing, Vt shift also increased.  For high   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress, a larger Vt shift is observed at the beginning  of Vg stress, Vt shift also increased as stress time increased. The  order of Vt shift at stress beginning is: low  $<$   middle < high Vg  stress, the order of the slope of Vt shift is: low  $>$   middle  $>$   high   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress, Vt shift converges as   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress time increases.

For regular Vt device, as gate was stressed with low, middle  and high voltage, a small Vt shift was observed, no Vt shift  convergence at long time Vt stress, as shown in Fig. 4 (b).

Fig. 5 figured out the Vt shift model during PBTI stress for  native HV device. At low   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress application, some electrons  are injected into gate oxide, as shown in Fig. 5 (a), Vt shift is  observed at the beginning of stress, Vt shift increases as stress  time increases. As higher   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress is applied, more electrons  are injected into the oxide as shown in Fig. 5 (b), and a larger Vt  shift is seen at the beginning of stress, Vt shift also increases as  stress time increases; however, a gentler slope of Vt shift is  observed. The native HV Vt shift is concentrated in the same

place as stress time elapses in both low and high   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress  voltage, it can be explained by the following description  regarding the native HV Vt shift concentration observed in both  low and high Vg stress Voltage: At initial stress, gate oxide bulk  is electrically neutral, the electrons can be easily injected into  the gate oxide, as stress time increases, more electrons exist in  the gate oxide as shown in Fig. 5 (c), gate oxide bulk with  negative charge then obstructs the electrons from the channel to  the gate oxide, Vt shift will concentrate in the same place as  stress time elapses in both low and high   $\mathrm{Vg}$   stress voltage.

Fig. 6 (a) and (b) show small Vt shift caused during PBTI  stress for regular HV device. At PBTI stress beginning, either  with low or high  $\mathrm{Vg}$   voltage, very few electrons are injected into  the gate oxide due to thin inversion layer by w/i Vt implantation  of regular HV device; after a long period PBTI stress, still very  few electrons are injected into gate oxide.

In Fig. 7, the Ig-Vg curve of native and regular HV devices  are measured to verify the electron injection from the channel  region, the source/ drain/ body of bias conditions are set the  same as PBTI stress. At lower Vg voltage, native HV device is  observed to have a higher Ig, meaning more electrons are  injected into the gate from the channel, source and drain regions,  leading to an obvious Vt shift at the beginning of stress.

## IV. CONCLUSION  

In this paper, the Vt shift under PBTI stress of native and  regular HV devices are investigated. For native HV device’s, a  larger Vt shift is observed at the beginning of Vg stress, which is  related to the gate bias magnitude due to the thick inversion  layer. Meanwhile, when the same gate bias magnitude is applied  to the regular HV device, a small Vt shift is observed due to the  thin inversion layer by w/i Vt implantation of the regular HV  device. The Ig-Vg curve verified the mechanism.
