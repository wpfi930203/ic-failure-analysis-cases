## A Novel Reverse Conducting SOI-LIGBT with  Double Integrated NMOS for Enhanced   Reverse Recovery    

Ajiang Li, Shaohong Li, Long Zhang, Jing Zhu, Tian Tian, Yanqin Zou, Guichuang Zhu  and Weifeng Sun\*  National ASIC System Engineering Research Center, Southeast University, Nanjing, China    Phone: 86-25-83795811   Fax: 86-25-83795077 \*swffrog@seu.edu.cn

Abstract-   The reverse recovery failure of the inherent diode in  Separated-Shorted-Anode lateral insulated gate bipolar transistor  (SSA-LIGBT) is investigated through Sentaurus TCAD. During  reverse recovery process, high current commutating rate di/dt will  result in large reverse recovery current peak. It is found that large  reverse recovery current peak flowing through the P-body can  easily trigger the parasitic NPN transistor at the emitter side.  Subsequently, the triggered NPN transistor finally results in the  reverse recovery failure of the inherent diode in SSA-LIGBT. A  novel structure with double integrated NMOS is proposed to  achieve high reverse recovery robustness. Furthermore, the new  structure can eliminate Negative Differential Resistance regime  completely at the same time.

Keywords  –  SOI-LIGBT, Double Integrated NMOS, Parasitic  NPN, Reverse recovery failure, Negative Differential Resistance  Regime .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

In recent years, SOI-LIGBTs have been the key components  in Power Integrated Circuit (PIC) due to their high forward  current density, high input impedance and feasibility of  integration.  Separated-Shorted-Anode LIGBTs (SSA-LIGBTs)  [1] are famous for high turn-off speed and great reverse  conducting capability. Therefore, SSA-LIGBTs can be used as  power switchers in half-bridge topology with no extra  antiparallel diode, because the inherent diodes in SSA-LIGBTs  can act as freewheeling diode. As a result, the whole circuit  volume could be greatly reduced. Unfortunately, in on-state,  SSA-LIGBTs exist severe Negative Differential Resistance  regime (NDR regime), which would cause current filament  failure. In order to overcome this issue, many efforts, such as  the STA-LIGBT [2], the MSA-LIGBT [3] and the  PRTC-LIGBT [4] have been proposed successively to suppress  the NDR regime. However, the inherent diodes in these  RC-LIGBTs mentioned above would easily suffer from  unexpected reverse recovery failure during reverse recovery  period, which limits their usage in high frequency circuit.  Therefore, apart from suppressing the NDR regime, it is also  urgent for RC-LIGBT designers to improve the robustness of  reverse recovery of the inherent diodes in these RC-LIGBTs  mentioned above.

Fig. 4 shows cross-section of the proposed DN-LIGBT. The  proposed structure features double integrated NMOS. The  NMOS located at collector side is marked as NMOS1. Another  NMOS (which is marked as NMOS2) is connected to the

$\mathbf{N}^{+}.$  -emitter and isolated by deep oxide trench at emitter side.  Therefore, as shown in Fig. 4, the current conducting path is  divided into two parts in the proposed structure at the collector  side. The first path (marked as path-I) is to   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   region through  electron conduction channel in NMOS1. The second path  (marked as path-II) is to  $\mathrm{{P^{+}}}$   region through inherent PN junction  at the collector side. Fig. 5 (b) shows the distribution of total  forward conducting current among two current conduction  paths at the collector side during the initial forward conduction  stage in the DN-LIGBT. We find that path-II starts to conduct  current and  $\mathbf{P}^{+}$    region begins injecting hole carriers into the drift  region at about 0.7V. As a result, conductivity modulation  occurs in the drift region at the initial forward conduction stage.  Through optimizing the doping concentration of PB region (the  P-body region of NMOS1 which is labelled in Fig. 4), the  NMOS1, however, is turned on and starts to conduct electron  current only if the forward voltage of the collector electrode is  nearly above 1V. Therefore the proposed DN-LIGBT will  operate in bipolar mode from beginning to end and avoid the  transition from unipolar mode to bipolar mode, which could  cause Negative Differential Resistance regime.

The DN-LIGBTs have reverse conducting capability as well.  Its reverse current conduction path is composed of two  embedded diode (D1 and D2, which are labelled in Fig. 4)  connected in series and is indicated by red dotted line in Fig. 6.  Fig. 5 (c) shows reverse conducting characteristics of  SSA-LIGBT and DN-LIGBT. The DN-LIGBT internal diode

shows a little higher onset voltage than the internal diode in  SSA-LIGBT. Fig. 7 shows the reverse recovery waveforms of  the inherent diode in DN-LIGBT and SSA-LIGBT under  different di/dt at the operating current of 0.5A. Reverse  recovery failure take place in SSA-LIGBT under di/dt =   $30\mathrm{A}/\upmu\mathrm{s}$  , however, DN-LIGBT shows no reverse recovery  failure under different di/dt ranging from   $60\mathrm{A}/\upmu\mathrm{s}$   to   $680\mathrm{A}/\upmu\mathrm{s}$  .  Fig. 8 shows hole current path under   $\mathrm{{dt}/\mathrm{{dt}}\,=\,680A/\upmu s}$   in the  DN-LIGBT at time T2. T2 is the moment when reverse  recovery current reach its peak value. At time T2, even though  high-density hole current flow through P-body under   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$  -emitter region, the parasitic NPN transistor would not be  triggered. This is because that the NMOS2 is turned off at this  moment, as a result,   $\mathbf{N}^{+}.$  -emitter would not inject electron into  the n-drift region. Consequently, the reverse recovery failure  caused by triggered parasitic transistor can be effectively  avoided. Fig. 9 shows the dependence of BV,   $\mathrm{V_{on}}$  (at 1A) and  $\mathrm{t_{rr}}$    of inherent diode on  $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{s}}$   (distance between N1 and N2, which is  labelled in Fig. 4) in DN-LIGBT. When   $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{s}}$  is decreased from   $10\upmu\mathrm{m}$   to   $20\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , BV firstly increases and then is kept at about  610V. In addition, the floating metal electrode [6], which is  labelled in Fig. 4, could accelerates the recombination of  electron-hole pair at the collector side. As a result, shorter   $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{s}}$   is  beneficial to improving reverse recovery time   $\mathrm{tr}$   but adversely  increase the on-state voltage  $\mathrm{V_{on}}$  .

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, the failure mechanism of reverse recovery for  SSA-LIGBT is investigated. It is found that the triggering of  parasitic NPN transistor is the key reason for reverse recovery  failure in SSA-LIGBT. The proposed DN-LIGBT not only  avoid the reverse recovery failure successfully but also  eliminate the NDR regime completely. Consequently, the  DN-LIGBT can achieve high robustness both in forward  conduction phase and reverse conduction phase.
