## Degradation Mechanism of Threshold Voltage of 1.2-kV  Planar-Gate SiC MOSFETs Under the Combined Action  of Surge Current and HTGB Stress   

Dezhi Ma 1, 2 , Tianyi Sha 2 , Liang   $\mathrm{He}^{2}$  , Zhiyuan He 2, 3 , Yuan Chen 2 , Yijun Shi 2 , Xinghuan Chen 2 , Jia-Yue Yang 1 , Yiqiang Chen 2 , Guoguang Lu 2  1  School of Energy and Power Engineering, Shandong University, Jinan 250100, China;   2  China Electronic Product Reliability and Environmental Testing Research Institute (CEPREI), Guangzhou 511370, China;   3  School of Integrated Circuits, Guangdong University of Technology, Guangzhou 510006, China.   Email: heliang@ceprei.com, xhchen01@outlook.com, jy_yang@sdu.edu.cn  CEPREI proposal and implement this work.

Abstract —This paper investigates the degradation  mechanism of threshold voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{th}})$   of 1.2-kV planargate SiC MOSFETs under surge current and high  temperature gate bias (HTGB) cross-stress. By measuring  and comparing the electrical parameters of the device  under test (DUT), we analyzed the drift mechanism of  V th   of DUT under cross-stress. Compared with HTGB stress,  DUT under cross-stress has lower   V th  drift. We  investigated the trap characteristics of DUT under crossstress using low-frequency noise (LFN) technology. The  introduced surge current stress can improve the interface  quality of degraded DUT.   The results may provide useful  guidance for the design and long-term application of SiC  MOSFETs.

Keywords — SiC MOSFETs, surge current, HTGB,  threshold voltage, trap

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Silicon carbide (SiC) MOSFETs are widely used in  energy conversion fields such as rail transit and electric  vehicles due to their excellent electrical and thermal  properties[1-3].   However,   devices   may   experience  performance degradation or even failure during application,  and reliability remains a bottleneck that restricts the largescale commercial application of SiC MOSFETs. Considering  the harsh conditions in actual working conditions, evaluating  the reliability of devices through aging experiments is of great  significance for the safe operation of power electronic  equipment[4, 5].

In recent years, the potential of body diodes in SiC  MOSFETs to replace anti-parallel diodes has made them a  research hotspot. Surge test is a commonly used method to  evaluate the reliability of SiC metal-oxide-semiconductor  field-effect transistor (MOSFET) body diodes. At the moment  the circuit is turned on, the inductive load discharge causes the  body diode to withstand a considerable surge current stress.  Many groups have studied the degradation mechanism of SiC  MOSFETs under surge current stress. Palanisamy et al.  investigated the thermomechanical behavior of devices under  surge current stress[6]. Jiang et al. investigated the  degradation of device electrical parameters under repeated  surge current stress[7]. Ma et al. established the relationship  between electrical parameter degradation and microscopic  defects[8]. However, the surge current stress in these works is  achieved by applying periodic surge pulses. In actual working  conditions, SiC MOSFETs may experience both gate bias  stress and surge current stress simultaneously, and the

degradation mechanism of the device under the synergistic  effect of these two stresses has not been studied yet.

In this paper, we investigated the degradation mechanism  of the threshold voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{th}})$   of SiC MOSFETs under the  cross-stress of surge current and high temperature gate bias  (HTGB). In the cross-stress experiment, we regularly  measured the electrical parameters of the device and discussed  and analyzed the drift mechanism of threshold voltage and  trap characteristics. The results may provide useful guidance  for the design and long-term application of SiC MOSFETs.

This work is based on two types of stress test: surge  current and HTGB. The experiment was divided into three  groups, namely the cross-stress experimental group subjected  to two types of stress and the control group only subjected to  surge current or HTGB stress. The gate voltage for HTGB  stress is  $20\;\mathrm{V}$   and the temperature is  $150~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  . The gate voltage  for surge current stress is  $-5\mathrm{~V~}$  , with an amplitude of 90 A.  For the cross-stress experimental group, each cycle includes  100 hours of HTGB stress and 200 subsequent surge pulses  introduced. The threshold voltage is measured after HTGB  stress and every 100 surge pulses. The aging and measuring  stages of the cross-stress experimental group and the HTGB  stress control group were conducted together. Subsequently,  we increased the stress while shortening the stress time,  exploring the influence of surge current stress conditions on  the experimental results.

## C.   Variation in other electrical parameters  

We also measured the gate leakage current   $\left(I_{\mathrm{gss}}\right)$   and  drain leakage current   $\left(I_{\mathrm{dss}}\right)$   of the DUT before and after crossstress to evaluate gate oxide integrity and blocking  characteristics. As shown in Fig. 7 (a), both  $I_{\mathrm{d}s s}$   and  $I_{\mathrm{gss}}$   were  slightly reduced and within the safe range after cross-stress.  Fig. 7 (b) shows the third quadrant characteristics of the DUT  before and after cross-stress, which were measured under the  condition of channel closure. The almost overlapping curves  indicate that the stress in this study did not introduce package  degradation[12].

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

This work reveals the degradation mechanism of SiC  MOSFETs threshold voltage under the combined action of  surge current and HTGB stress. The   $V_{\mathrm{th}}$   drift of the device  under cross-stress is smaller than that under HTGB stress, and  its physical mechanism is that surge current induces hole  injection and accumulates in the gate oxide. Meanwhile, the  introduced surge current can alleviate the severe degradation  caused by HTGB stress. The results of LFN indicate that the  interface quality is also improved with the recovery of   $V_{\mathrm{th}}$  . The  results of this article contribute to the establishment of an  accurate lifetime model for SiC MOSFETs in power  conversion systems.
