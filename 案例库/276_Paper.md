## The Impact of Endurance Degradation in Analog  RRAM for In-Situ Training  

Yuyi Liu, Bin Gao \* , Meiran Zhao, Huaqiang Wu, and He Qian   1   Institute of Microelectronics, Tsinghua University, Beijing, 100084, China.  \* Email: gaob1@tsinghua.edu.cn

Abstract-   The analog resistive random access memory (RRAM) is  attractive for neuromorphic computing systems as synaptic  weights. However, cycling endurance is one of the most important  limitations to reliability. In this work, two endurance degradation  models for analog RRAM are proposed based on the  measurement results. Then, the models are utilized to investigate  the impact on in-situ training accuracy. We use the MNIST  dataset trained in a 3-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP), with the  cycling endurance of RRAM degrading. The results indicate that  endurance degradation will decrease the in-situ training accuracy.

Keywords  – analog RRAM, endurance degradation (ED), in-situ  training, accuracy, weight

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

The resistive random access memory (RRAM) is a promising  candidate to implement a neuromorphic system due to its high  speed, high density and analog switching character [1]. Besides,  RRAM can exhibits analog memory functionality with  multilevel conductance [2]. The analog switching characteristic  enables RRAM to realize the analog weights. Therefore,  RRAM is important for neural networks training in-situ.  Recently, neuromorphic or neural network algorithms using  RRAM have been extensively investigated, e.g., 1Kb  neuromorphic RRAM arrays recognizing brainwave signal [3],  12x12 RRAM arrays based convolution kernel [4], 1Kb RRAM  array based the sparse coding [5], 10x10 phase-change memory  array based Restricted Boltzmann Machine and image feature  extraction [6]. In addition, a lot of works have demonstrated the  in-situ training on a neural network system built with a RRAM  array [1][7][8]. However, the synapse weight or the  conductance of RRAM changes frequently in in-situ training  with the effect of pulses. Endurance degrades with the pulse  number increasing and RRAM can fail at last. Although some  previous works have investigated the impact of endurance  degradation on training a neural network, few work has  comprehensively evaluate the endurance influence with the real  measured   degradation   characteristics,   especially   the  degradation behaviors with variability. This paper analyzes the  impact of endurance degradation for in-situ training  systematically. First, experimental results are provided for the  RRAM endurance degradation phenomenon and two models  are proposed based on the results. Second, a 3-layer multilayer  perceptron (MLP) is used to train the MNIST dataset [9]. Then,  we discuss the simulation results of different models  respectively. At last, we conclude the impact of endurance  degradation on in-situ training accuracy.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

First, the experiment was implemented to study the  endurance degradation behavior. In this paper, analog RRAM  device, which is TiN/ETML/HfOx/TiN stack, is used. An  electro-thermal modulation layer (ETML) [2] contributes to  excellent linear analog switching behaviors. According to the  experimental results, the endurance degradation shows gradual  changing for some devices (Fig. 1 (a)) [10]. While for other  devices, the endurance degradation shows sudden change (Fig.  1(b)) [11]. Based on these experiment results, two endurance  degradation models were proposed, as shown in Fig. 2 (a) and  (b), the gradual changing and the sudden changing respectively.  For the gradual changing endurance degradation model (Fig. 2  (a)), four detailed cases were considered. Each case has a  different failure position, which is called conductance  cross-point (GX). In addition to endurance degradation, the  nonlinearity, dynamic range, and variations were taken into  account in the device behavioral model.

A three-layer fully connected neural network (Fig. 3) was  used to investigate the impact of endurance degradation of  analog switching. The training set is the MNIST dataset. The  number of neurons of input layer is 784, which is equal to the  picture pixel of the MNIST dataset. The neurons of the hidden  layer are 200, and the output layer is 10. It is worth noted that  the hidden layer is redundancy, and normally less than 100  neurons are enough. The conductance of RRAM represents the  synaptic weight. Therefore, the RRAM crossbar implements  matrix-vector multiplication as the role of weigh matrix in  in-situ training. The weight update follows the batch Manhattan  rule. This learning rule just points out the switching direction.  Thus, the SET/RESET operation was implemented in each  cycle based on the gradient direction of the backpropagation  algorithm. In order to observe the effect of endurance  degradation for neural network, 300 times of trainings were  repeated on the RRAM crossbar.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

This paper analyzes the impact of endurance degradation  (ED) for in-situ training systematically. The simulation was  conducted based on the gradual and sudden changing endurance  degradation models. ED will cause the accuracy loss, while the  accuracy loss of gradual ED is smaller and slower than the  sudden ED model (Fig. 4). For gradual ED model, the accuracy  loss is smaller when the GX is closer to middle conductance

position. Besides, cell failure ratio is positive correlation with  conductance position, which reveals that the trend of training is  in the direction of small weights. In general, the accuracy loss  and cell failure are both related to the failure position, however  there is no correlation between the two, which means the  decrease of cell failure ratio will not cause the reduction of  accuracy (Fig. 5). For sudden endurance degradation model, the  accuracy loss is more significant than the gradual ED, so as the  cell failure ratio. A large proportion of cells failure cause the  huge accuracy loss for sudden ED model (Fig. 6). The neural  network will adapt to cell failure situation with different weight  patterns. The higher accuracy loss causes the more centered  weight distribution, and the weight distributes around zero,  which also indicates that the trend of training is in the direction  of small weights (Fig. 7).

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

The impact of analog RRAM endurance degradation in  in-situ training is analyzed in this work. Two model are  proposed and the simulation reveals the gradual ED model  cause less and slower accuracy loss than the sudden ED model.  The trend of training is in the direction of  small weights, which  has certain guiding significance for the algorithms.
