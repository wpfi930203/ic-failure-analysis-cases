## An effective monitored and improved method to  control moisture for outgoing FOSB   

Yi-Ying Chen \* ,  Chiu-E Tseng , Hsin-Wen Fan, Chih-Chao Pai  Affiliation –QA10/20/30, Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation   No.12, Lixing 1st Rd., East Dist., Hsinchu City 300, Taiwan (R.O.C.)  Phone: +886 03-5795000 ext 3054 Email: danielchen@powerchip.com

Abstract - Quality control of outgoing wafer products is a critical  item in the technical industry. An appropriate keeping  environment (Humidity/Temperature…etc.) is necessary after the  complex wafer manufacturing process to ensure the wafer quality.  FOSB is an essential material during the wafer packing process  and it can provide an isolated environment to protect outgoing  wafers. But each FOSB has different properties, like different  used times, clean recipe, moisture content…etc. During vacuum  packaging, the humidity and pressure between the FOSB outside  wall and packing bag reduces, and causes the moisture inside the  FOSB to diffuse out. This then causes a humidity increase inside  the FOSB, where wafers are placed (Experiment result humidity  increase   ${\tt>}{\pmb5}\%$  ).   But the general method for detecting solid  moisture content (like moisture analyser) is not suitable in FOSB,  as it is such a big and unbreakable item. In this study, we  understood the mechanism for inside humidity change after  vacuum packing and found an effective monitored/improved  method to control moisture for outgoing FOSB   and ensure our  packing processes are suitable, uniform, and the environmental  status can be controlled precisely. Through this method we can  enhance our quality control abilities and provide more assurance  for both customers and producers.

Keywords – Humidity, FOSB, Moisture, Diffusion.

## I. Introduction  

Humidity is an important factor in the wafer keeping  procedure. A high humidity may lead to pad corrosion (Fig 1),  but a low humidity may cause ESD risk [1, 2]. So how to keep  an optimal condition during the wafer outgoing process is an  important item.

FOSB is a container used in wafer packing, composed  mainly of polycarbonate (PC), it can maintain an isolated  environment to protect the products inside. It is well known that  PC is an absorbent material, so some moisture inside FOSB is  unavoidable. Also, each FOSB is not all in the same spec, and  may be differ due to different vendors, clean recipes, dry  situations…etc.

From Fick’s second law (Fig 2), substance will flux from a  high concentration region to a low concentration region and the  driving force for flux comes from the concentration gradient.  During the outgoing packing process, the vacuum environment  will lead to “moisture gradient” and induce the moisture inside  the FOSB to diffuse out (Fig 3). The following experiment

shows varied results for the quantity of moisture diffusing out  for each FOSB due to the different moisture content.

Step 1. System is in equilibrium  $\Rightarrow$   Step 2. Vacuum process leads to moisture  concentration gradient between packing bags and the outside wall of the FOSB   $\Rightarrow$   Step 3. Due to moisture concentration gradient, moisture captured in FOSB  will diffuse out in all directions.

In this study, we find that FOSB with different clean recipes  may lead to a more than  $5\%$   humidity difference (inside FOSB)  after the vacuum packing process. This may cause quality  difference between different FOSBs, and may pose as a quality  concern after a long storage period.

In quality control, this uncontrollable difference is  unacceptable. The general method for detection of solid  moisture content is not suitable in FOSBs, which are such big  and unbreakable items. So in the following experiment we try  to find an effect way to monitor the moisture content and avoid  significant quality difference between each FOSB.

## II. Experiment  

In this study, we used the following materials to conduct our  experiments: two different kinds of 12 inch FOSBs (mainly  composed of polycarbonate, or PC; PC is an absorbent plastic  material): 1.A grade level 2.B grade level from two different  FOSB vendors; Aluminum/antistatic bags (Formosa Flexible  Packaging corporation, Taiwan / Jushuo plastic applied  material, Taiwan); Desiccants and humidity cards (Tai Chiang  chemical industry corporation, Taiwan); Humidity meters  (TECPEL, Taiwan); Vacuum packing machine (JIY FUN  Enterprise corporation, Taiwan).

## Short-term storage experiment (7 days)  

The following experimental steps are designed to simulate  the wafer outgoing process. At the beginning, we put humidity  meters inside the two different kinds of FOSBs. After locking  the FOSB, we paste desiccants (0/2/5 packages) and a humidity  card outside the FOSB. Finally, the FOSB is covered with  aluminum/antistatic bags and air is removed by the vacuum  packing machine (Suction time  $\scriptstyle{\varepsilon=12}$   seconds which can reduce  pressure to about 0.2 atm inside packing bags; and N 2  purge  time  $\scriptstyle{=6/4}$   seconds for antistatic/ aluminum bags due to our

packing recipes). Humidity meter is set to record humidity data  (each hour) during the 7 day experiment. Experiment steps as  shown in Fig 4.

## Short-term storage experiment (7 days) result  

Fig 5 and Fig 6 are the 7 days storage experiment results. All  groups after the vacuum packing procedure, exhibited a  humidity increase   $(0.3{\sim}6.5\%$  , each group is different) at the  beginning. After vibration, the system will reach a steady  period with a nearly constant dehydration ability which comes  from desiccants.

In accordance to Fick’s second law, the force of vibration  should come from the moisture concentration gradient. After  vacuum procedure is done, the disequilibrium of moisture  concentration between the FOSB material (PC, an absorbent  plastic which contains moisture) and the air inside  aluminum/antistatic bags will cause the moisture captured in  the FOSB to diffuse out.

We will discuss these results using three experiment factors  in the following:

##   A/B grade level FOSB  

Experimental results show that there is a huge quality  difference between the two different kinds of FOSB. No  matter the aluminum/antistatic bags, the B grade level  FOSB will lead to an obvious humidity increase after the  vacuum procedure.

The reason for this huge difference should come from  different FOSB clean recipes and dry methods. In the  experimental results, humidity level increase of B grade  FOSB are  ${>}6\%$   (Sky blue line in Fig 5; Purple line in Fig  6). However, the humidity increase for A grade FOSB  was only   $0.3{\sim}2\%$   (Other lines in Fig 5 and Fig 6).

In quality control, the difference is unacceptable and  may cause non-uniformity between our products, even if  they were made in the same manufacturing process. So  we try to find a way to monitor and define a specific spec  with the following  quality controlling experiment.

##   The amounts of desiccants  

In this study, we chose 0/2/5 bags of desiccants to  confirm the relationship between the amount of  desiccants and the humidity control ability. After the  humidity rose at the beginning, the system will reach a  near steady state period. The slope (Humidity decrease to  time), which means the humidity controlled capability, is  nearly consistent.

The slope is proportional to the amount of desiccants,  more desiccants will show a stronger dehydration

capability.  By calculating the ratio of the amount of  humidity level descent to time (After vibration to 7 days),  we can generate the slope, which means the ability of  dehydration. The slope of the humidity line is -1.23/-2.19  (2/5 bags of desiccants) in antistatic bag groups. In  aluminum bag groups the slope is -1.28/-1.65 (2/5 bags  of desiccants).

From the linear result, we can roughly estimate the  environment inside for each group. But we need to know  when and how long the system needs to reach and  maintain a stable humidity status. If we can detect the  humidity value during a stable state, then we can know  the relation between desiccants amounts and humidity  decrease amounts. So we planned a  long-term (90 days)  storage experiment  to find out the ”stable period”.

##    Aluminum/Antistatic bags  

In different package materials, aluminum bags show a  much more obvious humidity increase than antistatic  bags at the beginning (A grade FOSB in Fig 5 compared  to Fig 6 at the beginning period). It is supposed to come  from the difference of the vacuum degree between  aluminum and antistatic bags.

As we know, aluminum bags can offer a better system  protection ability than antistatic bags. Aluminum bags  with a higher vacuum degree will lead to a bigger  moisture concentration gradient, so the humidity rising  will be more obvious than antistatic bags.

But in short-term experiment, aluminum and antistatic  bags are applied to different N 2  purge times, due to  current packing specifications in our fab. So we will try  to confirm that the moisture increase difference between  a luminum and antistatic bags mainly comes from either  the packing materials, or N 2  purge times difference in   the following  quality controlling experiment. .

When a steady state period is reached, the slopes with  the same amount of desiccants are close. This means the  groups with a different package material, but the same  amount of desiccants, will show a similar dehydration  capability.

## Quality controlling experiment  

Short term storage experiment results show a result which is  worth our discussion. In the beginning period   $(<\!24\mathrm{hr})$  , the B  grade level FOSB groups show an obvious rise in humidity  inside the FOSB (maximum rising amounts  ${>}6\%$   humidity); but  the A grade level FOSB shows a much more stable humidity  curve.

The distinct difference may cause a big problem, products  may suffer from quality issue. Due to the requests of  cost/multiple sources, different grades FOSB from multiple  suppliers are unavoidable.

Moisture inside solids is usually detected by a moisture  analyser, but this analyser is not suitable in FOSB, which is  such a big and unbreakable item. So we tried to find a way to  detect the moisture levels inside FOSB to maintain stable  quality by the following moisture level inspection method.

## Moisture content inspection method-Vacuum inspection  method  

For quality control, we need to check the moisture of each  FOSB at the nearby spec and avoid quality gap that can lead to  uncontrollable results between products. So, we found a fast  and convenient method called the “Vacuum inspection  method” to monitor the moisture level inside each FOSB. The  following is our experiment steps.

Put humidity meters inside the tested FOSB (A grade/B  grade) and cover FOSB with aluminum/antistatic bags. Then  air is removed with vacuum packing machine with different  suction times (Vacuum time  ${=}6$  , 12, 18, 24 seconds with N2  purge time  $\mathord{:=}6$   seconds). Record the humidity data per minute  during the 3 day experimental period (Fig 7).

Different suction times means a different vacuum degree, we  derived the curve (humidity addition versus experiment time).  There will be an inside humidity increase after the vacuum  procedure, and the degree of increase is decided by our suction  time and packing materials. From the curves we can determine  a specific suction time and detect the moisture level for  incoming FOSBs. Then we can define a suitable spec that can  be available for incoming FOSB quality control.

## Quality controlling experiment result  

From results of the vacuum inspection methods (B grade  FOSB), we can prove that the vacuum time is strongly  correlated to the amount of moisture diffusing out. At suction  time=6s, humidity increase less; and as suction time increases,  humidity increased at first then decreased (suction time  $=\!18s$  with the maximum humidity increase amount, Fig 8).

We predicted the results came from the concentration  gradient change. Generally, vacuum ability of vacuum packing  machines should follow figures that resemble Fig 9. It will  reach a saturated state after a specific suction time (Saturated  time); after the saturated time, the degree of vacuum will not  increase as suction time is being prolonged. In general packing  cases, the saturated time is the most popular choice of vacuum  packing time because of its efficiency. In our case, 12 seconds  was our saturated suction time. Using this, we predicted the  status of each suction time (12/18/24 seconds) explained by the  following mechanism (Fig 10).

In B grade FOSB cases, at suction time  $\scriptstyle{\stackrel{\scriptstyle=}{12}}$   seconds, a part of  moisture inside the packing bag is taken away with a medium  moisture gradient; and this led to a medium moisture increase.  When suction  $\scriptstyle=18$   seconds, almost all the moisture inside the  packing bag is out, so there is the highest moisture gradient,  which induced the greatest moisture increase. At suction  $=\!24$  seconds, not only the moisture in the air, but also the moisture  inside the FOSB is taken away, so the moisture gradient  and  amount of moisture increase of this group is smaller than the  group of suction time=18 seconds.

These results hint that by changing suction time we can  improve the moisture content of FOSB. Extending the FOSB  suction time (longer than the saturated time), it can help to  enhance moisture to diffuse out. After the change, more  moisture within the FOSB can be “guided” to diffuse out and  the moisture within the FOSB during the packing period can  reduce.

From the comparison between aluminum and antistatic bags,  aluminum bag show a slightly higher rising trend than antistatic  bags, but the difference is not obvious. So we assume that the  different suction time, which means a different degree in  vacuum status is the dominant reason behind the inside  humidity change.

Following, we used an A grade FOSB to do the vacuum  inspection experiment to confirm the relationship between  different grade FOSBs and the degree of humidity increase.  From the results (Fig 11) we can prove that A grade FOSB can  provide a stable humidity environment (humidity change is  much less than B grade FOSB). Usually moisture in FAB is  controlled between   $40–45\%$  , in A grade FOSB cases after  vacuum packing the humidity can maintain at nearly   $40{\pm}2\%$  ,  which is known as the appropriate wafer keeping environment.

And the hypothetical mechanism of Fig 10 can be proved,  too. The sequence of humidity addition (A grade FOSB) is 12   ${\mathrm{sec.}}>18\ {\mathrm{sec.}}>6$   sec., and it’s similar with the result of B grade  FOSB (18 sec.  ${>}12$   sec. > 24 sec.). The results show a curve  with a maximum humidity increase at a middle suction time.  The moisture gradient will increase as suction time is  prolonged at first; after reaching the maximum value, the  moisture gradient will fall down due to moisture near the  outside wall is being removed, and this leads to a decrease in  driving force.

Another point worth our discussion is that a B grade FOSB  shows a “positive humidity change” at equilibrium, but an A  grade FOSB shows a “negative humidity change” at  equilibrium. These results are due to the vacuum procedure  tacking out a part of the moisture inside the packing bag, and  then some moisture in the FOSB will also diffuse out. Because  A grade FOSB has less moisture content, meaning the amount  of moisture removed from the vacuum procedure is larger than  the moisture diffusing out from the FOSB; so in A grade FOSB  cases, after initial inside humidity increase, the system will  reach a balanced state by vent valve, and the balanced moisture  level is less than the initial value. In B grade FOSB cases, the  amount of moisture diffusing out is larger than the moisture  taken away by the vacuum process, so after balance by vent  valve, it will show a positive humidity change.

For the abovementioned results, we can choose the  appropriate vacuum time by each company’s outgoing packing  recipe. For example, if the recipe for outgoing suction time  $\scriptstyle=10$  seconds and N 2  purge time  $\mathord{:=}6$   seconds, then we can cover the  incoming FOSB with packing bags and follow with a 10 second  suction period. Then we can obtain how much moisture will  diffuse out during the packing period. If it’s too high we can fit  it by appropriately prolonging the suction time to remove more  moisture.

## Long-term storage experiment (90 days)  

Although we can know the initial humidity profile by  short-term storage experiment, it had not reached the stable  period and status. So we planned a 90 day experiment to verify  it. To simulate the common products packing flows, we design  three experimental groups as following:

1. A grade FOSB + 2 desiccants + aluminum bag.    2. B grade FOSB + 2 desiccants  $^+$   aluminum bag.    3. B grade FOSB + 0 desiccants  $^+$   antistatic bag.


And the experimental steps are similar to short-term storage  experiment. First, put humidity meters inside a A/B grade  FOSB. After locking the FOSB, we paste desiccants (0/2  packages, simulating the common outgoing flow) and a  humidity card outside the FOSB. Finally the FOSB is covered  with aluminum/antistatic bags and the air is removed by the  vacuum packing machine (Suction time=12 seconds and N 2 purge time  ${=}6$   seconds, can reduce pressure to about 0.2 atm  inside packing bags). Humidity meter is set to record humidity  data (each half hour) during the 90 days experiment (Fig 12).

From long-term results (Fig 13), B grade FOSB groups show  similar results with short-term experiments. After vacuum  packing, inside humidity of B grade FOSB will increase first  but A grade FOSB can avoid the unstable vibration.

There is another interesting point worth our discussion. From  above results, the groups with desiccants finally reach stable  with an extremely low humidity   $(10–15\%)$  . If products do not  be properly handled, such low humidity may lead to higher  ESD risk which compared with normal FAB humidity   $(40–45\%)$  . ## III. Result and Discussion  

Moisture in FOSB is a critical but easily neglected item. If it  is not controlled well, it may cause serious product quality  concern like pad corrosion or ESD risk…etc [3, 4]. From the  above mentioned short-term experiment result we know the

vacuum packing procedure will lead to moisture increase inside  the FOSB, and the value will differ for each source of FOSB.

Due to cost and multiple sources’ requests, FOSB of  different sources are unavoidable. In our experiments we  verified the quality difference between different grades of  FOSBs, which result from different clean recipes and dry  methods. Based on these results we designed a simple and  convenient “Vacuum inspection method” to inspect the quality  of outgoing FOSB quickly. By this method, moisture level of  FOSB can be detected and controlled within a specific spec; for  quality control, it’s very helpful and can protect both  manufacturers and customers.

The results of the vacuum inspection methods can also verify  the relation between different suction time and the degree of  humidity increase. Generally, we choose the saturated suction  time for vacuum packing process due to its high efficiency, but  from the abovementioned results, we know we can prolong the  suction time to allow more moisture to be removed. These  results give us another choice for a different packing suction  time.

Finally the effects of different amounts of desiccants and  packing material are clarified in this study too. Same as our  speculations, aluminum bags can offer a greater isolating  ability than antistatic bags, which led to a bigger moisture  increase. Unexpectedly, too much desiccants may lead to  inverse effect. After packing, the desiccants within isolated  system will continuously absorb lots of moisture and may cause  extremely low humidity (By 90 days experiment results,  humidity may reduce to   $10–15\%$  ). Such low humidity may  increase the ESD risk, this is a point we need to carefully  consider when choosing products packing recipe.

## IV. Conclusion  

From above mentioned results (short-term and long-term  experiments), we can roughly predict the environment inside  outgoing packages after the vacuum process. It is very potential  for us to design a more suitable keeping condition than before.

And in different product outgoing cases, it can be slightly  adjusted (by each case), then applied to them, too. From these  data results with individual corrections for each manufacturer,  the most suitable method for fab outgoing goods can be decided  and be a much better fit for both manufacturers and customers.
