## A Robust Dual Directional SCR without Current  Saturation Effect for ESD Applications  

Feibo Du  1, \* , Xiaoyu Dong  1 , Chengjin Yang  2 , YiChen Xu  1 , Zhiwei Liu  1 , Jizhi Liu  1  and Juin J. Liou  3 ,  Fellow,  IEEE     1  State Key Laboratory of Electronic Thin Films and Integrate Devices, University of Electronic   Science and Technology of China, Chengdu, Sichuan, China 610054.  2  Shenzhen SGKS Technology Co., Ltd, Shenzhen, Guangdong, China 518000.  3  School of Microelectronics, Zhengzhou University, Zhengzhou, Henan, China 450001.  \*Email: dufeibo@outlook.com

Abstract-   A robust dual directional SCR (RDDSCR) is proposed to  suppress the current saturation effect in conventional dual  directional SCR (DDSCR). By introducing a new current path  with low resistance, the self-heating effect in NWELL regions of  RDDSCR can be well avoided. The ESD I-V characteristics of  RDDSCR and DDSCR are measured with the transmission line  pulsing (TLP) tester. Moreover, TCAD simulation is also carried  out to explore the physics mechanism in depth. Compared with  DDSCR, the new RDDSCR possesses an improvement in failure  current of  $16.5\%$   and an increase in holding voltage of about 2V.  Furthermore, the influence of the external resistance on ESD  performance of RDDSCR is also studied, where the resistance  value of zero  Ω  has been demonstrated to be a better option.  Compared with the existing optimization methods, RDDSCR  method has been proven to be more efficient in inhibiting current  saturation effect of DDSCR without any compromise on turn-on  characteristic.

Keywords  –   Electrostatic discharge(ESD), dual-directional  SCR(ddSCR), current saturation effect, TCAD simulation.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

As semiconductor technology enters the deep submicron  stage, the chip failure caused by electrostatic discharge (ESD)  has become one of the main factors restricting the reliability of  integrated circuit (IC) products [1-3]. Therefore, it is necessary  to provide customized on-chip ESD protection solutions for  various IC chips. For protecting pins with operating voltage  ranging between negative and positive values, the dualdirectional SCR (DDSCR) is an excellent candidate due to its  ability to provide bidirectional ESD protection as well as its  very high robustness [4-7]. However, compared with  unidirectional SCR (USCR), the DDSCR devices are more  prone to current saturation behavior, which has been  demonstrated to have a great impact on the ESD performance  of DDSCR in terms of effectiveness and robustness [8, 9]. At  present, except for [8], few studies have focused on the  suppression of current saturation effect in DDSCR.

In this paper, a new and robust DDSCR (RDDSCR) has been  proposed. Compared with the conventional DDSCR device, the  novel RDDSCR is immune to current saturation effect and thus  achieves larger failure current (i.e.,   $I_{t2}$  ) as well as better  effectiveness of ESD protection.

The cross-sectional views of conventional DDSCR and  proposed RDDSCR are shown in Fig. 1(a) and Fig. 1(b),  respectively. Both devices are constituted by the PNPNP  structure, where the P-type active area, N-WELL (HVNDDD)  and PWHV make up of the vertical parasitic P-N-P transistors,  and the N-WELL (HVNDDD), PWHV and N-WELL  (HVNDDD) make up of the horizonal parasitic N-P-N  transistors. Moreover, as shown in Fig. 1(a), a pair of back-toback high voltage (HV) diodes (P+/PWHV/HVNDDD/NWELL/N+) are also integrated into the PNPNP structure to  reduce the device trigger voltage. Additionally, both devices are  isolated from P-type substrate by DVNWELL region as well.

On the basis of DDSCR, two  $\Nu+$   blocks in the same NWELL  in RDDSCR are connected together through external  resistances (i.e.,   $R_{E X T}$  ), thus forming new current discharge  paths, as shown in Fig. 1(b). The resistance value of   $R_{E X T}$   is  variable and remains much smaller than that of   $R_{N W}$  .  Consequently, when the Terminal1 of RDDSCR is subject to a  positive ESD pulse and Terminal2 is grounded, as illustrated in

Fig. 2, the ESD current in RDDSCR is mainly discharged by   $Q_{5}$  ,   $Q_{4}$   and  $R_{E X T}$   in series (yellow dash line in Fig. 2(b)) rather than   $Q_{2}$  ,   $Q_{1}$   and   $R_{N W}$   in series (yellow dash line in Fig. 2(a)) in the  DDSCR. As a result, the self-heating effect in the NWELL  region of RDDSCR at large current will be greatly weakened by  the elimination of large   $R_{N W}$    in the main current path, thus  inhibiting the occurrence of current saturation effect.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

The DDSCR and RDDSCR are realized with the same width  of   $50\;\mathrm{~\textmu m}$   in a   $0.18{\mathrm{-}}{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   Bipolar-CMOS-DMOS (BCD)  technology. The I-V characteristics are measured using a  transmission line pulsing (TLP) tester with 10ns rise time and  100ns pulse width. Besides, non-isothermal Technology  Computer-Aided Design (TCAD) simulation has also been  carried out to further explore the physics mechanism of  RDDSCR. For the sake of simplicity, the P-type guard ring at  the periphery of device is replaced with a substrate electrode at  the bottom of the silicon substrate.

Measured TLP I–V and leakage characteristics of DDSCR  and the RDDSCR with  $R_{E X T}$   of zero   $\Omega$   (i.e., RDDSCR_1) are  shown in Fig. 3. Obviously, when the current rises to about  2.7A, the voltage drop on DDSCR increases dramatically,  showing the so-called current saturation effect, which will  seriously deteriorate the ESD robustness and effectiveness of  the device. On the other hand, the on resistance of RDDSCR_1  does not increase significantly at high current until the device  fails, which indicates that the RDDSCR_1 is free from current  saturation effect. Fig. 4 depicts the simulated current density of  DDSCR and RDDSCR before/after triggered, respectively. As  depicted in Fig. 4(c) and (d), the new route consisting of the

internal parasitic   $Q_{5}\,,\;Q_{4}$   and external   $R_{E X T}$   in RDDSCR_1  provides a current discharge path with low on resistance,  averting the self-heating effect of   $R_{N W}$   at high current level and  thus the current saturation effect of DDSCR, which is consistent  with the TLP results. As a result, the RDDSCR_1 could obtain  an improvement in failure current of   $16.5\%$  . In addition, the  holding voltage (i.e.,   $V_{h_{\perp}}$  ) of RDDSCR_1 is about 2V higher  than that of DDSCR. This is due to the fact that the back-toback HV diodes and the internal SCR share an   $\Nu+$   cathode in  RDDSCR, which could prompt more current to flow through  the back-to-back HV diodes path rather than the parallel SCR  path, resulting in a weakening of the conductivity modulation  effect in WELL regions and thus a higher  $V_{h}$   [10, 11].

Fig. 5 illustrates the TLP measurement results of RDDSCRs  with three different  $R_{E X T}$  . When enlarging   $R_{E X T}$   from 0 to  $2\;\Omega$  ,  the  $I_{t2}$   decreases by about 2.3A and the on resistance increases

significantly, suggesting that a larger   $R_{E X T}$  will weaken its  ability to suppress the current saturation effect in DDSCR,  which can be demonstrated by the variation of the branch  current of N1 electrode and N2 electrode in RDDSCR. As  shown in Fig. 6, the ESD current flowing through N1 and N2  electrodes increases and decreases dramatically with an  enlarged  $R_{E X T}$  , respectively. It is noticeable that when the   $R_{E X T}$    is  $0\,\Omega$  , the ESD current flows mainly through the N1 electrode.  However, when the   $R_{E X T}$   is greater than   $1\ \Omega$  , the ESD current  changes to flow mainly through the N2 electrode, indicating that  the electrical behavior of the RDDSCR is more inclined to that  of DDSCR at this point. Consequently, the RDDSCR with a  larger   $R_{E X T}$   will suffer from more serious current saturation  effect.

According to Fig. 4(a) and (b), the turn-on of the P-N-P  transistor   $Q_{5}$   in RDDSCR may be slightly later than that   $(Q_{2})$   in  DDSCR. To evaluate whether the turn-on characteristic of  RDDSCR may have been compromised due to the introduced  resistance   $R_{E X T}$   path, the transient response waveforms of  RDDSCRs and DDSCR under a 2A-TLP current stress have also  been captured by the oscilloscope [12, 13]. As depicted in Fig.  7, all the devices considered possess almost identical voltage  waveforms with a peak voltage of about 77 V, indicating that the  turn-on characteristics of RDDSCRs are comparable to that of  DDSCR regardless of the value of  $R_{E X T}$  .

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

A new bidirectional SCR device (RDDSCR) has been  developed to restrain the current saturation effect in traditional  DDSCR by adding a new current discharge path. Compared  with existing optimization methods, the RDDSCR is more  efficient in suppressing the current saturation effect prevalent in  DDSCR without any deterioration in triggering characteristic.  In addition, a smaller external resistance   $R_{E X T}$   of RDDSCR is  recommended for suppressing the current saturation effect in  DDSCR effectively.
