## Comparison of NBTI kinetics in RMG Si p-FinFETs  featuring Atomic Layer Deposition Tungsten (ALD W)  Filling Metal Using B 2 H 6  and SiH 4  Precursors  

Longda Zhou 1,2 , Guilei Wang 1,2 , Xiaogen Yin 1,2 , Bo Tang 2 , Qianqian Liu 2 , Zhigang   $\mathrm{J}\mathrm{i}^{3}$  , Hao  $\mathrm{{Nu}}^{2}$  , Zhenzhen Kong 2 ,    Haojie Jiang 2 , Ying Luo 2 , Eddy Simoen 4 , Hong Yang 1,2\* , Huaxiang   ${\mathrm{Yin}}^{1,2}$  , Anyan   $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{u}^{2}$  , Huilong   $Z\mathrm{{h}u^{2}}$  , Chao Zhao 1,2 Wenwu Wang 1,2\*  and Tianchun Ye  1 Microelectronics Institute, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China  2 Key Laboratory of Microelectronics Devices & Integrated Technology, Institute of Microelectronics, Chinese Academy of  Sciences, Beijing 100029, China  3 Liverpool John Moores University, 5.09a, James Parsons Building, Byrom street, L3 3AF.  4 IMEC, Kapeldreef 75, 3001 Leuven, Belgium  Phone: +86 82995508 Fax: +86 82995508 \*Email: yanghong@ime.ac.cn, wangwenwu@ime.ac.cn

Abstract-   Fast characterization methods are used to study negative  bias temperature instability (NBTI) kinetics in replacement metal  gate Si p-FinFETs filled by atomic layer deposition (ALD)  tungsten (W) using  $\bf{B}_{2}H_{6}$   or SiH 4  precursors. The impact of ALD  W filling metal process on threshold voltage shift (ΔV T ), generated  interface traps   $\left(\mathbf{CN_{IT}}\right)$  , pre-existing hole traps   $\left(\mathbf{CN_{HT}}\right)$  , stress bias,  and temperature dependence of degradation is analyzed.  SiH 4 -based devices show lower ΔV T  compared to   $\mathbf{B}_{2}\mathbf{H}_{6}$  -based  devices under identical stress conditions due to lower ΔN HT  and  ΔN IT . More fluoride-passivated interface traps and higher  compressive strain along the channel for ALD W using   $\mathbf{S_{iH_{4}}}$   are  utilized to explain a lower ΔN IT  than ALD W using B 2 H 6 . The time  kinetics of NBTI degradation is then modeled for long-term  prediction and the maximum overdrive voltage is determined.  Devices filled with ALD W using   $\mathbf{S_{iH_{4}}}$   show superior reliability  and a higher operation overdrive voltage, and can be adopted in  the 16/14-nm node CMOS technology and beyond.

Keywords  –  ALD W filling metal,   fluorine, strain, NBTI,  p-FinFETs, high-k and metal gate (HKMG) .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Gap-filling capability in a gate trench with high aspect ratio is  one of the biggest challenges in the replacement metal gate  (RMG) process [1]. Because of the good step coverage and  conformity of deposited thin films, atomic layer deposition  (ALD) Tungsten (W) has been widely accepted as the filling  metal in contact holes [2] and gate trenches [3]. Diborane   $\mathrm{(B_{2}H_{6})}$  and silane (SiH 4 ) are two widely used precursors for the ALD  W process [4-8]. Detailed studies on ALD W using   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$   or   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$   have been performed to investigate trench-filling  capability, fluoride content in the gate stack, drive capability,  and impact on channel strain [4, 5]. However, ALD W filling  metal using   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$   or   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$   in RMG FinFET and its impact on  negative bias temperature instability (NBTI) has not been  systematically studied yet.

In this study, the kinetics of threshold voltage shift  $(\Delta\mathrm{V_{T}})$   was  extensively characterized using ultrafast    $({\sim}10\ \ \ \upmu s$   delay)  methods. Si channel RMG p-FinFETs were used, with ALD W  filling metal using    $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$   or    $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  . Two degradation

subcomponents—pre-existing hole traps   $(\Delta\aleph_{\mathrm{HT}})$   and interface  trap generation   $(\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}})$  —were independently characterized and  analyzed. The impact of stress bias (V G-STR ) and temperature (T)  on NBTI degradation is studied, and the underlying physical  mechanism is explained. The NBTI degradation is modelled  and the maximum operation overdrive voltages (V OV ) are  predicted and compared for p-FinFETs with two ALD W  processes.

## II.   D EVICE AND  E XPERIMENTAL  M ETHODS   

The RMG Si p-FinFETs were fabricated using a full-gate-last  process. The major steps of the gate stack process flow are as  follows: (1) dummy poly-Si/SiO 2  gate stack removal; (2)  growing interfacial layer (IL)  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   by chemical oxidation of  $\mathrm{O}_{3}$  ;  (3) ALD deposition of   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$  ; (4) deposition of work function  metal stack, i.e., ALD TiN/ALD TaN/CVD TiN; (5) ALD  deposition of W films using   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$   or   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$   precursors to fill the  gate trench. The entire device fabrication was finished by  metallization and forming gas annealing (FGA) at  $400\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 30  min. During ALD W cycles,   $\mathrm{WF_{6}}$   reacted with   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}/\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$   to  produce W and other byproducts (i.e., HF,   $\mathrm{BF}_{3}$  ,   $\mathrm{SiF_{4}}$  , and   $\mathrm{H}_{2}$  ).  Most byproducts were pumped out of the chamber, but some  residual F still remained in the grown W film. The FGA  annealing facilitated the diffusion of F into gate stacks and  provided the necessary activation energy for passivating Si  dangling bonds and/or substituting Hf-H and Si-H bonds to  form strong Hf-F and Si-F bonds at the interfaces.

The devices under test had two combinations of channel  length and fin number: p-FinFETs having a channel length of  40 nm and 32 fins were used for measuring threshold voltage   $\left({{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{T}}}\right)$   and the time evolution of   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{T}}$  ; p-FinFETs with a channel  length of 500 nm and 4800 fins were used for charge pumping  (CP)   and   direct   current   current-voltage   (DCIV)  characterization.

The constant current method was used to extract   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$  and  monitor   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{T}}$   by sensing at a target I DLIN  of   $20\ \mathrm{nA\!\times\!W/L}$  . The  time evolution of   $\Delta{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{T}}}$   was measured under constant voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{G}})$   stress [9] and constant field   $\left(\operatorname{E_{ON}}\right)$   stress [10, 11] with a  delay of   $10~\upmu\mathrm{{s}}$  . The CP method was used to estimate the

interface state density   $(\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{IT}})$   of the fresh device [12, 13]. The  time evolution of   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$   during NBTI stress was characterized  using the DCIV method [14] with a delay of  ${\sim}10\,\mathrm{s}$  , after suitable  delay correction [15] and bandgap correction [16]. All data  were collected on multiple devices for each test condition, and  the averaged results are shown in this paper.

A previous study [4] reported that ALD W using   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$    introduces higher F content in the W film and may also result in  more F in the gate stack than   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based W. Previous work [5] found that ALD W results in  metal-gate-induced strain.    $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based ALD W has a  polycrystalline phase and higher tensile stress, whereas   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based ALD W has an amorphous phase and lower tensile  stress. ALD W with tensile stress could introduce a  compressive strain in the channel. Technology computer-aided  design (TCAD) simulation was performed to compare strain  induced from the W gate in the channel for two ALD W  processes. Three times higher tensile strain was used in the   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based metal gate than in the  $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based metal gate in the  simulation. Fig. 3 shows that  $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices have a larger  compressive stress along the channel length than   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based  devices.

Fig.   4   shows   that   linear   drain-current   I DLIN    and  transconductance   $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   of   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices are higher than  those of  $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based devices. Higher I DLIN  and  $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   correspond to  higher mobility, which can be explained by the lower interface  states and higher compressive strain along the channel direction  for   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices.

The discharging-based energy profiling technique [18] was  used to investigate the   $\mathrm{V_{OD}}$   dependence of   $\Delta{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{HT}}}$  . Here   $\Delta{\bf V}_{\mathrm{HT}}$    denotes inherent defects, depending on the process of gate stack,  responsible for fast hole trapping during NBTI stress. The  saturation level of  $\Delta{\bf V}_{\mathrm{HT}}$   is determined by the energy level of  $E_{f}$  with respect to  $E_{\nu}$    or the overdrive voltage, and will not increase

with stress time. Fig. 8 indicates the lower content of   $\Delta{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{HT}}}$   for  ALD W using   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  , indicating passivation of oxide traps by F.   $\Delta{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{HT}}}$   is then converted into the effective trap density   $(\Delta\aleph_{\mathrm{HT}})$    with the equation   $\Delta\mathrm{N_{HT}\,=\,(C_{O X}\,\times\,\Delta V_{H T})/q}$  , where q is the  electronic charge and   $\mathrm{CX}$   is the oxide capacitance. The energy  distribution of  $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{HT}}$   is below Ev. Higher  $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{HT}}$   is observed for  ALD W using   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  , which could account for higher   $\Delta{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{T}}}$   for  the  $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based device.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

The effect of ALD W filling metal using   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$   or   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$    precursors on electrical and NBTI reliability characteristics of  RMG Si p-FinFETs was investigated. It was found that,  compared to devices filled by ALD W using   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  ,   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based  devices show lower   $\Delta{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{T}}}$  , lower   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{HT}}$  , and lower   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$   under  identical stress conditions. The physical mechanism underlying  this reliability improvement can be explained by fluorine  incorporation and the strain profile in the channel. Compared to   $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based devices,  $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices contain higher fluorine  incorporation at the interfaces, and larger compressive stress  applied to the channel through TCAD simulation. In terms of  electrical performance, lower interface states and better drive  capability shown for   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices are due to enhanced  fluorine passivation and compressive strain along the channel  length. More number of robust Si-F bonds formed at the  interfaces and reduced bulk oxide traps by F passivation are  responsible for lower  $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$  , lower   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{HT}}$  , and higher  $\mathrm{E_{A}}$   of   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$    for   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices. The higher strain in the channel results  in lower   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$  , higher VAE of   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$  , and higher FAE of the  overall   $\Delta{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{T}}}$   for   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices. The lower relative  contribution from   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{HT}}$   and higher  $\mathrm{E_{A}}$   of   $\Delta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{IT}}$   result in higher  E A  of the overall   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{T}}$   for   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$  -based devices. The NBTI  degradation is modeled for long-term prediction, and devices  filled with ALD W using   $\mathrm{SiH_{4}}$   precursor show superior  reliability and a   $71\ \mathrm{mV}$   larger maximum operation overdrive  voltage than  $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$  -based devices.
