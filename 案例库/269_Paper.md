## Leakage Current Degradation in SiC Junction Barrier  Schottky Diodes under Heavy Ion Microbeam  

Cao Shuang, Yu Qingkui \* , Du Guanghua, Guo Jinlong, Wang He, Zhang Hongwei and Sun Yi  1   China Academy of Space Technology, 5 Minzuyuan Road, Beijing, 100029, China.  2   Institute of Modem Physics, Chinese Academy of Science, 509 Nanchang Road, Lanzhou, 730000, China.  \* Phone: 86 10 68116447   Fax: 86 10 68116426   Email:  yuqingkui@263.net

Abstract-   Leakage current degradation of SiC junction barrier  Schottky diodes were studied under heavy ion microbeam.  Leakage current increased linearly with fluence and was  positively related to bias voltage. It was proposed that leakage  current occurred as a result of the accumulation of multiple  leakage paths. The explanation that the increased leakage current  was related to leakage paths formed by damage in the mechanism  of “micro-SEB” was verified.

Keywords  –   SiC JBSD, Leakage current, Degradation,  Irradiation, Heavy ion, Microbeam .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

SiC Junction Barrier Schottky Diode (JBSD) is an ideal  high-voltage power device for spacecraft power systems  because of its good properties in high current, high breakdown  voltage, fast switching speed and strong surge current  resistance. However, SiC JBSDs were sensitive to single event  effect [1]-[12]. Irradiated at bias far below the rated voltage,  SiC JBSDs showed an increase in leakge current and even  Single Event Burnout (SEB). Yu Qingkui et al. proposed an  explanation of "micro-SEB"[4]. They believed heavy ions  caused local damage in the Schottky junction and leakage  paths were formed, resulting in increased leakage current and  even SEB. A. Javanainen et al. held similar views [10]. The  cross section diagram of SiC JBSD “micro-SEB” is shown in  Fig.1. The dotted area is the inferred leakage current path.  However, the mechanism of heavy ion induced leakage current  degradation in SiC JBSDs has not been clearly understood.  The available test results are obtained by heavy ion broad  beam. The leakage current increases with irradiation fluence.  Whether the increase of irradiation fluence results in a larger  damage leakage path or an accumulation of multiple damage  leakage paths, which is a key issue for the development of  ground effect assessment methods and failure criteria.

To investigate the mechanism of heavy ion induced leakage  current degradation in SiC JBSDs, samples of SiC JBSDs  were irradiated under heavy ion microbeam. The relationship  of leakage current degradation with fluence and applied bias  voltage were studied. The leakage current degradation was  analyzed based on the mechanism of “micro-SEB”.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

The SiC JBSDs used in this study were commercial and  decapsulated. The two samples were numbered   $12\#$   and   $13\#$  .  The maximum ratings for breakdown voltage and forward  current of samples are 1200V and 10A respectively.

The heavy ion microbeam test was conducted at Heavy Ion  Research Facility in Lanzhou (HIRFL) at the Institute of  Modern Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

The ion Kr with 2.15GeV of energy and   $18.5\mathrm{MeV}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{cm}^{2}/\mathrm{mg}$  of Linear Energy Transfer (LET) was used for heavy ion  microbeam test. The size of beam is   $40\upmu\mathrm{m}{\times}40\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The flux is  5\~10ions/s.

During the microbeam irradiation process, an active area of   $200\upmu\mathrm{m}{\times}200\upmu\mathrm{m}$   of the sample was irradiated according to the  scanning unit of   $7{\times}7$   matrix as shown in Fig.2. The scanning  track follows the “Z” shape. The movement of the platform  from one position to another was with a step of   $33.3\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . It  took about 50s to irradiate the entire area of   $200\upmu\mathrm{m}{\times}200\upmu\mathrm{m}$  .  The samples were static reverse biased during irradiation. The  leakage current was measured using KEITHIEY 6482. Before  and after irradiation the reverse and forward I/V characteristics  were measured using Keysight B1500A.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

i. The relationship between leakage current and incident ion  fluence

Leakage current degradation of sample   $12\#$   at 358V bias  during irradiation was shown in Fig.3. The fluence  accumulates over time, so it could be seen from Fig.3 that the  leakage current increased linearly with fluence, which was  consistent with the results under heavy ion broad beam [5]-[8].  Besides, when the beam was off, the leakage remained  unchanged. It suggested that the increased leakage current was  a result of cumulative effect and strongly depended on incident  ion fluence.

After irradiation, leakage current degradation was not  recovered. The reverse and forward IV characteristics of  sample  $12\#$   before and after irradiation were shown in Fig.4(a)  and (b). The  incident ion fluence determined the number of local damage.

iii. The relationship between leakage current and leakage  path formed by incident heavy ion due to "micro-SEB"

According to the mechanism of “micro-SEB”, incident ions  caused local damage and leakage paths were formed, and the  number of leakage paths increased with fluence, eventually  leading to an increase in leakage current. Firstly, the leakage  current increased with fluence. Secondly, the increased  leakage current was due to the formation of new leakage paths.

During the microbeam irradiation, an active area of the  sample was irradiated according to the scanning unit of   $7{\times}7$    matrix as shown in Fig.2. It could be inferred that each ion  strikes a different location on the surface of the device. During  the scanning process, the leakage current changed with time as  shown in Fig.3. And Fig.6 showed a partial enlargement of  100s\~150s in Fig.3. It was obvious that the leakage current  increased with fluence, which was a support for the  mechanism of “micro-SEB”.

What’s more, the scanning process determined that each  time the ions hit a different position. This indicated that the  increased leakage current was due to the formation of new  leakage paths. Besides, as shown in Fig.3, the leakage current  remained constant when the beam was off. It was suggested  that no leakage path was formed when there was no incident  ion. In conclusion, the leakage current was related to the

accumulation of leakage path formed by ion incidence damage,  which verified the explanation about the leakage current  increment and the number of leakage paths formed by damage  in the mechanism of “micro-SEB”.

In addition, the increment of leakage current was related to  the size of leakage paths. According to part ii, the higher the  applied bias voltage, the bigger leakage paths would be.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

It was indicated that, under heavy ion microbeam irradiation,  the leakage current of SiC JBSDs increased linearly with  fluence and the leakage current increment caused by an ion  was positively related to applied bias voltage. The leakage  current was expected to occur as the result of the accumulation  of multiple leakage paths, which verified the explanation about  the increased leakage current and the number of leakage paths  formed by damage in the mechanism of “micro-SEB”.

It was believed that incident ions caused local damage and  leakage paths were formed, eventually leading to an increase  in leakage current. The increment of leakage current was  related to the number and size of leakage paths. The number of  leakage paths increased with fluence. The size of leakage paths  was positively related to the bias voltage.

The mechanism of SiC JBSD leakage current degradation  under heavy ion irradiation needs to be further studied to  provide theoretical guidance for device radiation hardness.
