## Effect of Active Layer Thickness on Device  Performance and Hot Carrier Instability in Metal  Induced Crystallized Polycrystalline Silicon  Thin-Film Transistors  

Zhendong Jiang 1 ,  \* Meng Zhang 1 , Xiaotong  $\mathrm{Ma}^{1}$  , Yan Yan 1 , Guijun Li 1 , Sunbin Deng 2 ,  Wei Zhou 2 , Rongsheng Chen 2, 3 , Man Wong 2  and Hoi-Sing Kwok 2   1 Institute of Microscale Optoelectronics (IMO), Shenzhen University, Shenzhen 518060, China  2 State Key Laboratory of Advanced Displays and Optoelectronics Technologies, The Hong Kong University of Science and  Technology, Clear Water Bay, Kowloon, Hong Kong  3 School of Electronic and Information Engineering, South China University of Technology, Guangzhou 510640, China  \* Phone: +86-755-26925737   Fax: +86-755-26534831   Email:  zhangmeng@szu.edu.cn

Abstract-   In this paper, the effect of active layer (AC) thickness on  the device performance and hot carrier (HC) instability of  polycrystalline silicon (poly-Si) thin-film transistors (TFTs) based  on metal induced crystallization (MIC) is investigated. The  thinner AC thickness of MIC poly-Si TFTs brings better device  performance and HC instability, which may be respectively  attributed to the better gate control to the active channel and  different electric field distributions in the active channel and gate  oxide.

Keywords  –  Active channel thickness, Hot carriers, Poly-Si, Thin  film transistors .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Thin film transistors (TFTs) based on the low temperature  polysilicon (poly-Si) thin film have been widely used in  high-resolution small-size flat panel displays (FPDs) because of  their excellent device performance and reliability [1], compared  to TFTs based on the amorphous silicon (a-Si) thin films [2] and  metal oxide thin films [3]. There are three main methods to  generate poly-Si thin film, namely solid phase crystalline (SPC)  method [4], excimer laser annealing (ELA) method [5] and  metal induced crystalline (MIC) method [6]. Among these  technologies, MIC method can produce high-performance  TFTs with low cost and good uniformity. Therefore, MIC  ploy-Si   TFTs   has   great   potential   in   high-resolution  medium-size FPD applications. Until now, the influence of  active layer (AC) thickness on the device characteristic and hot  carrier (HC) instability in MIC TFTs is seldom studied.

In this work, the effect of AC thickness on the device  characteristics and HC instability of MIC poly-Si TFTs is  investigated. Decreasing the AC thickness of MIC poly-Si thin  film can generate devices with higher mobility and stepper  subthreshold swing due to the better gate control to the active  channel. However, when the AC thickness is reduced to 30 nm,  large contact resistance/source-drain resistance begins to limit  the on current. MIC poly-Si TFTs with thinner AC thickness  also has better HC reliability. Different electric field

distributions in the active channel and gate oxide may be  responsible.

## II.   EXPERIMENTAL  

The self-aligned and top-gated TFTs is used in this work. The  cross-section of MIC poly-Si TFT is shown in Fig. 1. The  process started with a substrate, which is covered with a layer of  thermal oxide. A layer of a-Si thin film (30 nm, 40 nm and 50  nm) was subsequently deposited using chemical vapor  deposition method at low pressure (LPCVD). After the  deposition,   5   nm-thick   nickel   was   evaporated.   The  crystallization process was conducted at   $550\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  for 12 hours in  nitrogen ambient, followed by nickel residue removal. After  that, the AC layer was patterned and the gate oxide was  deposited using LPCVD method. After the gate oxide  deposition, 100 nm-thick aluminum was DC sputtered. The gate  was patterned, followed by boron S/D implantation. Next, a  layer of passivation layer was deposited, followed by the  opening of the contact holes. After the opening, 5000 nm-thick  Al-  $1\%\mathrm{Si}$  thin film was DC sputtered, followed by G/S/D  electrodes pattering. Finally, a Forming gas annealing was  performed.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

Transfer curve comparison for MIC poly-Si TFTs with  various AC thicknesses is shown in Fig.  $2\mathrm{a}\sim3\mathrm{a}$  . The insets are  the transfer curves plotted in the linear region.

Clearly, for the on-current, the MIC poly-Si TFT with  thickness of 40 nm AC layer shows the largest value (Fig. 2b)  while the MIC poly-Si TFT which has 30 nm-thick AC layer  (Fig. 2a) shows the smallest value. For the minimum current  measured at   $V_{d s}\,{=}\,{-}5\;\mathrm{V}$  , the MIC poly-Si TFT with the thickness  of AC layer  $=30\ \mathrm{nm}$   shows the smallest value (Fig. 2a) while  the MIC poly-Si TFT with the thickness of AC layer  $=50\;\mathrm{nm}$  shows the largest value (Fig. 2c).

Shown in Fig. 2d \~2f are the output curves of MIC poly-Si  TFTs with different thicknesses of AC layers. Consistent to  transfer curves, the MIC poly-Si TFT with the thickness of AC  layer  $=40~\mathrm{nm}$   has largest on-current (Fig. 2e). It is also found

that the on-current of the MIC poly-Si TFTs with 30 nm AC  layer is suppressed at high   $V_{g s}$   and low   $V_{d s},$   as shown in the inset  of Fig. 2a. Furthermore, drain current crowding at low   $V_{d s}$   [7] is  also observed, as shown in Fig. 2d. Both suppressed on current  and current crowding indicate large contact resistance and  source-drain resistance, which also explains the smallest on  current of the MIC TFT with 30 nm AC layers (Fig. 2a and 2d).  Compared to the MIC poly-Si TFT with thickness of 50  nm-thick AC layer (Fig. 2c and 2f), MIC poly-Si TFT with the  thickness of AC layer  $=40\;\mathrm{nm}$   has better gate control to the AC  (Fig. 2b and 2e), resulting in the largest on current and smaller  off current.

The HC degradation of MIC poly-Si TFTs with different AC  layer thicknesses under typical HC stress is shown in Fig. 3.  The data in Fig.  $3\mathrm{a}\sim3\mathrm{c}$   are plotted in the semi-logarithm region  and the corresponding data in Fig.   $3\mathrm{d}\sim3\mathrm{f}$  are plotted in the  linear   region.   Decreased   on-current   and   unchanged  subthreshold region are the typical HC degradation behaviors  [8], as shown in Fig. 3a, 3b and 3c. Clearly, the MIC poly-Si  TFT with thickness of 50 nm-thick AC layer shows worst HC  degradation (Fig. 3c and 3f) while the MIC poly-Si TFT with  thickness of 30 nm-thick AC layer is hardly affected (Fig. 3a  and 3d) by the same HC stress.

The  $I_{o n}$   degradation dependent on stress time is extracted and  shown in Fig. 4. It can be observed that the   $I_{o n}$   degenerates by   ${\sim}43.5\%$   in MIC poly-Si TFTs with 50 nm AC layer after 3000 s  HC stress while the   $I_{o n}$   only degenerates by   ${\sim}1.5\%$   in in MIC  poly-Si TFTs with 30 nm AC layer. This striking difference  may be caused by the different electric field distributions in the  active channel and gate oxide. The large contact resistance may  share a major part of voltage drop, resulting in little HC  degradation.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, the influence of AC thickness on the device  performance and HC instability of MIC poly-Si TFTs is  studied. The thinner AC thickness of MIC poly-Si TFTs brings  better device performance and HC instability. Worse contact  resistance/source-drain resistance caused by the thinner AC  layer limits the on-current but improves HC reliability.
