## Punch-through and DIBL Effects Exposing Nanonode SOI FinFETs under  H eat Stress  

Mu-Chun Wang 1,\* , Tien-Szu Shen 1 ,  Hui-Yun Bor 2 ,   Chao ‐ Nan Wei 2 , Wen ‐ Shiang Liao 1  and Wen-How Lan 3 1 Department Electronic Engineering, Minghsin University of Science and Technology, Hsinchu 30401, Taiwan  2 Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology, Taoyuan 32546, Taiwan  3 Department of Electrical Engineering, National University of Kaohsiung, Kaohsiung 81148, Taiwan.   \* Phone: +886-3-5593142-3143   Fax: +886-3-5591402   Email:  mucwang@must.edu.tw

Abstract-   Through the electrical measurement plus the heat stress  to enhance the existed or latent defects of FinFETs in the nanonode process flow is a useful metrology. This method not only  effectively and timely provides the mapping analysis in a whole  wafer, but the sensed data may be correlated to the process  variation and optimization in statistical analysis. Besides the  common electrical characteristics in ON/OFF current, the punchthrough and drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL) effects like a  pair of detectors are good tools to probe the channel integrity.  More process parameters of   $\mathbf{V_{T}}$   implantation related to these two  effects are discussed.

Keywords  –  Punch-through, DIBL, FinFET, Heat stress,  Channel integrity, implantation .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

As the high performance speed in IC applications is requested  desirably, the fin field-effect-transistor (FinFET) device is  indeed a good choice, not only in the high drive current, but the  great gate controllability. Because the FinFET can provide the  high aspect ratio with 3-D fin-shape, the drive current can be  kept well, compared with the previous process generation, and  not decreased in an IC chip. This tremendous benefit allows the  designers use more devices in function extension. The recent  manufacturing technology is addressed which can be elongated  to 7nm-node or below. It’s a breakthrough achievement in the  semiconductor fields. However, the challenges come one after  another, such as physical limitation of manufacturing  technology [1], gate dielectric growth [2], lithography [3,4],  etching [5,6], device performance [7,8], yield maintenance,  reliability consideration [9], signal integrity [10], etc. Each view  is strongly related to the success of product development. In this  work, we focus on the channel integrity impacting from the gate  dielectric growth and channel implantation. The related process  parameters will have the threshold-voltage   $\left({{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{T}}}\right)$   implantation in  dosage and energy. The tested samples for N-type FinFETs  (nFinFETs) cover a fixed channel width (W)   $0.115\upmu\mathrm{m}$   and  couple channel lengths (L) 0.16, 0.24, 0.5, 2, and   $10\upmu\mathrm{m}$   on  drawn mask. Using the electrical measurement in punchthrough and drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL) effects as a  pliers plus the heat stress is a good metrology to probe the  channel integrity under different  $\mathrm{V_{T}}$   implant recipe. Because the  bulk FinFETs [11] demonstrate more junction capacitance  which deteriorates the signal transmission and prolongs the  delay time. Therefore, the main stream in nano-node  manufacturing technology is silicon-on-insulator (SOI)-based.

## II.  BRIEF PROCESS DEMONSTRATION 

After cleaning the prepared SOI wafers, the high-aspect ratio  3-D FinFETs with a conventional ULSI sub-65nm generation  logic technology was fabricated. In order to shape this fin  structure, the crystalline silicon (c-Si) layer on SOI wafer was  narrowed down with oxidation-and-etch process steps and the  eventual c-Si thickness was expectedly controlled up to 80\~90  nm. Moreover, a furnace silicon oxidation processing was  rendered to get a suitable oxide layer for the subsequent Si-fin  photolithography definition and plasma etching to obtain a Sifin width such as   $8.6{\cdot}11.4\ \mathrm{nm}$  , as shown in Fig. 2 [12,13].  Again, a sacrificial thin oxide was grown upon both sides of the  fin height to play as a screen oxide and to delete the plasma etch  damaged layer close to the Si-fin surface. Continuously, to gain  more uniformly distributed implanted dopant profiles, boron  (B) or arsenic (As) ions based on the TCAD simulation results  with a   $45^{\mathrm{{o}}}$  tilted implantation angle were applied for the  threshold voltage adjustment of n- or p-channel FinFETs, as  shown in Fig. 3 [14].

Removing the screening oxide, the growth of a 1.4nm plasma  nitrided oxide (physical thickness) as the gate dielectric layer  and an un-doped polysilicon gate layer was deposited by low  temperature chemical vapor deposition (LTCVD). Furthermore,

the high phosphorus (P) dosage implantation was employed for  the reduction of n-channel poly-Si gate resistance and the high  boron (B or   $\mathrm{BF_{x}}$  ) dosage was for p-channel gate resistance.  Owing to the consideration obtaining a poly-Si gate length of  20nm or below, an oxide hard mask and a 193nm scanner were  adopted. P-channel S/D extension implantation was skipped to  avoid the excess boron diffusion in this work.

Soon after the gate spacer formation both  $\mathsf{n}^{+}$   (As) and  $\mathsf{p}^{+}$   (B)  source/drain ion implantations were executed. A cobalt fully  silicidation process for the poly-Si gate was also adopted  followed by a standard copper interconnect process. Finally, a  passivation of PECVD-  $\mathrm{{SiO_{x}}}$   and   $\mathrm{SiN_{x}}$   composite layer was  deposited and followed by the formation of aluminum bonding  pads.

FinFETs are valuable in the deep nano-node era. However,  the channel integrity is more and more difficultly controlled  well. The inner behavior of carrier movement in channel is more  complicated, such as current crowding [18] inducing more  unpredictable side effects. Using the electrical measurement  accompanied with feasible stress voltage and heat stress, the  infant or latent defects in device can be possibly screened out.  Furthermore, the process splits linking these extracted  parameters in wafer mapping and statistical data containing the  standard deviation and error bar will effectively probe the chief  root causes. In this analysis, the lower   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$  implantation energy  seems to show the better electrical performance in DIBL and   $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{PT}}$   even though the temperature is increased.
