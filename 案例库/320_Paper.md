## Sample Preparation in Recover Physical Defects on  MIM Related failure for Different Devices In Wafer  Fabrication  

H.P. Ng, N.Y. Xu, D. Nagalingam, F. Rivai, A.W. Teo, P.K. Tan, C.Q. Chen  QRA-EFA, GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore  60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, Singapore 738406

In wafer fabrication, sample preparation is becoming increasingly  challenging as IC devices evolve to smaller feature sizes and higher  densities. [1] Thus, the task of performing successful failure analysis  (FA) is more challenging and difficult. Sample preparation is a key  activity in material and failure analysis. In semiconductor, there are  many different devices, process and design according to the  customer requirement. Different FA approach for different devices  and failure mode plays a very important role to help identify the root  cause of the failure. This paper discusses the techniques used to  ensure samples are kept in the best possible condition for the failure  analyst. Also, we will discussed in-depth device study and the beauty  of   good   sample   preparation   methodology   through  mechanical/chemical parallel polishing or etching together with  correct FA approach or methodology to ensure good success rate in  identifying failure on MIM breakdown. Two case studies will be  discussed here to demonstrate how to identify the physical defect on  Cu and Al process on MIM issue.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

In wafer fabrication, integrated circuits are fabricated by  superimposing several layers of alternating conductor and  dielectric thin films. Removing selected layers to isolate a  structure is called delayering. It can be accomplished by  chemical etching using liquid or plasma chemistry, or by  mechanical means such as polishing off each unwanted layer.  [2].

This paper will focus on the PFA methodology used to  identify short failures in MIM structure. In order to locate the  MIM breakdown location, electrical fault isolation techniques  by Thermal Induced Voltage Alteration (TIVA) was used to  locate the defect site. Unfortunately, for theses 2 MIM case  studies, diffused TIVA signal was seen and suspected to be  induced ones. This poses challenges in physical analysis.  Hence, a different physical PFA approach and methodology  need to be implementing here on MIM structure for different  devices and process constraint.

Metal–Insulator–Metal (MIM) capacitors are extensively  used in many microelectronic products such as BICMOS for  automotive radar applications, but failure analysis process is  very laborious due to peculiar structure [3]. MIM capacitor is  composed of two parallel plates with a dielectric layer between  the plates. Contact between the top electrode plate and  overlying metal is made by Cu or W vias depending on the  device. The bottom electrode layer is in contact with the

underlying interconnect metal. Possibility of MIM failure could  be due to MIM dielectric breakdown, Tungsten via extrusion  resulted shorted between top and bottom MIM plate, insulator  film thickness and properties, defect due to particle, plasma  charging damage, hillock, Via punch through shorted to bottom  MIM and etc. 2 case studies related to MIM failure were being  discussed in the paper.

## II.   C ASE  S TUDY  1:   MIM  BREAKDOWN ON  UTM  DEVICE 

For this case study, the analyzed device is RF device with two  ultra-thick metal (UTM) layers as top metal and metal 4 layers.  A Metal-Insulator-Metal (MIM) capacitor is underneath the  UTM top metal and metal 4 layer as illustrated in Fig 1.  For this  device, the top metal/M4 layer is approximately   $40\%$  . The  thickness of these 2 metal layers is approximate around 3um  each respectably. The MIM capacitors are rather large in size  with several hundred micrometers wide as depending on the  capacitance. Generally, larger capacitance area is much larger  than those transistors in the memory cell. With such thick top  metal and M4 layers together with large MIM area to be  inspected and analyzed, it often pose a great challenge in the  sample preparation particularly to achieve good planarization  during sample deprocessing. By evenly delayered the large  MIM structure especially on targeted MIM plate will often  increase the success rate in finding defect. In order to overcome  such challenge, this paper will discuss and show how a new  method can be applied to successfully identify the physical  defect.

How to proceed on? Detailed layout study was then carried  out. Layout tracing showed that the reported Gnd pad was  connected to the MIM structure below the pad. We believe that  the root cause of such failure was highly possible to be related to  this MIM issue. So, further electrical fault isolation was  performed and focused on targeted MIM structure location.  However, no TIVA spot was detected. It was suspected the spot  was blocked by the thick metal layers above the MIM plate.  Hence, alternative FA approach was proposed by removing the  UTM top metal and delayer till M4 layer. This was followed by  repeating electrical fault isolation again on the suspected MIM  structure.

Fig. 3 Two mirrored like VDD pin are not internally connected, similarly to  Gnd pad.

With the conventional top down mechanical polishing and  plasma etching method, sample exhibited poor uniformity and  unevenness. The thick UTM and less metal density at proximity  create difficulty in achieving good planarization during physical  de-processing on this singulated die. Due to unsuccessfulness  with the mechanical polishing method, we switched to engaging  in chemical etch, mechanical plus RIE plasma etching. In this  paper, an alternative sample preparation technique is being  proposed to overcome the common issues associated with  traditional de-processing technique. Firstly, passivation layer  was removed by using RIE plasma etch, then removed the  Aluminum bondpad with HCl, followed by RIE etch again to  expose UTM top metal. Mixture of 1: 1 Sulfuric acid and  hydrogen peroxide was then used to etch Cu top metal, followed  by mechanical polish the thin barrier M5 layer on both MIM  structure located at left and right site of the die. Based on layout

tracing, microprobing at M4 layer observed MIM short between  top plate and bottom plate on this analyzed die as illustrated in  Fig. 5(a). This measurement helped to narrow down the  defective MIM structure on left site of the die. However,  electrical fault isolation on M4 layer still showed no distinct spot  was detected.  To determine the exact location of the short, a  current of 2mA was forced through the failed MIM structure;  diffused spot at the MIM structure was detected as illustrated in  Fig. 5(b). Without the exact short location, progressive FIB cuts  were chosen with slide and view; it took longer time and needed  multiple cuts before defect was found. In addition, vigilance in  stopping for frequent SEM images is required or else a defect  could have been lost in between cuts. With this progressive FIB  cut, we observed anomaly nearby spot location which suspected  to be due to MIM breakdown. To further understand the defect,  TEM analysis was performed and it showed only M3 void as  illustrated in Fig. 6.

For this analyzed device, the MIM capacitor is composed of  two parallel Al plates with a dielectric layer between the plates.  Contact between the top electrode plate and overlying metal is  made by W vias. The bottom electrode layer is in intimate  contact with the underlying interconnect metal. When the  problem arises due to Via punch through, it could be analyzed  by using an alternative sample preparation method as proposed.  This method can view and verify the via punch issue on big  MIM plate structure easily and more effectively. As sample was  deprocessed to Via 4 layer, we used wet etching by H2O2 to  selectively remove the Tungsten Via. Upon removal of Via4 W  plug, surface SEM inspection found a big hole at the top MIM  plate and hole at bottom MIM plate. In Fig.16, it is clearly  showed that the W Via on top MIM plate shorted to the bottom  MIM plate resulted fail. If there were no via punch through  between top plate and bottom MIM plate, the top plate will not  have a hole as the W Via only landed on the top MIM plate. Fig.  17 showed step by step sample preparation method for this  analysis. With this method, we able to locate and identify the  root cause of the MIM via punch thru issue quickly and  efficiently without wasted time to perform progressive FIB cut  on the big MIM plates.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Sample preparation in failure analysis is a very important  analysis step which helps us to identify the physical defect for  further root cause and mechanism understanding of the issue.  Throughout these 2 case studies in uncovering the defect in  MIM structures with new sample preparation methodology, we  have clearly demonstrated how the causes of these failures are  being identified. Without a correct FA approach and good  physical sample methodology, the likelihood to identify MIM  defect such as MIM breakdown underneath UTM layers and via  punch through issue will be extremely low.
