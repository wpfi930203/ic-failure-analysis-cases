## Self-heating aware EM Reliability Prediction of  Advanced CMOS Technology by   Kinetic Monte Carlo Method  

Linlin Cai, Yudi Zhao + , Wangyong Chen, Peng Huang, Xiaoyan Liu, and Xing Zhang \* Institute of Microelectronics, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China.  \* Phone: +86 010-62755839       Email:  + zhaoyd@pku.edu.cn ;  \* zhx@pku.edu.cn

Abstract-   A physics-based kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) method is  developed to investigate the electromigration (EM) induced  resistance evolution of interconnects. The microscopic physical  mechanisms describing the transportation of metal ions and void  formation are proposed and implemented in the simulator. The  self-heating aware EM degradation of BEOL based on nanosheet  MOSFETs in advanced technology node is predicted by the  experiment-calibrated KMC simulator. The results show that  self-heating of device induced thermal crosstalk can aggravate the  EM degradation in multi-level interconnects with the confined  metal pitch and increased power density.

Keywords  –  Kinetic Monte Carlo method, Electromigration,  BEOL, Self-heating .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

With the scaled technology node, electromigration (EM) of  BEOL becomes more serious due to the reduced interconnects  dimensions and the increased current density [1-3]. What’s  more, the rapidly increased self-heating (SH) of the device  dissipated to interconnects aggravates the EM degradation [4-5].  However, the experiment-based lifetime prediction faces the  difficulty of combined influence of EM and SH. The simulation  of the EM reliability is necessary to give the microscopic  understanding of void formation [6]. Therefore, in this work,  we develop a physics-based kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC)  simulator to investigate the void evolution of interconnects  during the EM degradation. The physical mechanism about the  transportation of metal ions is included in the developed  simulator to provide the self-heating aware EM lifetime  prediction for sub-7nm technology node.

## II.   S IMULATION  M ETHOD AND  V ALIDATION 

The crystal lattice of metals are composed of the bonded  metal ions surrounded by the nearly-free-electron-like gas. Fig.  1 shows the physical mechanisms about the metal ions  movement during the EM process, including the metal ions  activation, ions aggregation and ions hopping. The activation  energy is modulated according to the number of the nearest ions,  corresponding to the adatom, step and vacancy sites [7]. The  activated ions can hop along the interfaces and bulk  interconnects driven by the electron wind, stress gradient, and  temperature. The metal ions have the tendency of aggregating  towards the lower energy locations, which contributes to the  void formation. The kinetic probability equations are described  as (1)-(3).

## III. R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSION 

To investigate the impacts of SH induced thermal crosstalk  on EM degradation in BEOL, we take the 5-stack and 4-finger  nanosheet MOSFETs as an example for obtaining the higher  drive current based on the 7nm ground rules [15]. Fig. 4  illustrates the 3D finite element modeling (FEM) simulation   [16] of temperature distribution under   $80~\upmu\mathrm{W}$   power input of  each MOSFET with the multi-level interconnects marked on  the y-z cross section. The width and height of interconnect M1  are 15nm and 30nm respectively. The width and height of  interconnect M2 are   $18\mathrm{nm}$   and 36nm respectively [17]. The  length of M1 and M2 depends on the device size and layout  design. Due to the confined structure dimension and reduced  material thermal conductivity, the SH in device can reach over  400K, especially at drain regions of MOSFET. The generated  heat is dissipated to interconnects, aggravating the EM  reliability. The peak temperature in device, M1 and M2 under  different power inputs of each MOSFET   $\left(\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{T}}\right)$   are shown in Fig.  5. The results indicate that the increasing P T  elevates the SH in  device as well as the temperature differences ( T) between  different levels of interconnects. The same trend in similar  device structure can be observed in experiment results [18].

Fig. 6 shows the resistance evolution of M1, M2 during the  EM simulation with or without SH effect. It can be found that  considering the SH in device, the M1 shows the earlier EM  degradation compared with M2, because the higher temperature  increases the hopping probability of metal ions, promoting the  void formation. Both the M1 and M2 without SH are resistant to  the EM degradation within the same simulated time. The  detailed void morphology and the related potential distribution  of M1 and M2 at 3000 hours considering the SH reveal that the  full void formation in M1 significantly increases the local  potential with the constant current method, as shown in Fig. 7,  which further leads to the EM failure. Here the EM failure is  defined as  $20\%$   resistance increase relative to initial resistance  state. The simulated statistical failure distributions of  thermal-aware EM in M1 and M2 are plotted in Fig. 8, which  complies with the log normal distribution. The results show that  the EM degradation of M1 with higher temperature in  comparison with that of M2 suffers the shorter lifetime as well  as the larger variability.

The  increased power not only reduces the EM lifetime but also  enlarges the differences of TTF between interconnects due to  the increased temperature differences. The 3  EM lifetime of  M2 compared with that of M1 can be obtained under 100  m W/FET. The thermal aware EM lifetime evaluation is shown  in Fig. 10. The fitting line means the TTF prediction by Black’s  empirical equation (7) [19].

$$\mathrm{TTF}=\mathbf{A}\cdot\boldsymbol{j}^{-n}\exp\bigl(E_{\scriptscriptstyle A}\,/\,k_{\scriptscriptstyle B}T\bigr)$$
Where A is the process dependent factor,  $j$   is the current density  of interconnects,  $n$   is the exponent (=1),   $E_{A}$   is the EM activation  energy   $(=0.8\mathrm{~}\mathrm{eV}$   for copper),  k B  is the Boltzmann’s constant,  and   $T$   is the stress temperature. The results show that the  empirical   prediction   of   EM   degradation   would   be  over-optimistic without considering the SH coupling effect in  interconnects.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, a KMC simulator featuring the microscopic  physical mechanism of metal ions transportation is developed  to visualize the EM-induced void formation of interconnects.  The self-heating aware EM degradation of BEOL based on  nanosheet MOSFETs is predicted in scaled technology node.  The results show that increasing the device power aggravates  the SH of interconnects as well as the temperature differences  between different levels of interconnects. What’s more, the  thermal effect from the device will shorten the EM lifetime of  multi-level interconnects.
