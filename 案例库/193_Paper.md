A Study of n-LDMOS Off-state Breakdown

Degradation with 0.18μm BCD Technology

Feng Lin, Bin Yang\*, Guipeng Sun, Shuxian Chen, Chunxu Li, Yu Huang, Qiong Wang, Siyang Liu   Technology Development Division, CSMC Technologies Corporation, Wuxi, China  \*  Phone: 86-510-88113628 Fax: 86-510-88115555-3628 Email:  yangbin210@csmc.crmicro.com

Abstract-   The off-state BV degradation was studied by TCAD  simulations and silicon experiments. The degradation was caused  by high electrical field in the silicon surface and poor reduced  surface field (RESURF) effect during on-state, as the serious  Kirk-effect made hot holes trap into the field plate. A high rated  n-LDMOS off-state BV and R dson  improvement could be  optimized by drift engineering to solve the off-state BV  degradation issue.

Keywords – n-LDMOS, off-state BV degradation, high electrical  field, impact ionization , charge pumping test, hot holes injection,  drift engineering  .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

In power applications, lateral double-diffused metal oxide  semiconductor (LDMOS) transistors are widely used, because  they have low on-resistance (R dson ) and are easily integrated  into complementary metal oxide semiconductor (CMOS)  process [1]-[3]. However, people take serious concern on the  hot-carrier injection (HCI) reliability as the operating voltage of  LDMOS devices is very high. The existing LDMOS  characterization methodology mostly focuses on   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{dsun}}$   or linear  current of drain   $\left(\mathrm{I_{d\_linear}}\right)$   degradation after on-state and off-state  measurement [4]-[7]. In this paper, the off-state breakdown  voltage (BV) degradation has been further studied, in parallel  with   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{dsun}}$   and   $\mathrm{I_{d\_linear}}$   test. Our work demonstrates that the  off-state BV degradation can be improved by drift engineering.

## II.   E XPERIMENTS 

Because of the lower R dson  requirement for design, the  n-LDMOS architecture does not adopt the conventional  shallow trench isolation (STI) as field plate. The cross-section  of   the   n-LDMOS   fabricated   with    $0.18\upmu\mathrm{{m}}$    Bipolar-CMOS-DMOS (BCD) technology is shown in Fig.1.  To increase the current and decrease   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{dsun}}$   value further, thick  oxide (two-step-oxide) and shallow drift junction are  implemented in the LDMOS structure. Meanwhile, the  n-LDMOS is designed as a surface-dense type device with  floating poly and metal RESURF to enhance surface depletion,  reduce surface electric field, and increase breakdown voltage.

To study the off-state BV degradation, three different devices  are designed by changing the number of floating poly and the  length of the metal field of the drain end. These devices can  work at 50V, 60V, 65V, respectively. The effective length of  the channel region is   $0.7\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The thicknesses of gate oxide and  field oxide are 13nm and 110nm, respectively. The number of

multiple floating poly-gate field plates set upon the field oxide  is five. The width and spacing of floating plates are   $0.18\upmu\mathrm{m}$   and   $0.25\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , respectively. The threshold voltages   $(\mathrm{V_{th}})$   are all about  1.4V. The operating gate voltage of the devices are 5V. The  degradation is tested at room temperature with 1.1 times drain  operating voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{d})}$  , while the gate voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{g})}$   sweeps from  0V to 5V.

The charge pumping (CP) measurements are operated with  the constant gate voltage pulse amplitude of 8V, the frequency  of gate pulse of 1 MHz, and the base voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{base})}$   of the pulse  varied from -12V to 1V. The rising and falling times of the  pulse are both 100ns. The drain and source are grounded, and  the CP current   $(\mathrm{I_{cp}})$   is measured from the p-body to reveal the  hot-carrier trapping and the interface states generation.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

Instead of decreasing  after stress like normal devices with STI field plate, the I d_linear increases about   $5\%$   to   $7\%$  . It seems that hot holes have injected  into the above oxide layer during stress, as the trapped holes  will increase I d_linear  because the inducing negative mirror  charges enhance the effective N-drift doping. Meanwhile,  devices also suffer seriously off-state BV degradation after  stress. It  can be seen that  higher working voltage may lead to  worse off-state BV degradation.

Fig.3 shows the high electrical field and impact ionization  respectively during off-state and on-state by technology  computer aided design (TCAD) simulations. Under this  structure, the surface electric field is more severe than the  conventional structure (STI-based). The off-state breakdown

mainly occurs in the area of step oxide and the edge of gate poly  or dummy poly. When working at on-state, the position of  impact ionization is obviously closer to surface than that of  STI-based devices. That means increasing current density could  bring in more impact ionization rate, which is closer to the  surface. Serious Kirk-effect makes more hot holes trapped into  the oxide near the field plate. As a result the HCI reliability is  more seriously affected. When the injected holes near drain  electrode increase, the actual voltage drop between drain and  source is reduced; the breakdown position and electric field  intensity of off-state are changed. That gives explanation to the  off-state BV degradation.

In Fig. 4, the CP curve has been measured before and after  on-state stress. The inset is the expression of   $\mathrm{I_{cp}}$   obtained by  Groesenken et al., which indicates that  $\mathrm{I_{cp}}$   is proportional to the  average interface state density [8]. The shape of the CP curve is  basically unchanged after stress, but the peak current increases  and the curve shifts horizontally to a negative direction. This is  because there are more new interface states produced in the  stress process, which leads to the increase of CP current. At the  same time, a positive charge trap will be trapped into the field  plate layer during the stress process, which makes the threshold  voltage and the flat-band voltage of the device move to the  negative direction horizontally[9][10].

Corresponding device structure, a large number of hot holes  inject and interface states generate at the poly end. There are a  lot of interface states at step oxide area, also there may be hot  holes injection near the drain electrode. This is consistent with  the simulation results of TCAD and the experimental data  discussed above.

In order to characterize the stability of voltage degradation, a  long-time stress of 500s has been applied on devices. After that  the off-state BV has been scanned repeatedly. Take the result of  65V device as an example in Fig.5, off-state BV will gradually  recover and finally stabilize during continuous test. It is not like  the common junction walk-out as 1-2V shift, while around 10V

change because of the hot holes trapping effect. After first  stress, some of the hot holes are not completely captured by  charge traps in thick field plate. As the inset image shows, after  stress multi times, these free holes will gradually discharge  from field plate. However, partial HCI damage is irreversible,  that finally results in a BV degradation about 6V. This walk-out  phenomenon is repeatable among the n-LDMOS devices.

Based on the above analyses, BV degradation can be  improved by drift engineering optimization. By adjusting the  concentration distribution in the drift region, optimizing the  influence of dummy poly and metal RESURF on the surface,  the electric field intensity and impact ionization density of the  device can be reduced. Meanwhile, the impact ionization region  can be pushed to the bottom as far as possible. The direction of  the electric field near the drain electrode also can be more  paralleled to the surface, which helps reduce the hot holes  injection.

As shown in Fig.6 (a), through drift engineering the high  electric field is reduced a half and the impact ionization is  decreased three orders of magnitude. The change is particularly  evident between the second and fifth dummy poly. These two  locations are inferred as large injection areas of hot holes above.  Fig.6 (b) shows the specific differences of the impact ionization  before and after drift engineering, which indicates an extended  current path in cross section. It can not only deepen the current  path, but also ensure that the overall current density will not be  much reduced. This adjustment will also lead to some changes  in off-state BV, the final result is shown in Fig.7. After drift  engineering, the off-state BV of 50V and 60V devices increased  by 8V or 10V, while the 65V device decreased by about 5V.  After stress, they all show only 1-2V degradation, and  $\mathrm{\bf{R}}_{\mathrm{dsun}}$   also  maintains basically as the same level.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Our work implies that, in both TCAD simulations and  experimental results, the off-state BV degradation is worse  while I d_linear  degradation is toward negative way. This is caused  by high electrical field in the silicon surface and poor RESURF  effect during on-state, as the serious Kirk-effect makes hot  holes trapping into the field plate. By optimizing drift  engineering, the off-state BV degradation issue is solved. Our  investigation finally gets a high rated n-LDMOS off-state BV  and  $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{dsun}}$   improvement.

With CMOS compatible process, some key steps and  machines will lead to the unsteady oxide-silicon interface and  the change of N-drift surface condition. With the more and

more upgrading   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{dsun}}$   requirement, the LDMOS structure is  innovating to optimize the drain-side engineering which  focuses on current path and parasitic capacitor from gate to  drain. On the other hand, in order to make core high voltage  devices more compatible with the generic baseline, the thermal  cycle and oxide loss control will constrain some special process  integration schemes. Under this condition, drift area is usually  high doped at the surface region, while junction depth cannot be  deep enough for fully isolated application. Then, the severe  HCI and kirk effects should be checked more carefully.
