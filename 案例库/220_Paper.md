## Application of SIMS for the characterization of  Nitrogen in TaN film  

Yun Wang\*, Han Wei Teo, Kian Kok Ong, Nyuk Lin Phang, Poh Chuan Ang, Xiaoxuan Li, Ramesh Rao Nistala and  Zhi Qiang Mo  QRA-PFA Department, GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte. Ltd.  60 Woodlands Industrial Park D Street 2, Singapore 738406.  \*Phone: +65 66701530  Fax: +65 63604592  Email:  yun.wang@globalfoundries.com

Abstract -   Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry (SIMS) analysis of  nitrogen content in Tantalum Nitride (TaN) film is used for a new  set-up process monitoring. Technical Challenges facing by the  application of SIMS analysis are include a) Selection of  appropriate representative elemental molecular; b) Analysis  polarity and primary beam sputtering energy; c) Repeatability  and quantification. SIMS is one of the most capable tools to detect  low nitrogen content in the film compared with XPS or Auger, so  as to be chosen to ensure an excellent controllability of deposition  process for thin films. In this paper, the methodology of  characterization of low nitrogen concentration in the thin film by  SIMS will be investigated and demonstrated.

Keywords  –  Tatntalum nitride, Roughness, Nitrogen flow .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Thin deposited film of TaN are often considered for  providing resistor functionality in compatible integrated circuit  technologies such as monolithic microwave integrated circuits  or even CMOS technologies. TaN is a promising candidate  employed as a reliable resistor. In numerous publications, it has  been shown that the properties of thin tantalum nitride layer  depend very strongly on the deposition technique and the  process conditions [1]. There are 2 types of deposition process –  chemical ‐ vapor ‐ deposited(CVD) and physical ‐ vapor ‐ deposited  (PVD) TaN between layers. The PVD TaN film can act as a  better resistor than the CVD TaN film. The higher thermal  stability of PVD TaN than CVD TaN can be accounted by their  difference in microstructures [2]. In this paper, PVD TaN film  is applied. Several methods to analyze the nitrogen in TaN thin  film such as X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS),  Time-of-fight   SIMS   (TOF-SIMS),   Auger   Electron  Spectroscopy (AES) and Dynamic SIMS (DSIMS) are  considered. XPS is one of the commonly used methods for  quantifying Ta:N ratio. However, due to low N content in TaN  film, nitrogen 1s peak is overlapping with Ta 4p and results in  inaccuracy of quantification. Simultaneously, AES and TOF-SIMS suffer from insufficient sensitivity problems either. Due  to the requirement to monitor and optimize the   $\Nu_{2}$   flow control  within a very stringent variation in the PVD process, Dynamic  SIMS technique is studied to determine N content in TaN film.  One of the main applications of Dynamic SIMS is the in-depth  distribution analysis of trace elements, in which ion impact  energy is adjusted depending on the depth of interest and  required depth resolution. DSIMS is well known to have  extremely high sensitivity for all elements analysis and for bulk

composition with high dynamic range (more than 5 decades)  [3].  On the other hand, one of the technical challenges in  quantification of SIMS is the ‘matrix effect’. Ion yield of a  species from different matrix is different; correlation of  secondary ion yield and concentration will be different for  different matrix. Therefore, it causes challenges to quantify  elements in particular substrate when the proper standard  sample is unavailable. To overcome this problem, a secondary  standard sample is adopted for every batch of the analysis.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION   

In SIMS analysis, the intensity of the detected secondary ions  is influenced by the chemical composition of the surrounding  matrix material. To reduce matrix effect,   $\mathrm{M}\mathrm{C}\mathrm{s}^{+}$  technique is  conducted which with Cs itself working as a reference element  analyzed under positive mode. As the cluster Cs has ultra-high  ion yield during analysis, the Cs ion signal will be switched to  another detector called Faraday cup, whereas the NCs ion signal  detected by EM detector, hence the reference element and trace  elements are determined by different detectors, the detectors  non-linearity will affect result’s accuracy and precision.   To  maintain all elements signal under same detector (EM), the  analysis impact energy cannot be set high. A low energy   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$  primary beam was sputtered on sample surface.  Fig. 2 showed  the depth profile of TaN film. The ion clusters of NCs, and Cs  intensity are displayed as a function of time.

From Fig. 2, the intensity of NCs cluster   $({\sim}300~\mathrm{~c/s})$   is  detected. As the  $\Nu_{2}$   flow specification is extraordinarily strict in  the process monitoring, the low intensity of N will get larger  data variation resulting in deviation of the process control. The  analyzed data repeatability and reproductively is not able to  control the process specification accurately.

To verify the speculation, SEM imaging is conducted on   $\mathrm{TaN}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface which was bombarded by   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$  beam as  shown in Fig. 4. A very rough surface is observed which  implies atomic mixing severely before the   $\mathrm{TaN}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface  reached during the sample sputtering. AES spectrum  acquisition on sputtered   $\mathrm{{TaN}/\mathrm{{SiO}}_{2}}$   interface is shown in Fig 5.  Ta was detected on the flower like area, whereas Si and O were  detected on the surrounding. It is demonstrated that high energy  Cs ion sputtering formed film roughness, the average TaN  intensity should be calculated within the profile stable region.

For sample with homogeneously dispersed analyte, faster  sputter rates will increase the secondary ion signal in the  analysis and reduce the analysis time. Higher impact energy  and a moderate beam current are applied on sample analysis. In  SIMS, there are many secondary ions or clusters bombarded off  the sample surface, the selection of suitable cluster ion is  therefore of supreme important and challenging [4]. In this  case, TaN cluster representing nitrogen element is traced by  negative mode aiming to get high intensity. Fig. 3 showed TaN  and Si depth profiles.

The intensity of TaN is   $1000\mathbf{x}$   higher than low energy Cs  primary beam positive mode testing and reached   $10^{5}~\mathrm{c/s}$  , the  TaN depth profile is stable and obtained sufficient data points.  However, from Si profile, it is observed that a ramping up from  350s until   $\mathrm{TaN}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface. One hypothesis is Cs ions  bombardment causes film roughness and the profile distortion.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In summary, Dynamic SIMS is an excellent technique to  characterize low nitrogen content in TaN film. It has high  accuracy, stability and repeatability to monitor and control the  deposition process very well and ensure the film parameters  meet the quality specifications.  By using Cs high energy beam  test condition, and by averaging the TaN intensity within the  stable region, the nitrogen content by PVD process deposition  is able to be monitored to meet the process control requirement.
