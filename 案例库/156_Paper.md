## Comparison of Cl effect on Au-Al and Cu-Al HTS and  bHAST wire bond reliability performance  

Wang Bisheng 1, Lois Liao Jinzhi  $2^{*}$  , Zhang Xi 3, Li Xiaomin 4, Hua Younan 5, Tee Weikok 6, Yee Boonhwa 7, Mao  Songlin 8, Yao Qinghong 9  1 Huawei Technologies Co Ltd, Bantian Huawei Base, Longgang District, Shenzhen, 518129, China  2,3,4,5,9 Wintech Nano-Technology Services Pte Ltd, The Alpha #03-26,10 Science Park Rd, Science Park II, 117684, Singapore  6,7 Sumitomo Bakelite Singapore Pte Ltd, 1 Senoko S Rd, 758069, Singapore     8 Heraeus Zhaoyuan Precious Metal Materials Co. Abstract-   Wire bond industry is transitioning from Au (gold)  bonding wire to Cu (copper) bonding wire, due to the high cost of  Au and comparatively much lower cost and good electricity  conductivity of Cu. Whilst Cu is less reliable than Au. Cu is  susceptible to corrosion especially under the presence of moisture  and Cl (chloride). In this work, Au and Cu wires were ball bonded  to Al pad and molded with EMC (epoxy mold compound) with  different ranges of Cl. Cl was purposely added into EMC to  trigger the failure. All EMC samples were prepared with pH  range of 5 – 7. Reliability tests bHAST (biased highly accelerated  stress test) and HTS (high temperature storage test) were  conducted. The results showed that under dry condition, Cl was  not easy to attack the wire bond, and failure did not occur unless  high content of Cl (up to   $\mathbf{500ppm)}$   was present. On the contrary,  under humid environment, even several ppm content of Cl would  cause failure of Cu-Al wire bond. By comparison, under humid  environment, as Au-Al wire bond formed corrosion products  which locked the Cl, therefore Cl could not repeatedly serve as a  catalyst. Au-Al failure occurred only with high ppm content of Cl  (up to 500ppm). In addition, it is found that Cl will promote the  Au-Al IMC growth in HTS and bHAST.

Keywords  –  Wire bond, Reliability, Corrosion, Chlorine.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Au price is soaring in recent years, and wire bond industry is  transitioning from Au (gold) bonding wire to Cu (copper)  bonding wire, due to the high cost and Au and comparatively  much lower cost and good electricity conductivity of Cu.  However, Cu is less reliable than Au. Cu is susceptible to  corrosion especially with the presence of Cl.

Polymer encapsulated components are almost universally  used in consumable applications over the conventional ceramic  or metal electronic packages. Polymer encapsulants for  packaging commonly contain corrosive chloride ions and  absorbing moisture from environment. There are many studies  reporting the Cu wire bond failures caused by Cl corrosion,  especially under temperature humidity environment [1-5].

This work compared Cl effect on Au-Al and Cu-Al HTS and  bHAST wire bond reliability performance. High contents of Cl  were purposely added into EMC to accelerate the failure.  Efforts was made to study the failure mechanism of Au-Al and  Cu-Al wire bond under Cl attack. This work tried to provide  reference to the practical issues encountered in production.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

4NAu and 4NCu wires with diameter of 0.8mil were used in  this study. The bonding wires were provided by Heraeus. The  wires were ball bonded to SOP16L lead frame, with   $0.6\mathrm{{um}}$  thickness Al-0.5Cu using K&S ProCu bonder. EMCs with 4  different contents of Cl were provided by Sumitomo. The Cl  contents are <20ppm, 50ppm, 100ppm, 500ppm, respectively.  The Au-Al and Cu-Al bonded devices were molded using the  Cl-contained EMCs. Table I shows the materials and key  factors in this study.

Reliability tests bHAST    $(130^{0}\mathrm{C}/85\%\mathrm{RH}/20\mathrm{V})$   and HTS   $(150^{0}\mathrm{C})$   were performed to accelerate the failure to see the Cl  effects on Au-Al and Cu-Al wire bond reliability. Table II  shows the experiment runs and reliability tests. Electricity  resistance of the molded devices after reliability tests was  recorded. When the device resistance is larger than   $20\%$   of the  original device, the device is considered failed.

To study the failure modes, the samples were prepared by  epoxy mounting, mechanical grinding, ion milling and FIB  (focus ion beam). Optical microscopy, scanning electron  microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM)  with EDX (energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy) were used to  characterized the samples. SEM model is FEI Helios NanoLab  600i, and TEM model is FEI Tecnai G2 F20, respectively.

Because Au-Al IMC thickness grows faster than that of  Cu-Al IMC (about 5 times), Au-Al samples were taken out at  HTS 0h, 24h, 48h, 72h, 192h, 1000h and were cross-sectioned  to see the IMC growth progress. Run #1 sample ball and pad  interfaces were normal. However, there was obvious interfacial  crack at Run #4 at HTS 1000hrs.

It can be seen from Fig. 1 that IMC thickness of Run #4  (500ppm Cl in EMC) is thicker than that of Run #1 (  $\mathrm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{<}20\mathrm{ppm}\,\mathrm{Cl}$  in EMC). IMC thicknesses were measured of both Run # and  Run4 at different time point, as illustrated in Fig. 2. It is  interesting to find that before HTS   $150^{\circ}\mathbf{C}$   24h, the IMC  thickens of Run #1 and #4 are similar. However, after HTS 24h,  run #4 IMC grew much faster than that of #1, which was about  1.3-1.5times. The results showed that in HTS, Cl not only

accelerated the failure but also promoted the IMC growth in  Au-Al wire bond.

Fig. 11 shows the cross-section optical images of Cu-Al Run  #5 (<20ppm Cl in EMC) and Run #8 (500ppm Cl in EMC)  bHAST samples. For both Run #5 and #8, IMC thickness is thin  and is about several hundred nanometers. No abnormality was  observed in terms of IMC growth. For Cu-Al Run #8 at bHAST  96 and 192h, there are interfacial cracks. TEM analysis was  performed on sample Run #8 after bHAST 192h (see Fig. 12).  There were interfacial cracks observed. Further EDX study  revealed the crack contains nanometer-size Cu particles, Cl, O  and Al, as shown in Fig. 10b.

Compared with TEM analysis of Fig. 10 and 12 (Au-Al # 4  and Cu-Al #5), both samples failed with 500ppm Cl in EMC. Cl  element was detected at the interface of both samples. However,  at the Au-Al interface, corrosion compound AlClO was found,  which was absence in the Cu-Al sample. It is suggested that in  the Au-Al, Cl reacted with Al and O, and formed compound  AlClO. During this process, Cl was locked by the AlClO and  could not continue to attack the interface. Whilst, in Cu-Al  interface, the Cl acted as catalyst and did not involve into the  compound formation. Cl could continue to attack the Cu-Al  interface and to repeat the corrosion. This can explain the  reason why several ppm level of Cl can cause failure of Cu-Al  wire bond. On the contrary, Au-Al is more reliable to Cl attack  under the same condition. It is suggested that chemical equation  during Au-Al and Cu-Al bHAST is like below:

$$\mathrm{A u_{y}A l_{x}+C l^{-}+H_{2}O\longrightarrow A u+A l C l O+H_{2}}$$

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Molded wire bond devices Au-Al and Cu-Al HTS and  bHAST performances were studied with different contents of  Cl in EMCs. It is found that:

(1)   Under HTS dry condition, for both Au-Al and Cu-Al  samples, Cl was not easy to attack the wire bond, and  failure did not occur unless high content of Cl (up to  500ppm) was present.     (2)   On the contrary, under bHAST humid environment, even  several ppm content of Cl would cause failure of Cu-Al.  By comparison, under humid environment, as Au-Al  wire bond will form corrosion products which lock the  Cl, therefore Cl could not repeatedly act as catalyst.

Au-Al failure occurred only with high ppm content of Cl  (up to 500ppm).   (3)   It is found that high content of Cl (500ppm in this study)  obviously promoted Au-Al IMC growth in HTS and  bHAST
