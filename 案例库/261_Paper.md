## Improvement of sensitivity of ultrasonic beam  induced resistance change (SOBIRCH) method with  ultrasound resonance in mold resin  

Takuto Matsui 1 , Kosuke Tatsumi 1 , Tomohiro Kawashima 1 , Yoshinobu Murakami 1 ,   Naohiro Hozumi 1\* , Shigeru Eura 2 , and Toru Matsumoto 2   1 Dept. Electrical & Electronic Information Engineering, Toyohashi University of Technology,  1-1 Hibarigaoka, Tempaku-cho, Toyohashi, Japan.  2 Dept. 18 Systems division, HAMAMATSU PHOTONICS K.K.,  812 Joko-cho, Higashi-ku, Hamamatsu, 431-3196, Japan.    \*Phone: +81 53 244 6958 Fax: +81 53 244 6757 Email: hozumi@ee.tut.ac.jp

Abstract-   In some cases of failure analysis of semiconductor  devices, package state analysis without mold decapsulation is  required. The ultrasonic beam induced resistance change  (SOBIRCH) method has been proposed to carry out the analysis  without decapsulation. In the previous research, it was suggested  by numerical simulation that ultrasonic resonance in mold resin  improves the sensitivity of the SOBIRCH. In this report, the  sensitivity improvement with ultrasound resonance was verified  experimentally.

Keywords  –  IR-OBIRCH, SOBIRCH, Ultrasonic stimulation,  Package failure analysis .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

In the failure analysis of semiconductor devices, the infrared  optical beam induced resistance change (IR-OBIRCH) [1,2]  method is widely used to localize faults on wires. In the IROBIRCH method, a small change of current relating to a  resistance change induced by an optical beam heating is  observed as a signal [3]. Since the mold resin of a  semiconductor device is not optically transparent, the IROBIRCH method generally requires a decapsulation of mold  resin. However, this decapsulation of the mold resin may  damage the device, leading to a reduction in reproducibility of  the failure condition [4].

As a solution to this problem, the authors have proposed the  ultrasonic beam induced resistance change (SOBIRCH) method  that can realize the fault location without mold decapsulation  [5] [6]. Fig. 1 shows the comparison of concepts between the  IR-OBIRCH method and SOBIRCH. In the SOBIRCH, the  laser in the IR-OBIRCH method was replaced by a focused  ultrasonic wave. While the principle of the IR-OBIRCH method  is based on a laser stimulation, the principle of the SOBIRCH  is based on an ultrasonic stimulation.

As will be exhibited in this paper the focal spot of the  employed ultrasound is as small as several tens of micrometers  in diameter. It is expected that the SOBIRCH may be superior  to the conventional lock-in thermography (LIT) method in  failure localization, if a high sensitivity at high carrier frequency

has been realized [6]. Furthermore, as the wire is actively  stimulated by ultrasound, the SOBIRCH can observe faults with  a lower bias voltage than LIT in some cases [7]. In addition, the  superimpose of an ultrasonic reflection image and the SOBRCH  image in the same field of view is helpful to localize faults  without mold decapsulation.

In the previous report [8], it was suggested by means of  numerical simulation that a resonance of ultrasonic wave in the  mold resin has the potential to improve the sensitivity of the  SOBIRCH.   Based   on   the   numerical   simulation,   by  appropriately tuning the frequency of ultrasound to thin mold  resin, it is possible to significantly strengthen the intensity of  the SOBIRCH signal. In this report, it is experimentally verified  that the above effect of the ultrasound resonance in mold resin  improves the sensitivity of SOBIRCH.

## II.   S OBIRCH PROCEDURE 

Fig. 2 shows the schematic diagram of the measurement  system of the SOBICH. An ultrasonic wave, its carrier  frequency being several tens of MHz, is modulated by a square  wave of several kHz. The modulated ultrasonic wave from the  transducer stimulates the wire, and induces a small change in its  local resistance, leading to a change of current [9]. Since the  burst signal of ultrasound is created by the square wave, the

current change has the same frequency component as the square  wave. This small modulated current change is finally detected  as the SOBIRCH signal by a lock-in amplifier in order to retain  high sensitivity [10]. Prior to the SOBIRCH, an ultrasonic  reflection image is acquired in order to locate the field of view  of SOBIRCH observation .

## III. N UMERICAL ANALYSIS OF ULTRASOUND   ACCOMPANIED BY RESONANCE   

In this section, in order to verify the possibility of  improvement of signal intensity by means of ultrasound  resonance, the result of numerical analysis prior to experimental  study will be described. At first, a numerical model for the  SOBIRCH measurement was constructed [9]. An analysis was  performed assuming that only traveling wave propagates  through the resin layer. In addition, when the resin layer is very  thin, a resonance of ultrasound is expected to enhance the sound  field in the resin layer, leading to an increase in SOBIRCH  signal intensity. An analysis for such a case was performed as  well by assuming that the acoustic wave in the thin resin layer  could be approximated as a plane wave [8].

Fig. 4 presents the results of numerical analysis of the signal  intensity of the SOBIRCH as a function of the thickness of mold  resin. This figure shows the result with the constant carrier  frequency at   $40\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{\MHz}}$  . The solid line is the result with the  standing wave in the mold resin. The dashed line is the result  without the standing wave. The propagation of ultrasonic waves  inside the resin is approximated as a plane wave when  resonance was taken into account. Both the curves in Fig. 4 are  normalized by the maximum of the curve without the standing  wave. The resin absorbs the acoustic wave, and the temperature  of the resin increase. The temperature of the wiring surface  increase accompanied by the temperature rise of the resin.  When the resin layer becomes thick, the SOBIRCH signal tends  to become strong. However, when the thickness exceeds a  certain amount, the SOBIRCH signal weakens because the  ultrasonic wave attenuates and does not reach the wiring  surface. Therefore, when numerical analysis without the  resonance is performed, the SOBIRCH signal has one  maximum in relation to the resin thickness. Meanwhile, in  numerical analysis results considering the effect of resonance,  periodic maximums and minimums appear in relation to resin  thickness. Considering the acoustic impedance of the layer in  contact with the resin layer, the maximum appears when the Eq.  (1) holds.

$$L={\frac{2n+1}{4}}\cdot{\frac{c}{f}}\quad(n=1,2,3,\cdot\cdot\,)$$
(1)

here,  $L,c$  , and  f  are the resin thickness, the speed of sound in the  resin and the carrier frequency, respectively. From this result, it  is found that the SOBIRCH signal intensity shows about a three  times difference due to the slight difference in resin thickness.  In other words, the intensity of the SOBIRCH signal can be  improved by appropriately selecting the resin thickness.

However, it is difficult to accurately control the thickness of  mold resin by polishing. On the other hand, it is easy to change  the carrier frequency at the measurement while keeping the  resin thickness constant. Fig. 5 shows the calculated SOBIRCH  signal when the carrier frequency is varied by fixing the  thickness of mold resin at either   $50~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  or   $320~\mathrm{\upmum}$  . The  calculation results are normalized by the peak value of curve (a)  in Fig. 5. A clear maximum appears at a resin thickness of 50   $\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . Considering the same consideration as in Fig. 4, it is

assumed that the peak values appear when Ep. (1) is established.  Although the peak values of curve (b) appear even when the  resin thickness is  $320\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , the attenuation of the ultrasonic wave  inside the resin layer becomes large. Therefore, the signal  intensity is not significantly enhanced by the ultrasound  resonance. It is conceivable that the sensitivity of the SOBIRCH  signal can be improved by appropriately tuning the carrier  frequency in relation to the thickness of the mold resin.  Moreover, the effect is thought to be more prominent as the  resin is thinner.

## A. Sample preparation  

An experimental study was carried out in order to confirm the  result of numerical simulation. A silicon chip sample with  wiring was prepared, and the sample was observed by  SOBIRCH. Fig. 6 shows the structure of the sample. Two types  of samples were prepared, their resin layer thicknesses being 50   $\upmu\mathrm{m}$   and   $320\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , respectively. In Fig. 6, the cross section of the   $50{\mathrm{-}}{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  -type sample is shown. The 320-μm-sample was  prepared maintaining the resin layer without recessing. Mold  resin of the sample includes a filler of which the average particle  size is  $16\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$   in diameter. Fig. 7 shows the wiring pattern on the  chip of the sample. In this report, the wire with   $2\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$   in width is  chosen as the target of measurement.

## IV. D ISCUSSION 

For quantitative evaluation of experimental results, Fig. 9  shows the frequency dependence of the signal intensity  obtained from the above experimental results together with the  numerical analysis results. The plots in Fig. 9 show the  measurement result, and the dashed curves show the calculation  results that are the same as in Fig. 5. The measurement results  in Fig. 9 are normalized by the signal intensity at 46 MHz at a  resin thickness of  $50\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The calculation results are normalized  by the maximum at a resin thickness of  $50\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . To minimize the  sum of square error between the measurement result and the  numerical analysis result, the numerical analysis result was  corrected. The resin with a thickness of   $50~\ensuremath{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   showed the  highest signal intensity when the carrier frequency was  $46\,\mathrm{MHz}$  .  For resins with a thickness of   $320\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , the signal intensities had  no significant change regarding the change of carrier frequency.  Therefore, the above result was confirmed that the frequency  dependence of the intensity of the SOBIRCH signal is caused  by the resonance of ultrasonic wave inside the resin. As a result,  it was suggested that the intensity of the SOBIRCH signal can  be significantly improved by appropriately tuning the carrier  frequency for thin mold resin.

## V. C ONCLUSION   

The improvement of the sensitivity of the SOBIRCH with  ultrasound resonance in mold resin was discussed through  theoretical and the experimental approaches. By considering the  standing wave in mold resin, the numerical analysis suggested  that the intensity of SOBIRCH signal can be enhanced by  appropriately tuning the carrier frequency of ultrasound into the  resonance frequency in mold resin. In order to verify the result  of numerical analysis, an experiment was carried out. In the  experiment, the effect of the resonance of ultrasound leading to  the enhancement of the SOBIRCH signal was remarkable at 50   $\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . However, at   $320~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   the resonance effect becomes less  significant due to the internal attenuation through the mold resin.  The results confirmed that the sensitivity of SOBIRCH can be  upgraded by making use of the ultrasound resonance in the mold  resin.

In practical observation it may not be easy to determine the  resonant frequency of the resin layer, as both speed of sound  and thickness are not precisely known. Therefore, trial and error  may be needed in order to find a good carrier frequency to  optimize the sensitivity of SOBIRCH.   However, if the echo  profile of the sample is available there is a possibility of  determining the resonant frequency by checking the echo  spectrum prior to the SOBIRCH observation.   The authors  believe that such an attempt will significantly improve both  rapidness and sensitiveness of the SOBIRCH observation.
