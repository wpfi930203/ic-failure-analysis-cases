## Application of Nano-indentation method to  characterize adhesion strength of polyimide film  

Xiaoxuan LI\*, Xintong ZHU, Yi LIU, Xiaodong LI, Ramasamy CHOCKALINGAM, Ramesh Rao NISTALA and Zhi  Qiang MO  GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park D Street 2, Singapore 738406  Phone: +65 66701551 Fax: +65 63622938 \*Email: xiaoxuan.li@globalfoundries.com

Abstract-   Thick polyimide layer has been used as buffer layer to  absorb stress imposed by molding compounds for many years.  This paper studied the adhesion strength of the thick polyimide  layer under different process conditions. The stress was induced  by indentation, and delamination was confirmed by FIB and SEM  imaging. The calculated adhesion energy for polyimide film was  highest, around   $\mathbf{30~J}/\mathbf{m}2$  , for the wafer processed with an extra  pad protection layer Nblok (Nitrogen-doped barrier on top of  copper, SiCN) plasma clean before the spin coating of polyimide  film.

Keywords  –  Adhesion, Oxygen Plasma, Thin film, polyimide

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

The polyimides are lightweight and have good mechanical  tensile strength. In the IC manufacturing industry it is typically  used as stress buffer layer between passivation and molding  compounds of the device in semiconductor industry for many  years [1]. A thick polyimide film is designed to absorb the  stress imposed by the molding resin, however, delamination of  the thick polyimide film is sometimes observed after  temperature cycling (TC) and high temperature storage (HTS).

Due to its native poor adhesion to many materials, the  adhesive strength of polyimide is very critical in modern  microchip packaging, especially for the applications in the field  with high reliability requirements. Plasma treatments have been  widely employed to enhance the adhesion for polyimide film to  protected layer [4], which is able to modify the surface  condition of material to be protected and be favorable for  adhering strongly to subsequent polyimide film. In prior  research, it is reported that oxygen plasma treatment can  increase oxygen content at the surface and alter the top of SiC  film to improve adhesion property [5]. In this study, we  introduce the similar O2 plasma treatment on SiCN  anti-oxidization film on the top of Cu and passivation SiN/SiO2  stacks.

In this paper the impact of wafer process conditions on the  adhesion energy of thick polyimide film was investigated. A  commercial nano-indenter equipped with high load and  standard load dual head transducer with Berkovich tip was used  in the study, and a method to estimate the crack length induced  by nano-indenter was proposed and evaluated. The test method  was able to induce delamination of thick polyimide film, and  the calculated adhesion energy of polyimide film with different  process conditions were compared in this study.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

In total four wafers processed under different conditions were  tested, the process conditions are listed in table 1. This  controlled experiment aimed to study the effect of device alloy,  thin Nblok, and post thin Nblok plasma clean on adhesion  strength between Nblok and polyimide.

High load indentation were done at the center of test wafers  where the polyimide film was homogeneous and uniform as  shown in figure 6(a), 5 indents were performed for each test  wafer for accuracy of the results. The distance between two  measurement locations was far enough for the indenter to land  in a stain-free film. Figure 1 shows the optical image for the five  indents on one test sample. An in-situ XPM image was scanned  after each indent as shown in figure 2(a).  Figure 2(b) showed  the line scan profile for the indent in figure 2(a), line AB crosses  the center of the indent, the distance between point A and B was  used to represent the length of crack a. The measured crack  length was used in the following equation developed by  Rosenfeld for calculation of adhesion energy [2]:

$$G=\frac{0.627H^{2}h(1-v^{2})}{E}*\frac{1}{[1+v+2(1-v)H a^{2}/P]^{2}}$$
Where G is adhesion energy, H is the film hardness, v is  Poisson’s ratio, h is film thickness, P is indentation load, and E  is film’s Young’s modulus.

The film thickness h for polyimide film at wafer center was  measured by cross-sectional SEM imaging as shown in figure  6(a). The uniformity of polyimide film was observed, and no  defect was presented at the interface of polyimide and Nblok.

The film hardness H, and Young’s modulus E were measured  at the wafer center for each sample. Normal load transducer and  Berkovich tip were used for the measurements. Basic trapezoid  load function was applied as shown in figure 3, and the contact  depth for each measurement is around   $20\%$   of the polyimide  film thickness. The average hardness and modulus for 25  measurements were used to represent the properties of the film.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION   

## Delamination with high load indentation  

Figure 4 shows the schematic diagram of indentation-induced  delamination process. When the tip is in contact with the film,  an indent of contact radius, a, is created and stress is induced in  the film. As the applied load is gradually increased, the stress in  the tip presses down harder on to the film, thus increasing the  stress as well as the indent size or contact radius. The film will  debond at critical radius  $\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{c}}$  , i.e. the film will lift out and crack  propagates when contact radius   $\mathrm{~a~}>\mathrm{~a_{c}~}$  , the crack length is  indicated as ‘r’ the figures 3(c) and 3(d) [3].

## Crack radius measurement by XPM vs. SEM  

After every indent, FIB at indentation location need to be  performed followed by SEM imaging. To get the exact crack  radius, FIB should cut through the center and one edge of the  indent mark. This process, however, is very time consuming,  and therefore, the crack radius measurement by nano-indenter’s  in-situ XPM was adopted. After indent optical image (Fig 7)  and in-situ XPM image (Fig 2a) showed film buckling along the  three edges of triangle shaped indent mark. Based on the  delamination mechanism schematics illustrated in Fig 4, the  buckling was caused by film lift out. Assuming a uniform  delamination across all three edges of indent mark, a circular  shaped delamination centered on indenter tip will be formed  after indentation. The distance between point A and B,  highlighted in Fig 2(a), can be measured in a topographic line  scan of XPM image, which is shown in Fig 2(b). The line scan  crosses one of the sharp edges of indent mark and the indent  center, therefore, AB represents the diameter R of delamination  induced by indentation and the crack radius is one half of R.  Line scan was done for three edges for each indent, and the  average of 3 crack radius is the XPM measured crack radius for  the indent.

## Adhesion energy calculation  

The crack radius measured by XPM was used to calculate the  adhesion energy of polyimide film with Rosenfeld equation.  The average adhesion energy of five indents and standard  deviation for the four samples are listed in table 2. The adhesion  energy of the four samples ranged from 28 to   $31\;\mathrm{J}/\mathrm{m}2$  . Sample 2  which is without device alloy appeared to have the lowest  adhesion energy, and sample 3 which includes an additional  thin Nblk clean step appeared to have the highest adhesion  energy among the four samples, the difference is around   $9\%$  .  The standard deviations for four samples are from   $0.7\%$   to   $5.2\%$  .

In conclusion, nano-indentation method was adopted to  induce delamination and measure the adhesion energy of  polyimide film. A time efficient way of measuring crack radius  with Nano-indenter’s in-situ XPM function was also developed  and used to test the four samples. The difference of adhesion  strength between the four samples was observed    $(9\%$    difference) with acceptable standard deviation level.
