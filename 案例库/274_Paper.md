## Breakdown Mechanism of AlGaN/GaN-based HFET With Carbon-doped GaN Buffer  Layer grown on Si substrate   

Yiqiang   $\mathrm{Ni^{l,2^{*}}}$  , Liuan  $\mathrm{Li}^{2}$  , Liang   $\mathrm{He}^{2}$  , Yang LIU 2\*     1 Reliability Research and Analysis Center, No.5 Electronics Research Institute of the Ministry of Industry and Information    Technology, Guangzhou, 510610, People’s Republic of China     2 School of Electronics and Information Technology, Sun Yat-Sen University, Guangzhou 510275, People’s Republic of China  \*   Phone: +20-87237007   Fax: +86-020-87237185  Email: niyiqiang@ceprei.com , liuy69@mail.sysu.edu.cn

## Abstract 

In this paper, we evaluated the effect of carbon doping on the breakdown mechanism of GaN buffer as well as the material and  electrical properties of AlGaN/GaN HFETs. The introduction of carbon will slightly increase the surface roughness and degrade  the 2DEG carrier density, while leakage current (breakdown voltage) can be suppressed (enhanced) significantly   $(346~\mathrm{V}@1$     $\upmu\mathrm{A/mm}$   and   $748\;\mathrm{V}@1\;\mathrm{mA/mm})$  ). Leakage current of GaN can be explained using trap charge limited space-charge limited current  (SCLC) model and shows an obvious dependency on carbon concentration. The transient drain current measurements  demonstrate that the traps in the unintentional doped GaN buffer are mainly acceptor traps   $\mathrm{(C_{N})}$   with an energy level of   $\mathrm{textstyleV+538}$    meV, while both acceptor and donor traps (  $\mathrm{Ca}$   with an energy level of   $\mathrm{E_{C}–600\ m e V)}$   coexist in the carbon doped GaN buffer  layer.

## Introduction 

AlGaN/GaN heterostructure field effect transistors (HFETs)  is promising for high-power electronic device because of its  wide bandgap, high breakdown electric field, and high figure of  merit   $\mathrm{B}\mathrm{V}^{2}/\bar{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{on}}}$  .[1-4] To realize small drain leakage current,  sharp pinch-off behavior, and high off-state breakdown voltage,  highly resistive GaN layer is a critical requirement. However,  the conventional GaN material grown by metal-organic  chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) presents a natural n-type  conductivity because of the significant background carrier  concentration. Intentional doping with transition metals, such  as Fe or Mg,[5,6] is proposed to create acceptor states which  can compensate the background donors. But, the Fe or Mg  doping shows disadvantage of surface segregation and memory  effects. Alternatively, by adjusting the growth parameters (such  as the V/III ratio) of MOCVD, carbon atoms can be introduced  as the self-compensation into GaN, avoiding the reactor  contamination issues.[7]

Previous works report that carbon atoms in GaN lattice have  different incorporation locations, replacing N site or Ga site,  according to the formation energy of   $\mathrm{C_{N}}$   and   $\mathrm{C_{Ga}}$  . [8,9]  Combining   the   first   principle   calculation   and  photoluminescence (PL) spectrum analysis [10,11], for a  relatively low doping concentration, the carbon atom mainly  tend to form deep level acceptor   $\mathrm{C_{N}}$   due to the smaller  formation energy. At high carbon concentration, the formation  energy of the deep level donor   $\mathrm{C_{Ga}}$   becomes smaller and then  carbon in GaN lattice tend to exist in the Ga position. Generally,  both the acceptor and donor traps in the GaN buffer/transition  layer can strongly interact with the high electric field (close to  the vertical breakdown voltage) via charging/discharging  processes. Such interactions are expected to be closely  correlated with the vertical leakage/breakdown behaviors.

In this work, we studied the breakdown mechanism of  AlGaN/GaN HFETs with an unintentional doped or carbon  doped GaN buffer. The effect of carbon doping on the  morphology,   crystalline   quality,   leakage   current   and  breakdown behavior is evaluated. Combining the SCLC model  and transient drain current measurement, the discrepancy in  breakdown behavior between the samples with unintentional

doped and carbon doped GaN buffer is ascribed to the different  trap type and density.

## Experiment  

The AlGaN/GaN heterostructure on silicon substrate (Fig.1)  was grown by MOCVD, employing Trimethylgallium (TMGa),  trimethylaluminum (TMAl), and ammonia (NH3) as the Ga, Al,  and N precursors, respectively.[12,13] Hydrogen was used as  the carrier gas. After a short aluminum pre-deposition using  TMAl, a 100 nm HT-AlN nucleation layer was deposited at  around   $1060\ \ {}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  . Then, 140 nm AlGaN and GaN/AlN  superlattice buffer with a thickness of   $1.5\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$   were grown as the  strain buffer layer. And next, a 700 nm top GaN buffer layer  was grown on the superlattice buffer layer, followed by a 200  nm unintentionally doped GaN channel layer. It is worth  mentioning that the carbon concentration in the unintentionally  doped GaN buffer (labeled as sample A) is approximately   $5.{\dot{2}}{\times}10^{17}~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$   obtained by secondary ion mass spectroscopy  (SIMS) measurements. By adjusting the chamber pressure and  V/III ratio of GaN during the growth, the carbon concentration  in the doped GaN buffer (labeled as sample B) is increased to  approximately   $1.2{\times}10^{20}~\mathrm{cm}^{3}$  . Finally, a 30 nm AlGaN was  deposited as barrier layer with  $30\%$   Al composition obtained by  fitting the XRD 2θ-ω scan of (002) diffraction plane. The  leakage current of samples were carried out using a two  terminal lateral structure. The interval between the electrodes is   $100~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   and the AlGaN barrier layer between the electrodes  were removed with   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}/\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$  -based dry etching. Ohmic contact  metal consisting of Ti/Al/Ni/Au (  $15/80/25/60\ \mathrm{nm})$   is deposited  with electron beam evaporation and annealed at  $870\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 30 s  in nitrogen ambient.

## Characteristics and Discussion 

The  $5\upmu\mathrm{m}\times5\upmu\mathrm{m}$   scaled surface morphology and root mean  square roughness (RMS) of the samples were characterized  without any treatment by atomic force microscope (AFM). As  shown in Fig. 2, both samples show typical step-terrace  structure with a relatively smooth surfaces. The RMS of sample  A is approximately 0.62 nm, indicating a smooth surface. While  for sample B, the introduction of carbon doping shows  evidently impact on the surface roughness   $(\mathrm{RMS}{=}1.95~\mathrm{nm})$  ).  The crystalline quality was further evaluated by rocking curve.  The full width of half maximum values (FWHM) of (002) and  (102) peaks were used to estimate the screw- and edge-type  threading dislocation (TD) densities in GaN films (Fig. 3). [14]  The results indicate that the unintentionally doped sample  shows a lower TD density with the (002) and (102) FWHM are  397 arcsec and 865 arcsec, respectively. It is obviously that the  carbon incorporation can cause the increase of FWHM, whose  (002) and (102) FWHM are 858 arcsec and 1564 arcsec,  respectively. Therefore, the carbon doping will deteriorate the  crystalline quality and surface morphology, which may further  influence the 2DEG conduction properties. This also can be  confirmed by the Hall measurements. The sheet resistance  (average mobility) of the two-dimensional electron gas  degraded from approximately   $339\,\mathrm{\Omega/sq}$     $(1713\,\mathrm{\cm}^{2}\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{-}1}\mathrm{s}^{\mathrm{-}1})$   to  approximately   $405~\Omega/\mathrm{sq}$     $(1496~\mathrm{cm}^{2}\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1})$  , although the carrier  density are comparable for both samples (approximately   $1.1\!\times\!10^{13}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$  ). Our previous device results demonstrate that the  carbon doping decreases the output current slightly and  deteriorate the dynamic on resistance (R ON ) characteristics. [15]

Buffer leakage current is a common criteria to evaluate the

breakdown properties. The leakage current of unintentionally  doped and carbon doped samples (as shown in Fig. 4) were  carried out using a two terminal lateral structure. Based on our  previous works [16], a   $100\ \mathrm{nm}$   interval between the ohmic  electrodes is large enough to ensure the breakdown voltage (BV)  saturation, and the breakdown is dominated by the vertical GaN  material. Obviously, a relatively higher leakage current and  lower BV can be observed in sample A. The corresponding  voltages for sample A at leakage current of   $1~\upmu\mathrm{A/mm}$   and  1mA/mm are approximately   $80\,\mathrm{~V~}$   and   $420\;\mathrm{~V~}$  , respectively.  While the leakage current (breakdown voltage) can be  suppressed (enhanced) significantly for carbon doped material  (sample B)   $(346\,\mathrm{V}@1\,\upmu\mathrm{A/mm}$   and   $748\;\mathrm{V}@1\;\mathrm{mA/mm})$  ). Finally,  sample A and sample B show a hard BV of approximately 550  V and 891 V, respectively. The high density carbon dopant will  segregate at or near edge-type TD, playing as a compensating  acceptor that can result in the enhanced breakdown property.  [17] Usually, there is a conductive path between the HT-AlN  buffer and Silicon substrate, which can provide a current  transport route after the vertical breakdown. Therefore, the  breakdown voltage of a lateral structure is nearly equal to two  times of that in vertical direction. This can also be confirmed  from Fig. 5.

To examine the breakdown mechanism of GaN material,  we etched away the AlGaN/GaN heterostructure by ICP with a

residual GaN channel layer (about   $30~\mathrm{{nm}}$  ). The Ti/Al/Ni/Au  ohmic electrode area is   $100~\upmu\mathrm{m}{\times}200~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The contact property  is not as good as that on a heterostructure, but it shows no  obvious   influence   on   the   breakdown   measurements.  Current-voltage (I-V) characteristics were recorded and plotted  in log-log style (Fig. 5). The plot exhibits three distinct regions  depending on the applied bias voltage for sample A. The  current in low forward voltages   $\scriptstyle(<=20\ \mathrm{V},$  , region I) follows a  linear relation. When the voltage higher than trap-filled-limited  threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{TFL1})}$   (between 20 and 70 V, region II), the  I−V curve featured a sudden increase that was proportional to  V 6 . Then the current is proportional to   $\mathrm{V}^{2}$   in region III. The  origin of the three regions can be explained using space-charge  limited current (SCLC) model. [18] The SCLC model assumes  that carriers are injected into an insulator from the ohmic  contact. When the density of injected carriers is lower than that  of intrinsic free carriers in the insulator, current will follow  Ohmn’s law (region I). When the acceptor traps are completely  filled with electrons, current is dominated by the space charge  of trapped carriers (region II). At higher voltage the current  exhibits a trap-free square-law dependence.

We also can find three regions in the I-V curve of sample  B, but the curve is proportional to V, V 5  and   $\mathrm{V}^{10}$  , respectively,  showing two trap-filled-limited threshold voltages. Compared  with sample A, the carbon doped sample presents a higher  V TFL1 , which is enhanced from approximately   $20\ \mathrm{~V~}$   to  approximately 50 V. Those phenomenon can be attributed to  the higher trap density in the carbon doped sample. Therefore, a  higher external voltage is needed to trigger out the SCLC. The  two trap-filled-limited threshold voltages for sample B means  that there maybe two kinds of trap levels. Between   $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{TFL1}}$   and   $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{TFL}2}$  , the slope increases to 5, suggesting a process of the  Fermi level moving up toward the conduction band and filling  up the ionized donor traps. At voltage higher than   $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{TFL}2}$  , the  current started to increase at a much faster rate and finally  reached hard-breakdown point before the trap-free square-law  dependence.

Temperature   dependent   transient   back-gating  measurements were performed on un-gated AlGaN/GaN  HFETs (from 25 to   $50~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   in a   $5\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   step) to identify the trap  energy levels in the GaN buffer/transition layer. The drain  current at   $\mathrm{V_{DS}=}1\ \mathrm{V}$   (with the source grounded) was monitored  when the substrate bias  $\mathrm{V_{sub}}$   is switched from 0 to  $-60\;\mathrm{V}$   at the  time   $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{t}}=0{\mathrm{s}}$  . Typical normalized transient drain current after

applying the negative substrate bias was plotted in Fig. 6. For  sample A, the acceptor traps (N D ) pull down the Fermi level  close to the acceptor trap level. During the transient  measurement, the acceptor captures the electron (ionization of  acceptor traps) and generates negative space charges in the  GaN buffer/transition layer. Then, those negative space charges  deplete 2DEG in the AlGaN/GaN interface, resulting in the  decrease in drain current.[19] The Arrhenius plot (inset of Fig.  6) of the fitted time constants at various temperatures show that  acceptor traps with an active energy of   $_\mathrm{E_{V}+538\ m e V}$   exist in  sample A. While for sample B, the transient drain current first  decreased because of the ionization of acceptor traps. When the  ionization of acceptor finished, the Fermi level will move up  close to the donor trap level. The ionization of donor traps (N D )  generate many free electrons in conduction band and positive  space charges in the buffer/transition layers, resulting in the  increase in drain current. Two time constants are extracted at  each temperature, from which the acceptor trap level at  $_\mathrm{Ev+534}$    meV and the donor trap level at  $\mathrm{E_{C}}{-}600\,\mathrm{meV}$  . The acceptor and  donor trap levels are ascribed to the  $\mathrm{C_{N}}$   and   $\mathrm{C_{Ga}}$  , respectively, as  discussed in our previous report. [11]

Finally, the   $\mathrm{V_{TFL}}$   is given by the following: [20,21]

(1)

$$\begin{array}{l l}{V_{T F L1}=\frac{q(N_{A}-N_{D})}{2\vartheta}d^{2}}\\ {V_{T F L2}=\frac{q N_{A}}{2\vartheta}d^{2}}\end{array}$$
where   $\mathbf{q}$   is the electron charge,   $\mathrm{N_{A}}$   is the acceptor trap  density,  $\mathrm{N_{D}}$   is the donor trap density, ε is the permittivity of the  carbon doped GaN layer, and d is the distance between the two  electrodes. Given that   $\mathrm{V_{TFL1}}{=}50\;\mathrm{V}$   and   $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{TFL}2}{=}200\;\mathrm{V}$   for sample  B,  $\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}{=}2.6{\times}10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm}^{3}$   and  $\mathrm{{N_{D}}{=}1.8{\times}10^{16}\;\mathrm{{cm}}^{-3}}$   are estimated. For  sample A (without   $\mathrm{N_{D}}$  ),   $\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{=}2.6\mathrm{\times}10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$   can be estimated  from equation (1).

Furthermore, the leakage current-voltage curves for both  samples are examined at various temperatures as shown in Fig.  7 and 8. The leakage current for both samples increased with  the increasing temperature, making the   $\mathrm{V_{TFL}}$   between different  regions negatively shift. The negatively shift of   $\mathrm{V_{TFL}}$   indicates  that the vertical “breakdown” in this sample is not dominated  by impact ionization (which typically exhibits a positive  temperature coefficient). The three regions became more  indistinct because of the injected carriers at higher temperature  have enough energy to cross over the traps and thus the  breakdown mechanism is tend to the trap-free square-law  dependence gradually. It is also noting that this trap-weaken  effect is relatively smaller in the carbon doped sample owing to  the existence of higher trap concentration.

## Conclusion 

We studied the breakdown mechanism of GaN layer grown  on Si substrate with and without intentional carbon doping.  The carbon doping will deteriorate the crystalline quality and  surface morphology, which may further influence the 2DEG  conduction properties. But, the I-V characteristics measured  on a two terminal structure demonstrate that the introduction of  carbon will suppress the leakage current and enhance the  breakdown voltage. The log-log I-V plot shows that the  leakage current in the carbon doped GaN can be explained  using space-charge limited current (SCLC) model. The  transient drain current measurements demonstrate that the  traps in the unintentional doped GaN buffer are mainly  acceptor traps   $\mathrm{(C_{N})}$   with an energy level of   $_{\mathrm{Ev}+538\,\mathrm{\meV}}$  ,  while both acceptor traps and donor traps (  $\mathrm{C_{Ga}}$   with an energy  level of  $\mathrm{Ec}\mathrm{-}600\;\mathrm{meV})$  ) coexist in the carbon doped GaN buffer  layer. The leakage current increase at higher temperature for  both sample and the breakdown mechanism is tend to the  trap-free square-law dependence gradually.
