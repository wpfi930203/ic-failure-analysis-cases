## Experiment Characterization of Front and Back  Interfaces Impact on Back Gate Modulation in  UTBB-FDSOI MOSFETs  

Wangyong Chen 1 , Linlin Cai 1 , Yongfeng Cao 2 ,Duanquan Liao 2 , Ming Tian 2 , Xing Zhang 1 , Xiaoyan Liu 1\*  and Gang  Du 1\*   1 Institute of Microelectronics, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China.  2 Shanghai Huali Microelectronics Corporation, Shanghai 201203, China.   Phone: +86 13810000259  \* Email:  gangdu@pku.edu.cn ,  xyliu@ime.pku.edu.cn

Abstract-   An analytical method considering the charge coupling  effect in UTBB FDSOI device is proposed to extract trap densities  at front and back interfaces. The back-gate modulation with the  dependence of interface traps is investigated in the advanced  UTBB FDSOI device stressed by hot carrier injection (HCI). The  experimental results show that the initial back-interface states are  more than that of the front interface. Both the trap densities of the  front and back interfaces increase with longer HCI stress.  Moreover, the HCI stress weakens back-biasing capability  especially at the scenario of the high-performance application.  Larger forward back bias leads to the more charged  back-interface states, thereby reducing the modulation range of  back biasing.

Keywords  –  Back gate modulation, Hot carrier injection, UTBB  FDSOI, Interface state .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Ultra-thin body and buried oxide (UTBB) FDSOI device  gains high attention in mobile and IoT applications since it  exhibits low device variability, outstanding short channel  control, especially enables to manipulate threshold voltage   $(V_{t h})$    through back biasing [1-5]. The traps located at the front  oxide/channel interface as well as the buried oxide/channel  interface significantly affect the device reliability due to the  ultra-thin silicon film [6, 7]. Thus, the simple and efficient  approach to extract both front and back interface state densities   $(D_{i t})$   is highly desired for reliability monitor. Besides, the  physical   understanding   of   trap-dependent   back-gate  modulation is essential for dynamic   $V_{t h}$   control. In this work, an  analytical model based on the subthreshold swings ( SS ) of the  front and back gates considering charge coupling effect is  proposed and characterized in the advanced UTBB FDSOI  device with 6-nm silicon film to extract   $D_{i t}$   at front and back  gate interfaces, respectively. Moreover, the capability of  back-biasing is compared between fresh UTBB FDSOI  nMOSFETs and the aged devices subjected to hot carrier  injection (HCI) with different stress times. The impact of  interface traps on back-gate modulation is clarified from the  energy band perspective based on TCAD simulation.

## II.   T HEORY  A NALYSIS 

The schematic of UTBB FDSOI device is depicted in Fig. 1.  The specific back-bias control via the independent back gate

has been paid much attention for both high-performance and  low-power application [8, 9]. The root cause of the adaptive   $V_{t h}$  control is charge coupling effect between front and back  interfaces. The corresponding surface potentials describing the  charge coupling between front and back gates are expressed as  follows.

$$V_{_{G F}}=V_{_{f b F}}+\varphi_{_{s F}}(1\!+\!\frac{C_{_{d e p}}+C_{_{i t F}}}{C_{_{o x F}}})\!-\!\varphi_{_{s B}}\frac{C_{_{d e p}}}{C_{_{o x F}}}\!-\!\frac{Q_{_{d e p}}}{2C_{o x F}}$$
$$V_{_{G B}}=V_{_{\beta b B}}+\varphi_{_{s B}}(1\!+\!\frac{C_{_{d e p}}+C_{_{i t B}}}{C_{_{o x B}}})\!-\!\varphi_{_{s F}}\frac{C_{_{d e p}}}{C_{_{o x B}}}\!-\!\frac{Q_{_{d e p}}}{2C_{_{o x B}}}$$
where   $C_{d e p}=\,\varepsilon_{S i}/T_{S i}$  is depletion capacitance of silicon film,   $C_{i t F,B}=q D_{i t F,B}$   are interface trap capacitances of front and back  oxides, respectively [10, 11].   $C_{o x F}=\mathrm{~}\varepsilon_{\!S i O\!z}/E O T$  and   $C_{o x B}\,=$   $\varepsilon_{S i O2}/T_{B o x}$   represent the intrinsic oxide capacitances of front and  back gates. The  SS  of front gate can be given similar to the bulk  case as follows [12].

$${\cal S}{\cal S}_{G F}=(\frac{d\log_{10}I_{D S}}{d V_{G F}})^{-1}\simeq\ln(10)\frac{k T}{q}\frac{\partial V_{G F}}{\partial\varphi_{s F}}$$
Substitute the   $\varphi_{s B}$   in Eq. (2) into Eq. (1), and the corresponding   $S S G F$   can be derived in Eq. (4).

$$\centering S S_{G F}=\ln(10)\frac{k T}{q}(1+\frac{C_{i t F}}{C_{o x F}}+\alpha_{G F}\frac{C_{d e p}}{C_{o x F}})=n_{G F}T$$
where   $\alpha_{G F}=(C_{o x B}+C_{i t B})/(C_{d e p}+C_{o x B}+C_{i t B})$  . Similarly,   $S S_{G B}$  can  be expressed in Eq. (5),

$$\displaystyle S S_{G B}=\ln(10)\frac{k T}{q}(1+\frac{C_{i t B}}{C_{o x B}}+\alpha_{G B}\,\frac{C_{d e p}}{C_{o x B}})=n_{G B}T$$
where   $\alpha_{G B}=(C_{o x F}+C_{i t F})/(C_{d e p}+C_{o x F}+C_{i t F})$  .  α GF ,  α GB  denote the  coupling effect introduced by buried oxide and interface traps.  It should be noted that the   $S S G F$   and   $S S_{G B}$   are measured with  back gate and front gate depleted, respectively. The  $D_{i t F,B}$   can be  obtained by solving Eqs. (4) and (5) based on experimental  results of temperature dependent   $S S G F$  and   $S S_{G B}$  .

The larger line slopes  n GF  and  n GB  in Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 indicates  more generation of interface states during stress. With the  proposed analytical formula Eqs. (4) and (5), the extracted  initial  $D_{i t B}$   is  ${\sim}9.8\times$  of the fresh   $D_{i t F}$  case as shown in Fig. 5. The   $D_{i t F}$   increases  ${\sim}1.4\times$   at 200s and   ${\sim}2.3\times$   at 400s under HCI stress.  Moreover, the  $D_{i t B}$   increases   ${\sim}1.2\times$   and  ${\sim}1.5\times$  compared to the  fresh case after 200s and 400s HCI stress, respectively. Thus,  HCI stress applied on the front gate leads to the trap generation  at both front and back gate interfaces of the UTBB devices. It  also implies the SOI interface quality should be enhanced for  device reliability since the back interface exhibits great impact  on electrical characteristics as reported previously [13, 14].

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

An analytical approach based on the subthreshold swing is  proposed to determine front and back interface densities and  carried out on the UTBB FDSOI device. The results indicate  that HCI stress induces the generation of interface states at both  front and back interfaces. What’s more, the   $V_{t h}$   range modulated  through back gate is reduced after HCI stress, which is more

serious in the regime of larger forward back bias due to the  screening effect of more charged traps at back interface.
