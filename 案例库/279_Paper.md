## Impacts of Channel Doping on NBTI Reliability and  Variability in Nanoscale FinFETs  

Zhe Zhang, Runsheng Wang \* , Yangyuan Wang, and Ru Huang  Institute of Microelectronics, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China ( \* email: r.wang@pku.edu.cn)

Abstract  - In this paper, the channel doping concentration   $(\mathbf{N_{ch}})$    dependence of negative bias temperature instability (NBTI)  reliability and variability is comprehensively studied using  ‘atomistic’ TCAD simulations. The  V th  distributions and current  density distributions at different  $\mathbf{N_{ch}}$   are investigated. It is helpful  for understanding of NBTI degradation in nanoscale devices.

Keywords  –  NBTI, FinFET, Reliability, Variability, TCAD .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

With the downscaling of MOSFET dimensions, device  reliability and variability has raised increasing attention, since it  becomes a severe challenge for device and circuit stability in  nanoscale technology [1-6]. The parameter shifts in single  device induced by BTI turn to be the dominant degradation  mechanism, and the resulted dynamic variation interacted with  static variation leading to larger degradation has been paid more  attention [3-6]. In this paper, the impact of channel doping  concentration on BTI of pFinFET has been comprehensively  studied. The physical mechanisms are discussed in detail. These  understandings can help to device design and process  optimization in FinFET technology.

The current density re-distributed phenomenon is caused by  non-ideal shape of the Fin structure (Fig. 6). For devices with  ‘ideal’ structure, such as planar device and ideal-shaped  double-gate (DG) FET, the   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}$   remains almost unchanged  with a slight downward trend, which is shown in Fig. 10. In this  case, the devices are uniform (without random variations) and  traps   are   distributed   uniformly.   The   current   density  distributions in Fig. 11 show that the intrinsic current  percolations do not exist in non-trapezoid devices. The center of  current density approaches to surface at higher   $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{ch}}$  , which  contributes to the slight downward trend of   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}$   due to carrier  screening effect.

When a Tri-gate device (inset in Fig.12) is considered, the  statistical simulation results show that the   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}$   decreases at low   $\mathrm{N_{ch}}$   and then increases at high   $\mathrm{N_{ch}}$  . The reason for the  downtrend is same with it in the pFinFET discussed above.

However, the current density re-distributed phenomenon is not  obvious in Tri-gate device due to its ideal device structure. The  influence of traps impacting on current percolation path is  predominant. Therefore, when   $\mathrm{N_{ch}}$   increases, more fluctuated  current percolation path will lead to higher mean  $\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}$  , which is  similar with the experimental results in [6].

## IV. C ONCLUSIONS 

The impacts of channel doping concentration on BTI  variability are comprehensively studied using ‘atomistic’  TCAD simulations. It is found that complex doping dependence  of   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}$   is introduced by device type and Fin structure. The  results are helpful for process optimization and understanding  trap-related reliability issues in nanoscale devices.
