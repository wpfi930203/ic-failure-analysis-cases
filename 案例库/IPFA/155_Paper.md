## Improving Reliability of IWO Thin Film Transistors with  ZrAlO x  via Varying AlO x /ZrO x  Deposition Sequence in ALD  

Yi-Xuan Chen 1 , Fu-Jyuan  $\mathrm{Li}^{3}$  , Yi-Lin Wang 3 ,   Hui-Hsuan  $\mathrm{Li}^{2}$  , Meng-Chien Lee 2 , Yu-Hsien Lin 3 \* and Chao-Hsin  Chien 2 \*  1 Department of Institute of Pioneer Semiconductor Innovation, National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, Hsinchu,  Taiwan.  2 Institute of Electronics, National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, Hsinchu, Taiwan  3 Department of Electronic Engineering, National United University, Miaoli, Taiwan.  Phone: +886 03 5712121,  2 \*Email: chchien@faculty.nctu.edu.tw  Phone: +886 037 382533,  3 \*Email: yhlin@nuu.edu.tw

Abstract — In this study, we report on the electrical  characteristics and material analyses of indium tungsten oxide  thin film transistors (IWO-TFTs) with zirconium aluminum  oxide (ZrAlO x ) through various   $\mathbf{A l O_{x}/Z r O_{x}}$   deposition sequences  using atomic layer deposition (ALD). The fabricated device had a  positive threshold voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{T}})$   of 0.6 V, an impressive   $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$  value of approximately  ${\bf7.2\!\times\!10^{7}}$  , a subthreshold swing ( SS ) of 93  mV/dec, an interface trap density   $\mathbf{(Nit)}$   of   $\mathbf{4.7{\times}10^{12}\ C m^{-2}}$  , and a  remarkably high degree of stability against positive bias stress  (PBS). In conclusion, aluminum oxide into zirconium oxide which  resulted in a smaller dielectric surface roughness   $\mathbf{(0.19~\nm)}$  ,  thereby obtaining the enhanced mobility of IWO-TFTs.  Consequently, zirconium oxide   $(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{r}\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{x}})$   rather than aluminum  oxide (AlO x ) as the contacted material for IWO film makes the  device outperform by adjusting the  $\mathbf{Z r O_{x}/A l O_{x}}$   deposition order.  These results contribute to optimizing IWO TFTs with   $\mathbf{Z r A l O_{x}}$  dielectric by showcasing improved stability by adjusting the  interface between the channel and dielectric layer.

Index Terms —  $\mathbf{\nabla}.\mathbf{Z}r\mathbf{4}l\mathbf{O}_{x},$   IWO film, Reliability, ALD

## I.   INTRODUCTION  

Oxide semiconductors (OSs) have garnered extensive  attention as thin-film transistors (TFTs) in various applications,  including flexible electronics, backplane displays, solar cells,  and back-end-of-line (BEOL) compatible transistor channels  [1-2]. This popularity is attributed to their advantageous  properties, such as high carrier mobility, optical transparency,  and a high bandgap [3]. Additionally, OSs can be deposited at  lower process temperatures, facilitating uniform and large-area  production, thus reducing process costs and ensuring  compatibility with Si processes. Indium oxide   $(\mathrm{In}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3})$   has  been widely utilized among OSs due to its exceptional  mobility. However, it faces reliability challenges related to  weak bond dissociation energy, leading to the generation of  oxygen vacancies [4]. To address this issue, doping   $\mathrm{In}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$    with  metal cations possessing higher bond dissociation energy has  been explored to prevent oxygen vacancy formation [4].  Extensive literature reviews have suggested the replacement of  gallium with tungsten due to its higher oxygen bond  dissociation energy [5-6]. Consequently, introducing tungsten  into   $\mathrm{In}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$    has enhanced the stability of the threshold voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{T}})$   [7].

Different gate dielectric layers can significantly influence  the electrical characteristics and stress stability of thin-film  transistors (TFTs) [8-9]. Zirconium oxide   $(\mathrm{ZrO_{x}})$   is a  commonly used dielectric material in semiconductor processes

owing to its high dielectric constant   $({\sim}25)$  . Adding aluminum   $(\mathrm{{AlO}_{\mathrm{x}})}$   in   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$   lowers crystallization temperatures and  increases band gaps. Recent studies have reported on the  tunability of zirconium aluminum oxide   $(Z\mathrm{r}\mathrm{A l O_{x}})$   through  variations   in   annealing   temperatures   and   doping  concentrations [10-11]. However, an investigation into the  effects of different deposition sequences at the same  concentration is still lacking.

In this study, we explored the impact of   $\mathrm{ZrAlO_{x}}$   thin films  deposited via atomic layer deposition (ALD) by adjusting the   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   deposition sequence on indium tungsten oxide (IWO)  TFTs. Through varying   $\mathrm{SiO_{x}}$   sequences, we observed  significant enhancements in electrical characteristics, resulting  in notable improvements in the positive bias stress (PBS) test  outcomes.

## II.   EXPERIMENTAL  

Fig. 1 presents the device schematic of an   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$  TFT. Firstly, a heavily p-type doped Si substrate was used as  the gate electrode. A   $4.5\;\mathrm{\nm}\;\mathrm{\ZArA l O_{x}}$   dielectric layer was  deposited by ALD at   $250~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  .

Fig. 2 (a)-(c) presents growth methods for   $\mathrm{ZrAlO_{x}}$   thin  films. Tetrakis (dimethylamino)zirconium (TDMAZr) and  trimethylaluminum (TMA) were used as the zirconium and  aluminum metal precursors, respectively, with   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   plasma  serving as the source of oxygen. During the   $\mathrm{ZrAlO_{x}}$  deposition, the cycle ratio of Zr to Al was modified to adjust  the concentration of  $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   within the  $\mathrm{ZrAlO_{x}}$  , with a total of 45  cycles performed. For the 1A4Z and 1A8Z conditions,   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$  and   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$   cycle ratios were set to 1:4 and 1:8, respectively. In  the 1A4Z case, each super-cycle included a single   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   cycle  followed by four   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$   cycles, repeated nine times. In the  1A8Z case, each super-cycle included a single   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   followed  by eight cycles of   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$  , repeated five times. In the 1A8Z case,  the cycle ratio of   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   and   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$   was 1:8, but the deposition  sequence started with a single cycle of   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$  . The main  difference between the 1A8Z and 8Z1A cases was their  deposition sequence. Subsequently,   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   was defined by the  wet etching process. Following this, a 2.5 nm IWO channel  layer was sputter-deposited and was isolated by lift-off. 250   $^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   RTO (rapid thermal oxidation) annealing was conducted  for 60 s. Finally, 50 nm nickel (Ni) was sputter-deposited as  the source/drain electrode. The channel width ( W ) and length   $(L)$   were respectively  $50\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$   and   $8~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   for  $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs.  All electrical characterization was conducted using the  Keysight B1500A semiconductor device analyzer [30].

Fig. 3 presents the transfer curves of   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs  with varying   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   doping concentrations and sequences at  drain voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{D}})$   of   $1\,\mathrm{~V~}$  . The   $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}} $  ratio was calculated by  comparing the maximum on-current to the minimum offcurrent under a gate voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{G}})$   ranging from   $^{-2\mathrm{~V~}}$   to  $+4\mathrm{~V~}$  ,  with a constant   $V_{\mathrm{D}}$   of 1 V. The   $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$  ratio of 1A4Z, 1A8Z,  and 8Z1A was   $1.6\times10^{7}$  ,  $7.2\times10^{7},$    and  $2.1\times10^{7}$  , respectively.  The threshold voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{T}})$   was determined by the constant  current method [12] with a drain current   $(I_{\mathrm{D}})$   of   $10^{-8}~\mathrm{A}/\upmu\mathrm{m}$   at   $V_{\mathrm{D}}~=~1~\mathrm{~V~}$  . The   $V_{\mathrm{T}}$   value of the   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs was  approximately 0 V, indicating that the devices operated in  enhancement mode. In comparing 1A4Z and 1A8Z, a gradual  decrease in the subthreshold swing ( SS ) as   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   doping  increases. It implies that the concentration of   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   has  attained saturation, and a surplus of   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   may result in the  generation of interstitial defects, thereby causing an elevation  in  SS  and hysteresis. Subsequently, we would discuss the case  of exchanging the deposition sequence of   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   at the same  concentration. The  SS  was associated with interface trap  density   $\mathrm{(N_{it})}$   [13]. The   $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{it}}$   of 1A8Z and 8Z1A was   $4.7\!\times\!10^{12}$  and   $6.8{\times}10^{12}\;\mathrm{cm}^{2}$  . It pointed out that contact with the channel  layer with   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$    was less trap than   $\mathrm{SiO_{x}}$  .  The mobility   $(\upmu)$   of  the 1A8Z and 8Z1A cases was 7.2 and 1.6   $(\mathrm{cm}^{2}/\mathrm{V}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{s})$  .

Fig. 4 (a) presents   the hysteresis of   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs with  varying   $\mathrm{SiO_{x}}$   doping sequences. The hysteresis of 1A8Z and  8Z1A were 0.11 and 0.23 V, respectively. The hysteresis value  of 8Z1A was higher than 1A8Z, suggesting the existence of a  defect between the channel layer and gate dielectric layer [14].  Fig. 4 (b) presents the output characteristics of   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$  TFTs with different   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   doping sequences. The   $I_{\mathrm{D}}$    of the  1A8Z case was 7.5 times higher than that of the 8Z1A case.  Fig. 4 (c) presents the statistical distributions for the cases of  8Z1A and 1A8Z, with at least 15 devices measured for each  case.

Fig. 5 (a)-(b) presents the atomic force microscope (AFM)  results for different   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   doping sequences. Specifically, the  AFM results of 1A8Z and 8Z1A were 0.19 and   $0.41\;\mathrm{\nm}$  ,  respectively. Higher roughness leads to surface scattering,  causing reduced mobility [15].

Fig. 6 presents the change in inverse mobility   $(1/\upmu_{\mathrm{FE}})$  concerning the measured temperature for   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs  featuring different   $\mathrm{SiO_{x}}$   sequences. It was noted that the  mobility values at a   $\mathrm{{V_{ov}}}$   of   $2\,\mathrm{~V~}$  . According to Matthiessen’s  rule, the mobility degradation in oxide semiconductor TFTs  due to scattering mechanisms is investigated including,  Coulomb scattering, phonon scattering, and surface roughness  scattering [16]. In mobility, both Coulomb and phonon  scattering are affected by temperature, with phonon scattering  causing a decrease in mobility as temperature increases [17- 18].  In contrast, Coulomb scattering increases mobility with  increasing temperature [19]. However, based on the estimated  results from the plot of   $1/\upmu_{\mathrm{FE}}$   versus temperature, the impact of  temperature on mobility is relatively small. Therefore,  roughness scattering is believed to be the main scattering  mechanism affecting mobility. It was supported by AFM  results showing that the roughness of 8Z1A is greater than that  of 1A8Z, resulting in lower mobility for 8Z1A.

Fig. 7 (a)-(b) presents transfer curves of   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs  with different   $\mathrm{SiO_{x}}$   doping under the positive bias stress  (PBS) test for 1000 s at   $25\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   under 6.5 MV/cm. Fig. 7 (c)  presents the   $\Delta V_{\mathrm{T}}$   (threshold voltage shift) trend of the applied  PBS time for the   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   TFTs. In the PBS test  conducted on the 8Z1A and 1A18Z cases,  $\Delta V_{\mathrm{T}}$   values of 0.75  V and   $0.39\;\mathrm{~V~}$   were measured after 1000 s. These results  indicated that the interface material, specifically   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$  , in  contact with the channel layer exhibited superior PBS  performance. Compared to   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$  ,   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$   showed higher bond  dissociation [20], reducing trap production during PBS.  During PBS, it was observed that the   $\Delta V_{\mathrm{T}}$   in 1A8Z was  comparatively smaller than in 1A8Z, indicating a discrepancy.  The main factor influencing the PBS was the trapping state  within the interface between the gate insulator and channel  layers [21-22]. The primary mechanism for the contact  between the 1A8Z and 8Z1A interface and the IWO channel  layer lies in bond dissociation energy. Due to the higher bond  dissociation energy of Zr-O bonds    $(760\ \ \mathrm{kJ/mol})$   [20]

compared to Al-O bonds   $(512\;\mathrm{kJ/mol})$  ) [20], Zr-O can capture  the oxygen in In-O, making it difficult for the In-O with lower  bond dissociation energy to lose oxygen.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

In conclusion, we explored the influence of different   $\mathrm{ZrAlO_{x}}$   sequences with   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$   doping on the electrical  properties and reliability of IWO-TFTs through a meticulously  designed ALD process. Notably, the 1A8Z case demonstrated  superior electrical performance compared to the 8Z1A case,  reaching an impressive    $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$   ratio of   $7.2\!\times\!10^{7}$  , a   $V_{\mathrm{T}}$   of   $0.6~\mathrm{V}$  ,  and an  SS  of 93 mV/dec. Additionally, the 1A8Z configuration  demonstrated enhanced stability in the PBS test.

Furthermore, the AFM results indicated that the 1A8Z case  displayed lower roughness, translating to higher mobility.  When   $\mathrm{ZrAlO_{x}}$   served as the dielectric layer in tandem with  IWO,   $\mathrm{ZrO_{x}}$   demonstrated superior performance compared to   $\mathrm{{AlO}_{x}}$  . As a result, the   $\mathrm{IWO/Zr A l O_{x}}$   combination exhibited  outstanding electrical properties and stability, underscoring its  potential as a promising material for the future fabrication of  thin-film transistors (TFTs).
