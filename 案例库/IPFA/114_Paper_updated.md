## Design Optimization of Stacked-NMOS ESD  Protection for Mixed-Voltage Application  

Kyongjin Hwang 1 , Ting Yang 1, 2 , Jie (Jack) Zeng 1 , Ajay 3 , Guowei Zhang 1  and Tupei Chen 2    1 ESD & Latchup, GLOBALFOUNDRIES, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, 738406, Singapore    2 School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Nanyang Technological University, 639798 Singapore    3 Global TCAD, GLOBALFOUNDRIES, 9th Floor, N1 Building, Manyata Embassy Business Park, India  Phone: (65)   +6566701623, Email:  kyongjin.hwang@globalfoundries.com

Abstract —In this study, we optimized the stacked-NMOS  design in  ${\bf0.13\,\mu m}$   BCD technology to improve ESD performance  and overvoltage tolerance. This was achieved by modifying the   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   drain junction to reduce the electric field at the poly/drain  overlap region and ensure a more uniformly and deep total  current distribution.

Keywords—Stacked-NMOS, ESD, electric field, current  uniformly, deep current distribution.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

One of the challenges in Electrostatic Discharge (ESD)  protection lies in protecting the output driver within a mixedvoltage input/output (I/O) pin. When an external voltage  surpasses the specified power supply voltage of the Integrated  Circuit (IC) and is applied to the I/O pad, the vulnerability of  the I/O pad to potential damage becomes a primary concern  [1]. In response to this vulnerability, the stacked NMOS ESD  scheme, which is widely used in mixed-voltage I/O schemes,  has been introduced to prevent gate-oxide reliability issues  caused by external overvoltage, as shown in Fig. 1 [1, 2].

While stacked NMOS devices have proven effective in  averting gate-oxide reliability issues arising from external  overvoltage, their application in scenarios requiring  operational voltages of 5 V or higher often exhibits low ESD  current discharge performance. This phenomenon can be due  to several factors, including the extended base width of the  lateral n-p-n Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT), relatively  complex design parameters, the influence of parasitic circuit  effects [3], and also the peak electric field,   $E_{\mathrm{max}},$   at the top  LDD/PWELL junction [4]. Several studies have investigated  the correlations between Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) and  reliability issues concerning gate oxide devices. Observations  reveal that hot carrier degradation occurs subsequent to ESD  stress, particularly in top-gate devices [3]. This phenomenon  is linked to the formation of oxide traps or interface states  induced by ESD stress [5-7]. The degradation under hot

carrier (HC) stress is associated with the gradual and uniform  injection of electrons and holes into the oxide layer [3].

To delve deeper into the challenge of stacked NMOS, we  split 5 different structures over the reference conventional  stacked NOMS structure including with and without   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$    pullback (PB) and  $\pm\;10\%$   dose of lightly-doped drain (LDD).  We analyzed data from more than nine silicon sites and noted  not only low-level variations in failure current (I t2 ) but also  substantial site-to-site discrepancies in  $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{t}2}$   during 100 ns pulse  width with 10 ns rise time Transmission Line Pulse (TLP)  testing. For the conventional stacked NMOS, we have  measured TLP current-voltage (I-V) as illustrated in Fig. 2 (a).  This configuration, employing the same LDD and   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$    source/drain junction design as the baseline NMOS, exhibits  instability in the leakage current, prompting our interest in  conducting a detailed study.

In response to these challenges, our research found the  critical role of the   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   PB from the top NMOS in stacked  schemes. Thus, our experimental design aims to address the  leakage current instability prevention. This innovative  modification is strategically devised to enhance overvoltage  tolerance in I/O operations. Fig. 2 (b) showcases the  effectiveness of the modified   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   junction, which exhibits  heightened stability and high I t2  results compared to the  conventional stacked NMOS scheme, even with variations in  the LDD dose process.

To illustrate our proposed solution, Section II will specify  the modifications implemented. By studying the   $\bar{\bf N}^{+}$  PB  design, our goal was to understand how this enhancement  could mitigate leakage current instability in stacked NMOS  configurations. By doing so, our study not only highlights the  challenges but also offers a promising avenue for elevating the  reliability of ESD protection in mixed-voltage I/O scenarios.

## II.   D EVICE  S TRUCTURE AND  F ABRICATION 

The devices studied in this paper for the stacked scheme  are 5 V NMOS transistors. They have been implemented in   $0.13\,\mathrm{\upmum}$   technology with silicide process. Both transistors  have a gate length of  $0.6\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . Fig. 3 presents the cross-sections  of the stacked NMOS devices with design parameters. A  silicide block layer (SBLK) is employed at the drain for  securing ESD protection performance. The values for DCGS  (Drain Contact to Gate Space) and SCGS (Source Contact to  Gate Space) are   $2.5~\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$   and   $0.13~\ensuremath{\,\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  , respectively. Unless  otherwise specified, the source side of the device does not  have an SBLK, and the minimum spacing design is applied  between two gates. All the devices discussed in this paper  have common dimensions. Each device has   $40~\ensuremath{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   wide, 12  fingers, and has a total width of  $480~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  .

The primary design experiment parameters involve the  presence or absence of LDD formation, in combination with  the adjustment of N +  PB at the drain of the top NMOS (NMOS  with top gate), as depicted in Fig. 3. The majority of hot carrier  issues arise from the increase in the peak lateral electric field,   $E_{\mathrm{max}}$  . Typically, researchers and engineers mitigate   $E_{\mathrm{max}}$   by  employing techniques such as double diffusion drain [8] and  LDD [9] structures. However, under ESD stress conditions,  these adjustments fall short of achieving the requisite  robustness for ESD devices. Non-uniform doping or abrupt  transitions in doping concentration in LDD can lead to  variations in the electric field, resulting in current crowding at  the surface junction. Therefore, we propose a novel approach  — either moving the   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   region back (so-called pullback) or  eliminating the LDD entirely. This strategic alteration ensures  a continued reduction in Emax at the LDD/PWELL junction  [4]. We aim for the ESD current to flow through the deeper  PWELL region rather than localization near silicon surface.

A comprehensive analysis and verification on various  stacked NMOS designs are conducted using TLP testing with  10 ns rising time and 100 ns pulse width, wafer-level HBM  testing, and technology computer-aided design (TCAD)  simulations.

Unless otherwise specified, the drain of the top NMOS is  zapped, the source of the last NMOS is grounded, the top gate  remains floating during TLP and HBM tests, and the bottom  gate is connected to the source. In practical applications, the  top gate is typically connected to   $\mathrm{V_{dd}}$  . However, in this work,  during the TLP test, the top gate is intentionally left floating.  In contrast, during the TLP-Safe Operation Area (TLP-SOA)  test, the top gate is connected to the power supply to bias the  gate. This will be helpful in assessing the robustness of  stacked NMOS ESD devices under different gate bias.

Consequently, the ESD  current is able to flow through the deeper PWELL, leading to

an increase in the I t2  level. However, the standard deviation  data is less than expected as compared to other structures,  indicating that the device demonstrates lower ESD robustness  despite a reduced LDD dose.

TCAD simulations highlight the imperative need for an  evenly distributed and controlled deep electric field to ensure  reliable and stable device performance. In semiconductor  devices, the term "deep" typically refers to regions away from  the surface. During an ESD event, the stress of a high-voltage  pulse generates electron-hole pairs within the LDD/PWELL  junction. Deep electric fields play a critical role in guiding the  movement and distribution of these carriers throughout the  device, and the deep electric field indicates that most of the  current will flow into the PWELL region, thus reducing the  surface current.

Hence, in the design of effective ESD protection  structures, careful consideration of the deep electric field is  important. The Stacked NMOS LDD/N +  PB structure  manages the electric field distribution to prevent local  breakdown or damage during an ESD event. Furthermore,  deep electric fields significantly influence the overall ESD  robustness of the device, as shown in Fig. 7. Even when  voltage applied to the top gate, the performance of the ESD  device is always maintained stable.

## IV.   C ONCLUSIONS   

Low level and site variations of   $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{t}2}$   were observed in  conventional stacked NMOS devices, which could be  attributed to the non-uniform turn-on and current crowding  near the silicon surface due to the doping profile of drain  junction in top NMOS. In this study, we present an optimized  design that involves the presence and absence of the LDD  junction combined with a pullback of   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   for the top  transistor’s drain region. With this approach, the current  density near the silicon surface close to the poly of the drain  region is significantly reduced, allowing for a deeper current  path toward the silicon bulk and alleviating the current  crowding during ESD events. It is essential to determine the  optimal value of   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   PB for both high ESD performance and  meeting DCBV requirements based on experimental findings.  Additionally, when considering the requirements of  breakdown voltage and operating voltage constraints, an  alternative option worth exploring is the incorporation of an  LDD in the   $\mathbf{N}^{+}$   PB structure. This approach provides  flexibility in tailoring the design to meet specific ESD design  windows.
