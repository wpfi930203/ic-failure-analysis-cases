## Development of FIB Sampling Technology for  large area TEM Analysis  

Joon Uh 1 , Jaseon Lee 1 , Yongho Yoo, 1  Sangmin Han, 1  Sungho Lee, 1  1 Samsung Electronics Advanced Analysis Science & Engineering Team, Sansungjeonja-ro, Hwaseong-si, Geyonggi-do, Korea  Phone : (82)031-208-1134, Email :  june.uh@samsung.com

Abstract  — In the semiconductor industry which conducts  ultra-fine processes,   Transmission  Electron Microscope  (TEM) which can analyze down to 'nm' units or less, has become  very important. Focused Ion Beam (FIB) allows engineers to  produce specimens so that they can perform TEM analysis on  specific areas they want. However, considering that the TEM  analysis area size through the traditional FIB method is 4um,  the traditional method not only provides insufficient data to  determine the direction of the process, but also has the problem  of creating multiple samples to see the large area. This paper  will discuss how to produce a 'large area TEM specimen' which  is more than 5 times wider than that of existing TEM specimen  through bending control. The bending phenomenon has been a  problem for large area specimens and we could overcome it by  introducing the concept of 'support' into FIB sampling  technology. Through this study, the limitations of analysis  coverage, which had been pointed out as the limitations of TEM  analysis, were overcome, and the foundation for TEM Massive  Critical Dimension (CD) measurement has been laid.

## II.    EXPERIMENT  

In the semiconductor industry, which currently deals with  processes below 'nm', Transmission Electron Microscope  (TEM) has become an essential analysis tool for product  development. In the past, SEM (Scanning Electron  Microscope) analysis equipment could handle most of the  analysis demands, but as devices shrink, a large portion of the  analysis capacity has shifted from SEM to TEM. As the  difficulty and complexity of our product development  increase, the dependence on TEM analysis and demand for  increased production of samples observed across all products,  including Dynamic Random Access Memory (DRAM).(Fig.  1.) In particular, in the case of DRAM, the difficulty of  development increased rapidly as the design rule of the  product entered the 10nm range, and each module is in a  situation where it is necessary to strictly manage the Module  Target Spec (MTS) for each step below 'nm'. Despite the  disadvantage that TEM analysis has a very limited analysis  coverage, its dependence on TEM analysis continues to  increase due to the advantage of being able to accurately  measure Å units. In the semiconductor industry, where  process scatter management is important, it is very difficult  to determine the direction of the process with data from the  4um analysis area, so multiple samples are created for each  decision, which naturally leads to an increase in facility  investment. However, it may be difficult to fully respond to  various future analysis demands only by increasing the  capacity of facilities without improving the technology and  application development and the productivity of Focused Ion  Beam (FIB). In this study, we will discuss how to produce  TEM specimens at least 5 times wider than before by  introducing the concept of 'supporter' in lamella preparation.

The FIB facility used in this study is a product  manufactured by Thermo-Fisher-Scientific and the model  name is Helios 5. DRAM was used as the evaluation  specimen in the experiment. For the unity of the experiment,  specimens were produced with the same target in the same  chip.   To optimize the specimen fabrication, acceleration  voltage, beam current, and carbon deposition were adjusted.  Thermo- Fisher-Scientific F200 was used as a device for  photographing the evaluated specimen. (Fig. 2.)

III.    R ESULT AND  D ISCUSSION

The size of the traditional TEM specimen production  using FIB is 4um, which is the size that can analyze cells in a  very limited area based on DRAM products. This is a very  small area considering that there are more than 1M main cells  in one unit- block. Since it represents an area of less than  $10\%$    of one side, it is not enough area for process control and  optimization or to have a representative of the measurement  data of the chip. (Fig. 3.) To solve this problem, we are  currently obtaining massive data by producing multiple  samples and determining the direction of the process. In terms  of data acquisition, it is better to have larger specimen size,  but the size of the specimen cannot be increased recklessly  because it can cause bending problems. When bending occurs  during the specimen production process, the target area to be  analyzed cannot be accurately secured, and it becomes  difficult to adjust the thickness. This not only reduces  productivity by further delaying the specimen production  time, but also increases the probability of specimen  production failure. In this study, we conducted an experiment  to optimize the conditions of FIB's acceleration voltage,  sampling size, carbon, and platinum deposition from the  perspective of productivity, and set the thickness of the  specimen to 20um target. In terms of bending, the marginal  experiment was conducted by increasing the size of the  processing area by 1um at a time, and it was confirmed that  the bending phenomenon occurred in the specimen when the  analysis area expanded to more than 7um.  (Fig. 4.)

After confirming that bending occurs when the specimen  area size becomes more than 7um during the TEM specimen  production process, we introduced the concept of 'supporter'  to secure a wider analysis area through the production of a  single specimen. (Fig. 5.) The supporter is a structure that  supports the gap between each analysis area and is processed  by not intentionally milling between the analysis areas. The  most important thing in this experiment was to secure the  maximum analysis area and process a supporter with a  minimum thickness that does not cause bending for data  continuity. As confirmed in the above sample bending  experiment, the analysis area was fixed to 7um. The supporter  size between each analysis area started from 2um and we  reduced it by   $0.2\mathrm{up}$  . It was concluded that the minimum  supporter thickness to prevent bending was 1um. The final  large-area analysis specimen was conducted with a horizontal  size of 23um, and the analysis area was 7um in three areas.  The final height in this experiment was   $4.5\mathrm{{um}}$  , and the  optimized thickness was 1um. In addition, the target of the  specimen production was one DRAM main cell which was  under 20 nm thickness, and when compared to the sample  processed by the traditional method, it was confirmed that  there was no problem in terms of quality. In this experiment,  we set the target specimen area to 23um in consideration of  the grid size we are using, but we believe that it is possible to  produce a larger specimen if not limited by the grid size.

V.      L ARGE AREA TEM QUALITY VERIFICATION

Maintaining the quality of the specimen is very important for  the utilization of large-area TEM specimen manufacturing  technology. In particular, as the difficulty of product  development has increased recently, measurement in unit  processes of 1nm or less has become very important, so even  if large-area TEM is secured, the utilization of technology  will inevitably decrease if the quality of the image is not  guaranteed. Based on the results obtained through the sample  bending   experiment   and   the   supporter   introduction  experiment, 7um of analysis area and 1um of supporter were  processed, and FIB milling was performed under the same  acceleration voltage and conditions as the traditional method,  and it was confirmed that a good quality TEM image could  be secured as shown in Figure 6. In large-area TEM specimen  manufacturing technology, reproducibility is as important as  maintaining quality. After optimizing the size, acceleration  voltage, and deposition time of the specimen, we conducted  20 repetitions of specimen production, and the success rate of  specimen production was confirmed to be   $85\%$   (17/20). The  criterion for successful specimen production was compared  with a sample produced by the traditional method, and if an  equivalent quality CD could be measured, it was considered  a success. Considering that the success of TEM specimen  production is influenced by the skill level of the operator, it  is expected that achieving a success rate of over  $90\%$   will not  be difficult when the operation is fully established. Also, the  meaning of a success rate of over  $90\%$   suggests that it can be  easily utilized in other operators and products.

## VI.    P RODUCTIVITY COMPARISON EXPERIMENT 

In this experiment, in order to improve the productivity of  the TEM specimen, optimization experiments were  conducted on various variables such as FIB acceleration  voltage, sampling size, carbon that protects the damage of the  specimen, and deposition time of PT. (Fig. 7.). The expansion  of the analysis area clearly means a lot in product  development, but as the analysis area increases, if the  specimen production time increases proportionally, the

meaning of large-scale analysis can be greatly halved, so  optimization of the large-scale TEM specimen production  process is also very important. The general process of  producing a TEM specimen is 1) Sampling 2) Probing 3)  Milling. (Fig. 8.). The main focus of this experiment is to  shorten the time by using the minimum number of resources  available to the FIB without damage to the sample. Among  the various variables, by reducing the vertical size of  Sampling from 1um to 0.6um, the carbon and PT deposition  time could be greatly reduced, and productivity could be  greatly improved by finding the minimum deposition  condition in which the 21um specimen did not receive  damage. (Table 1) The productivity comparison criteria in  this experiment were compared with the number of DRAM  cells that can be secured per FIB time (min), and after  optimization, productivity improved by about   $127\%$  from  2.85 cells/min to 6.48 cells/min in the traditional method.  Considering the current difficult situation in product  development, an improvement in productivity of   $127\%$    through the use of large-area specimens is expected to be of  great help in the development of various products.

## VII.    CONCLUSION 

In conclusion, this study introduced the concept of  "Supporter," which did not exist in the existing TEM sample  preparation method within the company. The optimized  "Supporter" with a specimen thickness of  $20\mathrm{nm}$   or less had a  height of   $4.5\mathrm{{um}}$   and a thickness of 1um, and could be  produced by intentionally not milling the spaces between the  analysis areas. This has been experimentally confirmed to  expand the analysis coverage, which has been pointed out as  a limitation of TEM analysis, more than five times compared  to before. If a grid suitable for large-area sample preparation  can be secured, it was confirmed that it is possible to produce  large-area samples of   $23\mathrm{um}$   or more shown in this paper. In  terms of maximizing the productivity of TEM sample  preparation, an improvement of   $127\%$   compared to the  productivity of the existing production method (Cell/min)  was confirmed through the optimization of various variables  such as acceleration voltage, sampling size, and carbon

deposition time. In addition, in terms of repeatability, an  $85\%$  success rate was achieved in more than 20 experiments, and  the quality of the large-area samples was also confirmed to  be satisfactory. This suggests that it can be continuously  utilized in the semiconductor industry, not just in one-time  experiments. In this study, the experiment of large-area TEM  specimen   production   was   conducted   with   DRAM  devices, but the technology is expected to be broadly utilized  and the technique can be applied to several other devices  including Vertical NAND (V-NAND), Static random access  memory (SRAM) and Magnetoresistive random access  memory (MRAM). Until the large-area TEM specimen  production recipe using this 'supporter' was introduced, it had  been impossible to provide sufficient measurement data for  the process development with one TEM specimen  production. However, if large-area TEM sample preparation  is developed through more experiments and analysis in the  future and the final setup is completed, it is expected to be  actively utilized in various fields by overcoming the  limitations of the analysis range. Considering the  increasingly important situation of massive data analysis for  scatter management in the semiconductor industry, it is  believed that large-area sample preparation, when used in  conjunction with TEM CD automatic measurement, will be a  major innovation in product development.
