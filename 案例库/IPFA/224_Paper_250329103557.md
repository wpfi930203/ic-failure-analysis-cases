## Denoising of Transmission Electron Microscopy  Images for Atomic Defect Identification  

Shiyi Zhang, Qing Zhang, Xu Ran, Xing Wu, Yan Wang\*, Chaolun Wang\*  Shanghai Key Laboratory of Multidimensional Information Processing, East China Normal University, 500 Dongchuan Road, Shanghai,  200241, China

Phone: (0086)2154345399, E-mails: ywang@cee.ecnu.edu.cn; clwang@cee.ecnu.edu.cn

Abstract —Two-dimensional (2D) materials possess remarkable  electrical, mechanical, thermal, and optical properties, making  them applicable in the fields of electronics and optoelectronics.  Atomic defects could significantly affect device performance and  reliability. Clearly identify and analysis of atomic defects in 2D  materials is crucial. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) with  atomic resolution serves as an ideal method for the defect  characterization of 2D materials. However, TEM images often  suffer from various types of noise, which severely hampers the  analysis of 2D materials. In this study, we propose a neural  network based on the optimized CycleGAN architecture for TEM  image denoising even when obtaining strictly paired datasets for  TEM is challenging. The optimized CycleGAN model with  attention gate could improve the signal-to-noise ratio and  structural similarity of the denoised images. The effectiveness of  this method was validated on the scanning transmission electron  microscopy image dataset.   Enhancing the visibility of atomic  contrast and improving the accuracy of identifying individual  atomic structures are achieved by removing the surrounding noise  from the original image.

Keywords—   image   denoising,   two-dimensional   materials,  CycleGAN, transmission electron microscopy

## I.   I NTRODUCTION 

Two-dimensional (2D) materials [1-3], have garnered  widespread research interest due to their unique properties.  These materials boast atomic-level thinness and exhibit  outstanding electronic, mechanical, thermal, and optical  characteristics, rendering them suitable for a wide array of  applications   across   fields   such   as   electronics   and  optoelectronics. The reliability of 2D materials is critically  important, especially given their extremely thin nature and the  propensity for defects to arise during the fabrication process.  These defects can significantly impact the performance of  devices. Being able to clearly visualize and identify defects  allows for a thorough analysis of the causes of failure, which is  essential for improving the reliability of devices made from 2D  materials.

Traditional 2D material image denoising techniques  encompass spatial domain methods [4-6] (e.g. mean filtering,  median filtering, and adaptive filtering) and frequency domain  methods [7] (e.g. Fourier transform and wavelet transform),  both of which aim to decrease noise while maintaining image  details. Although appropriate for real-time processing or  scenarios with limited computational resources, these methods  are constrained by stringent prior assumptions, such as the type  and distribution of noise. Machine learning-based denoising  approaches involve feature extraction and dimensionality  reduction [8, 9], supervised learning [10, 11], and unsupervised  learning [12]. By learning mappings, classifier selection, and  image grouping, these methods minimize noise; however, their  performance is contingent upon training data, parameter  settings, and initial conditions. Neural network denoising  methods leverage deep learning techniques to automatically  extract image features [13 , 14], effectively eliminating noise in  2D material images. With robust denoising capabilities and  adaptability, this method can be applied to various types of 2D

material image processing, enhancing image quality. Previously,  this issue was typically addressed through denoising methods  based on supervised deep learning. However, supervised  denoisers rely on a large number of noisy-clean image pairs.  Due to the requirement for paired datasets during training, the  application of these methods in TEM dynamic imaging systems  remains limited. Paired datasets consist of clean and noisy  versions of identical images. Acquiring such datasets within  TEM dynamic imaging systems poses considerable challenges,  as the inherent motion of the probe makes it difficult to obtain  strictly one-to-one corresponding datasets.

To overcome the drawbacks of previous approaches, we  propose an unsupervised learning technique utilizing unpaired  clean and noisy image datasets. Our work leverages the clean  images from a synthetic dataset in conjunction with noisy  images from a real one for training. We employ the improved  CycleGAN model to extract clear targets from noisy inputs,  eliminating the need for paired supervision or prior knowledge  of noise distribution. This methodology could enhance the  resolution of TEM images, improve atomic-scale feature  interpretation, and deepen the comprehension of material  properties.

## II.   E XPERIMENTAL  D ETAILS 

## A.   Dataset   

To build an effective image denoising model, a dataset that  contains pairs of clean images and noisy images is typically  required. However, in the real world, it is often impractical to  obtain true noise-free images, as images acquired in practice are  always affected by various types of noise. Therefore, our dataset  includes two types of images: real and synthetic. The real data  only includes noisy images, as we are unable to obtain  completely clean and noise-free images. The synthetic dataset  consists of pairs of data that include noise-free images with  artificially added scan and Poisson noise introduced by [15].  The dataset comprises 14000 images, with   $70\%$  ,   $20\%$   and   $10\%$    of the data utilized for training, testing and validation,  respectively.

The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) dataset  consists of a collection of high-resolution images used for  assessing the structure and properties of materials. These TEM  images are generated using a Titan 80-300 TEM equipped with  an aberration corrector. The accelerating voltage is set at 200  kV. TEM datasets have extensive applications in material  science, and other scientific fields as they provide detailed  information about atomic structures and compositional  variations. The aberration corrector of the Titan 80-300 TEM  aids in enhancing image quality, enabling researchers to  evaluate sample characteristics more accurately.

The synthetic annular dark-field scanning transmission  electron microscopy (ADF-STEM) dataset, another type of  TEM dataset, introduced by [15] is used for evaluating  denoising and image processing algorithms. It incorporates  sophisticated noise models that emulate the appearance of  experimental ADF-STEM images, showcasing diverse atomic

structures and an array of crystallographic orientations.  Distinguished by its high resolution and stark contrast, the  ADF-STEM dataset excels in unveiling the intricate atomic  configurations and the subtle compositional variances present  within materials.

## B.   Neural network architecture  

In our research, we focus on the mapping between the  noisy image domain  $X$    and the clean image domain  Y  using two  cross-domain mapping functions  $F$    and   $G$  , as shown in Fig. 1.  Within this framework,  $F$   acts as a denoiser, while   $G$    plays the  role of the inverse function of   $F$  , that is, as a noise generator,  with the goal of adding noise to a given clean image. To  effectively train  $F$    and   $G$  , we introduced two discriminators,  $D_{X}$  and  $D_{Y}$    , whose tasks are to distinguish between real and fake  samples, i.e., samples transformed from another domain.  Traditional adversarial losses are insufficient for the  discriminators to ideally convert noisy  samples x into clean  samples y, as the mapping space of   $F(x)$    could be very broad.  For this reason, CycleGAN proposed cycle consistency to  create a tighter mapping space for   $x$  , namely   $x{\approx}G(F(x))$    and   $y{\approx}F(G(y))$  , where   $x$    and  $y$    represent the data distributions of  noisy observations and clean sources, respectively.

In our practical work, the two cross-domain mapping  functions   $F$    and   $G$    utilize a U-net based Convolutional Neural  Network (CNN) structure, which is combined with an attention  mechanism to enhance the model’s ability to focus on relevant  features, as shown in Fig. 2. The enhanced U-net architecture  features an encoder-decoder structure and integrates attention  blocks at each decoding stage to refine the feature maps,  enabling the network to concentrate on salient features. The  encoder (our network’s contracting path) is composed of a  series of encoding modules, each including two 3x3  convolutional layers followed by LeakyReLU activation and a  2x2 max pooling operation for down sampling. The decoder  (the expanding path) mirrors the encoder but includes upconvolutions and attention blocks for up sampling. At the end  of the network, texture features are enhanced through a threelayer Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) structure. With such  a structural design, our network can effectively concentrate on  important features, thus achieving more accurate mapping from

noisy to clean images in unpaired image-to-image translation  tasks.

The attention gate is designed to selectively focus on  salient features of the input data that are more relevant to the  denoising task, effectively allowing the model to pay more  "attention" to the non-noise parts while suppressing the noise  information. This mechanism is extremely useful in denoising  tasks because the non-noise regions of the image are more  important than those containing noise.

The attention gate is constructed as a neural network  module that computes attention coefficients, which can be used  to weight the input features, as shown in Fig. 3. The gate  consists of the following components: Gating Signal  Transformation: This involves a linear transformation, which in  our work is a 1x1 convolution, applied to the gating signal  features, followed by batch normalization. The gating signal  usually comes from a higher-level or coarser feature map and is  meant to direct the attention mechanism. Local Feature  Transformation: This entails another 1x1 convolution, applied  to the local input features that the mechanism aims to attend to,  succeeded by batch normalization. These features often  represent finer-resolution features from a preceding layer or the  same layer within the network. Combination and Activation:  The transformed gating signal and local features are  subsequently combined, often through addition, and then  activated using a ReLU activation function. Attention  Coefficient Computation: A final transformation is applied to  the combined signal, using a 1x1 convolution, to reduce the  number of feature channels to 1, followed by batch  normalization and a sigmoid activation function. This yields the  attention coefficients, which are constrained between 0 and 1.

The initial idea for incorporating attention into a model is  based on the observation that not all parts of the input data are  equally informative for a given task. Neural network attention  mechanisms aim to emulate this selective focus, enabling the  model to prioritize and process the most task-relevant  information. This is particularly useful for complex tasks where  the relevance of features can vary significantly across the input  space, and the model needs to be robust to noise or irrelevant  variations in the data.

For denoising TEM images, we employ the PatchGAN  discriminator, which helps to meticulously evaluate the  distinction between noise and signal, particularly within local  regions of the image. The local discrimination feature of the  PatchGAN discriminator allows it to focus on noise  characteristics within the texture and details of the image,  thereby more effectively guiding the generator to remove noise  and restore the image’s true structure. This method is not only  more   computationally   efficient,   reducing   resource  consumption, but also flexible in terms of input image size,  enabling CycleGAN to process TEM images of various sizes.  Ultimately, through the PatchGAN discriminator’s stringent

evaluation of local quality, our model is better able to learn how  to generate clear, low-noise TEM images, significantly  improving the denoising results.

## C.    Training details   

The training code employs a composite loss function and  a strategic training regimen to facilitate the generation of highfidelity denoised images, with an emphasis on maintaining  consistency between the source and target domains. A  combination of adversarial, cycle-consistency, identity, and  supervised losses directs the training process to minimize  discrepancies and enhance the quality of the denoised electron  microscopy images.

The adversarial loss is integral to training the  discriminators and generators. Discriminators are trained to  distinguish real images from synthesized ones, while  generators strive to produce images that the discriminators will  classify as real. The adversarial loss is formulated as a Mean  Squared Error (MSE) for heightened stability:

$$L_{G A N}=\mathsf E_{y}[\log(D(y))]+\mathsf E_{x}[\log(1-\!D(G(x)))]$$
where   $D$    represents the discriminator,   $G$    the generator,  $x$    an image from the noise domain, and  $y$    an image from the clean  domain.

To ensure that an image translated from one domain to  the other can be accurately reverted to its original form, the  cycle- consistency loss is employed. It is computed as the L1  norm between the source image and the reconstructed image  after a round-trip translation:

$$L_{c y c l e}=\mathsf{E}_{x}[||G_{y}(G_{x}(x))\ -x||_{1}]\,+\,\mathsf{E}_{y}[||G_{x}(G_{y}(y))\ -y||_{1}]$$
The identity loss is applied to the generators to enforce that  an image from the target domain remains unchanged after  processing. It is expressed as the L1 norm between the input  image and the image output by the generator:

$$L_{i d e n t i t y}\,=\,\mathsf{E}_{y}[||G_{y}(y)\,\lnot y||_{1}]\,+\,\mathsf{E}_{x}[||G_{x}(x)\,\lnot x||_{1}]$$
A supervised loss component is incorporated to ensure  that the generated image closely approximates the ground truth.  This is also measured using the L1 norm:

$$L_{s u p e r v i s e}\;=\;\mathsf E_{x,y}[||G_{y}(x)\;{\neg}y||_{1}]\,+\,\mathsf E_{y,x}[||G_{x}(y)\;{\neg}x||_{1}]$$
The generators are optimized using a comprehensive loss  that combines the adversarial, cycle-consistency, identity, and  supervised loss components, each weighted by respective  hyperparameters:

$$\begin{array}{r}{L_{G}=L_{G4N}+\lambda_{c y c l e}\cdot L_{c y c l e}+\lambda_{i d e n t i t y}\cdot L_{i d e n t i t y}+}\\ {\lambda_{s u p}\cdot L_{s u p e r v i s e}}\end{array}$$
(5)

The relative importance of each term is modulated by  $\lambda_{\mathit{c y c l e};}$  ,   $\lambda_{i d e n t i t y}$  , and  $\lambda_{\mathit{s u p};}$  , which are hyperparameters tuned to achieve the  desired balance in the training process.

## D. Implementation Details  

For the datasets, the images are cropped into   $128\!\times\!128$  patches. We further incorporated data augmentation techniques,  including horizontal and vertical flipping, to enhance the  diversity of samples. By randomly cropping image patches  during each training iteration, we were able to significantly  increase the number of training images and enrich the dataset's  variability .  For training, the batch size is 16. Also, we employ  the Adam optimizer to minimize the loss function with  $\upbeta1=0.5$

and   $\upbeta2=0.999$  . The learning rate is initialized at 0.0002. The  network is trained for 200 epochs, which takes approximately 10  hours to converge and achieve the best results. Our  implementation is based on the PyTorch library using dual  NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION  

In this section, we demonstrate the capabilities of our  model by undertaking both quantitative and qualitative  assessments. Our quantitative evaluation is carried out on  synthetic datasets, while qualitative evaluation is performed on  real TEM datasets.

Our experimental investigations are divided into three  distinct parts: (1) Quantitative comparative experiments  involving supervised CycleGAN with paired images,  unsupervised CycleGAN, a semi-supervised CycleGAN that  integrates both supervised and unsupervised approaches, and  the optimized CycleGAN model with attention gate; (2)  Performance evaluation on an open source ADF-STEM  dataset; (3) Qualitative performance analysis on genuine TEM  images that are contaminated with film grain noise and charge  noise, in the absence of corresponding clean images as ground  truth.

The performance of four types of CycleGAN networks is  compared and listed in Table 1. The performance metrics for  supervised learning alone were a PSNR of 24.5490 and an  SSIM of 0.8922, while unsupervised learning achieved a PSNR  of 26.1844 and an SSIM of 0.8939. The combination of these  two methods into a hybrid training strategy resulted in a  substantial improvement, reaching a PSNR of 29.4090 and an  SSIM of 0.9386. Further enhancement in performance was  achieved by incorporating an attention mechanism, with the  PSNR marginally advancing to 29.4495 and the SSIM  increasing to 0.9397. This integrative approach highlights the  potential of combining various training strategies and  advanced techniques to develop highly efficient denoising  models.

## IV.   S UMMARY 

The application of CycleGAN in atomic image denoising  represents a significant advancement in the field of image  processing. It overcomes traditional constraints posed by noise,  particularly in the realm of atomic imaging, by successfully  employing unsupervised learning capabilities for image  enhancement.   Experimental   results   demonstrate   that  significant improvements in denoised image quality can be  achieved using the optimized CycleGAN model with attention  gate. This model not only improves the signal-to-noise ratio but  also more faithfully preserves the structural integrity of the  images. The model exhibits flexibility in handling various  forms and levels of noise, crucial for the unpredictable and  diverse noise attributes common in practical applications. This  innovation provides clearer and more accurate images for  atomic-scale analysis, fostering deeper insights into material

properties. The flexible training approach of the CycleGAN  model with an attention gate sets a new standard for noise  reduction in atomic imaging. Future efforts will likely extend  this method to other imaging techniques and refine training  algorithms for exceptional denoising performance.
