## Enhanced Robustness of Interfacial Layer (IL) for  PBTI Improvement of HfO 2 /TiN RMG Stacks by  Low-temperature Oxygen Plasma Treatment  

Songyi Jiang 1,2 , Qianqian Liu 1 , Mingyang Sun 1,2 , Hong Yang 1,2\* , Junjie   $\mathrm{Li^{1,2^{*}}}$  , Bo Tang 1 , Xiaobin   $\mathrm{He^{l}}$  , Shuai   $\mathrm{{Yang}^{l}}$  , Jun Luo 1,2  and Wenwu  Wang 1     1  Key Laboratory of Microelectronics Device & Integrated Technology, Institute of Microelectronics, Chinese Academy of Science, Beijing  100029, China  2  Microelectronics Institute, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China   Phone : (86)10-82995916, Email :  yanghong@ime.ac.cn, lijunjie@ime.ac.cn

Abstract —In this paper, in order to enhance the quality of  SiO 2  chemical interfacial layer (IL) and get better PBTI  characteristics of devices with   $\mathbf{Hf}\mathbf{O}_{2}/\mathbf{TiN}$  stacks, the atom  oxygen   $(\mathbf{O}^{*})$   plasma treatment after IL growth is applied at low  temperature   $\mathbf{(250^{\circ}C)}$   for 10 seconds. The experimental results  show that equivalent oxide thickness (EOT) is increased about  0.12nm by  ${\mathbf O}^{*}$   plasma treatment, which is due to the  $\mathbf{SiO}_{2}$   IL regrowth according to the TEM image and relationship between  leakage (LKG) and EOT. The initial interface trap of device  decreases about   $\mathbf{60\%}$  , while the generated interface trap in  PBTI at   $\scriptstyle{125^{\circ}\mathbf{C}}$   is obviously decreased, which is due to the  increase of hydrogen in the interface between IL and silicon as  shown in Secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) data. The  stress-induced leakage current (SILC) shows that the energy  level of generated oxide trap in HfO 2  by   ${\mathbf O}^{*}$   plasma treatment  shifts to Ev of silicon about 0.04eV due to the nitrogen  passivation in the oxide. This work gives a clue to improve the  IL quality and PBTI using low-temperature thermal budget.

Keywords—reliability,   PBTI,   MOS   Capacitor,   plasma  treatment, oxygen vacancy defects

## I.    I NTRODUCTION  

Recently, the stacking of multiple device layers in  seque ntial 3D integration[1, 2] has been proposed as a potential sol ution for further enhancing CMOS performance[3]. The inte gration process, which involves stacking nanosheets with clo se vertical spacing and includes high-temperature annealing,  becomes challenging. Consequently, novel approaches are im perative for ensuring an adequate level of BTI reliability in f uture CMOS nodes without relying on high-temperature ann ealing[4]. Hence, there is a pressing need for mechanistic stu dies on how to improve PBTI. In low thermal budget study, i t’s shown that employing atomic oxygen   $(\mathrm{O^{*}})$   and atomic hy drogen   $(\mathrm{H}^{\ast})$   treatment in the IL layer enhances BTI in the R MG stacks [5, 6]. In order to understand the trap generation i n low thermal budget, we applied atomic oxygen  $(\mathrm{O^{*}})$   treatm ent to the IL layer to improve PBTI of MOSCAP within the  RMG stack[7]. The fundamental electrical characteristics, PB TI and Stress Induced Leakage Current (SILC) of device are  analyzed to understand the trap-based degradation[8]. It prov ides valuable insights for subsequent optimizations of the seq uential 3D integration process.

## II.   D EVICE AND  E XPERIMENTAL 

The MOS capacitors, manufactured on 200mm N-type  (100) wafers as test substrates, serve as a testing platform for  PBTI (negative bias temperature instability) with stress  accumulation in the n-well. The fabrication process employs  a conventional vertical process for MOS capacitor(MOSCAP)  preparation, and the simplified RMG process is illustrated in

Fig. 1. Immediately after the formation of the chemical oxide  layer, exposure at  $250^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for varying durations in  $\mathrm{O^{*}}$   (remote  plasma) is conducted for comparison. The high-k gate stack  consists of a   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer and   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   high-k layer, TiN/TaN/TiN  work function metal layer, and W-metal. Transmission  electron microscopy (TEM) images of the cross-section of the  MOSCAP are also depicted in Fig. 1.

Unless otherwise specified, all measurements are  conducted at a temperature of   $125^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  . The Keysight B1500A  is used for electrical measurements, and equivalent oxide  thickness (EOT) and flat band voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{fb}})$   are estimated  from the measured C-V curves using the CVC-Hauser tool[9].

Through multi-frequency conductance measurements,  we investigated the impact of   $\mathrm{O^{*}}$   treatment on the initial  interface characteristics and the interface changes before and  after stress in MOSCAP. As evident from Fig. 7, following   $\mathrm{O^{*}} $  treatment, the interface trap density of MOSCAP  significantly decreased, leading to an improvement in the  initial interface characteristics. Moreover, the change in  interface trap density for MOSCAP after  $\mathrm{O^{*}}$   treatment under  stress was relatively small. We postulate that this reduction is  attributed to the  $\mathrm{O^{*}}$   passivation of oxygen vacancy defects in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  . To delve deeper into the physical mechanisms behind  the   $\mathrm{O^{*}}$  -dependent characteristics, we characterized the  distribution of impurity elements in the gate stack through  SIMS, as depicted in Fig. 8. In the MOSCAP fabrication  process,   $\mathrm{TiCl_{4}}$   and   $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$   were utilized as precursors for ALD  TiN, while   $\mathrm{WF_{6}}$   and  $\mathrm{B}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{6}$   were used for ALD W, introducing  the possibility of N and H elements into the gate stack. H  elements effectively passivated dangling bonds at the  interface[12], while N elements passivated defects in the highk gate dielectric layer[13].

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

The study systematically investigates the impact of   $\mathrm{O^{*}}$  treatment on the quality of the interfacial layer (IL) and the  Positive Bias Temperature Instability (PBTI) of   $\mathrm{HfO_{2}/T i N}$  RMGs. Results demonstrate that the  $\mathrm{O^{*,}}$  -treated Metal-OxideSemiconductor Capacitor (MOSCAP) exhibits a   $0.12\,\mathrm{\nm}$  increase in Equivalent Oxide Thickness (EOT) alongside a  negligible shift in Voltage Flat Band   $(\mathrm{V_{fb}})$  . The observed  current decay behavior of the   $\mathrm{O^{*}}$  -treated MOSCAP aligns  with iso-field reference lines for  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  , indicating   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   IL layer  re-growth, supported by TEM imagery. Moreover, PBTI  characteristics notably is improve following   ${\mathrm{O}}^{*}$  treatment,  particularly under low Vov, critical for device lifetime. This  improvement can be attributed to enhanced   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   IL quality  with reduced both interface and oxide traps, which is  due to  hydrogen passivation of dangling bonds and nitrogen  passivation in the high-k dielectric layer in   $\mathrm{O^{*}}$  -treated  devices. In summary, the atomic  $\mathrm{O^{*}}$   treatment post-interfacial  layer deposition, with a low-temperature thermal budget,  effectively improves IL quality and PBTI by suppressing  interface and oxide traps.
