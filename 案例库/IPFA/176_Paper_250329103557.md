## Study on the anti-crack propagation behavior of a  submicron pore-enriched elastomer composite  

Junhao Shen 1 , Pengyang Han 1 , Shaohua Zuo 3 , Fuwen Shi 3 , Canguan Gao 4 , Zhe  $\mathrm{Y}\mathbf{u}^{1}$  , Hengchang Bi 1 , Xing Wu 1,2,4\*  1 In Situ Devices Center, School of Integrated Circuits, East China Normal University, 500 Dongchuan Road, Shanghai, China  2 State Key Laboratory of Infrared Physics, Shanghai Institute of Technical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences  3 Engineering Research Center of Nanoelectronic Integration and Advanced Equipment, School of   Physics and Electronic Science, East  China Normal University, 500 Dongchuan Road, Shanghai, China  4 College of Electronic Engineering, Yili Normal University, 448 Jiefangxi Road, Yining, Xinjiang, China  Email: xwu@cee.ecnu.edu.cn

Abstract —Elastomeric materials play a pivotal role in the  composition of flexible sensors and actuators, garnering  significant attention within the realm of flexible electronics.  However, the failure reliability problem of intrinsically isotropic  elastomers due to crack propagation still exists. In this work, an  elastomeric composite enriched in submicron voids was  fabricated by overfilling silicone rubber with small-sized carbon  black (CB) particles. Different from the intrinsic silicone rubber  film, the porous carbon black/silicone rubber composite exhibits  unusual anti-crack propagation behavior. The dynamic  evolution of the morphology at the crack tip of the composite  was observed by in situ scanning electron microscopy (SEM),  revealing the anisotropic transformation mechanism caused by  the strain on the composite.

Keywords—elastomeric materials, anisotropy, anti-crack  propagation

## I.    INTRODUCTION  

The burgeoning growth of the flexible electronics  industry is propelled by rapid strides in materials science and  nanotechnology. Notably, the continuous advancement in  flexible technology has led to the widespread adoption of  flexible sensors [1‒6] and actuators [7‒11] in various  applications. Elastomeric materials lead the wave of change,  playing a pivotal role in manufacturing flexible devices.  Their unique properties have garnered significant attention,  underscoring their crucial contribution to the evolving  landscape of flexible electronics. However, there are still  difficulties and constraints in creating highly reliable  elastomeric materials. For example, weak intrinsic resistance  to crack propagation causes these elastomeric materials to fail  rapidly after localized damage.

Recent research has made significant strides in addressing  this issue, focusing particularly on anisotropic elastomeric  materials. These materials exhibit a remarkable improvement  in crack propagation resistance. A high fatigue threshold  conductive polymer hydrogel was fabricated based on a  process that combines freeze casting and salting out, which  can effectively inhibit crack propagation [12]. Fu et al. took  into account the crack resistance and toughness of the  material by embedding a directionally arranged core-shell  structure in soft self-healing polyurea [13]. Suo et al.  embedded high-modulus PDMS fibers in a low-modulus  PDMS matrix to achieve homogeneous composites with high  crack propagation characteristics and extremely low  hysteresis [14]. The direction of crack propagation is  perpendicular to the orientation of the PDMS fibers, which  dissipates the stress at the crack tip to the greatest extent.  Furthermore, Suo et al. achieved multi-scale dispersion of  crack tip stress through filler-filled highly entangled long  polymers, greatly increasing the fatigue threshold of rubber

[15]. Undoubtedly, the consensus is gradually forming  around the imperative to enhance material resistance to  cracks through the implementation of anisotropic elastic  material design strategies.

In this work, silicone rubber/CB composites with a threedimensional network structure of submicron pores were  prepared by overfilling fillers. Composite materials still have  strong crack propagation resistance when loaded with   $400\%$  strain, which is usually possessed by anisotropic materials.  Controlled   internal   morphological   characterization  demonstrates the initial isotropic behavior of the composites.  During the stretching process of this composite, the threedimensional network structure undergoes plastic deformation  and becomes a parallel-arranged fibrous structure. This  strain-induced anisotropic transformation effectively resists  crack tip propagation. In-situ tensile tests were performed to  observe the internal orientation transition of the material. The  dynamic evolution of directionally arranged fiber bundles  based on elastic-plastic deformation of the hole network is  revealed, validating the hypothesis of crack propagation  resistance behavior of silicone rubber/carbon black  composites and providing new ideas for the design of highreliability elastomer composites.

## II.   EXPERIMENTAL   DETAILS  

In this work, silicone rubber/CB composite films enriched  with submicron pores exhibit unique anti-crack propagation  properties. Therefore, it was selected as the research object.  The isotropic silicone rubber sample was selected for control  experiments with the anisotropic silicone rubber/CB sample.  Characterization of film-based samples mainly includes  Crack propagation reliability testing, static morphology  characterization, and in-situ tensile characterization. The  reliability evaluation focuses on the dynamic evolution  process of the morphology of the silicone rubber/CB sample  at the crack tip under in-situ tension. Mechanical testing of  film samples passed a universal testing machine (UH6502,  Youhong). The SEM model was Zeiss GeminiSEM450.The  morphology of the samples was observed under specific  conditions (accelerating voltage: 2 kV, probe current: 200  pA). In situ stretching tests were carried out by a customized  SEM. The in situ stretching process was recorded under  specific conditions (accelerating voltage:   $10\;\mathrm{~kV},$   probe  current: 300 pA).

## A.   Fabrication of Silicone rubber film  

The fabrication of silicone rubber film samples is  presented in the first part. Ecoflex 00-30 (Smooth-On) was  chosen as the ideal material for making silicone rubber  samples due to its good toughness and maximum elongation  of greater than   $800\%$  . In addition to this, Ecoflex 00-30  silicone rubber is isotropic and cannot resist crack expansion

during the stretching process, which is suitable as the bearer  of the experimental group. As shown in Fig. 1, Ecoflex 00-30  liquid A and liquid B were mixed in a ratio of 1:1 (w:w). The  mixed solution was stirred for 10 min at a speed of   $500\;\mathrm{rpm}$    and then vacuumed for 5 min to remove air bubbles. The  silicone rubber prepolymer is filled into a   $4\ \mathrm{cm}\times5\ \mathrm{cm}\times1$    mm mold after processing. Finally, the silicone rubber sample  was cured by heating at   $80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 1 h.

B.   Fabrication of silicone rubber/CB composite film

Crack propagation occurs in elastomeric materials due to  intrinsic isotropy. The reliability of elastic media is becoming  one of the main limitations hindering the industrialization  process of flexible electronics based on elastomeric  materials. In this work, submicron pore-enriched silicone  rubber/CB composites are fabricated to provide new ideas for  the design of elastomers resistant to crack propagation.  Liquid paraffin was used to dilute the Ecoflex 00-30 mixed  solution (A  $\therefore\mathbf{B}=1;\,1$  ). Carbon black particles (SuperP Li)  with a particle size of 40 nm were used as fillers to fill the  silicone rubber (Fig. 2). The ratio of Ecoflex solution, liquid  paraffin, and carbon black is 10: 1: 1 (w: w). The mixture is  stirred for 10 minutes at a speed of 500 rpm. High viscosity  is exhibited in silicone rubber/liquid paraffin/CB mixtures  due to the addition of excess filler. The high-viscosity mixture  is filled into the mold by knife coating. The final silicone  rubber/carbon black composite sample was obtained after  heating at  $80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 1 h. A large number of micro-nanobubbles  exist in the mixture and cannot be discharged. Therefore,  three-dimensional   network-structured   composites   with  submicron pore sizes were fabricated.

## III.   RESULTS   AND   DISCUSSIONS  

A.   Crack propagation reliability testing

Intrinsic isotropy is demonstrated during the stretching  process of Ecoflex 00-30 silicone rubber. After strain loading,  the preset cracks will propagate rapidly. Therefore, it is  suitable as an experimental control group for compounds with  anti-crack propagation behavior. The prepared silicone  rubber film sample is shown in the figure. The Ecoflex 00-30  sample is translucent. The thickness of the film is 1 mm. The  width of the strained region of the film is 4 cm and the height  is 3 cm. The default width of the crack defect is one-third of  the sample width. The stretching rate is   $50\,\mathrm{\mm/min}$  . The  strain loading range for crack growth testing of silicone  rubber films is  $0–75\%$  . As shown in Fig. 3, until the strain is

loaded to  $50\%$  , the crack propagation behavior of the silicone  rubber film is not obvious. The film fractured when it was  loaded to   $75\%$   strain. Cracks propagate rapidly. The ultimate  stretch length of Ecoflex 00-30 silicone rubber is   $800\%$   of the  original length. However, the silicone rubber sample failed at  small strains due to crack propagation. To further develop the  flexible electronics industry, it is necessary to improve the  crack propagation resistance of elastic materials.

Silicone rubber/carbon black composite films were  loaded from 0 to   $400\%$   to study the crack propagation  resistance behavior. The thickness of the film is 1 mm. The  width of the strained region of the film is 4 cm and the height  is  $3~\mathrm{{cm}}$  . The default width of the crack defect is one-third of  the sample width. The stretching rate is   $50\,\mathrm{\mm/min}$  . As  shown in Fig. 4, no obvious crack propagation behavior was  observed when the composite film was loaded to   $100\%$   and   $200\%$   strain. Notably, failure similar to that of intrinsic  silicone rubber films under strain loading of   ${>}400\%$   was not  observed in silicone rubber/carbon black composite films  which differ from conventional isotropic elastomeric  materials. The crack propagation-resistant behavior of the  composite film may transform into an oriented material  during the stretching process. In the following sections, the  morphology of the silicone rubber/CB composite as well as  the dynamic evolution during stretching will be focused to  prove this conclusion.

## B.   Static morphology characterization  

Scanning electron microscopy was used to study the  differential crack propagation behavior of intrinsic silicone  rubber materials and silicone rubber/CB composites. In this  work,   samples   were   trimmed   by   a   scalpel. The  characterization of the cross-sectional micromorphology of  the sample is the focus of attention. It should be noted that  during the sample preparation process, the mixed solution of  Ecoflex 00-30 was evacuated to remove air bubbles. The  interior of the silicone rubber material is intrinsically dense  (Fig. 5a). No significant submicron pores were found.

The viscosity of the silicone rubber/CB mixture is very

high due to the excessive filling of small particles. The  vacuum step cannot be performed during silicone  rubber/carbon black sample preparation. A large number of  submicron bubbles are retained in the mixture. After the  mixture solidifies, uniformly distributed submicron pores are  formed inside which are observed in the cross-section of the  sample (Fig. 5b). However, the anisotropic internal  morphology of silicone rubber/carbon black composites  cannot explain the crack propagation resistance behavior  unique to anisotropic materials. For this purpose, original  tensile tests were carried out to investigate the crack  propagation resistance behavior of silicone rubber/carbon  black composites.

## C.   In-situ tensile characterization  

In-situ tensile tests were conducted to further explore the  anisotropic crack growth resistance behavior of the silicone  rubber/carbon black composites. The development of in situ  testing is shown (Fig. 6a). The thickness of the test sample is  1 mm. The length and width of the strained region of the  sample are 3 cm and 1 cm. The sample was pre-cracked. The  crack width is one-third of the specimen width. The stretching  speed of the stretching stage of the in-situ SEM instrument is   $10~\upmu\mathrm{m/s}$  . The tensile loading range of the composite samples  was   $0–50\%$   throughout the in-situ tensile testing process.  When the sample is deformed to   $50\%$  , stretching stops.  Microscopic   morphology   at   different   scales   was  photographed (Fig. 6b and c). The micromorphology of the  tip of the crack was focused on observation. It is worth noting  that obvious crack growth behavior was not found in the  $50\%$    strain-loaded sample. The crack tip morphology at smaller  scales is characterized (Fig. 6c). The three-dimensional  network structure of submicron pores transforms into a  parallel-arranged fiber-like structure, which means that the  porous mixture becomes an anisotropic material after  stretching.

A streamlined principle analysis is carried out, based on  the micromorphological structure images obtained from insitu tensile experiments at   $0–50\%$   strain. The submicron  pore-enriched silicone rubber/CB porous composite changes  from isotropic to The material transforms into an anisotropic  material. Therefore, anti-crack propagation behavior is  observed in the composites. Among them, plasticity is the key  to structural transformation. The addition of liquid paraffin  acts as a plasticizer which increases the plasticity of the  composite. During the stretching process, the walls of the  pores plastically deform and transform into elongated fibrous  structures. The fibers are oriented parallel to the stretching  direction which results in the composite becoming an  anisotropic material (Fig. 7a). The direction of crack  propagation is perpendicular to the direction of fiber  arrangement, which disperses the force causing crack  propagation to the greatest extent (Fig. 7b).

## IV.   SUMMARY  

In this work, composites with uniformly distributed  submicron pores were fabricated by overfilling silicone  rubber with small particle size CB. The crack propagation  resistance of intrinsic silicone rubber and silicone  rubber/carbon black composites was evaluated. The weak  crack resistance properties of intrinsic silicone rubber films  were demonstrated, which are related to the anisotropy of the  material.   The   anti-crack   propagation   behavior   was

demonstrated during the stretching process of silicone  rubber/CB composites. The homogeneous morphology  within intrinsic silicone rubber and the porous morphology of  silicone rubber/CB are shown to illustrate the differences  between materials. In-situ tensile tests were performed to  further elucidate the material evolution of the composite  toward anisotropy during stretching.
