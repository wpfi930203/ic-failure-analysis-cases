## Optimization of Cathodoluminescence from GaN  Using Low-Voltage SEM  

Takeshi Otsuka 1, 3 , Shiori Kamijo 1 , Yoshiyuki Kubota 1 , Natsuko Asano 1, 4 , Konomi Ikita 1 , Seiichi Takami 3  and Shunsuke Asahina 1, 2

1  JEOL Ltd., 3-1-2 Musashino, Akishima, Tokyo 196-8558 Japan  2  Advanced Material Analysis Co-creation Research Center, Institute of Multidisciplinary Research for Advanced Materials, Tohoku  University, Sendai, 980-8577 Japan  3  Graduate School of Engineering, Nagoya University, Furo-cho, Chikusa-ku, Nagoya, Japan  4  Department of Applied Chemistry for Environment, Graduate School of Urban Environmental Sciences, Tokyo Metropolitan University,  1-1 Minami-Osawa, Hachioji, Tokyo 192-0397, Japan  Phone : (81)42 542 2185, Email :  sasahina@jeol.co.jp

Abstract —   GaN   wafers,   which   are   compound  semiconductors with large band gaps, are integral components  of power semiconductor devices. However, it is difficult to  synthesize crystals without any dislocation, and research on  crystal growth is still ongoing. In particular, there is an urgent  need to synthesize high-quality GaN wafers with few dislocation  defects for use in power semiconductor devices, and methods for  analyzing   these   defects   are   also   being   studied.  Photoluminescence (PL) analysis is generally used to analyze  dislocation defects in compound semiconductors such as GaN.  However, the spatial resolution of the PL method is in the order  of several hundred nanometers, making it difficult to find tiny  dislocation defects with nano-meter level in compound  semiconductors. The cross-sectioning is also crucial for failure  analysis of chips. In this paper, we investigate the optimized  conditions of analyzing dislocation defects in compound  semiconductors using different accelerating voltages with low  voltage   scanning   electron   microscope   (SEM)- cathodoluminescence (CL) including sample cross-sectioning.

GaN

Keywords—Compound   semiconductor,  Cathodoluminescence, SEM, Dislocation

## I.    INTRODUCTION  

Photoluminescence (PL) is a method for analyzing surface  dislocation defects in compound semiconductors such as GaN.  Furthermore, the PL method can analyze a 4-inch wafer in  approximately 15 min; therefore, it is used for shipping and  receiving inspections. On the other hand, the spatial resolution  of the PL method is of the order of several hundred  nanometers, making it difficult to find minute dislocation  defects in compound semiconductors, but it is possible to  roughly understand distribution of the dislocation defects of  the entire wafer. Therefore, we investigate the dislocation  defects in compound semiconductors using the scanning  electron microscope (SEM) - cathodoluminescence (CL)  method [1] to analyze minute dislocation defects at the nanolevel in wafers. SEM-CL using low accelerating voltages is  useful for visualizing dislocation defects [2]; however,  observation conditions suitable for visualizing minute  dislocation defects have rarely been investigated.   Therefore,  we compared different accelerating voltages   $(0.5{-}10\:\mathrm{kV})$   and  sample cross sections to optimize the conditions for  visualizing minute dislocation defects in wafers, which is a  problem in power semiconductor devices.

## II.   INSTRUMENTS  

## A.   Low voltage SEM  

The recent development of SEM has enabled us to achieve  a higher spatial resolution down to 1.0 nm or less, under low

voltage conditions. Furthermore, by choosing appropriate  observation conditions, we can selectively obtain different  information, such as material topology and composition.  Recently, we developed a new objective lens called the super  hybrid lens (SHL): a compound lens comprising both  magnetic and electrostatic lenses installed in JEOL JSM $\scriptstyle\mathrm{IT}800<\mathrm{SHL}>$  . The SHL can produce a small probe size even  at low accelerating voltages (for example 0.7 nm at 1 keV) [1].  In addition, a high-brightness Schottky emission electron gun  was used for the SEM. The system is suitable for CL because  of the use of a high probe current, even under low-voltage  conditions.

## B.   CL detectors  

CL detectors are used in several applications. Monarc Pro  CL (Gatan) was used under low-voltage conditions. A JEOL  panchromatic CL equipped with an Electron Probe Micro  Analyzer (EPMA) JEOL JXA-iSP100, and JXA-iHP200F  was used to compare the electron emitters.

## C.   Cross-sectioning with Ar ion milling  

We measure the CL at the cross section, in addition to plan  view. Fig. 1 shows a schematic of Ar ion beam milling socalled CROSS SECTION POLISHER™: CP (JEOL, IB19520CCP). A shield plate was placed on top of each sample  to determine its position. Only the portions uncovered by the  shield plate were milled using an Ar ion beam. The cutting  distance between the edge of the shield and the sample can be  adjusted with an accuracy of approximately   $\pm5~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  . In this  experiment, the distance was set to  $20\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The sample holder  was cooled using liquid   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   [3].

## B.   Comparison of different acceleration  voltages  

Fig. 3 shows the measurement results while changing the  accelerating voltage. A GaN substrate was used as a sample.  From the top: 0.5, 1, 3, 5, and   $10~\mathrm{kV}$  . Each observation was  performed at a fixed current of 100 pA. The working distance  (WD) was set to   $13\ \mathrm{mm}$   because a CL focusing mirror was  inserted between the objective lens and the sample. Under  these conditions, the diameter of the electron probe depends  on the accelerating voltage; that is, it increases at low  accelerating voltages and decreases at high accelerating  voltages. The images show that the dark contrast, which  indicates the dislocation contrast, is blurred at   $10\;\mathrm{~kV~}$  .  Moreover, concentrated dislocations appeared on large  islands. However, when the accelerating voltage is lower than   $5\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{\kV}}$  , densely packed dislocations could be distinguished.  Furthermore, the penetrating dislocations can be distinguished  under these conditions. The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)  decreased when the accelerating voltage was 0.5 and 1 kV.  Therefore,   $3\ \mathrm{~kV~}$   is suitable owing to both the electron

penetration and CL escape depth. The escape depth for CL  emission in GaN is approximately 100 nm or less.

Fig. 4 shows the penetration depth of the primary electron  beam acceleration voltage at 3 and   $10~\mathrm{kV}$   for GaN. It can be  observed that primary electrons penetrate more than 600 nm  at  $10\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{kV}}$  . It is approximately 70 nm, which is the same size as  the small sunspot of the threading transition at   $3\ \mathrm{~kV~}$  .  Therefore, it is believed that the image quality at   $3\,\mathrm{\kV}$   is  higher.

## D.   CL images with cross-sectional samples  

Fig. 6 shows cross-sectional images of the CL and  secondary electrons (SEs). The cleaved cross-section showed  a large nonuniformity. These irregularities cause contrasts  when acquiring CL images, making it difficult to confirm the  structure of the sample, including threading dislocations.  However, the cross section prepared by cross sectioning with  Ar-ion milling showed a uniform surface that enabled the  observation of clear CL images. Therefore, the threading  dislocations and buffer layer distributions were confirmed.

## IV.   SUMMARY  

SEM-CL is a useful method for analyzing the dislocations  of compound semiconductors such as GaN. In this study, we  focused on GaN for the first time. In this study, we optimized  the conditions for CL on GaN. Finally, we concluded that 3  kV is the optimal acceleration voltage for the CL in GaN. We  also tested low-voltage conditions. However, it is unsuitable  for use in CL imaging of dislocations from the surface.   Additionally, we obtained cross-sectional CL images. This is  because cross-sectioning is useful for failure analysis of the  chip. Finally, we found that the Ar-ion milling cross-section  could also be used for CL analysis.
