## Scalable production of two-dimensional materials  and its reliable resistive switching   

Conghui Zhang #1 , Xin Liu #1 , Shuohan Cheng 1 , Zhaohui Wang 1 , Peisong Liu 2 , Shuiren Liu 1  and Fei Hui 1 \*  1 School of Materials Science and Engineering, Zhengzhou University, Zhengzhou, China  2 Engineering Research Center for Nanomaterials (ERCN)  National & Local Joint Engineering Research Center for Applied Technology, Henan University, Zhengzhou, China   Email :  feihui@zzu.edu.cn

Abstract —Two-dimensional materials (2DM) have attracted  much attention and been widely utilized in the field of  optoelectronics because of their superior optical properties,  wide bandgaps, and intrinsic flexibility. However, preparing  large-area 2DM films is one of the main challenges in their  development in the industry. In this work, we developed a  universal and facile phase-interface transfer method for the  preparation of large-area and high-quality 2DM films  (including graphene oxides (GO), MXene) with Janus structure,  which are transferred on target substrates and constructed  Ag/Janus-2DM/ITO/PET flexible memristors. The results  indicate that Janus-2DM based memristors have presented  stable unipolar and bipolar resistive switching (RS) behaviors  by tuning the presence of surface modifier between the active  layer and top/bottom electrodes. In addition, the devices also  present low switching voltages (\~0.42 V) and high retention time  (\~3000 s), as well as reproducible RS behaviors under the  bending radius of 5 mm.

Keywords—resistive switching, reliability, 2D materials, Janus  structure, scalable fabrication

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

As electronic products gradually become lighter, the  requirements for chip integration density are getting higher  and higher. The size of Si-based complementary metal oxide  semiconductor (CMOS) devices is approaching its physical  limit, Moore's Law is facing failure. The traditional Von  Neumann storage and computing separation architecture  leads to substantial power consumption during the data  transmission process and generates data-delay, which makes  it increasingly difficult to meet the explosive demand for  high-efficiency   emerging   brain-inspired   computing  applications [1,2]. New materials or architectures are  necessary to be designed/proposed to address the above  issues. Two-dimensional (2D) materials (has a large family  member including semi-metallic graphene, semiconductor  transition metal sulfide, insulating hexagonal boron nitride,  among others), show great potential applications in  memristive devices, e.g., three-terminal transistors and twoterminal memristors, as metallic electrodes or dielectrics [3- 5]. Moreover, the introduction of atomically thin 2D  materials into memory and computing devices is expected to  reduce the device size and further increase the integration  density, promising to extend Moore's Law [6-9]. However,  the preparation of large-area high-quality 2D materials is a  key bottleneck restricting their applications in the industry.  Common methods for 2D materials preparation are  mechanical or liquid phase exfoliation, molecular beam  epitaxy,   and   physical/chemical   vapor   deposition  (PVD/CVD). Nevertheless, these methods all have their own  disadvantages, such as uncontrollable thickness and sizes,  harsh preparation conditions (e.g., high temperature and high  pressure), the introduction of impurities and other problems.

It is noted that enhanced performance of 2D materials  based electronic devices could be achieved by modifying the  surface of 2D materials benefiting from the rich functional  groups on the surface [10,11].   For example, ref. [12] reported  that the surface of MXene was modified with Celestine blue  (CB) and then used as functional layer (with a thickness of   ${\sim}300\ \mathrm{nm})$   to fabricate robust memristors (Ag/GO/MXeneCB/GO/ITO) via spin-coating method. These devices have  enhanced ultra low operating voltage   $(\mathord{\sim}0.9\ \mathrm{~V~})$   and long  retention time   $\scriptstyle(>10^{4}\ \mathrm{s})$  . Liu et al. [13] used defects-guided  self-assembly in atomically thin molecular layers to prepare  Janus colloidal particles with memristive properties  encapsulated in 2D materials. CVD-grown GO can  automatically form ellipsoid-shaped colloidal particles with  Janus structure. It’s based memristors show excellent  performances, including I ON / OFF  ratio of   $10^{4} $  and high  stability. However, no facile and stable methods are proposed  for the formation of large-area thin surface-modified 2D  films.

In this study, we develop a template-free and effective  phase-interface transfer method to obtain large-area 2DM  with Janus structure (Janus-2DM). We further build  memristors using Janus-2DM and evaluate the electrical  characteristics of the devices (e.g. endurance, retention,  variability). The result demonstrates that Ag/Janus2DM/ITO/PET flexible memristors present typical both  threshold RS and bipolar RS behavior, dependent with the  presence of surface modifier between the 2DM and  top/bottom electrodes. Specifically, Janus-2DM based  memristors exhibit low switching voltage (\~0.42 V), high  retention time (\~3000 s) and reliable endurance   $(\mathord{>}350)$  . This  high-yield and scalable fabrication method of 2DM has  promoted the application in the semiconductor industry.

## II.    R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

2D materials (e.g., GO, MXene) have abundant surface  terminal groups (-OH, -O-, -COOH, etc.), which makes  surface grafting an effective modification method [15,16]. In  this work, we grafted Hexadecyltrimethoxysilane (HDTMS),  a common silane coupling agent, onto the surface of 2DM to  make its surface hydrophobic. HDTMS can be hydrolyzed to  produce silanol, and react with hydroxyl or carboxyl groups  on the surface of 2DM to generate ester groups or hydrogen  bonds to realize organic-inorganic hybridization. By  ultrasonication, modified 2D stacks were exfoliated and  formed 2D flakes with Janus-structure (i.e., hydrophobic and  hydrophilic surfaces). Based on its properties, Janus-2D  nanosheets were accumulated and self-assembled on the  oil/water interface. As a result, large-area Janus-2D films can  be obtained by phase-interface transfer.

In order to verify the universality of the above  methodology, we modified two types of 2DM (i.e., metallic  MXene and insulating GO) and successfully obtained 2-inch-wafer-sized Janus-MXene and Janus-GO films (more details  can be found in ref. [17]). The preparation schematic of  Janus-MXene nanosheets is shown in Fig. 1a, and it mainly  includes:  i)  synthesis of MXene nanosheets: the MAX phase  (Ti 2 AlC 3 ) was etched with HCl and LiF to form MXene   $(\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{x}})$   by heating at   $50~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   and stirring for 30 hours to  remove Aluminum atoms. Repeated centrifugation was  employed to wash off residual acid solution and obtain single  or few layers of   $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{x}}$   nanosheets. The MXene dispersion  has the Tyndall effect, which proves that the nanosheets have  good dispersibility in water (Fig. 1b).  ii)  Surface modification  of MXene: we mixed HDTMS, methylbenzene with MXene  dispersion (see Fig. 1c), and stirred them at 700 rpm with  water-bath heating at   $60^{\mathrm{o}}\mathrm{C}$   for 2 hours. A continuous film  generated at the phase-interface (see Fig. 1d) indicates that  HDTMS has been successfully grafted onto the surface of  MXene sheets, finally generating Janus-MXene nanosheets.  For obtaining large-area 2DM films, we dispersed JanusMXene nanosheets into oil-water solution. Via migration and  self-assembly, modified MXene nanosheets can naturally  compose a continuous uniform Janus-MXene film at the  interface. As a result, the formed large-area Janus-MXene  films can be transferred onto the target substrates via phaseinterface transfer methodology. Figure 1e shows the process  of preparation of wafer-size (2 inch) Janus 2DM films. This  method we proposed here is template-free and without  introduction of impurities, resulting in obtaining Janus  materials with high quality and high yield.

In order to evaluate the quality of transferred Janus-GO  and   Janus-MXene   films,   we   carried   out   multiple  characterizations, including scanning electron microscopy  (SEM), atomic force microscopy (AFM), and Raman  Spectroscopy. Janus-GO and Janus-MXene films were  transferred on a piece of  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Si}$   wafer and analyzed via SEM  and AFM, respectively. SEM (Fig. 2a, d) and AFM (Fig. 2b,  e) images display that Janus-GO and Janus-MXene films are  continuous and uniform in a large area   $(>\!100~\upmu\mathrm{m})$  . From the  Raman spectra, we can see that  both Janus-MXene and  MXene nanosheets have similar typical peaks (  $(198\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$  , 302   $\mathrm{cm^{\cdot1}}$  ,   $377\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$  ,  $622\;\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$  ) before and after modification (Fig.

2c). This indicated that the phase structure of MXene did not  change much after modification. Raman spectra of GO and  Janus-GO show the typical D peak   $(\sim\!1348~\mathrm{cm^{-1}})$   and G peak   $(\sim\!1594~\mathrm{cm}^{-1})$  ) with   $\mathrm{{I_{G}}/\mathrm{{I_{D}}}}$   intensity ratios of 1.07 and 1.18,  which also prove high quality 2DM nanosheets after  modifying (Fig. 2f). We also verified the Janus structure (i.e.,  hydrophobic and hydrophilic) of 2DM via the contact angle  (CA) test. The result demonstrates that the CA of Janus-GO  and Janus-MXene are   $42.92^{\circ}$  ,   $35.66^{\circ}$  , and   $105.24^{\circ}$  ,   $87.03^{\circ}$  ,  respectively (Fig. 2g), indicating the formation of Janus-2DM  with hydrophilic surface and hydrophobic surface on each  side. In conclusion, high quality, large area and continuous  GO and MXene films can be successfully modified and  generated via surface modifications and phase-interface  transfer methodology.

In the next step, we transferred the formed Janus-2DM  film onto a flexible substrate and constructed an Ag/Janus2DM/ITO/PET device as shown in Fig. 2h, the individual cell  size is  $100\,\upmu\mathrm{m}{\times}100\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . All the electrical measurements were  conducted by probe station and Keithley 4200 semiconductor  parameter analyzer (SPA). We prepared two kinds of devices  (i.e., hydrophobic surface of Janus-2DM faces up to the top  or bottom electrodes) and investigated their electrical  behaviors under bias stresses. Generally, under the positive  bias, the devices present both excellent unipolar RS and  bipolar RS dependent with the modified surfaces (Fig. 3,4),  respectively. Specifically, when the hydrophobic surface of  Janus-2DM is facing up to the top Ag electrodes, the devices  show typical threshold RS (Fig. 3a, d) with a large  I ON /I OFF ratio of   $10^{3}$   and their operation voltages are of  ${\sim}0.42~\mathrm{V}$   and   ${\sim}0.57\,\mathrm{V}$  , respectively (Fig. 3b, e); It also shows favorable DC  sweep endurance of   ${\tt>}100$   cycles (Fig. 3c, f). On the other  hand, when the hydrophobic surface of Janus-2DM is facing  up to the bottom ITO/PET electrodes, we can observe typical  and reliable bipolar RS in both types of memristors (Fig. 4a,  d). They display similar performance, such as low SET

voltage   $(\mathord{<\!0.42}\,\mathrm{~V~})$  , excellent endurance   $(\mathord{>}350)$  , and long  retention time   $({\sim}3000\;\mathrm{s})$   (Fig. 4b-c, e-f).

In order to understand the RS mechanisms, we propose  that the RS behaviors are mainly related to the formation and  disruption of conductive filaments (CFs), resulting from the  migration of active   $\mathrm{Ag^{+}}$   ions and the hindrance of HDTMS  molecular (hydrophobic surface of Janus-2DM) under the  electrical bias. When the hydrophobic surface is facing up,  which means that a long carbon chain of modifier HDTMS  sets a barrier for the movement of   $\mathrm{Ag^{+}}$   ions to the bottom  electrode. With the continued bias stress,  $\mathrm{Ag^{+}}$   ions will break  the barrier and penetrate into the Janus-2DM layers, resulting  in a sudden current increase. While the   $\mathrm{Ag^{+}}$   -based CFs are  not stable and easily get recovered when the bias is removed,  resulting threshold RS (Fig. 5a-c). On the contrary, when the  hydrophobic surface is facing down,  strong   $\mathrm{Ag^{+}}$  -based CFs  are formed because of HDTMS contact-free with Ag top  electrodes. Therefore, the LRS of the devices can be  maintained for a long time, until the negative bias is applied,  the device could reset from LRS to HRS and exhibit  bipolarity (figure 5d, f).

In addition, we also investigated the possibility of Janus2DM applied in flexible electronics. In this work, we have  conducted in-situ bending tests of Janus-GO based  memristors at the device level. Generally, the devices were  bent with different curvature radius (20 mm, 15 mm, 10 mm,   $5\,\mathrm{\mm}$  ), and tested its electrical behaviors (Fig. 6). The  preliminary results show that although the operation voltages   $_{(>0.7\:\mathrm{V})}$   increased slightly (may be resulted from the induced  cracks of GO under bending or unstable conductivity of ITO  substrates, need to be confirmed in the future work), the  devices still show similar both threshold and bipolar RS  behaviors with distinct high and low resistive states. This  demonstrates the robust stability of the Janus-GO based  flexible memristors.

## III.    C ONCLUSIONS   

In conclusion, we have developed a cost-effective method  to obtain Janus 2DM with high quality and high yield, this  approach is universal for fabrication of diverse Janus-2DM.  Furthermore, we have demonstrated that Janus-GO and  Janus-MXene are good dielectrics and successfully be  employed in memristive devices. And they all exhibit reliable  resistive switching performance, especially low set voltage   $(\sim\!0.42\;\mathrm{V})$   and high endurance   $(\mathord{>}350)$  . It is further presenting  Janus-GO as a promising material in flexible electronic  devices.
