## The Effect of the P-substrate Connection on the  HBM Robustness of P-type ESD Device  

Jian-Hsing Lee 1 , Chih-Cherng Liao 1,2 , Wen-Hsin Lin 1 , Chih-Hsuan Lin 1 , Karuna Nidhi 3 , Yeh-Ning Jou 1 , and Ke-Horng Chen 2 1 Device Engineering Department, Vanguard International semiconductor Co., No. 9, Lixing rd., Hsin-Chu city, Taiwan     2 Department of Electrical and Computer Eng., National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, HsinChu City, 300093, Taiwan  3 ESD Department, Richtek Technology Corporation, ChuPei City, Taiwan  Phone: (886)0966595083, Email:  ano.lee2009@gmail.com

Abstract —Although the p-substrate connection of p-type  ESD device does not have any impact on the device’s It2 while  connecting the p-substrate to the ground degrades the humanbody model (HBM) robustness of the device significantly. From  the experiment results ,  adding N-type buried layer (NBL) to ptype ESD device can have   the device HBM robustness invariant  to the p-substrate connection. Based on the T-CAD simulations ,  the effect of the p-substrate connection on the HBM robustness  of p-type ESD device is revealed in this paper. As HBM raises  up the potential of the whole region   of p-type ESD device after  the onset of zapping for a while and then the high potential  region of the device decreases with decreasing HBM current if  the p-substrate is grounded, resulting in the current crowding.  On the contrary, the high potential region of p-type ESD device  does not decrease with decreasing HBM   current if the psubstrate is floating or NBL is added to the device, resulting in  the uniform current distribution.

## Keywords—ESD, It-2, TLP, HBM, T-CAD  

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

With the non-snapback behaviour, p-type electrostaticdischarge (ESD) devices are the prominent protection clamp  devices to prevent the power supply clamping at the low  voltage in the high-voltage (HV) BCD products [1]. To  prevent the latch-up [2-3], p-type ESD device often needs a   $\mathrm{P}+$   guard-ring (P-sub in Figs. 1 and 2) to surround it since HV  product is often exposed to the harsh noisy environment [4]- [9]. In general, most HV products have the multiple power  supplies in which the volage range might be from the negative  to positive [10]. Moreover, the lowest voltage of a chip  should be at the p-substrate. So, the two nodes   (collector and  P-sub) of p-type ESD device might connect to different pads  if they are in different power domains and might connect to  same pad if they are in same power domain. So, p-type ESD  device has two test conditions. One is applying ESD or  transmission line pulse (TLP) to the emitter with respect to  the grounded collector and P-sub; the other is applying ESD  or TLP to the emitter with respect to the grounded collector  and floating P-sub. When the P-sub and collector both are  connected to the ground, the P-sub becomes the collector of  the vertical bipolar (pnp v ) from Fig. 1. This implies that ptype ESD device might have the better ESD robustness under  this test condition since the P-sub can provide an additional  current path to sink the current of ESD or TLP. However, the  two test conditions do not lead to any It2 difference of p- type  ESD device from the TLP test result. It is also found that ptype ESD device is very susceptible to the human-body  model (HBM) for the test condition that the P-sub and  collector both are connected to the ground, compared to the  test condition that the collector is grounded while the P-sub  is floating. To solve this problem, a N-type buried layer  (NBL) is proposed to add below the p-type ESD device as  shown in Fig. 1(b). This is based on the study that NBL can

enhance the ESD robustness of N-LDMOS [11]-[14].  Although this scheme does not increase the It2 of p-type ESD  device, it makes the HBM robustness of p-type ESD device  with NBL invariant to the p-substrate connection. The It2 and  HBM threshold voltage of p-type ESD device with NBL  under the test condition that the P-sub and collector both are  connected to the ground are almost the same as that under the  test condition that the collector is grounded while the P-sub  is floating.

In this paper, the mechanism why the p-substrate  connection has the high   impact on the HBM robustness and  while does not show   the effect on the It2 of p-type ESD  device without NBL as shown in Fig. 1(a) is explored by the  TCAD simulations.   Moreover, the phenomenon why the  NBL can make the HBM robustness of p-type ESD device  invariant to the p-substate connection is also discussed.

## II.    E XPERIMENT   

The devices used for this study are two different 12V ptype ESD devices, which are fabricated on a  $0.15\ \upmu\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{HV}\,80\mathrm{V}$    to 1.8V bipolar-CMOS-DMOS (BCD) technology. In this  technology, there are three different types of N-wells for  different applications, which are N-well (NW), deep N-well  (DNW) and N-type buried layer (NBL). The only difference  for the two ESD devices is the NBL as shown in Fig.1. They  both are multi-finger devices with the same   layout and  surrounded by the   two guard-rings as shown in Fig. 2. The  device dimension is   $100\,\times\,65\,\,\upmu\mathrm{m}^{2}$  . Both p-types of ESD  devices have two different kinds of bipolar transistors, which  are the lateral pnp bipolar(pnp L ) and vertical pnp bipolar  (pnp V ). The NW guard-ring is the bases of the two pnp bipolar  transistors and the  $\mathrm{P}+$   guard-ring is the collector of the vertical  pnp bipolar.

The apparatuses used to evaluate the ESD robustness of ptype ESD device are the commercial TLP test system and  HBM test. The pulse width and rise time of TLP for this study  are 100ns and 10ns, respectively. Each point of TLP IV curve  is based on the average values of the voltage and current  waveforms of the device under a given current level TLP over  the time span between 70ns and 90ns. For each zapping  voltage, a series of three HBM pulses are applied to the zapped  node of the device, which follows the JEDEC standard [15],  [16]. The rise time and fall time of HBM pulse are 10ns and  150ns, respectively. Unlike the TLP system, the ESD test  system does not have the build-in oscilloscope for waveform  measurement. So, the current and voltage waveforms of the  device during HBM event is measured by a single  oscilloscope, which uses an active voltage probe   $(<2\mathsf{p F})$   to  catch voltage waveform and uses the current probe (Tek CT1) to catch the current waveform. After each TLP event and  HBM event, the leakage current or DC IV curve of the device  is measured by the DC meter of the TLP or HBM test system.  The device is defined as the failure if any DC IV curve or  leakage current of the device shifts or increases after each TLP  or HBM event.

The simulator used to explore the electric behavior of ptype ESD during the HBM event is Taurus TCAD simulator.  It includes the 2-D process simulator TSUPREM4 and  electrical simulator MEDICII. TSUPREM4 is used to  fabricate and generate the structures of the two ESD devices  in Fig. 1 based on the process flow of this technology and real  device layout. MEDICII is used to simulate the electrical  characteristics of the structure generated from TSUPREM4  during the HBM zapping event.

## III.    T EST  R ESULT 

## A.   TLP and HBM  

Fig. 3 shows the TLP IV curves for the two ESD devices  in Fig. 1 versus the P-sub connection. The same ESD device  has similar It2   regardless of whether the P-sub is floating or  grounded. The It2 value for the ESD device without NBL  (Fig. 1(a)) is 5.79 A and that with NBL (Fig. 1(b)) is 6.04 A.  However, the ESD device without NBL can only pass   $3\mathrm{~kV~}$    HBM if the P-sub is grounded, while it can pass 8 kV HBM  if the P-sub is floating. On the other hand, the p-type ESD  device with NBL can pass 8 kV HBM regardless of whether  the P-sub is floating or grounded.

## B.   Waveforms for ESD devices during TLP and HBM  

From Fig. 3, the p-substrate connection does not have any  impact on their It2 values. So, only the voltage and current

waveforms of the emitters of the two ESD devices in Fig. 1  under the   ${\sim}0.9$   A TLP events are illustrated in Fig. 4.  However, the p-substrate connection shows a great   impact on  the HBM robustness of the device without the NBL (Fig.  1(a)) from the HBM test result. So, Fig. 5 not only shows the  voltage and current waveforms of the emitters but also  illustrates the voltage waveforms of the P-subs for the two  ESD devices in Fig. 1 under the 1 kV HBM events. As can  be seen, the voltage of emitter (V E ) can always keep constant  after the snapback regardless of the TLP event or HBM event.  On the other hand, the current of the emitter   $\mathrm{(I_{E})}$   can keep  constant in whole TLP stress time after the snapback, while  that decreases with increasing time after reaching the peak  current of HBM. This is the main difference for the ESD  device during the TLP and HBM events.

## IV.    M ECHANISM   

In order to explore why the P-substrate connection has  the impact on the device HBM robustness, a TCAD is used  to investigate the electrical characteristics of p-type ESD  devices during the HBM zapping events. Figs. 6 and 7 show  the simulated structures and the distributions of potential,  hole concentration, electron concentration   of the two p-types  of ESD devices in Fig. 1 at the onset of HBM. The model of  MEDICI used to simulate the electrical characteristics of ptype ESD device during the HBM test is the AC analysis   with  circuit simulation as shown in Fig. 8 [17], [18]. Based on the  two simulated structures in Figs. 6(a) and 7(a), the substrate  potentials   $(\mathrm{V_{P-sub}})$   both can rise to the same voltages as that  of emitters   $\mathrm{(V_{E})}$   after snapbacks like the measured result in  Fig. 5 from the simulation result in Fig. 9. This infers that the  simulation can depict the electrical behaviors of p-type ESD  device during the HBM event to find the p-substrate  connection leading to what different mechanisms to give rise  to the different ESD robustness’s of the device.

## A.   P-type ESD Device without NBL  

Figs. 10-13 show the distributions of the simulated  current flowing lines, potential, hole concentration and  electron concentration of the structure in Fig. 6(a) during 1  kV HBM event at four different transients (t 0 , t 1 , t 2  and t 3 ) in  Fig. 9(a) if the p-substrate (P-sub) is grounded. t 0  is at the  transient within the rise time of HBM (  $\sim1$   ns), t 1  is at the rise  time of HBM (\~10 ns),  $\mathfrak{t}_{2}$   is at the transient (  $\mathord{\sim}100$   ns) before  the RC time constant (fall time) of HBM   $(1~{\mathrm{k}}\Omega\times100~{\mathrm{pF}}=$   ${\sim}150\,\mathrm{ns})$  ), and t 3  is at the transient (\~200 ns) after the fall time  of HBM. From Figs. 10-13, these electrical characteristics  (current, potential, hole, and electron distributions) of p-type  ESD device change with time. At   $\mathbf{t}_{0}$  , the HBM current can  distribute whole device uniformly since the electrons and  holes in the reversed biased pn junctions are forced to leave  the depletion region due to the depletion width increasing  with increasing voltage [1], [19], [20]. With the positive  charge, all holes can only flow to the lowest potential nodes  (collectors and P-sub) after leaving the depletion regions. It  pulls up the potentials of the substrate and DNW as shown in  Fig. 11(a). With the negative charge, all electrons can only  flow to the highest potential node   $\left(\mathrm{N+}\right)$   after leaving the  depletion regions. This pulls down the potential of NW (  $\mathrm{V_{B}}$  in Fig. 1). If the potential difference (V BE ) between   $\mathrm{V_{E}}$  and  V B  is > 0.7 V [21], the diode between the emitter and base is  forwarded, which triggers on the pnp bipolar. Then, the  emitter injects holes into the NW/DNW, which widens the  hole concentration regions below the two emitters compared  to that below the two collectors as shown in Fig. 12(a).  However, the highest potential node   $\Nu+$   (B) cannot sink  whole electrons completely, resulting in a lot of electrons  accumulated at the region below the region close to it as  shown in Fig. 13(b). Then, the holes move to this region to  retain the electric neutrality as shown in Fig. 12(b). With a lot  of electrons and holes, the region becomes an intrinsic region  (resistor) due to the heavy conductivity modulation effect  [10], [22]-[24]. This leads to the depletion region (red dashed  curves) between the p-substrate and DNW below the emitters  and collectors extinguishing and current crowding at the  emitter close to base node (B) at   $\mathrm{t}_{1}$   as shown in Fig. 10(b).  Without the depletion region, it makes the DNW short to the  p-substrate to pull the potential of the p-substrate below the  emitter and collector to the same voltage as that of DNW as  shown in Fig. 11(b). Moreover, it leads to more holes flowing  into the p-substrate due to no potential barrier between the

DNW and p-substrate. Then, the holes increase more and  more to lead to the same   number of electrons flowing into this  region for maintaining the electric neutrality. Eventually, the  electrons and holes both increase the level higher than the  background concentrations and overwhelm whole p-substrate  except the region close to the grounded P-sub at t 2  as shown  in Figs. 12(c) and 13(c). Due to this, the whole   region of the  device becomes the equal potential intrinsic region except the  region close to the P-sub as shown in Fig. 11(c) caused by the  heavy conductivity modulation effect.  With the same   potential, the current can distribute whole device uniformly  at t 2  as shown in Fig. 10(c). However, most HBM current  crowds at the emitter far away from the P-sub at t 3  as shown  in Fig. 11(d). This is caused by the HBM current decreasing   to the low current from the high current (Fig. 9(a)) .  So, HBM  head cannot supply enough   current to maintain the   current  flowing into the p-substrate same as that flowing outside the  p-substrate when the P-sub is grounded. This makes the hole  and electron concentrations at the region close to the  grounded P-sub lower than the background concentrations as  shown in Figs. 12(d) and 13(d). So, only the region far away  from the grounded P-sub is still the intrinsic equal potential  region (Fig. 11(d)) to keep the pnp bipolar transistors turning  on, resulting in the current crowding.

## B.   P-type ESD Device with NBL  

Figs. 18-21 show the distributions of the simulated  current flowing lines, potential, hole concentration and  electron concentration of the structure in Fig. 7(a) during 1  kV HBM event at four different transients (t 0 , t 1 , t 2  and t 3 ) in  Fig. 9(b) when the p-substrate (P-sub) is grounded. Since the  behaviors of the p-type ESD device with NBL during HBM  event are almost the same as that without NBL over the time  span between t 0  and t 2  when its p-substrate is grounded, it  will not discuss these phenomena again. The only differences  for the devices with and without NBL are at t 3 . This can be  attributed that the doping concentration of NBL is much  higher than that of DNW or p-substrate from Figs. 7(c) and  7(d). So, the electron and hole concentrations cannot rise to  the same level as the doping concentration of NBL from Figs.  20 and 21. Due to this, the depletion region (red dashed  lines), as well as the potential barrier, still exists at the NBL  junction from Fig. 18(d). With the potential barrier, most  electrons in the p-substrate from the inside of NBL over the  time span between t 0  and  $\mathfrak{t}_{2}$    cannot flow back to the original  place as shown in Fig. 21(d). This makes the holes away the  grounded P-sub still stay in the p-substrate to maintain the  electric neutrality as shown in Fig. 20(d). So, most regions of  the device are   still the intrinsic region and can keep equal  potential as shown in Fig. 19(d) since it is full of electrons  and holes. With the same potential, the current still can  distribute whole device uniformly as shown in Fig. 18(d).  This is why the p-type ESD device with NBL can pass the  high voltage HBM zapping even if its   p-substrate is  grounded.

Figs. 22-25 show the distributions of the simulated  current flowing lines, potential, hole concentration and  electron concentration of the structure in Fig. 7(a) during the  1 kV HBM event at four different transients (t 0 , t 1 , t 2  and t 3 )  in Fig. 9(b) when the p-substrate (P-sub) is floating. As can  be seen, the behaviors of the p-type NBL ESD devices during  HBM event when the p-substrate is floating are almost the  same as that is grounded, implying same mechanism. With  the same behaviors, it also can pass the high voltage HBM  zapping and withstand very high current stress from TLP.

## VI.    C ONCLUSION 

From the simulation result, the effect of the p-substrate  connection on the HBM robustness of p-type ESD device is  uncovered. It finds that the potential distribution of the base  of pnp bipolar varies over time. First, the whole region of the  device becomes the equal potential intrinsic region when the  pnp bipolar turns on. Then, the equal potential intrinsic region  of the device shrinks over time after the fall time of HBM if  the p-substrate is grounded. This induces the HBM current to  be localized at a tiny region of the p-type ESD device,  resulting in the device failing at   the low voltage HBM  zapping event. Although most region of p-type ESD device  can keep equal potential during whole zapping period if the  p-substrate is floating or the NBL is added, it is caused by  different mechanisms. If the p-substrate is floating, there is  no node to conduct the holes in the p-substrate to the ground.  So, the p-substrate is full of holes and electrons. With high  doping concentration, there exists a potential barrier at the  NBL junction to inhibit the electrons flowing back to the  inside of NBL if the NBL is added to the p-type ESD device.

As the p-substrate is also full of electrons and holes. Hence,  the whole   region of p-type ESD device can keep equal  potential, resulting in uniform current distribution.
