## Use of Low Dose Sample Preparation Method   for Low-K Material  

Ye Chen, Kok Wah Lee, Jili Wang, Jie Zhu   GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte Ltd, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, Singapore 738406  Phone:   $(+65)$   6670-0243.   Fax:   $(+65)$   6362-2938.     Email: ye.chen@globalfoundries.com

Abstract-  In this paper we investigated the low-K material  shrinkage effects under electron beam exposure in FIB for  TEM sample preparation. Different electron dose levels in  combination with electron beam voltage have been evaluated.  Optimal combination of electron beam current/voltage setting  for low dose sample preparation is implemented for  minimizing low-k material deformation to ensure accurate  trench measurements in the subsequent TEM analysis

Keywords – low-K material, shrinkage effects, low dose TEM  sample preparation

## I. INTRODUCTION  

In modern semiconductor manufacturing, as devices  become smaller and more densely packed, the reduction of  parasitic capacitance becomes more and more important to  maintain or improve device speed and energy efficiency.  Low-k materials with lower dielectric constant, have been  widely used as intermetal dielectrics (IMD) for advanced  technology nodes to reduce RC delay of devices. Those lowk dielectric materials have porous structures to achieve  lower dielectric constants. Thus, they are very electron beam  sensitive with poor thermal and electric conductivities [1].  The relative instability leads to great challenge for physical  characterization specifically under an electron-beam  exposure in FIB during Transmission Electron Microscopy  (TEM) sample preparation.

Low dose focused ion beam (FIB) sample preparation  is a technique used to minimize damage to low-k material  for accurate physical characterization. The key advantage of  low-dose FIB sample preparation is its ability to reduce  damage to the sample, minimize artifacts and provide more  accurate and reliable results during subsequent analysis [2].  As shown in Fig.1, TEM sample prepared by normal dose  sample preparation observed severe trench deformation with  trench width increment around   $42.5\%$   and height decrement  around  $20.4\%$   as compared to TEM sample prepared by low  dose sample preparation method.

Both electron beam voltage and current used in FIB  have impact on low-k materials. High electron beam voltage

might cause sample damage and increased penetration depth  while lower voltage can lead to less damage but more  charging. The electron current indirectly affects low-k  materials through the interactions it generates. The electron  current is used to neutralize the positive charge built up on  the sample surface caused by the ion beam. Better charge  neutralization can reduce sample damage due to charging  effects in low-k materials. However, excessively high  electron current might contribute to additional heating or  other interactions that could still impact the low-k sample.  Thus, it is important to find the right balance of the electron  beam voltage and current setting combination to minimize  the impact on low-k materials.

## II. EXPERIMENTAL  

In this work, Design of Experiments (DOE) samples were  prepared by focused-ion beam milling with ion beam  working voltage at   $30\mathrm{kV}$  . The varying parameter in the

experiment was electron beam condition used for SEM  viewing during FIB milling using FEI Helios450HP. The  first experiment fixed electron beam current at 1.6nA and  evaluated the electron beam voltage effect with 2kV, 5kV,  10kV 20kV and 30kV on sample deformation. The second  experiment fixed electron beam voltage at 30kV and  evaluated the electron beam current effect with 100pA,  200pA, 400pA, 800pA and 1.6nA on sample deformation.  TEM analysis was performed using Tecnai F20 operated at   $200~\mathrm{kV}$   working voltage using low dose imaging setting as  well. For TEM imaging, gun lens 3 spot size 6 with electron  beam current less than 1nA was used to minimize the dose  level.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION  

Samples for trench profile characterization have been  submitted to TEM as the accurate measurements for trench  Critical Dimensions (CD) and IMD thickness is essential for  the fab to fine tune the etching process. Before realizing the  inter-metal dielectric (IMD) layer for the process is low-k  material, normal TEM sample preparation method with  electron beam voltage at 5kV and current at 800pA was  used. As shown in Fig.2, the iso trench shows an abnormal  profile with a bit curved sidewall and IMD layer is not flat  as the IMD layer away from the iso trench is thinner than  that beside the trench (198.7nm and 211.1nm respectively).  Therefore, trench deformation is suspected under normal  TEM sample preparation method.

By comparing normal dose and low dose prepared samples,  it is obvious that normal dose sample preparation results in  trench expansion around   $23.6\%$  , IMD thickness shrinkage  beside the trench around  $9.6\%$   and IMD thickness shrinkage  far away from the trench around   $11.4\%$   respectively.

To further study how the electron beam dose level affects  low k material deformation, some DOE experiments have  been done to investigate how is the individual impact of the  electron beam voltage, the electron beam current on the low  k material and how to optimise the combination of electron  beam voltage and current to minimize the low k material  deformation and ensure more accurate measurements.

## 3.1. Electron Beam Voltage Impact on Low-k Dielectrics  

First set of DOE experiments is used to study the single  impact of electron beam voltage on low-k materials. 5  samples have been taken from adjecent dies on the same  wafer. Same FIB machine is used to prepared the samples  using different electron beam voltage to search for sample  locations and cross-sectional viewing during FIB cut. The  electron beam current used for all the 5 samples are 1.6nA  with electron beam voltage setting at 2kV, 5kV, 10kV, 20kV  and  $30\mathrm{kV}$   respectively. As shown in table 1, the trench width

increases while IMD thickness decreases with the increasing  electron beam voltage with the same electron beam current.

High electron beam voltage can cause such obvious low k  material deformation as the DOE experiment shown is due  to individual electrons carrying high energy when the  electron beam voltage is high. The penetration depth of the  electron beam is determined by its energy. Higher energy  electron beams can penetrate deeper into the material. At  lower energies, electrons interact primarily through elastic  scattering and their penetration depth can be several  nanometers to micrometers. However, at higher energies,  inelastic scattering processes become more significant, and  the penetration depth can be much larger, ranging from tens  to hundreds of nanometers as shown in Fig. 4(a). The  penetration of incident electrons is determined by the  electron stopping power of the specimen and decreases with  increasing atomic number Z. The penetration depth of  incident electrons can be given by the Kanaya-Okayama  Formula [3] as shown in Fig. 4(b). According to the formula,  electron beam penetration depth is proportional to the beam  energy which is related to beam voltage. The deeper  penetration into the low k material will result in more  interactions with atoms and potentially leading to  displacement or deformation of the material.

From the formula, the penetration depth is roughly inversely  proportional to the density of material. Low-k dielectric  films are designed to have a low density and porous structure  as compared to traditional dielectric films. This lower  density can make them more susceptible to the effects of  high energy electrons. The sample cut under high electron  beam voltage shows severe trench deformation while the  low k dielectric shrinks more as compared to the region near  the trench itself as shown in Fig.5.

During thinning, the trench is filled with amorphous carbon  film with low density ranging from   $1.8{\cdot}2.3\mathrm{g}/\mathrm{cm}^{3}$  . The  density of amorphous carbon is higher than that of many  low-k dielectric materials with low density ranging from   $1.0{-}1.7\mathrm{g}/\mathrm{cm}^{3}$  . According to the Kanaya-Okayama formula

mentioned previously, high energy electrons penetrate  deeper into the low k material as compared to carbon  material. Deeper penetration into low k material will result  in more interactions with atoms so low k dielectrics deform  relatively more severe as compared to carbon material.  Hence, under high electron beam voltage, low-k dielectrics  shrink more in the bulk region as compared to the region  nearby the trench itself.

## 3.2. Electron Beam Current Impact on Low-k Dielectrics  

A second set of DOE experiments is used to study the single  impact of electron beam current on low-k materials. 5  samples have been taken from adjecent dies on the same  wafer. Same FIB machine is used to prepared the samples  using different electron beam current to search for sample  locations and cross-sectional viewing during FIB cut. The  electron beam voltage used for all the 5 samples are 30kV  with electron beam current setting at 100pA, 200pA, 400pA,  800pA and 1.6nA respectively. As shown in table 2, the  trench width increases while IMD thickness decreases with  the increading electron beam current with the same electron  beam voltage.

High electron beam current can cause such obvious low k  material deformation as the DOE experiment shown is due  to the significant amount of heat generated within the  material. When a high current electron beam is directed at a  material, it transfers energy to the material in the form of  kinetic energy from the electrons. This kinetic energy is then  converted into thermal energy leading to an increase in  temperature within the material [4]. The heating effect can  be significant especially in materials with low thermal  conductivity because they are less efficient at dissipating  heat. Low-K dielectric materials are typically designed to  have low thermal conductivity. That is because when they  are used in semiconductor manufacturing for signal

interference and power consumption reduction, low thermal  conductivity helps insulate the electronic components from  each other and minimize heat transfer between them. This is  essential to prevent excessive heat which was built up in  electronic devices. Thus, the heating effect of high current  electron beam on low-k dielectric materials is significant.  Low-k dielectrics have a low thermal conductivity which  makes them more susceptible to temperature increases when  exposed to high current electron beam. Low- k materials can  absorb more energy to heat up resulting in temperature rises  and material deformation.

## 3.3. Combination Impact of Electron Voltage and Electron  Beam Current on Low-k Dielectrics  

As discussed in the previous two sections, both electron  beam voltage and electron beam current have individual  impact on the extent of low-k dielectric material  deformation. Table 3 below summarizes the trench width  and IMD thickness measured for samples prepared by  different combination of electron beam current and voltage.

Table  3  Trench Width and IMD thickness measured for sample prepared by  different combination of electron beam current and voltage.

From this table, it is obvious that combination of low voltage  (2kV) and low current (100pA) leads to least deformation  with narrower trench width and thicker IMD thickness. High  voltage (30kV) and high current (1.6nA) will result in severe  trench width increment  $\sim\!42\%$   and IMD thickness shrinkage  around   $20\%$  . One side reduction of either voltage or current  will reduce certain deformation but still observed trench  width increment  $\sim17\%$   and IMD thickness shrinkage  $\sim13\%$    as compared to complete low dose method. As the trench  width and IMD thickness are critical to the fab for the  etching recipe tuning, such deformation will lead to  inaccurate measurements for further analysis. To provide  reliable trench characterization results, low dose sample  preparation method with combination of low voltage (2kV)  and low current (100pA) need to be used for the low-k

dielectrics etch profile check after conducting above DOE  experiments.

## IV CONCLUSION  

In this work, severe trench deformation on low-k  dielectric sample is observed using normal dose sample  preparation method. DOE experiments have been done to  investigate the individual impact of electron voltage and  electron current on such low-k dielectrics deformation. It is  observed that the low dose sample preparation method with  both low electron voltage at 2kV and electron current at  100pA minimizes the trench profile deformation and leads  to the most accurate measurements for further analysis.
