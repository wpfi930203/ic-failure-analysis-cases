## Enhancing Performance   and Reliability of MISGaN HEMT by Advanced Supercritical Fluid  Nitridation Technique  

Sheng-Yao Chou 1 , Ting-Chang Chang 2,3,4\* , Ya-Huan Lee 2 , Yu-Bo Wang 2 ,  Yu-Ching Huang 4 ,  Yi Huang Chen 1 , and  Tsung-Ming Tsai 1,\*     1  Department of Materials and Optoelectronic Science, National Sun Yat-sen University, Kaohsiung City 80424, Taiwan   2  Department of Physics, National Sun Yat-sen University, Kaohsiung 80424, Taiwan  3  Center of Crystal Research, National Sun Yat-sen University, Kaohsiung 80424, Taiwan   4  Advanced Technology Research, National Sun Yat-Sen University, Kaohsiung, 804, Taiwan  \*  Email address of corresponding authors: tcchang3708@gmail.com; tmtsai@faculty.nsysu.edu.tw

Abstract —In this study, an advanced supercritical fluid  nitridation (SCF-N) treatment is employed to enhance the  electrical properties and reliability of GaN metal-insulatorsemiconductor high electron mobility transistors (MISHEMTs). According to electrical measurements, the treated  devices exhibit higher ON-state current, lower gate leakage  current, a larger ON/OFF ratio, and reduced OFF-state  leakage under high drain bias and temperature. Additionally,  the threshold voltage   $(\mathbf{V_{TH}})$   of the device remains unchanged  after treatment. This provides significant evidence that SCFN effectively eliminates material vacancy defects and interface  defects between AlGaN and the insulator. The suppression of  current collapse is also confirmed through dynamic Ron  measurements. High temperature reverse bias stress (HTRB)  testing further validates the passivation of defects by SCF-N,  leading to improved reliability. Consequently, the SCF-N  technique demonstrates great potential for enhancing power  devices, paving the way for their effective and practical  application in the future.

Keywords—Supercritical fluid (SCF), High electron  mobility transistors (HEMT), Defects passivation.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION  

Gallium nitride (GaN)-based power devices are  regarded as promising candidates for the next generation of  power conversion technology. This is attributed to their  outstanding electronic characteristics, including a wide  band gap, high mobility, good thermal stability, and high  breakdown voltage. GaN high electron mobility transistors  (HEMTs) are considered vital electrical devices for power  applications such as wireless communication systems,  electric vehicles, and photovoltaic systems[1]-[3].

However, the high gate leakage current and dynamic   $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   still impact the reliability of GaN HEMTs. These  degradations may arise from material vacancy defects in  the AlGaN/GaN layer and interface defects between the  passivation layer and AlGaN. Therefore, various methods  have been proposed to mitigate these degradations and  enhance the performance of HEMTs [4]-[6]. GaN HEMTs  with a metal-insulator-semiconductor structure, known as  MIS-HEMTs, exhibit lower gate leakage and improved  reliability   compared   to   conventional   AlGaN/GaN  HEMTs[7]-[8]. Some studies have reported several plasma  treatments to enhance HEMT performance, reducing  current   collapse   and   improving   reliability[9]-[11].  Moreover, certain research endeavors have introduced  novel techniques such as proton irradiation treatment and  supercritical fluid nitridation (SCF-N) treatment to achieve

## II.   E XPERIMENT 

The process flow of the MIS-HEMT in this study is  illustrated in Fig. 1(a) and (b). First, a   $4~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   C-doped GaN  buffer layer with a concentration of  ${\sim}10^{18}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$   is fabricated  through   metal-organic   chemical   vapor   deposition  (MOCVD) on a silicon substrate. Subsequently, a 400 nm  undoped GaN layer and a 25 nm AlGaN layer are deposited  on the buffer layer, forming a two-dimensional electron gas  (2DEG) due to spontaneous and piezoelectric polarizations.  A thin GaN cap layer is then deposited on the AlGaN layer  to reduce surface leakage current and prevent a reverse  piezoelectric effect. Following this, a   $25\,\mathrm{\nm}$  thick SiN  insulating layer is deposited using plasma-enhanced  chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) to decrease gate  leakage current. After inductively coupled plasma etching  to define the source/drain region, a stack of Ti/Al/Ni is  deposited using a physical vapor deposition (PVD) process.  The device is annealed at   $850\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 30 s in an   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$    atmosphere to form Ohmic contact. Subsequently, a   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$    passivation layer is deposited through plasma-enhanced  chemical vapor deposition. Finally, contact holes are  patterned using dry etching, and Au metallization is applied  to complete the device structure. After the device

fabrication, some devices undergo SCF-N treatment. The  SCF system instrument is shown in Fig. 2. To determine the  optimized SCF conditions, devices are subjected to various  SCF conditions. The reaction gas pressure is set at 200 atm  to ensure the gas successfully enters the SCF state, and the  temperature of the process is adjusted accordingly. The  improvement in the GaN MIS-HEMT device is dependent  on the reaction temperatures, as shown in Fig. 3. Higher  reaction temperatures bring the reactant closer to the  supercritical fluid state, resulting in higher chemical  activity. Therefore, the optimized SCF treatment condition  (  $25\%$   ammonia water mixed with 200 atm CO 2 ,   $250^{\circ}\mathsf{C}$  , and  1 hour) is applied in this study. All electrical measurements  are conducted using an Agilent B1505A semiconductor  parameter analyzer..

## III.   R ESULT AND  D ISCUSSION 

Following the fabrication of three types of devices, the  electrical properties of the MIS-HEMT are investigated  through electric measurements. The characteristic curves of  both untreated and treated devices are demonstrated in Fig.  3(a). Notably, the threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{TH})}$   does not exhibit  a significant shift after SCF-N treatment, indicating that  SCF-N significantly affect the drift region of the MISHEMT. Furthermore, the ON/OFF ratio of the device  improves with an increase in SCF-N treatment temperature.  The output characteristics at   $\mathrm{V_{GS}=-15\sim-8~V}$  , as shown in  Fig. 3(b), exhibit an enhancement in ON-current after the  treatment. Regarding device leakage, Fig. 3(c) and (d)  demonstrate the gate leakage and high-temperature punchthrough leakage. The primary gate leakage pathway is  formed by electron tunneling leakage from the gate, where  electrons travel along or close to the AlGaN surface towards  the drain contact. This electron movement occurs through a  mechanism of hopping from one trap to another. After the   optimized SCF-N treatment, the gate leakage get reduced,  indicating lower AlGaN surface traps in treated devices. In  GaN HEMT devices, the generation of punch-through  leakage can be attributed to defects, along with intense  thermal and electric fields. After the optimized SCF-N  treatment, the defects in GaN layer are eliminated, leading  the punch-through leakage get suppressed. The presence of material defects can potentially limit the  performance of GaN HEMT devices. Defects located in the  GaN/AlGaN layer and AlGaN/insulator interface may lead  to electron trapping, causing a floating gate effect that  impacts the ON-state current. Dynamic Ron in High  HEMTs refers to the changes in the on-resistance of the  device   under   various   operating   conditions.   This  characteristic is crucial for understanding how the HEMT  will perform in real-world applications, particularly in  power electronics where devices are subject to frequent  switching and varying loads. Dynamic   $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   is influenced by  several factors including temperature, voltage stress, and the  material defects in device. As the device operates, the onresistance will increase due to trapping effects and thermal  stress, potentially impacting electrical properties and  reliability. Accurately supressing dynamic   $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   is essential  for optimizing device performance and reliability,  especially in high-frequency and high-power applications.  Moreover, the dynamic   $\mathrm{R_{on}}$   measurement can be used to  examine the trapping phenomenon attributed to interface  and vacancy defects in the GaN device structure [13-15].  Therefore, dynamic Ron measurement is proposed to  investigate the material quality after SCF-N treatment. In addition to enhancing the electrical properties of  GaN MIS-HEMTs, the reliability of both untreated and  treated devices is also examined. High-temperature reverse  bias stress (HTRB) in power devices is a critical reliability  test that evaluates the robustness of these semiconductors  under extreme conditions. This type of stress testing  involves exposing the power devices to elevated  temperatures while applying a reverse bias voltage,  simulating harsh operational environments. The aim is to  uncover any potential failure mechanisms that could affect  the device's performance, such as degradation of the  semiconductor material and gate dielectric. This testing is  essential for ensuring that HEMT devices can reliably  perform in applications where high power and high  temperature are prevalent, such as in automotive  electronics,   satellite   communications,   and   military  hardware. The HEMT devices under high-temperature  reverse bias stress may occur leakage generation and  charge trapping induced by high thermal and electric fields,  leading to the degradation of the device. In this study, an  off-state condition with a constant gate and stress voltage   $\mathrm{V_{DS}}=200$    V;   $\mathrm{V_{GS}=-15~V}$  ,   $\mathrm{T}=150~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  ) is employed to  examine the reliability of untreated and treated devices. ## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

In this study, the SCF-N treatment demonstrates the  ability to penetrate deeply into GaN MIS-HEMT and  improve device performance. Following the treatment, the  threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{TH})}$   remains unchanged, while the ONstate current, gate leakage current, ON/OFF ratio, and OFFstate leakage under high drain bias and temperature are all  enhanced. This improvement is attributed to the effective  elimination of material vacancy defects and interface  defects between AlGaN and the insulator by SCF-N.  Furthermore, both dynamic Ron measurement and HTRB  stress tests indicate that the defects passivated by SCF-N  lead to the suppression of current collapse and improved  reliability. Based on these results, SCF-N treatment emerges  as a promising technique for enhancing the electron  properties and reliability of GaN MIS-HEMT. This

advancement   has   significant   implications   for   the  development of GaN-based power devices in the near  future.
