## Influence of N 2  Gas Flow on the Reliability of Indium Zinc  Oxide Thin-Film Transistors  

Bin Wang 1, 2 , Feilian Chen 1, 2 , Guanming   $Z\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}^{1,2}$  , Lung-Chien Chen 3 ,  \* Meng Zhang 1, 2   1 College of Electronics and Information Engineering, Shenzhen University, Shenzhen 518060, China  2 State Key Laboratory of Radio Frequency Heterogeneous Integration, Shenzhen 518060, China  3 Department of Electro-Optical Engineering, National Taipei University of Technology, Taipei 10608, Taiwan, China  Phone: 0086 755 26925737,  \* Email:  zhangmeng@connect.ust.hk

Abstract —This study investigates the effects of different  nitrogen (N 2 ) gas flow rates on the back-channel doping of  indium zinc oxide (IZO) thin-film transistors (TFTs), focusing  on the impact on device performance and stability. By  introducing different   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rates into the back-channel, the  device performance, negative bias stress reliability, and negative  bias illumination stress reliability are respectively examined.  The results confirm that introducing an appropriate amount of  nitrogen into the back-channel could enhance device  performance and reliability. N 2 , acting as a substitute for  oxygen, efficiently infiltrates oxygen vacancies within the  oxygen-rich film, effectively suppressing the formation of  oxygen vacancies and thereby enhancing the stability of a-IZO  TFTs. This research contributes to understanding the potential  of N 2  doping as a promising avenue for improving the reliability  of MO TFTs.

Keywords—Negative bias stress, Negative bias illumination  stress, InZnO, thin-film transistors

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Metal oxide (MO) TFTs are widely employed in active  matrix (AM) displays due to their high mobility and stability  [1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]. However, the escalating demand for  high-quality display panels has imposed greater requirements  on the mobility and reliability of TFTs. Research has revealed  a trade-off between the mobility and reliability of MO TFTs  [3], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]. Therefore, ensuring the  stability of the device while simultaneously maintaining a  high level of mobility becomes of utmost importance.

Reliability studies of MO TFTs have predominantly  focused on the incorporation of metal cations with strong  binding energy to oxygen, such as gallium, tungsten, and tin,  to enhance reliability by suppressing oxygen vacancies [7],  [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22]. A structure  of IGZO TFTs based on atomic layer deposition of gate  insulator has been reported, where the strong affinity between  gallium and oxygen enhances the stability of the device [7],  [14], [15], [23]. Research has found that enhancing the etch  resistance of the device by incorporating varying amounts of  tungsten can improve its stability [16], [17], [18], [24]. By  employing ultraviolet ozone treatment, impurities related to  CO in the active layer were removed at room temperature,  resulting in MO TFTs with excellent bias stability [19], [20],  [21], [21], [25]. Utilizing anions to restrain the number of  oxygen vacancies is simpler and more cost-effective  compared to introducing metal cations [23], [26], [27],[23],  [28]. Research has found that introducing nitrogen (N 2 ) into  MO can improve the stability of the devices [29].  Nevertheless, research on the impact of introducing nitrogen  into the back-channel of indium zinc oxide (IZO) on device  reliability is scarce.

IZO, a promising oxide semiconductor, amalgamates the  excellent uniformity of amorphous silicon (a-Si) with the high  mobility advantage of low-temperature polycrystalline silicon  (LTPS), along with the benefits of low-temperature  fabrication processes and high transparency in the visible light  spectrum [30], [31], [32], [33], [34], [35]. However, IZO does  not excel in stability. Under illumination and bias stress,  significant changes in electrical characteristics may occur [22],  [25], [32], [36]. One primary reason is the sensitivity of MO  semiconductors to the environment, where defects in the  active layer may capture or release electrons, thereby altering  the carrier density [36], [37], [38], [39], [40]. Therefore,  reducing defects in the active layer is crucial for enhancing the  stability of IZO.

In this study, we investigate the effects of different  $\Nu_{2}$   gas  flow rates on the performance and reliability of IZO TFTs,  specifically focusing on their impact on device mobility,  negative bias stress (NBS) reliability, and negative bias  illumination stress (NBIS) reliability. With an increase in   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$    gas flow rate introduced into the back channel of IZO, the  device mobility initially increases and then decreases. In IZO  without   $\Nu_{2}$   introduction, significant threshold voltage shift   $(\Delta V_{t h})$   occurs under negative bias stress (NBS) and negative  bias illumination stress (NBIS) conditions. Conversely, in  IZO with   $\Nu_{2} $  introduction, improved NBS and NBIS  performances are observed with reduced   $\Delta V_{t h}$  . As the   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas  flow rate increases, the  $\Delta V_{t h}$   under NBS and NBIS conditions  decreases initially and then increases, while subthreshold  swing ( SS ) and on-state current   $(I_{o n})$   remain stable. Through  X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) testing, conclusive  evidence has been provided to demonstrate that   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$  can  effectively suppress the concentration of oxygen vacancies in  the IZO film, thereby enhancing the stability of the device.  This research contributes to understanding the potential of   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$    doping as a promising approach for enhancing the reliability  of MO TFTs.

## II.   E XPERIMENTAL 

Fig. 1 illustrates the device structure employed in this  study. Bottom gate IZO TFTs were fabricated on heavily  doped p-type silicon wafers. A   $100\;\mathrm{\nm}\;\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer was  deposited on the wafer surface to serve as the gate insulator.  A 30 nm thick IZO film was deposited on the gate insulator  layer through RF magnetron sputtering. An IZO target with a  composition of  $\mathrm{In}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}/\mathrm{ZnO}$   in a  $90{:}10\,\mathrm{wt}\%$   ratio was used. The  sputtering power was set at 60 W, and the argon/oxygen   $(\mathrm{Ar/O}_{2})$   ratio was maintained at 20/30 sccm. During the final  3 nm of the sputtering process,   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas was introduced with  flow rates ranging from 0 to 10 sccm. Subsequently, a 100  nm thick aluminum source/drain electrode was deposited  using DC magnetron sputtering in a pure Ar gas environment.  Finally, the devices were annealed for 10 minutes in ambient  air at   $200\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  .

The width/length (W/L) for the TFT channel is  $500\,\upmu\mathrm{m}/200$   $\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The field effect mobility   $\left(\mu_{F E}\right)$   is derived from the  following equation:

$$\upmu_{\mathrm{FE}}\ =\ \mathrm{(W/L)}\left(\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}/\big(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{ds}}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{ON}}\big)\right)$$
where   $g_{m}$   and    $C_{o x}$   represent the maximum device  transconductance at   $V_{D S}~=~0.1~\mathrm{~V~}$   and gate insulator  capacitance per unit area, respectively. The threshold voltage   $(V_{t h})$   is defined as the   $V_{g s}$   corresponding to  $I_{d s}$   of 10 nA when   $V_{d s}$    is equal to 0.1 V. The reliability of the device is measured  by comparing the extent of   $V_{t h}$   shift under NBS and NBIS  conditions, denoted as   $\Delta V_{t h}$  .   $\Delta V_{t h}$   represents the difference  between   $V_{t h}$   values on the transfer characteristic curve before  and after stress. For NBS, the stress   $V_{g}$   was fixed at   $-20\ \mathrm{V}$  with the source and drain grounded. For NBIS, the  measurements were conducted with   $V_{g}\!=\!-20\:\mathrm{V}$  , a wavelength  (λ) of 450 nm, and a power of   $100~\upmu\mathrm{W}$  . The source and the   drain were grounded. Measurements and stress tests of the  devices were conducted using a Keysight B1500A  semiconductor parameter analyzer connected to a dark probe  station. The XPS results were obtained using the Thermo  Scientific K-Alpha instrument.

## III.   R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSION 

The transfer curves of the devices prepared with different   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rates passed into the back channel are shown in Fig.  2a. The effect of different  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rates on  $\mu_{F E}$   and   $V_{t h}$   of the  devices are shown in Fig. 2b. As the  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rate gradually  increases, the device's transfer curve remains mostly  unchanged. Specifically, only a small negative shift in   $V_{t h}$   is  observed initially, followed by a subsequent positive   $V_{t h}$   shift,  while  μ FE  first increases and then decreases. At an   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow  rate of 5 sccm, the maximum  μ FE  reaches   $50.95\;\mathrm{cm}^{2}\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$  .

The device degradation of IZO TFTs under NBS is  subsequently investigated, as shown in Fig. 3. Without   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$  introduction into the back channel, a significant negative   $\Delta V_{t h}$  is observed as shown in Fig. 3a. After introducing  $\Nu_{2}$   gas flow  of 1 sccm, the NBS reliability is greatly improved, as shown  in Fig. 3b. When increasing   $\Nu_{2}$   gas flow to 3 sccm, the NBS  reliability degenerates compared to that under   $\Nu_{2}$   gas flow of  1 sccm, as shown in Fig. 3c. Further increasing in  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas flow  leads to worse NBS reliability, as shown in Fig. 3d and Fig.  3e. Fig. 3(f) displays the   $\Delta V_{t h}$   under different   $\Nu_{2}$   flow rates  under NBS conditions. Under NBS conditions, an increasing   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rate results in a trend where   $\Delta V_{t h}$   initially decreases  and then increases. The analysis reveals that the most  significant improvement is achieved when the  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rate is  set at 1 sccm, resulting in a reduction of   $\Delta V_{t h}$   from  $-3.8~\mathrm{V}$   to   $-2.4\;\mathrm{~V~}$  , corresponding to a remarkable improvement of   $36.84\%$  . Introducing an appropriate amount of   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   into the  back-channel improves the NBS stability of the device.

Several experiments were conducted to validate the reliability  of the findings.

In previous research on MO TFTs, the mechanism behind  NBS primarily involves the capture of charges by interface  layer defects between the gate insulator layer and the active  layer, leading to additional electric field generation and   $V_{t h}$  shift [32], [27], [41]. Due to the close proximity in radius  between nitrogen anions   $(1.48\,\mathrm{\AA})$   and oxygen anions (1.42 Å)  [42]. It is postulated that   $\Nu_{2}$   can act as a substitute for   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  .  When the  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   concentration reaches saturation,   $\Nu_{2}$   can further  repair oxygen vacancies and enhance stability [26]. Therefore,  introducing 1 sccm of   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas into the back-channel of IZO  results in improved NBS stability. However, if the   $\Nu_{2}$    concentration is excessively high, it may dilute the   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$    concentration, leading to a deterioration in stability. For  instance, when  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   is set at 10 sccm, the NBS reliability of the  devices actually worsens.

In most cases, TFTs are operated in illuminated  environments. The study of the impact of illumination on  TFTs is important. In illuminated environments, defects  within the active layer are stimulated to generate electron-hole  pairs due to energy absorption [19]. Additionally, under NBS,  electrons gain sufficient energy to transition to the conduction  band, forming free charge carriers, while holes are captured  by defects at the interface, leading to premature device turnon and negative  $V_{t h}$   shift [27], [43]. Therefore, NBIS reliablity  test was performed.

Fig. 4 illustrates the NBIS measurement results obtained in  IZO TFTs with different  $\Nu_{2}$   flow rates. Initial_1 is the transfer  curve without NBIS, and initial_2 is the transfer curve with  illumination but without NBS. After introducing 1 sccm   $\Nu_{2}$  into the back channel, it can be seen that   $\Delta V_{t h}$  decreases,  indicating that the device's sensitivity to light is reduced, as  shown in Fig. 4a and Fig. 4b. The  $\Delta V_{t h}$   with different  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow  rates in NBIS conditions is shown in Fig. 4f. Under the dual  driving force of NBS and illumination,   $\Delta V_{t h}$  is relatively  large. The most notable improvement in stability is observed  when the   $\Nu_{2}$  flow rate is set at 5 sccm. At this flow rate, the   $\Delta V_{t h}$   decreases from   $-14.9\,\mathrm{~V~}$   to   $-11.4\;\mathrm{~V~}$  , representing a  remarkable improvement of   $23.49\%$  . Similar to NBS results,  introducing an appropriate amount of   $\Nu_{2}$   can further reduce  oxygen vacancies, thereby enhancing the stability of NBIS.  After conducting multiple repetitions of experiments,  consistent patterns were observed, eliminating the possibility  of experimental randomness.

Introducing an appropriate amount of   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   can further  repair oxygen vacancies, reducing the   $V_{t h}$   shift under both  NBS and NBIS conditions, thereby enhancing the stability of  the device. To validate this speculation, we conducted XPS  characterization on IZO thin films prepared with different  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$  flow rates. Representative XPS spectra are presented in Fig.  5a, Fig. 5b, Fig. 5c, Fig. 5d and Fig. 5e, corresponding to   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$  flow rates of 0, 1, 3, 5, and 10 sccm, respectively. The O 1s  peak is decomposed into three subpeaks, O1, O2 and O3,  which are fitted using Lorentz-Gauss curves. These subpeaks,

centered at   $530.0\pm0.5~\mathrm{eV},$     $531\pm0.5\ \mathrm{eV},$   and   $532\pm0.5~\mathrm{eV},$    represent   metal-oxygen   (M-O)   bonds,   metal-oxygen  vacancies (M-V O ), and metal-hydroxyl (M-OH) bonds,  respectively [21]. Comparing the IZO film without   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas  flow to the films with 5 sccm and   $10\;\mathrm{sccm}\,\mathrm{N}_{2}$   gas flow, the MV O  peak decreases from  $24.50\%$   to   $19.27\%$   and increases from   $24.50\%$   to   $28.89\%$  , respectively. The percentage of various  peaks for different   $\Nu_{2}$   flow rates is shown in Fig. 5f. Among  them, the   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   peak represents oxygen vacancies, and its  percentage decreases initially and then increases with  increasing  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   flow rate. The minimum percentage is reached  at an  $\Nu_{2}$   flow rate of 5 sccm, which is   $19.27\%$  . Consistent with  the aforementioned observations,   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$  , as a substitute for  oxygen, effectively adjusts the number of oxygen vacancies,  thereby influencing the stability of IZO TFTs.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

In summary, this study investigates the influence of  $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas  flow rate on the reliability of the back-channel channel of IZO  TFTs. Through NBS and NBIS testing, the impact of   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas  flow rate on   $\Delta V_{t h}$   of the devices was assessed. The results  demonstrate that introducing an appropriate amount of   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   gas  can significantly improve the NBS performance of the  devices by   $36.84\%$   and the NBIS performance by   $23.49\%$  .  Therefore, rational control of   $\Nu_{2}$   flow rate during the  manufacturing process of IZO TFTs has been proven to be an  effective method to enhance the stability of the devices under  NBS and NBIS conditions, while maintaining high mobility.  These findings provide important insights into the feasibility  of nitrogen doping as a viable strategy for enhancing the  reliability of MO TFTs.
