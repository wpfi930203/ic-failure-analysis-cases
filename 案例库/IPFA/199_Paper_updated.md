## Ge Depth Profile and Concentration Analysis by  SIMS in Graded SiGe Epitaxial Film  

Yun Wang 1 , Han Wei Teo, Xintong Zhu, Kian Kok Ong, Ramesh Rao Nistala, Zhi Qiang Mo  1 Physical Analytical Lab, GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte Ltd, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, Singapore 738406  Phone :   $(+65)$   6670 1530, Email:  yun.wang@globalfoundries.com

Abstract   —   Germanium   dopant   distribution   and  concentration in graded SiGe film have been analyzed by SIMS.  Non-linearity in Ge and Si ion intensity response is noticed in  the high Ge at% matrix under the  $\mathbf{Cs}^{+}$   negative test condition,  but not with   $\mathbf{O}_{2}{}^{+}$   positive analysis. Since the profile shape is  crucial for the graded SiGe epitaxial film, the paper discusses  the gradual decreasing Ge depth profile measured under both   $\mathbf{Cs}^{+}$    negative mode and  $\mathbf{O}_{2}{}^{+}$    positive mode. The accurate Ge at%  quantification is achieved with XRD data as a reference.

Keywords — Ge   $a t\%,$  , Graded profile, SIMS, and XRD  quantification

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

As is well known, the introduction of Ge into the base  region of the Si bipolar junction transistor (BJT) to form the  SiGe heterojunction bipolar transistor (HBT) significantly  improves transistor performance at a modest increase in  processing complexity, and thus SiGe HBT technology has  generated worldwide interest for emerging digital, analog, and  RF applications. Previous work shows that the characteristics  of SiGe HBTs are highly sensitive to the Ge concentration and  profile shape. Therefore, accurate analysis of Ge in the film  becomes a key factor [1].

SIMS (Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry) technique is  selected to characterize Ge performance in SiGe.  SIMS is  well-suited for the semiconductor industry because of its  combination of high sensitivity with good depth and lateral  resolution. The areas of interest for SIMS in semiconductors  include materials research, development of new processes,  and process control such as implantation, diffusion, and  contamination troubleshooting.

The advanced technology processes require precise  measurement to meet the functionality of devices. For this  study, the high depth resolution and accurate quantification  are necessary for the graded SiGe film analysis. Due to the  relatively small test area   $(100\,\upmu\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{x}\,\,100\,\upmu\mathrm{m})$   and thin films in  the nanometre range, a slow sputter rate is essential. To  address this, the SIMS tool is configured with low-impact  energy and low beam current.

SIMS ionization efficiencies are highly dependent on  instrumental setup.   ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$   bombardment increases the yield of  positive ions and   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$   bombardment increases the yield of  negative ions. For the group Ⅳ element Ge, a  $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$   source with  high mass resolution (HMR) is preferred. However, there is  boron doping in the HBT film concurrently ，  to detect boron,  the   ${\mathrm{O}_{2}}^{+}$   source is a better choice in terms of ion yield. If Ge  and B analysis is under 2 different modes, time-consuming  will be a concern for the fab manufacturing process control. In  fact, it is feasible to analyze the Ge depth profile using   ${\mathrm{O}_{2}}^{+}$  bombardment with a good ion yield. The benefit of   ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$  positive ion analysis is B and Ge can be measured  simultaneously. In this paper, whether to use negative ions or  positive ions for HBT film evaluation will be discussed.

Fig. 1 shows the TCAD (Technology Computer-Aided  Design) Ge   $\mathrm{{at\%}}$   and B concentration profile simulation. The  TCAD Ge curve indicates a steep slope during epitaxial  growth on the substrate, followed by a plateau at the highest   $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   and a gradual decrease to   $0\%$  . Simultaneously, a boron  doping profile appears in the SiGe film. The SIMS depth  profile is important for accurately reflecting the performance  of germanium and boron. The   $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   quantification is  determined by the average value within the steady-state  region.

It is well known that X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD) is a  non-destructive method offering comprehensive insights into  the crystallographic structure, chemical composition, and  physical properties of materials. In the present context, XRD  data will be used as a reference for quantifying   $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   in SIMS  analysis.

## II.   E XPERIMENTAL DETAILS 

A blanket wafer is prepared by epitaxy SiGe growth on a  Si substrate with a Si cap, the boron is doped in the SiGe layer  concurrently. The Si cap effectively avoids surface effects at  the transient regime during SIMS testing.

All the SIMS depth profiling is carried out using the  CAMECA IMS Wf instrument. The primary   ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$   or   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$   beam  accelerating voltage and the sample high voltage are set to 4   $\mathrm{keV}$   and   $3\;\mathrm{keV}$   respectively, so that the net impact energy is 1  keV at   $60^{\circ}$   incidence with positive or negative ion detection.  The primary beam at 2 -3 nA current is scanned over a raster  area of 3x of the analysis area to address the crater effect.

The XRD equipment, JV Delta-X, from erstwhile Jordan  Valley (now merged with Bruker), operates at   $40\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{\kV}}$   and  40mA using Cu Κα1radiation with X-ray wavelength   $\lambda{=}1.54\mathrm{\AA}$  . HRXRD measurements of (004) Bragg peaks are  measured in ω-2θ scan mode using a Ge (004) channel cut  crystal at the source side. The Channel Cut Collimator is  specially designed to provide high resolution and high  intensity when combined with a parabolic graded mirror for  high-quality epi-layers. Resolution is 8", as measured using  the Si (004) rocking curve, with an open detector (double-axis

mode). A step size of 10 to 20 arcsec is adopted for accuracy  and efficiency.

The ion yield of a particular element  in a binary elemental matrix system is generally higher with  increasing amount of the elemental composition. Quantifying the Ge  ${\mathrm{at}}\%$   is using a known Ge concentration  sample (obtained through HRXRD) to determine the  corresponding k value. As in SiGe compounds   $\mathrm{{XSi=1\cdotXGe}}$  ,  germanium concentration is given by relation [2].

$${\displaystyle{\frac{{}^{70}\mathrm{GeV}^{-}}{{}^{30}\mathrm{Si}^{-}}}=\frac{k[X_{\mathrm{Ge}}]}{[1-X_{\mathrm{Ge}}]}},~~~X_{\mathrm{Ge}}=\frac{{}^{70}\mathrm{Ge}^{-}/{}^{30}\mathrm{Si}^{-}}{\left({}^{70}\mathrm{Ge}^{-}/{}^{30}\mathrm{Si}^{-}\right)+k}$$
After point-to-point normalizing the Ge intensity to Si and  calculating the Ge concentration by using the k value, a graded  Ge depth profile is observed in Fig. 3. It aligns well with the  TCAD simulation. The Ge ion yield at the SiGe/substrate  interface as shown in Fig. 2 may not be increasing  proportional to the   $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   as it may be caused by the matrix  effect. This phenomenon is conformed to the Ref [3]. The Ge  ion intensity showed increasing at the low   $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   but did not  show increasing beyond   $20\%$   Ge. The phenomenon of the   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$  negative mode profile results of this study matched with the  explanation in Ref [3]. Therefore, the authentic Ge  ${\mathrm{at}}\%$   profile  can be obtained by normalizing to Si in  $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$   negative analysis.

Basically, SIMS analysis quantification relies highly on  the RSF value obtained from an appropriate standard sample.  We choose XRD data as a reference because, in a diffraction  experiment, the   $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   can be measured with high precision  (better than  $0.2\%$   repeatability in  $\mathrm{Ge}\%$   can be achieved on JV  Delta-X). ## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

The analysis of Ge in graded SiGe film can be conducted  by SIMS under both   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$   and  ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$   approaches. With   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$  , the  Ge intensity profile is distorted at the higher Ge % range,  requiring normalization to Si intensity for an accurate profile.  On the other hand, doped boron in SiGe needs to be tested in   ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$   source positive mode separately. While using   ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$  , Ge   ${\mathrm{at}}\%$   and B can be detected concurrently, leading to a   $30\%$    data points loss. Therefore, for the thin films, to ensure  sufficient data points,   $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}$   negative ions for Ge, and   ${\mathrm{O}}_{2}^{+}$
