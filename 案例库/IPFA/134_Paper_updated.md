## Degradation of Off-State Performance and Its  Reliability of NMOS with Deep N Well Induced by  Plasma Charging   

Hui Zheng 1,2 , Menghua Wang 1,2 , Zhimin Huang 2 , Leqi  $\mathrm{Li}^{2}$  , Zhikun Liu 2 , Niuyi Sun 2 , Jian Wang 2 1 State Key Laboratory of Mobile Network and Mobile Multimedia Technology, Shenzhen, China  2 Sanechips Technology Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China  Phone : (+86)18829290168, Email: wang.menghua@sanechips.com.cn

Abstract —Deep N well (DNW) can provide good noise  isolation and is widely used in RF circuits. However, it is usually  susceptible to plasma charging during manufacturing. This  study presents the off-state drain leakage, rather than gate  leakage that is traditionally focused on, can be much  deteriorated in DNW devices in comparison with those without  DNW, even if the antenna ratio of the former is much smaller. A  new plasma-induced damage (PID) mechanism is proposed  which concerns well injection of hot plasma charges during  manufacturing as a result of DNW isolation, followed by  generation of much more precursors of both donor-type and  acceptor-type interfacial states. It also results in larger  degradation of the on-state performance, as well as unusual  pinning or quick increasing of the off-state leakage in DNW  devices under HCI stress. The research may contribute to full  understanding and effective detection of the impact of PID on  the performance and reliability of DNW devices and facilitate  secure usage of them.

Index Terms -- deep n well, reliability, plasma-induced damage,  off-state leakage.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Deep N well (DNW) is widely used for noise isolation and  can provide additional freedom of device modulation with  independent body bias [1]. It is usually considered the  transistors with DNW possess comparable DC performance  and reliability with those normal devices, since the local  dimension and doping profile stay very close. However, it has  been demonstrated the latch-up immunity may be lowered due  to parasitic PNPN and PNPNPN structures formed based on  DNW-enclosed NMOS [2], [3]. Moreover, it was reported  DNW may be susceptible to plasma induced well charging  during manufacturing [4]-[6], and thus the local transistors, as  well as those remote transistors being connected to the well  diffusion area, may be easily damaged, which was recently  evidenced by increased initial gate leakage and increased  threshold voltage shift under reliability stress [7]. As a result,  not only plasma charges collected through gate antenna, but  also those through the antennas connected on other terminals,  could contribute to plasma-induced damage (PID). Therefore,  sufficient characterization of the impact of DNW on  transistors’ performance and reliability is necessary, and new  design constraints need to be developed for secure usage of  them [8]. However, previous researches were focused mainly

on the impact of PID on the gate oxide leakage, as well as the  on-state performance and its reliability, with rare devoted to  the initial off-state performance and its reliability.

In this paper, both the on-state and off-state performance,  as well as their degradation under HCI stress, of NMOS with  and without DNW is characterized and compared. It is found  there’s a considerable impact of PID on the off-state  performance and its reliability of the DNW devices, even if its  oxide quality stays weakly affected, which is opposite to that  in the devices without DNW. A new PID mechanism is  proposed which involves well injection of hot plasma charges  and following generation of interfacial states and their  precursors.

## III.    R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSION   

Firstly, the gate leakage and breakdown reliability are  displayed and analyzed in order to demonstrate the impact of  PID on the performance of the gate oxide. Fig. 2a shows the  gate leakages of NMOS_DNW1 and NMOS under normal  device operation voltage (0.9V) and a high stress voltage  (2.2V). It can be seen the normalized gate leakages of  NMOS_DNW1 manifest to be about 1.6 times smaller than  that of NMOS without DNW under both voltages, which may  be attributed to its much smaller gate AR. As well known, to  reduce gate AR can mitigate plasma-induced damage which  may be electron traps generated in the oxide in this case, and  thus the trap-assisted-tunnelling gate leakage could stay lower  in NMOS_DNW1. Moreover, the normalized time-to-failure  (TTF) distribution of TDDB of NMOS_DNW1 is also much  larger than that of NMOS, as shown in Fig. 2b, which is  consistent with the observation on the gate leakage. Therefore,  the gate oxide quality of NMOS_DNW1 should be better than  that of NMOS and would most probably have not been much  influenced by the plasma charges under a gate AR of a such  moderate level (\~1e4). In fact, to increase the gate AR by 3  times still can’t lead to considerable shift of the gate leakage,  as shown in Fig. 2c, where the gate leakages of  NMOS_DNW1 and NMOS_DNW2 are compared with fullmap data and manifest to almost coincide with each other with  no tails. It further confirms the influence of plasma charges  collected from the gate antenna is too weak to deteriorate the  oxide performance in NMOS with DNW in our case. It may  be attributed to small electrical thickness of the gate oxide  (\~1.5 nm in this case) which could facilitate charge tunnelling  through the gate oxide, and thus mitigate charge (potential)  accumulation and reduce charge trapping in the gate oxide.

It is  noticeable the values of the drain leakage of NMOS_DNW1  can reach several milliamperes at 0.9V which are of the same  order of magnitude with the on-state currents shown in Fig. 3a.  It indicates the initial off-state performance is indeed  significantly deteriorated in NMOS with DNW, rather than  that without DNW. Obviously, this is contrary to what may be  expected since the former has much smaller ARs on all the  four terminals than the latter, and thus should have collected  much less plasma charges during manufacturing. In our  opinion, it can be explained by the essential role of DNW in  PID in this case. As mentioned in the introduction part, the  enwrapping of DNW(NW) around the active transistor would  remarkably reduce the discharging of the plasma charges

collected by the metal antennas [4]-[8], because it forms a  PNP (P_well-DNW(NW)-P_sub.) structure between the Buck  and substrate. Consequently, much more charges may have  been accumulated in the P well of NMOS with DNW than that  without DNW, since the latter could readily dissipate the  charges to the substrate. That is to say, the potential of the P  well might have been raised to a higher level in NMOS with  DNW isolation during manufacturing. Herein, it is considered  such well potential could then push the subsequently injected  plasma charges toward the Si surface and accelerate them,  with some energetic charges colliding with the transistor and  thus resulting in some damage in NMOS with DNW. A  schematic description of the proposed well injection of hot  plasma charges is shown in Fig. 4, which is in fact analogous  to substrate injection of hot carriers by an intentional substrate  bias [9]. Also shown is a possibly beneficial consequence that  the accumulation of the gate charges might be somewhat  alleviated as a result of the build-up of the well potential,  which may be another reason why the gate oxide is lowly  affected in DNW devices. Since the ARs on the Drain and  Source are relatively small in NMOS with DNW compared to  the other two terminals, and to increase the gate AR  (NMOS_DNW2) by 3 times again has not leaded to further  degradation of the off-state performance (shown in Fig. 3b), it  is speculated the responsible plasma charges for the PID in  this case may be mainly those collected through the Buck  antenna.

## IV.    C ONCLUSION   

In summary, the performance and reliability of NMOS  devices with and without deep N well are characterized and  compared. It is found the off-state leakage of the DNW device  is significantly increased although its AR is much smaller,  while its gate leakage and gate oxide quality stays weakly  impacted. Besides, its HCI performance is also deteriorated  which manifests both on-state current degradation and offstate leakage increasing. The underlying mechanism may be  generation of precursors of both donor-type and acceptor-type  interfacial states during manufacturing as a result of well  injection of hot plasma charges when there’s DNW isolation,  while it is mainly generation of oxide traps due to gate  injection of plasma charges when there’s no DNW isolation.  The research demonstrates the necessity of careful evaluation  of the off-state performance and reliability which may be  more relevant than the gate leakage for PID of DNW devices  with a thin gate oxide. And designers should reduce the  antenna ratio on all the four terminals of the DNW devices,  rather than only tailoring that on the Gate.
