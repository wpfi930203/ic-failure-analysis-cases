## Enhancing the Thermal Conductivity of Graphenebased Thermal Interface Materials by Polyimide  Fiber Intercalation  

Xu Ran 1 , Yuhan Sun 1 , Xing  $\mathrm{{Wu}}^{1,2^{*}}$  , Hengchang Bi 1,3\*

1 In Situ Devices Center, School of Integrated Circuits, East China Normal University, 500 Dongchuan Road, Shanghai, China

2 State Key Laboratory of Infrared Physics, Shanghai Institute of Technical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences

3

Chongqing Key Laboratory of Precision Optics, Chongqing Institute of East China Normal University, Chongqing 401120, China

Email: xwu@cee.ecnu.edu.cn; hcbi@cee.ecnu.edu.cn

Abstract —Temperature is an important factor affecting the  reliability of electronic components. High temperature  environments have a direct negative impact on the performance  and lifetime of electronic devices. Graphene, a remarkable twodimensional carbon material renowned for its exceptional inplane thermal conductivity properties, faces a significant  challenge due to the high phonon transport barrier at the van  der Waals interface between its layers. This poses a limitation  for meeting the heat dissipation requirements of cutting-edge  electronic devices. In this study, a graphene composite film with  polyimide (PI) fiber intercalation is presented. Graphene oxide  (GO) and PI composite films were obtained by solution blending  of PI fibers and GO. Subsequently, the GO/PI film was  graphitised and calendered at high temperature to obtain the  dense   $\mathbf{g{-}G O/P I}$   film. The PI fiber intercalation constructs a  three-dimensional thermal conductivity network that enables  the g-GO/PI film to have high in-plane thermal conductivity  (1406 W m -1  K -1 ) and through-plane thermal conductivity (21.3   ${\bf W}\,\,\,{\bf m}^{-1}\,\,\,{\bf K}^{-1})$  ). The exceptional thermal conductivity of the  graphitized GO/PI film   $\scriptstyle(\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{GC}/\mathbf{PI})$   as a thermal interface  material confirms its superior thermal management capability  compared to copper foil. This presents an enticing prospect for  the integration of advanced thermal management systems in  future high-performance flexible electronic devices.

Keywords—thermal   interface   materials,   through-plane  thermal conductivity, graphene thermal conductive film

## I.    INTRODUCTION  

In the ever-evolving landscape of electronic devices, the  efficient management of heat generation has become  paramount to ensure their stable and reliable operation[1],  [2], [3]. The temperature at which these devices operate  directly impacts their reliability, as a mere   $10^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   rise can  reduce their lifespan by half[4]. In this context, thermal  interface materials have emerged as vital components in  microelectronics and microsystems, addressing the challenge  of effective heat dissipation within devices. These materials  facilitate the transfer of heat between the heat-generating unit  and the heat sink interface. For optimal heat transfer  efficiency, thermal interface materials must possess not only  high in-plane thermal conductivity but also effective throughplane thermal conductivity. Graphene, an extraordinary twodimensional carbon material, boasts a remarkable thermal  conductivity ranging from 3000 to   $5000\ \mathrm{W\m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$   due to its  unique lattice arrangement and structure[5], [6]. Given its  outstanding thermal properties, graphene stands out as a  prime candidate for enhancing heat transfer efficiency and  elevating the performance of next-generation thermal  management technologies.

Despite its exceptional thermal conductivity in the lateral  direction, graphene faces limitations when it comes to

vertical heat transfer due to the selective propagation of  phonons along specific directions in its lattice[7], [8].  Consequently, the through-plane thermal conductivity of  graphene films is relatively low, typically below  $4\:\mathrm{W}\:\mathrm{m}^{\cdot1}\:\mathrm{K}^{\cdot1}$  [9], [10], [11]. In recent developments, researchers have  taken on the challenge of enhancing the thermal conductivity  of graphene paper through surface modifications. For  instance, Jiang et al. successfully prepared a graphene  composite film by incorporating carbon nanorings between  graphene sheets using catalyst assistance[12]. However,  despite the successful embedding of carbon nanorings within  the graphene layers, the resulting film only exhibited a  through-plane thermal conductivity of 5.8 W m -1  K -1 .In  another approach, Lin et al. achieved improved results by  introducing a silicon source between graphene layers and  subsequently growing SiC nanorods via a carbothermal  reduction reaction. This led to a longitudinal thermal  conductivity of   $10.9\ \mathrm{W}\ \mathrm{m}^{\cdot1}\ \mathrm{K}^{\cdot1}$   for the composite film[13].  Moreover,   efforts   have   been   made   to   synthesize  monodisperse metal nanoparticles on the surface of graphene  nanosheets assembled into thin films[14]. While this  approach showed promise, the contact area between the  nanoparticles and the graphene sheet layer remained limited,  thus   restricting   the   overall   through-plane   thermal  conductivity.

In this work, we present a graphene composite film with  polyimide fiber intercalation. The process involved mixing PI  fibers with a Graphene oxide (GO) aqueous solution to create  the GO/PI film. Subsequently, the GO/PI films were  subjected to graphitization and calendering processes,  resulting in the formation of dense g-GO/PI films. The  addition of PI fibers through intercalation created a threedimensional thermal conductivity network within the gGO/PI films. This unique network structure contributed to the  films' exceptional ultra-high thermal conductivity in both  planar and vertical plane directions. The three-dimensional  thermal network constructed by PI intercalation led to a 6- fold increase in the through-plane thermal conductivity of  graphene films.

## II.    EXPERIMENTAL   DETAILS  

Graphene material is characterized by high thermal  conductivity, small thermal expansion coefficient of crystal  surface, low thermal resistance and light weight, which has  attracted extensive interest from material research scholars.  In this work,   PI fibers were introduced into a GO aqueous  solution, followed by the preparation of large-sized GO/PI  films using a scratch coating method. This method offers  advantages such as scalable raw material preparation, a  straightforward process, and the ability to form films over a  large area. The final step involved obtaining g-GO/PI films

through high-temperature graphitization and calendering  processes.

## A.   Synthesis and Characterization of Graphene Oxide  

Expandable graphite flakes with an expansion ratio of 270  to 350 and a particle size of 50 mesh were purchased from  Merck KGaA. In the preparation of GO,   $10\mathrm{~g~}$   of expanded  graphite flakes were mixed with   $6~\mathrm{g}$   of NaNO 3  and   $400~\mathrm{mL}$  of concentrated   $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$  .The mixture was then subjected to  high-energy mechanical stirring and ultrasonication to  promote both the exfoliation and oxidation of the graphite  layers. Following this,   $15~\mathrm{g}$   of   $\mathrm{KMnO_{4}}$   was gradually added  to the mixture while continuing to stir for a duration of 1 hour.  This process facilitated the introduction of oxygencontaining functional groups onto the graphene flakes,  resulting in the formation of GO.Afterward, the mixture was  precipitated at room temperature. Centrifugation and washing  with deionized water were performed to remove impurities  and residual chemicals. To obtain a homogeneous dispersion  of graphene oxide, the resultant GO suspension was  centrifuged at 8,000 rpm for 20 minutes, effectively  eliminating any remaining particles.

## B.   Fabrication of GO/PI films   

Graphene films are assembled by stacking twodimensional sheet layers. The high phonon transfer barrier at  the van der Waals interface of the lamellae reduces the  thermal conductivity of graphene films in the vertical  direction. It is expected that the thermal conductivity of the  film in the vertical direction can be improved by inserting  one-dimensional fibers between the graphene lamellae to  construct thermal conduction pathways. PI, as an organic  polymer, possesses a notable level of orientation. Through  the process of pressurized carbonization and graphitization, it  becomes feasible to obtain graphite films that are  characterized by remarkable high crystallinity and optimal  orientation.   To   improve   the   through-plane   thermal  conductivity of graphene film, PI fibers were introduced,  creating interlayer heat dissipation channels. PI fibers with a  diameter of about   $2\,\mathrm{\upmum}$   and a length of   $1.3\,\mathrm{\mm}$   were  proportionally added to an aqueous GO solution of   $10\,\mathrm{mg/ml}$  and stirred for 3 h for homogeneous dispersion. The dispersed  solution was then scraped onto a mixed cellulose filter  membrane with a pore size of   $0.45\,\mathrm{\upmu}$   and dried at room  temperature for 5 h. After drying, the GO/PI film could be  easily peeled off from the filter membrane. By adjusting the  concentration of GO and the amount of PI reference, GO/PI  films with different thicknesses could be obtained.

## C.   Graphitization and mechanical calendering of GO/PI  films.   

The GO/PI films were sandwiched between two polished  graphite plates, protected by argon gas and held at   $2~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{min}$    up to   $1200\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 2h, followed by annealing at   $5~^{\circ}\mathrm{C/min}$   up  to   $3000\ \mathrm{~}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 1h. During the high-temperature thermal  reduction of GO, the rapid decomposition of oxygencontaining functional groups inside GO generates gasses  leading to porous structures in the GO lamellae. After  annealing, the GO/PI films were sandwiched between two  smooth tungsten metal sheets and pressed with hydraulic  equipment at 150 MPa for 2 h to obtain the final dense films.

The chemical composition of GO and g-GO/PI films was  also analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) in  Fig.3. Oxygen-containing groups in graphene oxide form  $\mathfrak{s p}^{3}$    hybridization with carbon atoms in graphene, which destroys  the   $\mathfrak{s p}^{2}$   conjugated skeleton structure of graphene, leading to  a significant decrease in the thermal conductivity of  graphene. In order to transform the  ${\mathfrak{s p}}^{3}$   structure of graphene  oxide into  $\mathfrak{s p}^{2}$   structure and restore the original high thermal  conductivity of graphene, high-temperature annealing is an  effective and easy-to-control method. After high-temperature  graphitisation, the O1s peaks of the films diminish to almost  negligible levels. The C/O element ratio of g-GO/PI film  reaches 98, which is 36 times that of GO film. The sharp   $\scriptstyle\mathrm{C=C/-C}$   peak of the g-GO/PI film indicates restoration of the  sp2-bonded carbon lattice structure.

## B.   In-plane thermal conductivity of the g-GO/PI film  

Thermal conductivity, thermal diffusion coefficient, and  specific heat capacity are crucial thermal properties that play  a significant role in characterizing materials. In this study, we  focused on measuring the heat diffusion coefficient of the  material using the laser flash method at room temperature   $(25^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$  . To determine the specific heat capacity of the films,  we conducted a comparative analysis using a technique called  differential scanning calorimetry. The density of the films  was obtained using the mass-volume method. By multiplying  these three indicators, we were able to calculate the thermal  conductivity of the film. For the heat diffusion coefficient  measurement, we utilized a laser thermal conductivity meter  model LFA447. The graphitized thin film material was cut  into   $25~\mathrm{mm}$   diameter discs and placed within the in-plane  mold of the thermal conductivity meter. To measure the  longitudinal heat diffusion coefficient, the films were cut into  squares with sides measuring   $10\:\mathrm{\mm}$  . To determine the  specific heat capacity of the films, we employed a  Differential Scanning Calorimeter DSC204F1. Sapphire was  used as a reference object to establish the specific heat  capacity of the graphitized films at  $25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  .

## C.   Through -plane thermal conductivity of the g-GO/PI film  

Although the thermal conductivity of graphene films is  high in the in-plane direction, it is low in the vertical direction  due to the high phonon transfer barrier at the interlayer van  der Waals interface. By inserting fibers, a three-dimensional  thermal network can be constructed to reduce thermal  resistance   and   enhance   the   through-plane   thermal  conductivity of the film as shown in Fig. 6.

## IV.    SUMMARY  

In summary, we developed a fiber-inserted 3D hybrid gGO/PI thermally conductive film with excellent thermal  conductivity in both in-plane   $(1406\;\mathrm{W}\;\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{-}1}\;\mathrm{K}^{\mathrm{-}1})$  ) and  through   -plane   $(21.\dot{3}\mathrm{~W~m^{\cdot1}~K^{\cdot1}})$  ) orientations. The excellent thermal  conductivity is attributed to the good orientation of the PI  fibers and graphene nanosheets in the 3D structure, as well as

the lattice repair under high temperature conditions. The  ultra-high in-plane thermal conductivity significantly  enhances the thermal conductivity of the films compared to  copper foils as thermal interface materials. In addition, the  three-dimensional thermal network constructed by the PI  intercalation layer increases the  through -plane thermal  conductivity of the film by a factor of 6. The g-GO/PI films  offer a viable option for future thermal management of highpower flexible devices due to their flexible and bendable  thermal interface material and their super-metallic heat  dissipation properties.
