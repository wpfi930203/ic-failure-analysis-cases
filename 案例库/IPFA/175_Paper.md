## Identification Of Recovering  I DSS  Failure Mode Via  Novel Comprehensive Failure Analysis Technique  for MOSFET device  

Izhar Helmi Ahmad, Nazirul Izzat Bin Mohd Arifen, Mohd Fakhrul Mohamad,   Abdul Rahim Sultan Ahmad, Mohd Fakhrul Bin Mohamad,     Failure Analysis Laboratory, Nexperia Malaysia Sdn. Bhd.,     PT 12687, Tuanku Jaafar Industrial Park, Kawasan Perindustrian Tuanku Jaafar, 71450 Seremban, Negeri Sembilan, Malaysia.  Phone :   $(+60)$   676 6299, Email: Izhar.Helmi.Ahmad@nexperia.com

Abstract — Failure analysis (FA) plays an important  role in the semiconductor industry as the process technology  and new packaging continuously improve. Device recovery is a  growing challenge in the FA field. In this paper, a novel FA  method is introduced using combination of electrical  characteristics, a precise fault isolation and a Focused Ion Beam  (FIB) together with mechanical cross section. By employing this  technique, it helps to resolve the issue of failure recovery and  efficiently identify the precise root cause of the failure  mechanism. In the deficiency of proper FA steps perform on the  recoverable drain to source leakage failure might have the  tendency to miss the evidence and provide an impropriate FA  result or conclusion. The conclusion can be misleading if the  exact root cause is not properly addressed and corrected. The  chance of failure reoccurrence is very high. Two separate  investigations with varying failure mechanisms will discuss the  above-mentioned techniques. Significant results of wafer  related issue and foreign material issue were able to identify by  applying this novel FA technique. In summary, this work  introduces a novel comprehensive failure analysis that enables  the identification of a device's failure mechanism. Hence, the  device recovery issue is resolved, and the production team is  equipped with a precise failure mechanism to investigate the  cause of the failure.

Keywords—Power MOSFET, I DSS  Failure Mode,  Recovery Issue, Photoemission, Lock-in thermograph.

## I.    INTRODUCTION 

A Metal-Oxide Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor  (MOSFET) is a semiconductor device used to amplify and  switch electrical signals in electronic devices [1]. It has  become the most important fundamental building block for  creating integrated circuits and analogue devices, due to its  superb properties. MOSFET has three terminals, which are  the gate, source, and drain [2]. In a MOSFET’s device  structure, the drain terminal is located at the bulk silicon, and  the source tub is diffused inside the channel well. Aluminum  metallization connects these cells. The final passivation  layer is used to protect the underlying structures and improve  device reliability. Upon applying voltage to the gate  terminal, the electric field draws the majority carrier to the  channel's surface, allowing it to flow horizontally from the  source through the channel and vertically to the drain  substrate [3].

The MOSFET characteristic curve illustrates the correlation  between the Drain Current (I D ) and the Drain-to-Source  Voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{DS})}$   under various Gate-to-Source Voltage (V GS )  conditions.[4] These regions are divided into three  categories: the ohmic/linear region, the saturation/active  region, and the cutoff region. The Ohmic region, also known  as the constant resistance region. In this region, V DS  is  directly proportional to   $\mathrm{I_{D}}$  depending on the Gate-to-Source  Voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{GS}})$   applied. The second region is saturation/active  region and known as a constant-current region. In this  region, the  $\mathrm{V_{DS}}$   is higher than the V GS - V GS(TH).  which allows  the electron to spread through a broader region. The cutoff  region occurs when   $\mathrm{V_{GS}}$   is much lower than the Gate-toSource Threshold Voltage   $\mathrm{V_{GS(TH)}}$  .   Under these conditions,  the MOSFET behaves as an open circuit.

In a MOSFET, there are four important parameters needed to  be measured and analyzed. These are I DSS , BV DSS , I GSS  and  V GS(TH)  [1]. I DSS , is a critical parameter in the evaluation of  MOSFETs. This parameter measures the amount of current  flowing between the drain and source terminals when the gate  is shorted to the source, typically grounded. Conducting I DSS testing is imperative to assess the leakage current  characteristics of MOSFET devices, as excessive leakage can  lead to performance degradation or even device failure.

The occurrence of I DSS  failures can be attributed to various  factors, with two main root causes commonly identified.  There are two major failure mechanisms that result in the I DSS leakage: external foreign particles that bridge the drain and  source terminals, such as silver migration, excessive solder,  or the presence of external contamination within the molding  compound. Another failure mechanism related to the wafer  contributes to I DSS  leakage.

Despite the severity of IDSS failures, both wafer-related  defects and physical defects across drain-to-source terminals  tend to recover under specific conditions. Specifically,  exposure to high temperatures during traditional failure  analysis procedures can temporarily alleviate these defects.  This presents a significant challenge in accurately identifying  and analyzing I DSS  failures, as defects may become less  pronounced or transiently mitigated during testing.

## II.   METHODOLOGY   

Typically, in a conventional failure analysis flow, a  chemically decapsulation analysis approach is preferred Fig.1  illustrates the conventional failure analysis flow. Initially,  non-destructive analysis is performed to detect any internal  and external package anomalies. Subsequently, electrical  verification validates the reported failure. Once the failure  mode is confirmed, the failed sample undergoes chemical  decapsulation to expose the internal structure following the  non-destructive failure analysis procedure. However, this  chemical decapsulation process induces high temperatures in  the sample. The results are not always favorable and often  show either total recovery of the device or a defect artifact  that remains after washing away evidence. Cosmetic  anomalies, especially those that readily melt or wash away  when attempting to remove the clip, make it even harder to  prove their existence.

Since the I DSS  failure mode has a tendency to recover, a  backside FIB cut, or mechanical cross-section cut approach  was used to uncover the failure mechanism and prevent  device recovery issues. The failed sample underwent either  topside or backside polishing based on the electrical  characteristics' behavior. For the unit showing improvement  in IDSS leakage during electrical characterization, the failed  sample was backside polished, thinning down the silicon  substrate in preparation for the backside fault isolation  process. Micro-probing was conducted to establish  connections on the three terminals for the curve tracer.

In fault isolation, Photo Emission Microscopy (PEM)  technique was used since it was designed to detect the  photons emitted when the electrons/electric-hole pairs  reunite in the device. Backside PEM was done using the  topside setup which enables the PEM to be reflected using  the backside surface of the die as a reference. The sample  was then biased at I DSS  failure mode. If the leakage is too low  or marginally higher than the specification limits, subthreshold leakage biasing can be utilized.

Next, the sample was subjected to further backside polish to  make sure the die thickness was lower than 30 um to perform  backside FIB cut. Furthermore, since topside setup was used,  adjustment on the Z-measurement was necessary to attain  the smallest resolution of the Photoemission site. The X and  Y measurement from the PEM site to the nearest die corner  will then be recorded and used for FIB marking. The  localized measurement on the backside surface is then FIB  marked which was then used as a guide for physical analysis.

While, For units that did not show improvement in I DSS leakage during electrical characterization, the failed sample  underwent topside polishing to thin down the mold  compound and copper clip. This preparation was for the  topside fault isolation process. Micro-probing was then  conducted to establish connections on the three terminals for  the curve tracer.

In this type of failure, Lock-in Infrared Thermography (LIT)  is utilized as a non-destructive testing method for detecting  subsurface defects. By employing controlled heat excitation  and synchronized thermal imaging, LIT enables rapid, noncontact inspection with high sensitivity. Its advantages  include quick detection of hidden anomalies and the ability  to perform inspections at the package level without the need  to remove the mold compound. Following this, the sample  underwent mechanical cross-sectioning at the hotspot area.  Subsequently, Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and  Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) analyses were  conducted at the defect area to assess the content and  structure of the defect material.

## RESULT   AND   DISCUSSION  

In this paper two case studies will be discussed with dissimilar  failure mechanisms utilizing detailed I DSS  parameter  characterization and employing backside FIB cut approach  and mechanical cross section to uncover the failure  mechanism and successfully prevent the device to recover.

Its advantages  include quick detection of hidden anomalies and the ability to  perform inspections at the package level without the need to  remove the mold compound.

## IV.   CONCLUSION   

In this work, novel comprehensive FA method is introduce  using combination of electrical characteristics, a    precise  fault isolation and a FIB together with mechanical cross  section to solve the recovering I DSS  failure mode. By applying  this technique, it helps to resolve the issue of failure recovery  and efficiently identify the precise root cause of the failure  mechanism. In the deficiency of proper FA steps perform on  the recoverable drain to source leakage failure might have the  tendency to miss the evidence and provide an impropriate FA  result or conclusion. The conclusion can be misleading if the  exact root cause is not properly addressed and corrected.
