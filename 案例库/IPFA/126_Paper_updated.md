## Warpage Improvement for Die Thinning using  Thermal Flattening Technique for high Die-toPackage Area Ratio on FCBGA Package  

M. Z. Azlan, Y. X. Koh, S. Chandra Mohan, T. H. Chor, S. C. Loh   Corporate Quality Network, Intel Technology (M) Sdn. Bhd., Phase 3, Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone, 11900 Penang, Malaysia Email : muhammad.zariqx.bin.azlan@intel.com

Abstract —Si die warpage or curvature on a flip-chip  technology is commonly occurred once the Si chip is attached on  a package interposer substrate in the presence of high  temperature during the reflow process. In this paper, the  challenges introduced by high die-to-package area ratio on the  severity of warpage on Flip-Chip Ball Grid Array (FCBGA)  packages are highlighted. The effects of severe warpage in  mechanical Computer Numerical Control (CNC) die thinning  process on die thickness uniformity and grinding time are  examined. With high warpage, it is difficult to achieve good  thinning uniformity where the silicon final thickness is the same  throughout the die area. Additional thinning is needed for more  precise grinding which will take more time. To reduce the  warpage of FCBGA packages, this work proposed a thermal  flattening technique that applied vertical downward force using  a tensile tester. The method and outcomes of the flattening  process are discussed and its main goals to improve postthinning die uniformity and warpage are demonstrated within  certain identified constraints.

Keywords— FCBGA, Si thinning, die-to-package ratio, CNC,  thermal flattening, warpage

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Flip-Chip Ball Grid Array (FCBGA) package is a type of  integrated circuit packaging that is extensively used in  semiconductor devices for its capability in good thermal  control and electrical performance while also keeping small  package size [1]. Yet, due to the different materials assembled  in the package, large mismatch of Coefficient of Thermal  Expansion (CTE) between the die and the substrate is present  and inevitably causing warpage which is one of the most  fundamental challenges in FCBGA packages. During flipchip attachment to substrate, there is high temperature  involved as part of the reflow process of C4 bumps. Once the  package-die combination has cooled down, stress is generated  because of the CTE difference of the bonded materials. The  stress leads to surface warpage which is almost spherical in  shape since the stress is uniform throughout the interface  between the die and substrate [2]. For a successful die attach,  it needs to be as flat as possible especially for thin die and die  warpage compromises the interconnect reliability of the  attached die in an FCBGA package.

On the physical geometry perspective, one of the factors  of warpage is the ratio of die to package area. It is one of the  parameters simulated by F.X. Che et al. [3]   in their finite  element analysis model to reduce wafer warpage on Fan-Out  Wafer Level Packaging (FO-WLP). The simulation results  show wafer warpage induced in the mold-first process flow of  FO-WLP packaging is lower with smaller die to package ratio.  They also concluded that to reduce warpage, the FO-WLP  package should have large die to mold thickness ratio, thick  die and thin overmold. Though the process flow is not the

same as flip chip attachment in FCBGA packages, materials  with different CTE are bonded and still subjected to thermal  stress. In wafer assembly level of flip-chip packaging, a patent  has been filed by Intel Corporation aiming to control die  warpage specifically for thin die. At   $400\upmu\mathrm{m}$   die thickness or  below, silicon warpage is high when exposed to high chip  attach reflow temperatures [4]. A couple of backside  deposition approaches were introduced and may be  implemented at any stages before die attachment. The first one  is depositing metallic film such as copper with Silicon Nitride  (Si 3 N 4 ) as its adhesive and the second one is applying a  Titanium Tungsten (TiW) film at different levels of pre-stress  conditions. These layers act as a reinforcement for the die  backside for any defects induced during assembly and to  mitigate warpage.

While most studies focused on during assembly or preassembly stages of FCBGA packaging in terms of theoretical  and numerical approach with the finite element models, less  attempts have been seen on post-attachment warpage  solutions on a completely assembled package. Depending on  the application or the next usage of the package, the warpage  produced post-assembly may or may not be noteworthy. If the  package is going through silicon die thinning application, a  highly warped die brings multiple issues on the die surface  quality, die thickness uniformity, and longer thinning duration  to get to the target thickness. This is because there is a big  height difference from center to the corners of the die. The  thinner it is the die required to be from the prep, the more  difficult it is to maintain the final surface thickness variation  in an acceptable range.

One of the example of applications where thinner die is  required is Solid Immersion Lens (SIL) imaging for frontside  redistribution layer analysis. Depending on microscope used,  current acceptable die thickness variation limit is   $\pm\bar{0.005\mathrm{mm}}$  [5]. One of the possible ways to overcome this issue is by  thermally flattening the die surface prior thinning. Its  background and mechanical limitations were discussed by  Kirk A. Martin [2] where instead of heavy weight, the force is  generated by a thermal gradient that thermally expands the  substrate while keeping the die cool. Major gradient is created  when heating the substrate side because the die is subjected to  both radiative and convective losses.

The relationship of one sample’s die-to-package ratio and  the severity of die warpage is established in this abstract.  Warpage effects on die thinning uniformity and time taken to  reach desired silicon thickness are examined on un-flattened  and flattened FCBGA samples. The following method will  explore on thermally flattening silicon die combined with  applied vertical mechanical force as a solution to minimize die  warpage. The scope of die thinning is adhered to the  requirements of SIL imaging purposes.

## II.   W ARPAGE  E FFECT ON  S I  T HINNING   

Prior to SIL imaging analysis, die thinning is required for  silicon thickness of an FCBGA to be within the acceptable die  thickness range to perform clear imaging. Challenges are  faced when trying to perform SIL analysis on FCBGA dies  with thickness more than the tolerable range. The main  challenge is interference of the laser penetration due to free  valence electrons within the silicon semiconductor in a  powered state. Other limitations include optical aberrations,  light collection related issues, optical path length and  refractive index matching. Bulk thinning seeks to eliminate  these challenges by removing bulk silicon, addressing and  mitigating the difficulties. Die thinning requires high  precision and accuracy to fulfill the criteria for SIL  integration. To fulfill these requirements, die thinning using  CNC is accepted for its exceptional accuracy and precision  capabilities in executing the procedures.

One of the biggest challenges in die thinning is  overcoming warpage to produce a thinned die with uniform  thickness. Fig. 1 shows an example of warpage present on an  FCBGA sample. Both die and package surfaces become  noticeably warped or curved   post-attached  as a result of the  aforementioned CTE mismatch during reflow. A problem  often faced in die thinning due to high warpage on FCBGA  samples is non-uniform die thickness after thinning. This  affects the overall thinning quality and consequently, the  imaging quality during SIL analysis. Furthermore, a higher  warpage also results in longer process time which leads to  decreased productivity in the long run.

CNC die thinning generally consists of three main steps  which are mounting, surface profiling and die thinning.  Firstly, the sample is mounted onto the CNC sample fixture  using mounting wax to ensure that the sample is fixed in  position and is unable to move around. It is then placed into  the CNC machining bed. After that, the surface of the die was  probed for 64-point profiling and measurement of warpage.  The data generated from surface profiling generates the tool  path for the thinning process. Then, the sample proceeds for  die thinning. The two main material removal stages for CNC  die thinning are bulk and contour removal. With the touch  probe data, the bulk removal stage removes bulk silicon in a  2D plane from the highest point of the die surface until the  remaining silicon thickness is   $110\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . During this phase any  non-planar surface of the die will not be removed evenly.  Contour removal further removes and attempts to even out the  silicon thickness uniformity where bulk removal was not able  to. During contour process, the remaining die thickness of the  sample is measured using the built-in laser spectrometer to  generate another tool path to remove appropriate silicon  thickness and ensure uniformity. The remaining silicon  thickness was   $70\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . In cases where warpage is severe,  contour removal may not be sufficient to give the required  uniformity due to many factors such as tooling bit condition  and precision.

## B.   Impacted Die Thinning Criteria  

One of the challenges faced during die thinning due to  warpage is non-uniform die thickness. Large variations in the  die surface height from warpage often leads to inconsistent  contact between the silicon surface and the thinning apparatus.  Die surface areas that have larger contact would experience  more aggressive thinning than those with smaller contact  which leads to inconsistent die thickness across the die. Z. J.  Pei highlighted the importance of having a flat surface before  silicon thinning in their studies on fine grinding of silicon  wafers [6]. Taking as an example from sample   ${\bar{\mathrm{R_{B}}}}(0.15)$  , Fig.  3(a) demonstrates the die surface profile of with   $0.097\mathrm{mm}$

warpage and post-thinning die thickness profile. With this  considerably high warpage, it can be seen in the die thickness  profile in (b) that the die thickness range post-thinning is   $14\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The uniformity of the die surface is uneven for the  criteria of SIL analysis.

Warpage also presents the challenge of increasing the  overall process time. A more extensive curvature of the die  surface requires more process calibration and accommodation  especially during the contour removal die thinning process in  order to achieve uniform die thickness. In turn, the overall  process time is extended. This setback is more evident with  FCBGA package products with larger die and die to package  area ratio.

Before flattening the die, the package was first mounted  with wax as a mounting medium in the recess of a metal  fixture that can be used directly in the CNC machine chamber.  The mounting process was carried out in the tensile tester  which is the same equipment used for flattening process. As  shown in Fig. 4, the lower compression plate was connected  to the DC power supply and the voltage was increased to 14V  until the temperature of   $100^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   is measured on the  thermocouple .  This temperature is enough to increase the  viscosity the wax for the package to be able to be pressed onto  it. A piece of glass cover the size of the respective die size for  each sample was placed on the die as a protective cover for the  die surface and even force distribution during flattening.  Ideally, the glass cover thickness should be roughly the same  or thicker than the thickness of the die it is pressing on. The  upper compression plate descends, and the force is applied  vertically in a downward movement when it fully touches the  glass covered die for duration of dwelling time of 1020s at the  set force. During this time, the heat was turned off to let the  wax to cool down and harden. The package fixture was then  transferred to the CNC machine for die thinning.

## A.   Warpage of Thermally Flattened Die  

Fig. 6 shows the effect of die to package area ratio on die  warpage with and without flattening for all three different area  ratio samples   $\mathrm{R_{A}}(0.09)$  ,   $\mathrm{R_{B}}(0.15)$   and   $\mathrm{Rc}(0.35)$  . Without  undergone flattening process where the original die warpage  is untampered, it is observed that warpage linearly increased  with ratio of die to package area. The bigger the ratio, the  higher the die warpage. With flattening step implemented,  warpage managed to be reduced to less than   $0.05\mathrm{mm}$  regardless of the severity of the die’s prior warpage. This can  be seen when comparing   $\mathtt{R a}(0.09)$   and   $\operatorname{Rc}(0.35)$   where the  warpage reduced similarly even with big difference of original  warpage. With the warpage reduced, this ensures better die  thickness uniformity during the thinning process.

## V.   C ONCLUSION 

Die warpage in FCBGA package is a challenge due to  different CTE of materials under high temperature during  reflow and attachment process. Thermal flattening with  applied mechanical force has been shown to be able to  improve overall die warpage and consequently gives better  uniformity in die thinning. The flattening and die thinning  have been done on three FCBGA packages with die to  package ratio of 0.09, 0.15 and 0.35 represented as R A (0.09),   $\mathrm{R_{B}}(0.15)$   and   $\mathrm{Rc}(0.35)$  . Flattening was carried out using a  tensile tester with the pressing force of 500N and the die  thinning is done with CNC machining. Results shown  warpage was reduced to less than   $0.05\mathrm{mm}$   regardless of  original warpage. Die thinning results show biggest die  thickness range reduction of  $48\%$   with sample with the biggest  die to package ratio   $\mathrm{Rc}(0.35)$  . Lesser reduction of   $21\%$   with  sample with the lowest ratio   $\mathrm{{R}_{A}}(0.09)$  . Thinning time is  reduced the most for  $\operatorname{Rc}(0.35)$   by 599 minutes.
