## Degradation Analysis of Schottky GaN HEMT  between Hard Switching and Soft Switching Stress  

Ya-Huan Lee 1 , Ting-Chang Chang 1,2,3\* , Hung-Ming Kuo 1  and Yu-Jie Tsai 3     1   Department of Physics, National Sun Yat-sen University, Kaohsiung, 80424, Taiwan.  2  Center of Crystal Research, National Sun Yat-sen University, Kaohsiung 80424, Taiwan   3  Advanced Technology Research, National Sun Yat-Sen University, Kaohsiung, 80424, Taiwan  \*  Email address of corresponding authors: tcchang3708@gmail.com

Abstract — In this study, the degradation trend between hard  switching and soft switching under the long-term reliability test  of schottky GaN HEMT is analyzed. The degradation in the  output current becomes more severe as the switching waveform  transitions from soft switching to that of hard switching.  However, there is an unusual trend in the positive threshold  voltage, characterized by an initial rise followed by a decline.  The deterioration of soft switching is dominated by the off-state  leakage, where the gate leakage and the source leakage cause the  electron injection into the AlGaN and GaN layers. In the case of  the hard switching, the degradation is maintained by hot  electrons inject into the GaN layer, and the generated holes by  the trap-assisted thermionic emission (TA-TFE) recombine with  the electrons injected into the GaN layer under the gate. Longterm DC stress is also employed to clarify this unusual trend.  This degradation results from the chain reaction of the rapid  switching between off-state and on-state, making it challenging  to be observed under DC stress.

Keywords— Schottky HEMT, Hard/Soft Switching, Off-State  Stress, On-State Stress, Hot Electron, Trap-Assisted Thermionic  Emission.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

With the increasing demands and advancements in  semiconductor technology, III-V semiconductors have  become one of the primary trends in development. Among  them, GaN devices are widely used for high-frequency and  high-voltage operations due to their material characteristics  [1]-[4]. Therefore, reliability issues during rapid switching  becomes extremely important for the real operation situation.

The switching operation can be classified into the hard  switching and the soft switching. The hard switching involves  a simpler drive circuit, making the overall cost lower.  However, it generates unnecessary switching losses during  the switching operation, making it efficient only under low  power operations. Conversely, the soft switching offers  improved performance in terms of reduced switching losses.  Nevertheless, the complex circuitry needed to adjust the  output waveform increases costs. As a result, soft switching  proves stable and efficient under high-power operations. [5]- [11].

To characterize the operation condition, variable drain-togate delay (DGD) are used to quantify the degree between the  hard switching and the soft switching [12]-[17]. In the  previous study, dynamic   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{on}}$   and pulsed   $\mathrm{I_{d}-V_{d}}$   have been  utilized to investigate the difference in the degradation  behaviors between the hard switching and the soft switching.  However, both methods can solely examine electrical  differences associated with the output current, leaving other  parameters such as threshold voltage unclear. Consequently,  accurately determining carrier injection locations and the  underlying physical mechanisms remains challenging.

In this study, the long-term reliability testing is applied to  the device between the hard switching and the soft switching.  As the switch transitions from the soft switching to the hard  switching, the degradation in I d  becomes more severe.  Additionally, there is an observed trend in the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  , with an  initial increase followed by a smaller decrease. Long-term  DC reliability testing is employed to validate these  degradation mechanisms.

When the device is switched to the off state, the large gate  leakage induces electron injection into the drift region  between the gate and drain, while the source leakage causes  the electron injection into the buffer region. This dual  injection phenomenon leads to a decrease in I d  and an  increase in   $\mathrm{V_{th}}.$  . These outcomes predominantly govern the  degradation behavior in the soft switching region. In contrast,  during operation under the hard switching conditions, both  the hot-electron effect and the trap-assisted thermionic field  emission (TA-TFE) occur simultaneously. The hot electrons  inject into the drift region of the buffer layer, and the  generated holes recombine with previously injected electrons  below the gate under the off-state conditions, respectively.  The former results in a significant decrease in the I d , while  the latter causes a reverse trend in the  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift within the hard  switching region.

Therefore, these findings play a significant role in  elucidating the degradation mechanisms and the injection  positions during the transition between hard switch and soft  switch. This contributes to a better understanding of the root  causes of degradation under real operating conditions.

## II.   E XPERIMENT 

The schematic diagram of the Schottky HEMT is shown  in Fig. 1(a). The device was grown on the silicon substrate. A   $4\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$   buffer layer, known as the superlattice   layer, was grown  by metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD),  composed of multiple AlGaN and GaN layers. Then, a 200  nm GaN layer and a 25 nm AlGaN layer were deposited.  Subsequently, mesa isolation was used to isolate the devices.  Ti/Al/Ni/Au metal layer was deposited under an   $\Nu_{2}$  environment and annealed at   $900~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   to form the source and  drain ohmic contacts. Next,   $\mathrm{{Nu}/\mathrm{{Nu}/\mathrm{{Ti}}}}$   metal layers were  deposited as the gate metal. Lastly, a  $450\;\mathrm{nm}\;\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer as a  passivation layer was grown by PECVD. The device has a 3   $\upmu\mathrm{m}$   gate length, a  $3~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   gate to source length, a   $20\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$   gate to  drain length, and a   $100~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   channel width. The threshold  voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{th})}$   is defined as the gate voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{g})}$   at which the  drain current (I d ) is equal to   $0.1\,\mathrm{\mA/mm}$   when the drain  voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{d})}$   is 0.1 V. In terms of the measurements, the I-V  characteristics were analyzed by an Agilent B1500A  semiconductor device analyzer. Fig. 1(b) shows the   $\mathrm{I_{d-}V_{g}}$  curve at  $\mathrm{V_{d}}=0.1\;\mathrm{V}$  . The V th  of the device is approximately -  $2.7~\mathrm{V}$  , and the I d  is   $5.7\;\mathrm{mA/mm}$   when the   $\mathrm{Vg}$   arrived at 0 V.                        Fig. 1(c) shows the schematic diagram of the pulse waveform  of the stress condition. The pulse is first at off-state condition   $\mathrm{(V_{g}=0\ V}$  ,   $\mathrm{V_{d}}=40\;\mathrm{V}$  ), then switch to on-state (V g  = 6 V, V d  $=1\ \mathrm{V}$  ). The pulse width is   $10~\upmu\mathrm{s}$  . The rising time and falling  time are   $1\upmu\mathrm{s}$  . And the pulse period is  $50\,\upmu\mathrm{s}$  . To distinguish the  degree of the soft switch and hard switch, variable DGD are  used [12]-[15]. The DGD is defined as the time difference  between the drain voltage arrive to  $\mathrm{V_{d}}/2$   and the gate voltage  arrive to  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  . Therefore, the degradation analyzes between the  soft switching   $(\mathrm{{DGD}}>0)$  ) and the hard switching   $(\mathrm{{DG}})<0$  )  can be investigated.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

In conclusion, the long-term reliability testing for schottky  HEMT under both the hard switching and the soft switching  conditions has been discussed. The degradation in the I d  is  observed to become more severe as the operating conditions  approach the hard switching. However, there is a trend of an  initial rise followed by a decline in the positive shift of the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  . The degradation behaviors under the soft switching  conditions are primarily dominated by the off-state  degradation. Off-state leakage from the gate and source leads  to electron injection into the AlGaN and GaN layers, resulting  in a decrease in the I d  and an increase in the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  . However,  due to the different off-state time and relaxing time during the  soft switch condition, variations in degradation occur. The  condition under the soft switching region has a lager  degradation, approaching that of the hard switching region,  due to the longer off-state time and shorter relax time during  switching. In the degradation behaviors under the hard  switching conditions, besides the degradation during the offstate, the hot electron effect and the TA-TFE during the  switching also contribute to the device deterioration. The  former leads to the injection of the hot electrons into the drift  region of the buffer layer near the drain side, while the holes  generated by the latter cause the recombination of the  electrons injected into the GaN layer under the gate. This  results in a decrease in the I d  and a decrease in the positive  shift of   $\mathrm{V_{th,}}$  , respectively. Through this method, unlike the

previous method such as DC reliability test and the transient  analysis, this method enables the observation of mutual  effects within the device during actual switching. It is helpful  to accurately determine the degradation reasons of the device  under the real hard/soft switching conditions and clarify the  actual locations of the carrier injection and the degradation  mechanisms.
