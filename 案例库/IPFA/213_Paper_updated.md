## Case Study: Failure Analysis of Thin Quad Flat  Pack Package (TQFP) IC  

Khalid Usman 1 , Asad ur Rehman  1 , Muhammad Waqas  1 , Mustahsan Ali Khan  1 , Muhammad Yasir Qadri  1 , Sajid Baloch 1  1 Centre of Excellence in Science and Technology (CESAT), Islamabad, Pakistan  Email :  khalidusman240@gmail.com

Abstract —   This article describes systematic analysis of  failure occurred in a Thin Quad Flat Pack Package (TQFP)  integrated circuit. The electrical testing suggested that the  device failure occurred inside of the device package. The  sample was analyzed through Non-Destructive techniques of  X-Ray imaging and Scanning Acoustic Microscope (SAM) to  get insight into the internal structure and integrity of the  sample in order to identify any anomalies. Even though the  Nondestructive testing exhibited some early signs of the  failure but was not enough for a conclusion. Subsequently,  decapsulation of the sample was exercised to examine the  internal structure of the die to unveil the crucial insights into  the origin of problem. A specialized resistive (constant  voltage) micro-probing was opted and carried out at pad level  of the die which not only identified the internal circuit issues  but also revealed the associated impact of the chemicals i.e.,  sulfuric and nitric acid on the die, introducing further layer  of complexity in failure analysis methodologies. This  comprehensive study provided valuable understanding of  optimized methodologies for enhancing failure analysis  process and highlighted the importance of mitigating the  potential risks associated with the selection of chemical  procedures in semiconductor investigations.

Keywords—IC Failure Analysis, Non-destructive Testing,  Decapsulation, Micro-Probing

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

With the advancement of semiconductor fabrication  technologies, the integrated circuits are becoming more  complex with high density of transistor being fabricated on  small area of silicon wafer [1]. With the increasing density  of the electronic circuits on a single module, the packaging  of the ICs becomes a challenge. The die is encapsulated in  a rigid shell of plastic material called Epoxy Molding  Compound (EMC) which serve as a shield for  environmental protection, pin interconnection and to  provide heat dissipation [2]. With the miniaturization  trends, the die size and thickness of the package continue to  shrink accordingly [2]. All these advancements make the  development of smaller and more compact packages of the  integrated circuits. The demand of package designs enables  different orientation and symmetries for die encapsulation  [3-4]. Among all the available options, Thin Quad Flat Pack  Package (TQFP) offers much more versatility due to its  compact size and ease of manufacturing [5-7]. However,  like any other package it may encounter failures during  operation due to several reasons which may lead to  malfunction.

Modernization and in-situ advancement in the  diagnostic tools of failure analysis has developed highly  reliable practices aimed at identifying and rectifying issues  within ICs to enhance their reliability and longevity. To  avoid the causes of failure and its consequences, failure  analysts need to formulate effective strategies to ensure the  reliable operation of systems and devices. Moreover, it

helps in establishing effective processes to predict and  mitigate potential failures and to avoid and safeguarding  critical applications from catastrophe. Failure analysis not  only helps to avoid operational setbacks but also save costs,  time, and enhances the overall reliability and efficiency of  systems and devices [8].

Several invasive and non-invasive methods have been  developed and are utilized for the analysis of failures that  occur inside the epoxy mold IC packages. Among these,  famous and reliable techniques include optical microscopy,  X-ray analysis, scanning acoustic microscopy (SAM), laser  ablation, plasma or chemical etching and thermomechanical milling followed by micro probing and  scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis. Nondestructive testing methods, such as optical inspection, Xray imaging, and SAM analysis, play a key role in the fault  approximation and identification inside of the package. Xray is widely used to detect the internal defects of cracks,  voids and wire bond failures which also helps examine the  anomalies and to locate the fault site. SAM inspection  further enhances the analysis by detecting delamination,  voiding, and other defects within the package [7]. Sometime  failure identification at package level become difficult to  trace with the use of non-destructive techniques. To address  the issue, it becomes essential to remove the epoxy mold  compound (EMC) of the IC so that internal structure and  circuit can be tested to locate the causes of failure. Laser and  Chemical decapsulation is carried out to expose the circuit  die for further process of investigation followed by micro  probing to figure out electrical failure [9]. Chemical  decapsulation includes selectively removing the epoxy  material using etchants like concentrated sulfuric and  fuming nitric acid, allowing for direct access to the die for  further analysis. Acid etching can directly affect wire bonds  and metal pads on the die, emphasizing the importance of  careful selection regarding the composition of etchants and  the duration of the etching process [8]. By correlating  electrical measurements with physical defects observed  during   destructive   and   non-destructive   testing,   a  comprehensive understanding of the failure modes within  the IC can be achieved.

In this article, we explore the failure analysis of the  TQFP ICs and highlighted the significance of nondestructive and destructive testing by utilizing technique of  2D X-Ray imaging, Scanning Acoustic Microscopy (SAM)  and subsequent decapsulation process for die level testing.  The study also highlights the importance of root cause  identification mechanisms and diagnostic techniques for  semiconductor failure investigation.

## II.    E XPERIMENTAL 

In this section, a comprehensive examination of the  testing procedure is presented and divided into three main  sections:

## A.   Non-Destructive Analysis  

The analysis was performed on two different specimens  of the same 80 lead TQFP ICs. Package of the IC is plastic  material having size of   $14\mathrm{mm}\texttt{x}14\mathrm{mm}$  . During electrical  testing of the ICs, it was observed that multiple lead pins   individual ICs exhibited variations from the specifications.  Specifically, some of the ICs were found to be faulty. For  instance, pair of lead pins such as 1) VDD – VDD(Core)  and 2) VSS – VSS (IO) were expected to be shorted, but  instead, they were found opened. One of the failed parts was  initially inspected under Optical Microscope (OM) for  possible failure analysis at the outer side of the sample and  then was examined by 2D X-Ray imaging and Scanning  Acoustic Microscope for further investigation. X-ray  inspection of both the devices were conducted and is shown  in Fig. 1.

Delamination was detected in two locations within the  device: between the epoxy molding compound (EMC) and  the lead frame, and between the die attach and the lead  frame. Another location where delamination was observed  between the EMC and the bond wire/lead frame as  illustrated in Fig.1a. If the adhesive that holds the die to the  lead frame separates, it can cause the die to lift. This lifting  could then further cause the wire ball bonds to lift as well  and can cause the electrical connection to be opened [10].

Delamination between EMC and bond wires may occur due  to factors such as the viscosity and flow velocity of the  encapsulant, the presence of voids and fillers in the  encapsulant, poor wire bond geometry, or a delayed  packaging profile [11].

However, just looking at the percentage of delamination  was not enough to make a clear conclusion. Yet, after a  thorough review, no significant abnormalities were found in  the results. Subsequently, the failed IC was subjected to  decapsulation for further analysis.

## B.   Destructive Analysis  

It is the standard practice to decapsulate the package  with high energy laser prior to chemical processes, which is  already widely adopted by the industry [12], we purposely  followed milling process instead of laser system to explore  a new pathway for the decapsulation process. We chose to  investigate whether the milling process could effectively  substitute laser decapsulation. Additionally, we aimed to  mitigate the risk of damaging the die's active surface layer  and wire bonds associated with high-energy laser usage,  thus emphasizing the importance of selecting the  appropriate decapsulation method.

Chemical decapsulation was performed using a mix  solution of highly concentrated sulfuric acid   $(98\%)$   and  fuming nitric acid as illustrated in Fig. 3a. Precise milling  of the IC as shown in Fig. 3b was carried out at specified  package surface area and depth to make a cavity for targeted  chemical in order to expose the exact die under the plastic  mold while preserving the bonding wires. consequently, the  cavity serves as an accelerating factor for the decaping  process and reduces the contact time between wire bonds,  metal pads, and the die with chemical etchants. The faulty  IC was carefully immersed in a boiling solution and heated  on a hot plate until it reached predefined boiling  temperature of a specific degree. The sample was kept in the  boiling acid solution for a particular time. Following this,  the sample was allowed to cool in the air and then cleaned  with dry air and acetone to ensure the complete removal of  dissolved parts from the sample. This process was very  carefully repeated many times in a very calculated way in  order to remove the epoxy material from the IC. The  chemical arrangement for the decapsulation of failed IC is  displayed and shown in Fig. 3.

It is observed that the milling approach offered  complexity, as it involves manually creating a cavity for the  chemical etchants to effectively decapsulate the epoxy  material of the package. This process entails precise  estimation of depth profile and careful selection of package  surface layer to approximate the exact location of the die  under the epoxy encapsulation, which is susceptible to  human error. Therefore, it is crucial to meticulously

calculate and optimize the entire milling process to  minimize adverse effects on the semiconductor die's active  regions. Consequently, we suggest that the milling approach  could be implemented with great precision as an alternative  to laser decapsulation, particularly when followed prior to  chemical treatment. This insight could prove valuable for  failure analysis engineers when considering root causes of  failures during inspections.

Once the epoxy mold compound was removed from the  die, the sample was placed onto a wire bond puller machine  to precisely detach specific bond wires from the die, as  depicted in Fig. 4 for subsequent micro probing. A bond  wire puller was utilized to remove the specified wire bonds.  During this process, it was observed that some of the wire  bonds were loosely bound to the die, which may have  occurred either during the bonding and packaging process  or as a result of the effects of chemical etchants. However,  the majority of the wire bonds were observed firmly  attached to the bond pads. This step was crucial for  accessing the internal circuitry of the IC and facilitating  further analysis of its structure and electrical connectivity.

## C.   Microprobing  

Micro probing of the die was performed at particular  pads for the failure identification. Die level characterization  of the failed IC was performed using advanced microprobing station which involved analyzing the electrical  properties and behavior of the integrated circuit (IC) at  specific locations (pads) of the die. Probe tip needs to be  smaller than the pad opening to make a fine contact with the  pad on the die without exerting excessive force which could  induce any damage to the die. Overall, accurate placement  of the probe tip during electrical micro-probing is essential  for obtaining reliable measurement data and diagnosing  issues within the integrated circuit. Proper preparation,  alignment, and contact techniques are critical for successful  micro probing and accurate characterization of the die's  electrical properties. Optimized positioning of probe tip to  the pad is very crucial for the analysis as it is common to  place active circuitry just below the bond pad during  fabrication. Probe station was calibrated and the die was  again prepared and cleaned for contamination in a specific  manner prior to the probe measurement. Proper manual  alignment of the probe tip to the pad was conducted while  using a microscope for real time imaging. Resistive  measurements were taken at the probe points in a specific  way as depicted in Fig. 5. In this study, Non-conventional  method was opted instead of the conventionally used IV  characterizations to assess the functionality of the pads. In  conventional IV testing, the voltage (V) is varied as an

independent variable, and the current (I) is sensed as a  function of voltage (V) [8]. However, in our approach, we  chose   a   non-conventional   method   by   employing  galvanometric ohm meter testing. In this method, the  voltage level is kept constant, and the flow of current under  the subject voltage magnitude is monitored. This allows us  to examine the spreading resistance from the given pad's  combination (two pads). This test can also be conducted  using a common ohm meter or a digital multimeter (DMM)  in continuity or ohm-meter mode. The experimental setup  for pad probe testing is depicted in Fig. 5 below.

It was found that using a mixture of acid etchants  effectively removed the epoxy molding compound from the  sample. However, corrosion was also observed on a pad of  the failed decapsulated die, likely due to excessive chemical  treatment. Components involved in metallic interconnects  are particularly vulnerable to corrosion when exposed to  acids, so it's crucial to carefully control the combination of  chemicals and the duration of the decapsulation process[8].  If not managed properly, this chemical damage can spread

to wire bonds, metal pads, and even the surface of the die,  potentially causing electrical issues in the device [12].  Additionally, our study revealed delamination at the  interfaces between the die and lead frame, as well as  between the epoxy molding compound and wire  bonding/lead frame in the failed IC. This raises the  possibility of bond lifting within the chip, leading to open  circuits during pin testing. The open circuit problem was  also observed in the die-level testing of the failed IC. This  issue could result from a fault within the IC's circuitry,  possibly originating during wafer processing, or from  delamination during the packaging process, resulting in  interconnection problems within the die circuit, thus leading  to open circuit issues in the device.

## III.   CONCLUSION 

The work focused on detail investigation of failure  analysis of Thin Quad Flat Pack Package (TQFP) IC failed  during electrical testing. Analysis involved different  techniques of nondestructive and destructive testing of  semiconductor failure analysis to identify the cause of  failure. The analysis was carried out by combining 2D Xray imaging, Scanning Acoustic Microscopy followed by  decapsulation of epoxy mold compound by acid etching.  After the epoxy was removed, the die underwent electrical  testing at pad level using micro probing to evaluate the  circuit of the die. SAM analysis of the failed IC revealed  delamination at two different interfaces of the device:  between the die attach and the lead frame, and between the  wirebonds and the EMC interface. This suggests that it may  contribute to electrical disconnection issues. Furthermore,  electrical micro probing indicated the presence of a process  fault within the die circuit, which likely contributed to the  device failure. The study proposed that the chemical  decapsulation may impact the integrity of metal tracks and  pad opening during the decapsulation process. It also  suggests   that   chemical   treatment   requires   careful  consideration of factors such as composition, duration, and  temperature to ensure a reliable decapsulation process for  effective failure analysis of epoxy-packaged semiconductor  devices.R EFERENCES
