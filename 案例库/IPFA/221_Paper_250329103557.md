## The Influence of Gate-Series-Resistor on the ESD  Protection and Switching Speed for 650V GaNHEMT Circuit  

Jian-Hsing Lee 1 , Yeh-Jen Huang 1 , Li-Yang Hong 1 , Chih-Cherng Liao 2 , Brian Lien 1 ,   Karuna Nidhi 3 , Yeh-Ning Jou 1 , Ke-Horng Chen 2  1 DE Department, Vanguard International Semiconductor Corp., No. 123, Park  $3^{\mathrm{rd}}$  Ave., Science Based Indus. Park, HsinChu City, Taiwan     2 Department of Electrical and Computer Eng., National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, HsinChu City, 300093, Taiwan  3 ESD Department, Richtek Technology Corporation, ChuPei City, Taiwan  Phone: (886)0966595083, Email:  ano.lee2009@gmail.com

Abstract —   Unlike CMOS technology, there only exist two  components, HEMT and resistor, that can be used to design as  the electrostatic-discharge (ESD) protection circuit in GaN  technology. Although HEMT has very robust ESD capability, it  still cannot work very well without the resistor to clamp the ESD  current or pull up the gate voltage to turn on its channel. So,  most ESD protection circuits for protected HEMT are  composed of resistor and HEMT. In general, the protection  capability of an ESD protection circuit is determined by the size  of ESD HEMT and the resistor resistance. However, the highresistance resistor degrades the switching speed of protected  HEMT, while the low-resistance resistor degrades the  protection capability of ESD protection circuit. In this paper,  how   the turn-on gate resistor (Rg ON ) affects the protection  capability of all-terminal ESD protection circuit for a 650V GaN  HEM is studied. From the test results ,  this protection circuit is  demonstrated that can protect the gate of protected HEMT  against 1 kV HBM strike for any zapping condition even if the  Rg ON  is only 2ohm.

## Keywords—GaN, ESD, It2  

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Based on the protected terminals, the electrostatic  discharge (ESD) protection circuits for GaN HEMT can be  classified into two categories, namely, the drain ESD  protection circuit as shown in Fig. 1 and gate ESD protection  circuit as shown in Fig. 2 [1]-[5]. Regardless of the drain ESD  protection circuit or gate ESD protection circuit, they are all  designed by the HEMTs and resistor since this GaN  technology only can offer the two components. Although the  HEMT (ESD) is designed as the main ESD protection device  to conduct the ESD current, the ESD protection circuit cannot  work well without the resistor. This is because the ESD  current can induce the voltage drop across the resistor to pull  up   the gate voltage of HEMT to turn on the channel for  sinking the charges of ESD before it damages the protected  device (HEMT GAN ). Moreover, the resistor also can be used  to clamp the ESD current flowing through the drains, sources  and gates of HEMTs for ESD (HEMT ESD ) or protected device   $(\mathrm{HEMT_{G a N}})$  ) to damage them. So, the resistor often plays a key  role in the protection capability of ESD protection circuit.

However, it has been reported that the drain of GaNHEMT is very robust to electrostatic discharge (ESD) strike  and does not need the additional ESD device to protect this  terminal since it has the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) [1]. So, a GaN HEMT only needs the ESD protection  circuit to protect its vulnerable gate and the circuit in Fig. 2(b)  has been proven that can effectively protect all terminals of a  GaN HEMT. Unlike the drain ESD protection circuits in Fig.  1, the gate ESD protection circuit has a resistor (  $\mathrm{R_{g}}$   in Fig. 2)

between the gate of HEMT GaN  and gate terminal to clamp the  ESD current to prevent the gate damage. From the system  application, it needs two resistors between the gate and gate  driver to control the switching speed as shown in Fig. 3,  where   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$  is the turn-on gate resistor (  $10~\Omega$   to   $20~\Omega$  ) and   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gOFF}}$   is the turn-off gate resistor (1  Ω  to 3  Ω ) [6], [7].  However, it cannot effectively protect the gate of   $\mathrm{HEMT_{\mathrm{GaN}}}$  if the   $\mathrm{R_{g}}$  of the ESD protection circuit in Fig. 2(b) is too small.  In this paper, the TLP IV characteristics and HBM of allterminal ESD protection circuit based on Figs. 2(b) and (3)  are evaluated to study whether the two circuits can be  combined as one circuit.

## II.    E XPERIMENT   

The technology used for this study is a 650V E-mode GaN  HEMT, which is fabricated on 8” GaN on Qromis substrate  technology (QST TM ). Based on Figs. 2(b) and 3, the structure  used to study the influence of the gate resistance on the  protection capability of ESD protection circuit and the  switching speed of a GaN HEMT (HEMT GAN ) is proposed as  shown in Fig. 4. It includes a big 650V E-Mode GaN HEMT  (HEMT GAN ), four resistors, diode and two small HEMTs for  ESD (HEMT ESD ). The cross-section of HEMT in Fig. 4 is  shown in Fig. 5. The main difference for the two different  kinds of HEMTs is the drain to gate space (S) and field plate.  The S of GaN HEMT is much wider than that of HEMT for  the gate ESD protection since the drain needs to withstand the  high voltage (650 V), while the signal applied to the gate is  only 6 V. Although there are two parasitic diodes of the gate  of HEMT (D1 and D2) from Fig. 5, they cannot be separated  into two single diodes from the layout in this technology. So,  the diode (D OFF ) in series with a   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gOFF}}$   in Fig. 4 is still a  HEMT since it can be equivalent to the two components when  the gate and drain are connected as shown in Fig. 6 [8]. The  resistor for  $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   is a metal resistor, which the resistances used  for the study of system applications and ESD protections are  50  Ω , 30  Ω , 15  Ω , 5  Ω , 2  Ω  and  $0\,\Omega$  . The resistors for R1 and  R2 both are 2D-EG resistors. The total width for the protected  HEMT   $(\mathrm{HEMT_{GaN}})$    in Fig. 4(b) is   $36~\mathrm{mm}$   and there are two  different sizes for the two ESD HEMTs (HEMT ESD1  and  HEMT ESD2 ), which are 24 mm and 8 mm.

The instruments used to do the ESD evaluation for each  terminal of the test patterns are the commercial transmissionline pulse (TLP) test system and human-body model (HBM)  test. The pulse width and rise time of the TLP to characterize  the high current IV curves of the test patterns are 100 ns and  10 ns. The TLP IV curve is based on the averaged values of  the voltage waveform and current waveform at the time  interval between 70 ns and 90 ns.  The test procedure for  HBM follows the JEDEC standard [9], [10 ].  A series of three  HBM pulses are applied to the zapped terminal with-respectto the grounded terminal until the device damaged. The rise  time and decay time of the HBM pulse are 10 ns and 150 ns,  respectively. After each TLP event and HBM event, the  leakage currents of the gate and drain terminals are measured  by the semiconductor device parameter analyzer (Keysight  B1500A) manually. The failure criterion is based on the IV  curves measured by B1500A, and not based on the leakage  current measured by the built-in DC meter of the TLP test  system since it does not have enough   power to supply   $650~\mathrm{V}$  to the device for measurement. The failure is defined as any  manual IV curve of the test pattern shifts more than  $10\%$   after  each TLP or HBM stress event.

## A.   DC IV Characteristics of resistor  

Fig. 7 shows the DC IV characteristic of the gated GaN  diode in Fig. 6 for a diode in series with   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gOFF}}$  . Based on the  IV curve in logarithmic scale, the turn-on   threshold voltage  is approximately   $\sim\,0.6\,\mathrm{~V~}$  , which is close to that of a pn  junction diode. From the IV characteristic in linear scale, the  resistance for   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gOFF}}$   is almost   $2.5\;\Omega$  .   Fig. 8 shows the DC IV  curve of a metal resistor for   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   in Fig. 4. There are two  regions for metal resistors, which exhibit the linear  characteristic between 0 V to 1.5 V and exhibit the saturation characteristic if the applied voltage is higher than 2 V due to  Joule-heating [11]-[13]. With the saturation characteristic,  the   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   becomes a high impedance resistor to clamp the  ESD current at the high voltage.  Fig. 9 shows the DC IV  curve of a 2D-EG resistor for R1 and R2 in Fig. 4. Unlike the  metal resistor, the resistance of the 2D-EG resistor increases  with the voltage linearly.

## B.   Capacitance   

Fig. 10 shows the C-V curves of the   $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GS}}$   and   $\mathrm{C_{GD}}$   of  HEMT GaN  in Fig. 4. The capacitance of  $\mathrm{CGS}$   is larger than that   $\mathrm{C_{GD}}$   and only slightly decreases with increasing the drain  voltage below 50 V and then becomes saturated. However,  the capacitance of   $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{DS}}$   decreases with increasing the drain  voltage within 150 V and then saturates at a very low  capacitance. This matches the result published by EPC [14].

## C.   TLP and HBM Test Result  

Figs. 11-Fig. 14 show the positive TLP IV curves for the  gate terminal of   $\mathrm{HEMT_{\mathrm{GaN}}}$   with respect to grounded source

or grounded drain versus   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   based on the ESD protection  circuits of 24 mm HEMT ESD  and   $\mathrm{8\mm\HEMT_{ESD}}$   in Fig. 4.   The It2 values of the gate terminal of HEMT GaN  for both  stress conditions decrease with decreasing   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$  .   Figs. 15-18   show the negative TLP IV curves of the gate terminal of  HEMT GaN  with respect to grounded source or grounded drain  versus   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   based on the ESD protection circuits of   $24~\mathrm{mm}$    HEMT ESD  and 8 mm HEMT ESD  in Fig. 4. The   It2 values of  the gate terminal of HEMT GaN  for both stress conditions only  decrease with decreasing  $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   slightly except   ${\tt R}_{\mathrm{gON}}=0$  .

Tables I and II summarize the HBM threshold voltages  and It2 values of the gate terminal of   $\mathrm{HEMT_{GaN}}$   based on the  ESD protection circuits of 24 mm HEMT ESD  and   $8\,\mathrm{\mm}$    HEMT ESD  for all test conditions. Like the positive TLP stress  conditions, the positive HBM threshold voltage of the gate  terminal of   $\mathrm{HEMT_{GaN}}$   protected by the ESD protection circuit  decreases with decreasing   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   for both zapping conditions.  For the negative HBM, the gate terminal of   $\mathrm{HEMT_{\mathrm{GaN}}}$    protected by the ESD protection circuit can pass  $-8\;\mathrm{kV}$   in any  zapping condition. This infers that the gate of HEMT GaN  can  withstand much higher negative voltage or stress than the  positive voltage or current stress   since the breakdown voltage  of the PGaN to AlGaN diode D1 (-29V) is higher than that of  Schottky diode D2 (13V) of the gate from the test result in  our early published paper [1]. With the higher   breakdown  voltage of the diode D1, the gate can withstand much higher  negative TLP current or HBM voltage compared to the  positive TLP current or HBM voltage for any   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$  .

From Table I, the ESD protection circuit in Fig. 4 can  effectively protect the gate of HEMT GaN  against HBM for any  zapping condition. Even without the gate on-resistor (  $({\bf R}_{\mathrm{gON}}=$   $0\ \Omega_{c}$  ), it still can pass 1 kV HBM. This is caused by that the  TLP or ESD can couple the   voltages of the gates of HEMT ESD or  $\mathrm{HEMT_{\mathrm{GaN}}}$   through the overlap capacitors between the gate  to drain   $\mathrm{(C_{GD})}$   and gate to source (C GS ) or resistors to turn on  the channels of HEMTs to discharge the ESD or TLP current  as shown in Figs.19-22. For the positive ESD or TLP applied  to the gate terminal with respect to grounded source, the gate  voltage of HEMT ESD2    $(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{G}2})$   follows the RC time constant (R2   $\times\,\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GS}2})$   to rise since the resistor R2 connects the gate terminal  directly from Fig. 19. This also pulls up the gate voltage of  HEMT ESD1  (V G1 ) since it is on top of HEMT ESD2  and there is  another RC (R1 and C GS1 ) connected to the source. So, the  channels of both HEMTs for ESD can turn on to conduct the  ESD or TLP current to the ground from the zapped terminal.  For the negative ESD or TLP applied to the gate terminal with  respect to grounded source, the mechanism is almost the same  as the positive ESD applied to the gate terminal with respect  to the grounded source. Both the gate voltages of HEMT ESD1 (V G1 ) and HEMT ESD2  (V G2 ) can be pulled higher than the   $\mathrm{V_{ESD}}$   from Fig. 20. So, the channels of both HEMTs for ESD  also can turn on to conduct the ESD or TLP current from the  ground to the zapped terminal. For the positive ESD or TLP  applied to the gate terminal with respect to grounded drain,  the gate voltage of HEMT GaN  (V G ) follows the RC time  constant   $(\mathrm{R_{gON}}\times\mathrm{C_{GD}})$   to rise due to   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   connected the gate  terminal directly from Fig. 21. Moreover, the gate voltages of  HEMT ESD2    $(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{G}2})$   and HEMT ESD1  (V G1 ) follow the same  mechanism as the positive ESD or TLP applied to the gate  with respect to grounded source to rise. Thus, all channels of  the three HEMTs can turn on to conduct the ESD or TLP  current from the zapped terminal to the ground. For the  negative ESD or TLP applied to the gate terminal with respect  to grounded drain, the mechanism is almost the same as the

positive ESD applied to the gate terminal with respect to the  grounded drain. The gate voltages of the three HEMTs all can  be pulled higher than   $\mathrm{V_{ESD}}$   from Fig. 22. Thus, all channels  of the three HEMTs can turn on to conduct the ESD or TLP  current to the zapped terminal from the ground.

## D.   Switching speed vs.  $R_{g O N}$    

Fig. 23 shows the voltage waveform of the gate of  HEMT GAN  (V G ) in response to a pulse applied to the gate  terminal (G) in Figs. 19-22 versus   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$  . As can be seen, the  gate voltage waveforms overlap together and cannot be  distinguished for R gON  0  Ω , 2  Ω  and  $5\,\Omega$  . This infers that there  is no switching speed delay of the gate if   ${\mathrm{\boldmath~\cal~R}}_{\mathrm{gON}}\:\leq\:5\:~\Omega$  .  Moreover, it can find that the gate voltage waveforms for   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gON}}\ 15\ \Omega$   and   $30~\Omega$   overlap together and only are slightly  different from that for   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}\,\leq\,5\,\,\Omega$  . However, the voltage  waveform is much different from that for   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}\leq30\ \Omega$   as the  further increase in the R gON  (100  Ω ). Although there is no  switching speed delay of the gate if   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gON}}\leq5~\Omega$  , it has been  reported that too small  $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   induces the high dV/dt, resulting  in the drain current ring [7]. To optimize   the switching speed

and drain current ring, the recommended resistance for   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$  is between   $10\;\Omega$   and   $20\;\Omega$   from one application note [7].

## IV.    C ONCLUSION 

The incorporation of the ESD protection circuit with the  E-Mode power GaN HEMT to achieve the required ESD  performance and switching speed for real system application  is possible. From Tables I and II, the turn-on gate resistor   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   plays an important   key role in the gate protection  capability of the incorporating ESD protection circuit.  However,   the   turn-off gate resistor   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{gOFF}}$    has the less impact  on the gate protection capability of that circuit. Based on the  test results,   we demonstrate that the diode in series with R gOFF and   $\mathrm{\DeltaR_{gON}}$   for system applications can be built into the  incorporating ESD protection circuit to meet the resistance  criterion and required ESD level for power GaN HEMT. With  the embedded resistors and diode, the gate driver for power  GaN HEMT does not need the additional discrete resistors  and diode. This can save the cost of system house and prevent  the GaN HEMT damage during the assembling, testing or  transportation.
