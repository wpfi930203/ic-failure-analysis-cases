## Study of efficient design on latchup in 130nm BCD  technology for high voltage application  

Alwyn Rebello 1 , Jie (Jack) Zeng 1 , Kyong Jin Hwang 1 ,   Jianbo Zhou 2 , Mun Nam Chil 2 , Ong Shiang Yang 2 , Koo Jeoung Mo 2  1 DTE ESD EFUSE & LATCHUP, Globalfoundries, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park-D, Street-2, Singapore-738406  2 Power Development, Globalfoundries, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park-D, Street-2, Singapore-738406  Phone : (65)66703323, Email :  alwyn.rebello@globalfoundries.com

Abstract — We studied efficient design for high external  latchup (ELUP) robustness in 130nm BCD technology under  different Deep-Trench-Isolation (DTI) and N-EPI pickup  configurations (NCOMP). We show DTI is very effective in  reducing the space between I/O injector device and sensitive  logic device, which is an ideal requirement for a lower footprint  design with high latchup robustness. Furthermore, we  demonstrate the critical role of DTI, when NCOMP around high  voltage (HV) I/O FETs is biased at 0V, in latchup robustness of  power management designs.

Keywords—BCD, Deep Trench, High Voltage, Latchup,  Automotive

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

High latchup robustness of products (or designs) is  important in power management applications in BCD  technologies [1-4]. An optimized latchup design also helps to  achieve a floorplan with minimum distance between various  functional blocks. The devices in 130nm BCD technology are  present in n-doped epitaxial layer with very high doped   $\Nu+$  buried layer, and deep trench isolation (DTI) is employed to  ensure better immunity to parasitic coupling effects and  isolation between sensitive devices and power stages. In  130nm BCD, to avoid leakage issues, large N-EPI region  outside power stages is inadvertently tied to the Ground and  biased at 0V. This scenario may lead to grounded NWELL  latchup concern, like previous studies which have shown that  special circuit designs can lead to undesirable grounded  NWELL latchup concerns [5-6]. Importantly, high voltage  applications in 130nm BCD technology involves many high  side/ low side transistor switches in buck/boost circuit designs  and the parasitic latchup paths arising from different  combinations may pose latchup concern [1, 3].  To date, there  are very little reports available on the latchup characterization  in BCD technologies under grounded N-EPI (hot N-EPI)  scenario. In this report, we study the critical role of DTI and  N-EPI bias for efficient design free from any such latchup  concern.

Interestingly, I_trig at all  injector to detector spacing shows a significant increase when  DTI is present around the injector and detector. In the absence  of DTI, the NWELL region of both injector and detector are  electrically connected by N-Epi and therefore the electrons  injected from the N-Diode injector reach the SCR NWELL  easily at all injector to detector spacing, resulting in no  significant change in I_trig (\~61 mA) with increasing injtodet  spacing. ## III.   S UMMARY 

In summary, we showed DTI is very effective in  enhancing the latchup trigger current even at lower N diode  injector to sensitive detector spacing, which is desirable for  designs with high latchup robustness and lower footprint. We  studied the detrimental impact of HOT N-EPI regions around  the HV power FETs on latchup robustness, and  demonstrated the elimination of this latchup concern using  double DTI configuration, which is very pivotal in efficient  and robust power management designs.
