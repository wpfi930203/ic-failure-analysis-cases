## Case Study of High-Temperature Induced Slip  Defect Causing Overlay Issue  

K. H. Yip, K. J. Cheng, P. C. Ang, Z. Q. Mo  GLOBALFOUNDRIES (Singapore) Pte Ltd  60 Woodlands Industrial Park D Street 2, Singapore 738406  Phone : (65) 66701653, Email :  kimhong.yip@globalfoundries.com

Abstract — Lots of processes in Si device fabrication involve  high-temperature surroundings of up to   $\bf{1000^{o}C}$  , such as the  epitaxial growth of the Si epi layer. This high-temperature  process will be the main reason that causes Si-related defects  such as Si dislocation or slip defect. This defect may create  problems for subsequent wafer fabrication processes and in  severe cases may lead to device failure. A case study of high  temperature-induced slip defects was presented and discussed  in this paper. Confirmation of slip defect on affected wafer with  failure analysis finding helped to identify the root cause of the  issue. Hence, effective corrective action can be implemented to  prevent similar issues from reoccurrence.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

A larger wafer size is preferred in the wafer fabrication  process, as it will produce more samples per wafer and help to  reduce production costs. The gravitational and thermal  stresses will be higher in larger-size wafers, causing slip  defects more prone to happen during high-temperature  processes such as epitaxial growth of the Si epi layer.  Furthermore, the shrink in the feature size of IC devices will  make the advanced fabrication process more sensitive to slip  defects.

The high-temperature fabrication process will induce slip  defects when gravity-dependent stress and thermal stress  exceed the critical shear stress. A few reasons that caused slip  defects to occur have been considered as follows: i) wafer  surface and wafer edge fault, ii) residual strains in the wafer,  iii) thermal stress due to temperature gradient across the  wafer. The relation between the factor and the occurrence of  slip defects has been studied extensively for the past few  decades. Factors like wafer size, wafer vertical spacing, and  boat structure that affect thermal and gravitational stresses  which can cause slip defects been studied by Nilson et al. [1].  A method to prevent slip defects occurrence by calculating the  critical shear stress of silicon wafers containing oxygen  precipitation has been proposed by Jurkschat et al. [2]. Fischer  et al. established theoretical calculations of slip defects  generation conditions in silicon wafers for both vertical and  horizontal furnaces [3]. Fujise et al. conducted an  investigation   on   the   relationship   between   oxygen  concentration and slip defects in silicon wafers and found that  the value of critical stress will be affected by strain rate [4]. A  case study of high-temperature process-induced slip defects  will be presented and discussed in this paper.

## II.    E XPERIMENTAL 

A wafer with a line defect at the wafer edge (as seen in Fig.  1) has been subjected to failure analysis to determine the root  cause of the issue. Silicon defects etch was done with Secco  etch on both surface and cross-section samples. Crosssectioning samples were prepared by cleaving with SELA  MC600i [5]. Inspection of post-Secco etch samples was done  using the Thermal Fisher Verios G4 Scanning Electron  Microscopy system. A  $3\mathrm{-}\,\upmu\mathrm{m}$   thick TEM lamella was prepared  for better viewing of silicon dislocation. Sample preparation

of the lamella was done by FIB milling using a Thermal  Scientific Helios 5 HP dual beam system [6]. TEM viewing  was performed with FEI Titan Transmission Electron  Microscopy [7]. Surface profiling across the line defect was  carried out using the KLA Tencor Alpha Step IQ profilometer.

TEM analysis was engaged to confirm whether there is  silicon dislocation present at the line defect region. To  capture silicon dislocation effectively, a 3um thick lamella  with in-situ lift-out (Fig. 6) was prepared for TEM viewing.  No signature of silicon dislocation was observed with TEM  analysis at the line defect region till 6um deep into the silicon  epi layer as seen in Fig. 7. As both Secco etch and TEM  analysis were not able to find the failure nature of the line  defect, further analysis is needed. Surface profiling was  proposed to determine whether the line defect is related to  step height or surface roughness. A profilometer was used to  scan across the line defect and analysis data showed that there  is a step height of about 0.5um across the line defect as shown  in Fig. 8. This step height suggests that the line defect is a slip  defect which is likely caused by high temperature during  wafer fabrication process.

The line defect at the wafer edge was determined as a slip  defect by failure analysis. Based on this finding, inline  investigation will be focused on processes that involve high  temperatures. The investigation found that the silicon epi  layer deposition step of the two affected tools was tuned to  higher temperatures after preventive maintenance. However,  the etch/bake step did not get synchronized with the  deposition step temperature. This led to   $10\mathrm{{^oC}}$   lower than  baseline during etching causing the coating to accumulate  unevenly at certain pockets in the chamber. Due to uneven  coating, the wafer encounters non-uniform thermal stress at  localized locations within the wafer during the deposition  process. It is observed that the etch/bake temperature to  deposition temperature delta is higher at the wafer edge when  compared to the wafer center as shown in Fig. 9 a and Fig. 9  b. for tool 1 and tool 2 respectively. The high-temperature  delta at the wafer edge leads to higher thermal stress and  subsequently induces slip defect at the wafer edge. The step  height of the slip defect caused overlay issues in the  subsequent downstream process.

## IV.    C ONCLUSION 

Defective wafers were produced under high-temperature  processes, so it was highly suspected the line defect was  related to a silicon defect. However, both Secco etch and  TEM analysis observed no signature of silicon defect at the  line defect region. Confirmation of slip defect at the wafer  edge of the affected wafer by surface profiling provided  important information for root cause understanding. The  inline investigation focused on the high-temperature process  and found that unsynchronized temperature between  deposition and etch/bake after preventive maintenance  caused higher etch/bake to deposition temperature delta at the  wafer edge as compared to the wafer center. This caused  higher thermal stress and subsequently induced slip defects at  the wafer edge. The temperature-induced slip defect caused  overlay issues at the downstream process step.
