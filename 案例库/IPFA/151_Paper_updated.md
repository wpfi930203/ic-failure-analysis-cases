## Applications of TOF-SIMS Ion Color Mapping  Technology in Characterization of IC Al Alloys  and Wafer Fabrication Cleaning Process  Optimization   

Younan Hua 1,2 , Lei Zhu 1 , Lois Liao ,2 , Xiaomin Li 1,2,3 ,

1 Wintech Nano-Technology Services Pte Ltd, The Alpha #03-26, 10 Science Park Road,   Singapore Science Park II, Singapore 117684  2 Wintech-Nano (Nanjing) Co., Ltd. Floor 1, Building B, No.6, Mingyuan Road,   Yuhuatai District, Nanjing, China 210012  3 Wintech-Nano (Suzhou) Co., Ltd., Rm 507, Building 09, Northwest District, Nanopolis, No. 99, Jinjihu Ave,  Suzhou Industrial Park, Suzhou, China 215123  Phone : (65) 67777354  Email :  younan@wintech-nano.com

Abstract— Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry  (TOF-SIMS) can be used for high-end failure analysis and  materials analysis and characterization. It has high sensitivity,  large mass range, high detection efficiency, and full element  analysis. It can be applied to compositional analysis of organic  and inorganic materials of all elements and ion color imaging  analysis. In this paper, two practical application examples are  studied. We apply TOF-SIMS surface ion color mapping  analysis technology to study Al alloy materials used in  semiconductor integrated circuits and optimize cleaning  processes in wafer fabrication. These two application examples  demonstrated the analytical capabilities and application value  of TOF-SIMS analysis technology. Moreover, in the paper, the  method and chemical mechanism for optimizing cleaning  processes will be studied and discussed, especially for Al oxide  formation on Al metal surface during the cleaning process.

Keywords— Failure Analysis, TOF-SIMS, Ion Color Mapping Technology,  Al Alloy Characterization, Wafer Fab and  Cleaning  Process Optimization

## I.    I NTRODUCTION  

Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometer (TOFSIMS) is a multifunctional instrument that uses the flight time  of secondary ions to determine the ion charge-to-mass ratio.  It has the advantages of high sensitivity, large mass range,  high detection efficiency, full element analysis, and high  resolution. At present, time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometers are often used in the fields of high-end failure  analysis and material analysis, especially the analysis of both  organic and inorganic trace contaminants on the surface of  materials. It can not only perform mass spectrometry analysis  of contamination and depth profiling, but also perform highly  sensitive component color imaging. All these analytical  techniques are widely applied in semiconductor industries.       In wafer fabrication (fab), the traditional method is to use  vapor phase decomposition-inductively coupled plasmamass spectrometry (VPD-ICP-MS) and total reflection X-ray  fluorescence (TXRF) to analyze and evaluate the level of  contamination after the cleaning process. However, both  VPD-ICP-MS and TXRF have some disadvantages. For  example, using VPD-ICP-MS analysis technique, we have to  use chemicals (such as HF) to wash down the contamination

from the surface of the wafers and then the solution will be  analyzed using ICP-MS. It is with risk of cross contamination  during the sample preparation and analysis. While TXRF  technique is not suitable for analysis of the pattern wafers and  moreover limited to only analyze inorganic elements.      However,   TOF-SIMS   technique   will   be   no   cross  contamination as less sample preparation processes. This  paper attempts to apply TOF-SIMS to analyze and evaluate  the level of contamination after the cleaning process in wafer  fab, especially using ion color mapping analysis technology  to study Al alloy materials used in semiconductor integrated  circuits.

Compared to TXRF (total reflection X-ray fluorescence)  analyzer, TOF-SIMS (time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry)   technique   has   comparable   analytical  sensitivity, higher sensitivity for the analysis of light  elements, and a smaller analysis area. It is also suitable for  larger rough surfaces and surfaces with structure designs.  With the help of the sputtering ion gun, the time-of-flight  secondary ion mass spectrometer can also achieve in-depth  profiling. Working with different samples, the sputtering  conditions and detection conditions can be independently  adjusted and specifically optimized.

In this paper, we will apply the TOF-SIMS analysis  technology to discuss and demonstrate two practical  examples: applications of TOF-SIMS ion color mapping  technology in characterization of IC Al alloys and wafer  fabrication cleaning process optimization. In this study, we  will use sodium element as an example to control and  quantify the contamination level. In reality it can be used to  analysis for many elements. We choice sodium element as an  example as it is very common contaminant.

## II.   I NSTRUMENT AND  E XPERIMENTAL  C ONDITIONS 

The instrument we used in this study is: TOF-SIMS 5  model time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometer (TOFSIMS) manufactured by ionTOF company of Germany. The  vacuum in the analysis chamber is maintained below 2.0E-9  mbar to reduce the impact of air on sample surface oxidation  and to lower the detection limit of carbon, hydrogen and

oxygen. Two analytical methods, depth analysis and highresolution surface analysis, were applied in this study. In the  depth analysis, an ultrashort pulse  $\mathrm{Bi+}$   ion gun with an energy  of   $25\mathrm{keV}$   (current:   $1.0\mathrm{pA}$  , ion beam diameter: 3um, pulse  length: 11.7ns) was used as a primary ion gun to bombard the  sample surface to generate secondary ions. Another  $\mathrm{Cs}+$   ion  gun with an energy of 2keV (current: 100nA, ion beam  diameter: 20um) mills off the sample layer by layer to achieve  in-depth analysis. In high-resolution surface analysis, a   $\mathrm{Cs}+$  ion gun with a low energy of 1keV (current: 20nA, ion beam  diameter: 20um) was used to clean the sample surface to  remove the oxide layer on the sample surface and expose the  aluminum-copper alloy. Then, another long-pulse   $\mathrm{Bi+}$   ion  gun (current: 1.0pA, ion beam diameter:  $0.3\mathrm{{um}}$  , pulse length:  100ns) was used to bombard the sample surface to generate  secondary ions to obtain an ion color distribution map.

## III.   E XPERIMENTAL  R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSIONS 

In this paper, two practical application examples are  studied. We apply time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry surface ion color imaging analysis technology  to study aluminum alloy materials used in semiconductor  integrated circuits and optimize cleaning processes in wafer  manufacturing. From these 2 examples, it could demonstrate  the analytical capabilities and application value of time-offlight secondary ion mass spectrometry analysis technology.

## A:  Application of TOF-SIMS Ion Color Mapping Technology  in Studying of IC Al Alloys  

Aluminum alloys are widely used in the semiconductor  industry and wafer manufacturing due to their good electrical  properties, low density, and corrosion resistance. At present,  copper is one of the most commonly used alloy elements. The  concentration and distribution of copper in aluminum-copper  alloys will greatly affect the application performance of  aluminum alloy films. At this stage, doping aluminum alloys  with 0.5 atomic percent copper is a topic of great concern in  the semiconductor industry.

For example, doping an aluminum alloy with a small  amount of copper can greatly improve its electrical  properties. However, doping copper into aluminum can lead  to galvanic corrosion between aluminum-copper (Al-Cu)  binary metals. Such as, in the manufacturing of wafer discs  in the semiconductor industry, aluminum alloys doped with  0.5 atomic percent copper are often used on aluminum  bonding blocks. Thereby it greatly improves the electrical  conductivity. However, due to the addition of copper, the  aluminum bonding block often causes galvanic corrosion  between the aluminum-copper binary metal and causes some  microporous defects (called pinholes), which affects the  bonding between the aluminum bonding block and the gold  balls during assembly processes. Ultimately it causes nonadhesion (NSOP) problems [1-5]. Therefore, it is particularly  important to study the content and distribution of copper in  aluminum alloys.

As we all know, scanning electron microscopy/energydispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM/EDS) and transmission  electron microscopy/energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy  (TEM/EDS) can be used to detect the concentration and  distribution of copper in aluminum alloys. However, since the

detection limit of energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy  analysis is About   $0.1{\cdot}1\mathrm{at}\%$   (dependent on elements). For  alloys containing less than   $0.1{\cdot}1{\mathrm{at}}\%$   copper, it is difficult for  SEM/TEM/EDS analysis to give accurate analysis results.        In this study case, two different aluminum-copper alloy  samples were prepared/selected for analytical study:

Sample A: a 3um thick aluminum-copper alloy film  deposited on a silicon substrate, with a copper content of  approximately   $4.0\;\mathrm{at}\%$  ;

The results of surface ion color analysis by time-offlight secondary ion mass spectrometry are shown in Fig 1.         From the surface ion color mapping results of time-offlight secondary ion mass spectrometry (Fig. 1), it can be  clearly seen that sample A, a copper-aluminum alloy, is very  evenly distributed and shows almost no grains; while in  sample B, copper and aluminum are combined to form larger  grains. This also shows that the copper-aluminum alloy of  sample A has relatively poor electrical conductivity because  sample A no crystal grains  are  formed.  The copperaluminum alloy of sample B forms larger grains and has  relatively better electrical conductivity. This may be why the  semiconductor integrated circuit industries now generally  uses copper-aluminum alloys with the formula of Sample B  (Al-  $0.5\%$   Cu), to make chip metal lines and aluminum  bondpads.

However, nothing is perfect. For alloys using Al   $(0.5\%$  Cu), due to the uneven distribution of copper in the aluminum  alloy, it will combine with aluminum to form Al2Cu,  resulting in galvanic corrosion between the aluminum-copper  metals and bondpads [4 -5].

## B: Using TOF-SIMS Ion Color Mapping Technology to  Optimize Wafer Fab Cleaning Process  

In wafer disc manufacturing, contamination of metallic  and non-metallic materials can seriously impact quality and  yield. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), X-ray  photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), also known as electron  spectroscopy for chemical analysis (ESCA) and Auger  electron spectroscopy (AES) are widely used for analysing a  material’s surface chemistry. XPS can measure elemental  composition as well as the chemical and electronic state of  the atoms within a material. AES can be used to analyze some  contamination samples, but they are only suitable for  determining some relatively high-content contamination  components, such as   $0.1{\cdot}1.0\%$   and content above. But for  some low-level contaminants (ppm-ppb), time-of-flight  secondary ion mass spectrometry is a better choice. As such,  in this case study, we applied time-of-flight secondary ion  mass spectrometry to detect sodium ion contamination and  help determine and optimize an optimal wafer cleaning  process to ensure product quality and final yield.

For the analysis of organic and inorganic trace  contaminants on the surface of material samples, in addition  to providing ultra-low-level time-of-flight secondary ion  mass spectrometry analysis of contamination components,  we also use highly sensitive and high-resolution ion color  imaging technology to provide intuitive Analysis and  judgment. The analysis results of this case are summarized in  Fig. 2.

From Fig. 2, it can clearly be  seen the time-of-flight  secondary ion mass spectrometry analysis results of the  sample from no cleaning to 7 rinse cleanings in the wafer  cleaning process, as well as the sodium ion and aluminum ion  imaging diagrams.

We could see from Fig. 2 that if the wafer sample was not  cleaned, the image of time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry showed high contamination of sodium ions,  which had seriously affected the image of aluminum ions  (obviously gray). Therefore, cleaning and removal of sodium  ion contamination on the product surface must be carried out.  After cleaning the sample using the selected cleaning process  and reagents, the sample was analyzed by time-of-flight  secondary ion mass spectrometry.

The results of time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry analysis of the cleaned samples showed that  sodium ion contamination decreased with the number of  cleaning treatments. From Fig. 2 we can clearly see that from  no cleaning to 1 rinse cleaning, 3 rinse cleanings and 7 rinse  cleanings, the contamination of sodium ions decreased as the  number of cleaning processes increased. The time-of-flight  secondary ion mass spectrometry signal of sodium ions  decreased from 1.42E5 (no cleaning) to 1.18E4 (1-rinse  cleaning), 9.31E3 (3-rinse cleanings) and 4.91E3 (7-rinse  cleanings).

When selecting the best wafer cleaning process, from the  perspective of sodium ion contamination, the best result  should be 7 -rinse cleanings. However, we believe that when  selecting the best cleaning process, we should not only  consider removing sodium ion contamination, but also ensure  that the quality of the product is not affected by the cleaning  process. In this case, we must take into account the impact of  the cleaning process on the aluminum wire. Based on the  analysis results of aluminum by time-of-flight secondary ion  mass spectrometry, we recommended choosing a 3-rinse  cleanings process instead of a 7-rinse cleanings process.  Because we believe that although the contamination of  sodium ions could be further reduced by using a 7-rinse  cleanings process, after 7-rinse cleanings process, the  aluminum wire had been changed and an aluminum oxide  layer had been formed on the surface.

After performing studies, we think that the chemical  mechanism may be that: during the cleaning process,  aluminum wire will produce some aluminum ions:

$$\mathrm{Al}\;\rightarrow\;\mathrm{Al}^{+3}\;\;+\;3\mathrm{e}^{-}$$
(1)

During the cleaning process, the aluminum wire is  exposed to water and oxygen. Water and oxygen will have  the following chemical reaction:

O

$$\mathrm{O}_{2}+2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}+4\mathrm{e}^{\cdot}\rightarrow\ 4\,\mathrm{OH}^{\cdot}$$
(2)

Therefore, the OH -  ions in the Eqn. (2) will react  chemically with the   $A l^{+3}$   from Eqn. (1) and form aluminum  hydroxide   $[\mathrm{Al}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}]$  .

After dehydration, an oxide layer (Al2O3) will be formed  on the surface of the aluminum wires:

$$\begin{array}{r}{4\mathrm{Al}+3\mathrm{O}_{2}+6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}\rightarrow4\mathrm{Al}^{+3}+12\mathrm{OH}\rightarrow4\mathrm{Al}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}\downarrow}\\ {\downarrow\ -6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}}\end{array}$$
(3)

2Al2O3    （Formation of Al oxide layer on the surface of Al wires）

We can clearly see from Fig. 2 that the ion color mapping  results of time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry  analysis were the best (brightest) when the aluminum wire  was cleaned 3-rinse cleanings process. However, after 7-rinse  cleanings process, the aluminum wire had changed and an Al  oxide layer had formed on the surface of Al wires, and the ion  color imaging of time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry had become worse (darker). Therefore, we  recommended   a   3-rinse   cleanings   process   as   the  contamination had been mostly cleaned away and the Al  wires were in good quality.

The actual application results in manufacturing also  confirmed the accuracy of selecting 3-rinse cleanings  process. Because the experimental results showed that the 3- rinse cleaning process could not only clean most of the  sodium contamination on the product surface, but also  reduced the amount of sodium contamination below the  allowable control limit of the production line. It also ensured  that the quality of the product (Al wire) was not affected by  the cleaning process.

Furthermore, the production cost of the 3-rinse  cleanings process is much lower than the production cost of  the 7-rinse cleanings process.

## IV.   C ONCLUSIONS   

Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry can be  used for high-end failure analysis and materials analysis. It  has high sensitivity, large mass range, high detection  efficiency, and full element analysis. It can be applied to  compositional analysis of organic and inorganic materials of  all elements and ion color imaging analysis. In this paper, two  practical application examples are studied. We apply time-offlight secondary ion mass spectrometry surface ion color  imaging analysis technology to study aluminum alloy

materials used in semiconductor integrated circuits and  optimize cleaning processes in wafer manufacturing. These  two application examples fully demonstrated the analytical  capabilities and application value of time-of-flight secondary  ion mass spectrometry analysis technology.
