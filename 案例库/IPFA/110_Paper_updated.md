## Studies and Joint Application of TOF-SIMS   and XPS in Material Analysis and Characterization  

Younan Hua 1,2 , Lei Zhu 1 , Lois Liao ,2 , Xiaomin Li 1,2,3 ,

1 Wintech Nano-Technology Services Pte Ltd, The Alpha #03-26, 10 Science Park Road,   Singapore Science Park II, Singapore 117684  2 Wintech-Nano (Nanjing) Co., Ltd. Floor 1, Building B, No.6, Mingyuan Road,   Yuhuatai District, Nanjing, China 210012  3 Wintech-Nano (Suzhou) Co., Ltd., Rm 507, Building 09, Northwest District, Nanopolis, No. 99, Jinjihu Ave,  Suzhou Industrial Park, Suzhou, China 215123  Phone : (65) 67777354  Email :  younan@wintech-nano.com

Abstract — In this paper, these two analysis techniques of  TOF-SIMS and XPS will be jointly applied to the analysis of the  surface components of LED lead frame materials, especially the  analysis of the trace element selenium (Se). We will apply TOFSIMS for confirming the existence of the trace element Se and  ensuring and improving the accuracy of XPS quantitative  analysis results. XPS is often used to quantitatively analyze trace  elements, but for some elements with complex peak positions, it  is confusing and challenging to accurately determine the  elements. However, through TOF-SIMS confirmation analysis,  XPS’s misjudgment of trace elements can be avoided, thereby  obtaining more accurate quantitative analysis results. This  paper successfully used TOF-SIMS and XPS testing methods to  accurately and quantitatively analyze the surface components  (Se, etc.) of the LED lead frame samples.  TOF-SIMS has better  sensitivity than XPS, but its quantitative analysis is not as good  as XPS. The combination of the two techniques makes full use  of their respective advantages, not only to avoid missing  elements detected by XPS, but also to obtain more accurate XPS  quantitative analysis results.

Keywords—Failure analysis, XPS, TOF-SIMS, Selenium and  LED lead frame.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION  

In failure analysis and material characterization, Time-offlight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) and Xray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) have been widely used  [1-3]. Generally, the advantage of TOF-SIMS is that its  detection limit can reach ppm ( parts per million)  level or  lower. It is particularly suitable for the analysis of trace  elements or components. However, in the absence of the  standard samples, TOF-SIMS is currently unable to provide  quantitative analysis. The analytical advantage of XPS is that  it can perform quantitative analysis without standard  samples, but the detection limit of XPS is much higher than  that of TOF-SIMS, which is about  $0.1{\cdot}1.0\,\mathrm{at\%}$   (depend on the  elements analyzed) [4]. However, the qualitative analysis of  some trace elements is sometimes difficult using XPS,  especially for some elements with more complex  characteristic peaks. As such, we would propose to joint  application of TOF-SIMS and XPS.

In this paper, these two analysis techniques TOF-SIMS  and XPS will be jointly applied to the analysis of the surface  components of LED lead frame materials, especially the

analysis of the trace element selenium (Se). We will apply  TOF-SIMS for confirming the existence of the trace element  Se and ensuring and improving the accuracy of XPS  quantitative analysis results.

## II.   A NALYTICAL  I NSTRUMENTS AND  E XPERIMENTAL  C ONDITIONS   

In this study, XPS and TOF-SIMS techniques are used for  joint   application   in   failure   analysis   and   material  characterization.

## XPS Surface Analysis   

XPS is used to analyse the surface composition on the  LED lead frame. The model used for XPS analysis is Scanning  XPS Microscope PHI Quantera II (Aluminium source at  1486.6eV) from Japan. The sputtering rates were calibrated  against silica standards.

TOF-SIMS surface analysis:

TOF-SIMS is used to further confirm the presence of Se  and ensure the accuracy of XPS quantitative analysis results.  The model used for TOF-SIM analysis is ToF.SIMS 5  from  Germany. The primary ion beam used in TOF-SIMS surface  analysis is  $\mathrm{Bi+}$  , the energy is  $25\mathrm{keV}$  , the pulse current is 1pA,  the time of each scan is   $100\,\upmu\mathrm{s}$  , the analysis time is 100 s, and  the mass range of the collected ions is 1amu-800amu.

## III.   J OINT  A PPLICATION OF  XPS  AND  TOF-SIMS   -   A NALYSIS  R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSION   

In this study, when XPS is used to analyze the surface  materials of LED lead frames, it is found that this analysis  method is somewhat confusing and challenging for the  characterization of some trace elements. In particular, it is  easy to cause misjudgments for some trace elements with  more complex characteristic peaks.

For example, Fig. 1 shows XPS spectra collected on an  LED lead frame: (A): full spectrum scan, (B): high resolution  narrow spectrum scan in the 120-190eV range. In the highresolution spectrum Fig. 1 – (B), one can see that there are  obvious 3 peaks at 137eV, 161eV and 177eV. When we used  an online computer program to automatically identify these  peaks, the results showed that they were respectively  identified as the element Lead   $\mathrm{(Pb)}$  , Sulphur (S) and  Zirconium (Zr) elements.

For example, the peak positions of Se3d  (57eV [5]) and Ag4p (60eV [5]) are close, as Ag is the main  component of LED lead frame. Therefore, when the content of Se

is low, it will be more difficult to be identified by XPS  technique.

In order to confirm the above speculation, we proposed to  use TOF-SIMS for further elemental confirmation. Using the  selected analysis parameters, we used TOF-SIMS to analyze  the sample and the test results are shown in Fig. 2.

## V.   C ONCLUSIONS   

XPS is often used to quantitatively analyse trace elements,  but for some elements with complex peak positions, it is  confusing and challenging to accurately determine the  elements. However, through TOF-SIMS confirmation  analysis, XPS’s misjudgement of trace elements can be  avoided, thereby obtaining more accurate quantitative  analysis results. This paper successfully used TOF-SIMS and  XPS testing methods to accurately and quantitatively analyse  the surface components (Se, etc.) of the LED lead frame.
