## Analysis of Different Impact of LDD Missing and  Silicide Missing Defects on MOSFET Characteristic  

Shijun Zheng 1\* , Haijiao Bian 1\*  1 Failure Analysis Laboratory, NXP Semiconductors (Tianjin) Ltd, No.15 Xinghua Avenue, Xiqing District, Tianjin, China  Phone: (86)13702172070, Email: Shijun.zheng_1@nxp.com  \* The authors contributed to the work equally.

Abstract —Metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor  (MOSFET) is regarded as a most important modern electronic  semiconductor device that is widely used in automobile chips.  Nanoprobing analysis is considered to be an effective  methodology to detect and localize lightly doped drain (LDD)  missing and Ni- silicide missing defects hidden in failed  MOSFET. It reveals that above two structural defects exhibit  different impact on MOSFET transfer characteristic, so we  offer analysts idea to select an optimal physical analysis solution  especially critical for failure analysis (FA) success of end-user  returned single sample. Current-voltage (I-V) characteristic  shows that LDD missing defect causes 50-fold to around 100-fold  on-state current drop while silicide missing defect just generates  current decrease by about 50-fold or less. In addition, LDD  missing defect does not affect junction I-V characteristic. In  contrast, silicide missing defect increases drain (source) contact  resistance. Silicide missing increases series resistance at W-Si  interface while LDD missing brings severe hot electron effect to  device. Considering physical analytical methods, wet chemical  stain is a preferred technique to localize LDD missing site  whereas the silicide missing place contained in a lamella is  identified by transmission electron microscope (TEM). In  conclusion, nanoprobing analysis combined with right physical  analysis techniques correlates two failure mechanisms with  MOSFET low on-state current anomaly theoretically and  experimentally. The study enables wafer fabs to find out the root  cause of subtle defects in the front-end-of-line (FEOL) process.

Keywords—Nanoprobing analysis, LDD missing defect,  silicide missing defect, MOSFET, failure mechanism.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Over past years, failure analysis attracts more and more  attention as major automobile chip suppliers and wafer fabs  pursue for zero defect in very large-scale integration (VLSI)  products [1]. This results in continuous demanding of defect  reduction on silicon wafers to improve manufacturing yield  and chip reliability. Following the trend, nanoprobing analysis  grows to be an indispensable part of failure analysis workflow  since physical analysis without the assistance of nanoprobing  is less efficient in finding the fault that constrains device  reliability [2-4]. In other words, failure analysis will need to  be repeated on more devices and thus give rise to relatively  low success possibility. Suppose there is one failed sample, it  is critical to select right method to localize subtle defect.  Relying on nanoprobing analysis, analysts can isolate fault in  device level because nanoprober system uses multiple probing  positioners that can be manipulated and precisely placed on  the sample to directly characterize semiconductor device. That  merit overcomes the complexity of investigation of MOSFET  failure origins. Various resistive defects lower device driving  current that accounts for a considerable part of common faults.  In consequence, it becomes urgent to explore FA solution to  isolation of resistive defect in MOSFET. Here we find that  LDD missing and silicide missing defects lead to low on-state  current failures. However, they affect MOSFET characteristic

differently. The case study reveals that silicide missing defect  lowers on-state current by about 50-fold or less. LDD missing  defect results in MOSFET on-state current reduction by over  50-fold to around 100-fold. In addition, I-V characteristic of  drain-source junction indicates that silicide missing causes  current reduction by a half, but LDD missing does not induce  junction turn-on current decline. It is concluded that silicide  missing increases series resistance at W-Si interface while  LDD missing causes severe hot electron effect to device. The  quantitative analysis promotes understanding of two different  failure mechanisms impact on MOSFET performance.

## II.   E XPERIMENTS   

In the section, SEM-based nanoprobe system (FEI, nProber  II) is introduced. It integrates a Zeiss Gemini SEM to provide  high-resolution image at low electron accelerating voltage  (0.5kV). Low ebeam energy eliminates the irradiation effect  on MOSFET parameters. SEM owns maximum 100um beam  shift ability to help user observe wide area. The merit is  essential for the nanoprobe system because it guarantees  probe moving to destination over a large range. In addition,  eight probes are controlled by advanced nano-manipulators,  so it enables characterization of MOSFET and CMOS gates  when the probes are connected to Keithley 4200A parameter  analyzer. In our study, twelve failed chips of CMOS 40nm  technology node are examined. Electrical failure analysis  (EFA) finds electrical fault by systematic diagnosis and soft  defect localization (SDL) [5-6]. Later, the chips are delayered  to contact (CT) layer by the manual polishing method. The  sample preparation is described as following. The chip is  supplied on a flip-chip BGA package technology, so the  backside of die should be mechanically thinned to 80um  thickness for EOP analysis. After EFA work ends, the chip is  adhered to a dummy silicon plate with epoxy; perform facelapping from solder ball side with silicon carbide paper until  C4 bumps are exposed; remove underfill surrounding the die  in the way of dripping fuming nitric acid; immerse the die in  a customed acid mixture at room temperature to remove C4  balls; swash away residual acid with acetone quickly to stop  chemical over-etching; remove polyimide and passivation  layers under C4 bumps by applying reactive ion etching  (RIE). Different Allied ®  polishing clothes and slurry  combinations are selected alternatively for removal of high-k  and low-k layers while keeping good planarity. Eventually,  they are cleaned to remove undesired organic contamination.  Otherwise, undesired carbon deposition on sample increases  contact resistance. Our basic measurement configuration is  that four probe-tips of curvature radius of 50nm are put in  contact with MOSFET gate, source, drain and well terminals.  A semi-automatic procedure in system is appliable for soft  landing of probe-tip on target regions and prevention of the  probe from collision during workflow running.

## III.   R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

Nanoprobing analysis is performed to localize physical defect  in failed bitcell. We focus on collecting I-V relationship at CT  layer and studying I-V behavior on the condition that  LDD  missing and silicide missing in MOSFET because the defect  only alters the resistance and results in current variation. In the  section, MOSFET transfer characteristic in the linear-region  and saturation-region is studied under the forwards-reversed  bias condition.

## Discussion of failure mechanism for silicide missing  

Two failure mechanisms correlate the silicide missing and  LDD missing induced low on-state current in MOSFET are  introduced in the section. It is known that Ni-silicide covering  the entire source/drain regions is used to reduce the contact

resistance at the interface of tungsten (W) plug and heavily  doped silicon, and thus increases the driven current, thereby  the RC time constant is reduced as expected. A contact with  low contact resistivity permits a high flux of charged carriers  to transmit across the metal/semiconductor interface at a high  rate. Suppose the silicide layer disappears due to fab process  problem, tungsten plug (W) is in contact with heavily doped  silicon, and they form ohmic metal-semiconductor interface  as shown in Fig. 8(a). Although the junction between metal  and heavily doped semiconductor behaves as low-resistance  ohmic contact, it is not optimal enough. Schottky barrier  generates a series resistance at W-Si interface. Based on  semiconductor theory [7], metal-semiconductor specific  contact resistivity (ρ) is considered to be a major contributor  to source/drain resistance. ρ is expressed by equation (1).

$$\begin{array}{r}{\rho_{c}\propto e x p(\frac{4\pi\Phi_{B N}}{q h}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{s i}\cdot m^{*}}{N_{\mathrm{d}}}})}\end{array}$$

In the equation (1),   $h$   is Planck constant.  $m^{*}$   is effective mass,   $N_{\mathsf{d}}$   is surface doping concentration,   $N_{\mathrm{d}}{\approx}10^{20}\mathrm{cm}^{3}$  ,   $\mathrm{T{=}}300\mathrm{K}$  ,   $q\mathrm{{}\phi_{B N}{=}0.67e V}$   [8]. The calculated ohmic contact resistivity  (ρ ohmic ) between metal and heavily doped silicon is typically   $\uprho_{\mathrm{ohmic}}{=}10^{-6}\ \Omega{\cdot}\mathrm{cm}^{2}$   [9]. In addition, Ni-silicide has specific  contact resistivity of    $\uprho_{\mathrm{silicate}}{\approx}2.5{\times}10^{\cdot8}\Omega{\cdot}\mathrm{cm}^{2}$   [10]. By  calculation, theoretical maximum ratio of ρ ohmic /ρ silicide  is  about 40. In terms of sheet resistance, a literature states that  silicide reduces the sheet resistivity of   ${\mathfrak{n}}^{+}\left({\mathrm{or\;}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}\right)$   source-drain  region by a factor of ten [8]. So, the theoretical estimation  agrees with measurement data approximately.

LDD structure is designed to minimize breakdown and hot  electron effect in MOSFET. In an LDD design, there is a low  n-type doping concentration near channel region and a high ntype doping concentration close to drain and source regions.  The graded doping profile reduces depletion region width in  drain and source junctions. LDD decreases the peak electrical  field magnitude within depletion region. Therefore, the hot  electron in the depletion region does not gain enough kinetic  energy to affect MOSFET adversely [11]. Otherwise, mass of  hot electron gaining high energy from depletion region is  injected to the gate oxide layer and trapped as fixed oxide  charge as shown in Fig. 8(b). This process increases MOS flat  band voltage, and therefore   $\mathrm{V_{T}} $  [11]. With respect to  measurement result, V T  drift reaches  $+300\mathrm{mV}$   approximately.  It implies that severe hot electron effect happens in measured  nMOSFETs. Fig. 9 shows that in linear-region, single LDD  missing or double LDD missing does not change transfer

characteristic   symmetry dramatically. The data is consistent  with failure mechanism that   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$  drift mainly depends on the  hot electron effect severity no matter LDD missing locations.  However, above explanation is not applicable for MOSFET in  saturation-region. Fig. 10 shows the asymmetric MOSFET  transfer characteristic in saturation-region. LDD eliminates  hot-electron effect and gives an increase in drain resistance, so  the absence of LDD reduces series resistance [12]. nMOSFET  on-state current is the highest when single LDD missing  location is near the drain biased with 1V (blue curve in Fig.  10). The device pinch-off region coincides with single LDD  missing region. It is due to that LDD absence can generate  high peak electric field in the depletion region accelerates  electron to higher velocity, so the on-state current is high. If  the pinch-off region coincides with LDD region, LDD lowers  the peak electric field magnitude in the depletion region, so  the electron velocity is not as high as that without LDD. As a  result, the on-state current is relatively low (yellow curve in  Fig. 10). That is the origin of asymmetric transfer  characteristic. Note that double LDD missing gives rise to  higher on-state current than that single LDD missing in linearregion (Fig. 9). Overall, in saturation-region, the condition of  biasing N3 drain terminal together with single LDD missing  nearby produces highest on-state current. In contrast, on-state  current is lowest when N3 source is biased with 1V because  hot electron effect occurrence near N3 drain induces V T  drift.  The final physical analysis result is shown in Fig. 6. Note that  for N3 nMOSFET, single LDD is missed near drain region.  For N1 nMOSFET, double LDD are missed in drain-source  region. It agrees with the fact that transfer characteristic in  saturation region becomes asymmetric in N1 and N3.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION   

In this paper, we investigate two kinds of structural defects  with distinct failure mechanisms that lead to very low driving  current in failed MOSFETs. One type is Ni-silicide missing  defect. It produces high resistance at source or drain junctions  and it reduces MOSFET on-state current by 50-fold at most.  The current decrease is mainly due to ohmic contact resistance  increase. The defect isolation relies on precise TEM analysis.  The other type is LDD missing defect. It alters MOSFET   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$  obviously .  V T  drifts results in current reduction by about 50- fold to 100-fold. Because of that, a boundary condition is  firstly found to differentiate silicide missing and LDD missing  defects that matches with physical analysis results. In addition  to above statistical analysis, the failure mechanism for LDD  missing defect is discussed from the perspective of symmetric  transfer characteristic in the linear-region of MOSFET and  asymmetric transfer characteristic in the saturation-region OF  MOSFET. The underlying reason is that hot electron effect  takes place near drain-source region without LDD structure.  Hot electron effect changes MOS flat band voltage and  therefore the V T . LDD missing location is successfully found  by wet chemical stain analysis technique. In conclusion,  nanoprobing analysis combined with right physical analysis  technique correlates two different failure mechanisms with  MOSFET I-V behavior theoretically and experimentally. The  novel finding gives a reliable guide to distinguish silicide  missing and LDD missing defects so that guarantees failure  analysis success of similar subtle defects in the future.
