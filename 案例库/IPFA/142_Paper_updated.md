## Delamination Failure Analysis on FCBGA Package  with Heat Spreader   

Yanfei Zhao 1 , Tuanqiao  $\mathrm{{Nu}}^{1}$  , Zhiyuan Wang 1 , Shangsi Long 2 , Yazhou Zhou 1 , Lei Zhu 2 , Xiaomin Li 1,2  1 Wintech-Nano (Suzhou) Co., Ltd.,Rm507, Building 09, Northwest District, Nano City, No. 99, Jinjihu Ave,   Suzhou Industrial Park, Suzhou, China 215123.  2 Wintech Nano-Technology Services Pte Ltd, The Alpha #03-26, 10 Science Park Rd,   Singapore Science Park II, Singapore, 117684  Phone : (86)-512-62800006, Email :  zhaoyanfei@wintech-nano.com

Abstract  — With continuous increase in computing demand  in almost all semiconductor field, Flip-chip ball grid array  (FCBGA) packages are widely used. Most of failure analysis on  FCBGA were focused on the thermal performance and  reliability of thermal interface materials (TIM) in FCBGA  package. In this study, we focused on the delamination failure of  the FCBGA package with heat spreader after high temperature  operating life (HTOL) test.  Combined X-ray photoelectron  spectroscopy (XPS) and Time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry (TOFSIMS) technique has been found to be an  effective method for surface contamination analysis on FCBGA  packages. The results revealed that there is indeed a thin  siloxane related contamination on top surface of the failed heat  spreader, causing the delamination failure after HTOL test. In  addition, it is suggested that this failure may be reworkable by  applying Ar/O2 plasma cleaning. Further verification is in  progress.

Keywords—FCBGA package, heat spreader, XPS, TOFSIMS,  delamination, HTOL

## I.   I NTRODUCTION 

In order to meet the dramatically increase in capacity and  power requirements in the advanced integrated circuits (ICs),  Flip-chip ball grid array (FCBGA) packages [1-4] are widely  used in computing, networking, communication, consumer  electronics, auto-motive and so on. A metal heat spreader was  necessarily attached on the top of the FCBGA package for  efficient heat dissipation to ease the significant heat  generation in the flip chip devices. In this way, the die  junction temperature can be maintained at a relative low  level. The heat spreader also acts as a mechanical protection  to the die. Most failure analysis were focused on the thermal  performance and reliability of thermal interface materials  (TIM) in FCBGA package [5-7]. There are few studies on the  material failure analysis on heat spreader.

This study is focused on the delamination failure on the  FCBGA package with heat spreader after high temperature  operating life (HTOL) test [8]. The heat spreader is attached  on top of the die with thermal interface material above the  die. Structure adhesive is dispensed around perimeter of the  substrate for the adhesion of the heat spreader. However, after  HTOL reliability test, for a certain kind of heat spreader,  structure adhesive debonding occurs. By comparing different  kinds of heat spreader, the structure adhesive itself is  eliminated for causing the problem, whereas the problem  pinpoints to the heat spread material itself.

In this study, we performed both X-ray photoelectron  spectroscopy (XPS) and Time-of-flight secondary ion mass  spectrometry (TOFSIMS) to analyze the root cause of  adhesive debonding in FCBGA package after HTOL test  (Junction Temperature  $\mathrm{Tj}=125\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  ). The surface morphology

of the samples was observed by atomic force microscopy  (AFM). Five samples, including two different kinds of heat  spreader from different suppliers were analyzed. Based on the  XPS results, silicon was the only additional element found on  the surface of heat spreader from supplier A, where the  debonding failure occurs. TOFSIMS analysis further  confirmed that silicon exists in the form of siloxane, which  may come from the metal cleaning solvent. To further study  the failure mechanism, the incoming heat spreader from  supplier A without (w/o) cleaning process and the failed heat  spreader cleaned by Argon/Oxygen plasma were measured  for comparison. The results revealed there is indeed a thin  siloxane related contamination on top surface of the heat  spreader from supplier A after metal cleaning process.  This  failure could be reworkable by applying additional Ar/O2  plasma cleaning.

We performed both XPS and TOFSIMS surface analysis  to analyse the surface contamination on different heat spreader  samples.

XPS was carried out by using a Thermofisher Nexsa G2  system equipped with a monochromatic Al Kα (1486.6 eV)  X-ray source, to obtain the spectra of all samples. The base  pressure of the ultra-high vacuum chamber is about   $2\mathrm{~x~}10.9$  Torr. The analysis area is 200 um diameter. The core level  spectra were subtracted by a Shirley-type background and  fitted using combined Gaussian and Lorentzian line shapes.  The binding energy calibration was performed by using gold  (Au), silver (Ag), and copper   $(\mathrm{Cu})$   standard samples by  setting Au   $4\mathrm{f}_{7/2}$  , Ag 3d 5/2 , Cu 2p 3/2  peaks at binding energies

of   $83.96\pm0.1\ \mathrm{eV}$  ,   $368.21\pm0.1\ \mathrm{eV}$   and   $932.62\pm0.1\,\ \mathrm{eV},$  ,  respectively. The Fermi edge was calibrated using a pure and  in-situ  cleaned silver (Ag) standard sample and setting the  binding energy at   $0.00~\mathrm{eV}$  . To further calibrate the charging  effect, the spectra were referenced to the peak position of C1s  core levels to   $284.8\ \mathrm{eV}$   for each sample. The relative atomic  concentrations Ci (at   $\%$  ) were derived from the XPS narrow  scan spectra. The values were calculated by measuring  elemental integral area in the narrow scans:

$$\begin{array}{r}{C_{i}=\frac{I_{i}/S_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}/S_{i}}}\end{array}$$
Where  $\mathrm{{I_{i}}}$   is the integral area of the element i from the test  specimen.   $\mathrm{{S_{i}}}$   is the relative sensitivity factor of the element.

TOFSIMS data were collected using a TOF-SIMS 5  instrument (ION-TOF GmbH) in dual-beam mode. For  surface analysis, a short-pulsed (10 ns) primary ion beam  (Bi 1+ , 25keV energy,   ${\sim}1$   pA measured sample current) was  raster-scanned over a   $20\,\times\,20\,\,\upmu\mathrm{m}^{2}$    area. The analysis was  acquired at a base pressure of about   $5\times10^{-8}$   mbar with the  primary ion beam set in high current bunched mode. All  detected secondary ions had either negative or positive  polarity and the mass resolution is more than 4000 (m/δm).

## III.   R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

## A.   FCBGA Package  

The flip chip is packaged in a FCBGA with heat spreader.  A standard FCBGA with heat spreader package is illustrated  in Fig. 1. The flip chip is attached on substrate by bump  soldered on substrate pad after reflow. Epoxy underfill filled  in the gap of bumps to protect the die after cured. Thermal  interface material is necessary to fill the gap between die top  surface and heat spreader inner surface, and it will provide  the main heat diffusion path for this type package. The heat  spreader is assembled on top of thermal interface material and  structure adhesive around perimeter of the substrate.

After HTOL test, the heat spreader is separated from the  substrate. The phenomenon only appeared in the heat  spreader from supplier A.

The elements quantification by XPS was summarized in  Table II. Overall comparison of the Failed and OK sample  noted that Chromium, Phosphorus, and Vanadium observed  only on the OK sample indicated that Cr, P, V are not the  contamination that caused the failure. S1 and S2 has higher  Ni than S3 since S1 and S2 are from one supplier and S3 is  from another supplier which may has slightly different layer  structure thickness (Ni should be part of the layer and thus is  not considered as contamination contribution). solid evidence to show the root cause of the delamination  failure. In contrast to Cl-, the silicon was only observed on  the surface of failed sample, which becomes a key point of  concern.

## C.   TOFSIMS analysis  

To further study the contamination caused delamination  failure on FCBGA package, TOFSIMS analysis was  performed.  TOFSIMS has the advantage of higher sensitivity  (ppm level) than XPS. It is also  able to detect all elements in  the Periodic Table including hydrogen and helium which  cannot be detected by XPS. Fig. 5 showed the ion fragments  detected by TOFSIMS.  $\mathrm{Si+}$  ,  $\mathrm{Cr+}$   and   $\mathrm{Ni+}$   ions intensities are  consistent with the results found by XPS.  $\mathrm{Na+}$  ,  $\mathrm{Mg+}$  ,  $\mathrm{K}+$  ,  $\mathrm{Ca+}$    and   $\mathrm{Fe+}$   ions are new findings in TOFSIMS measurement,  however, since the trend is inconsistent with the failure  phenomenon, that these ions may not be the source of  delamination. Further analysis from TOFSIMS results  showed some siloxane species   $\mathrm{(SiCH+)}$   enhanced on the  failed sample in comparison with the OK sample and control  sample. It is believed the delamination failure occurred when  there is siloxane related residual on the heat spreader surface  after metal cleaning process. In addition, the siloxane species   $\mathrm{(SiCH+)}$   can be obviously reduced by Ar/O2 plasma cleaning.

## D.   AFM analysis  

To gain further insights in the surface  morphology  of  different samples, atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker  Dimension Icon) was performed on the samples S1 to S5. The   $50\upmu\mathrm{m}\times50\upmu\mathrm{m}$   AFM surface morphology from S1 to S5 were  shown in Fig. 7 (a) - 7 (e) respectively.  The Arithmetic  roughness (Ra) and root mean square (RMS Rq) roughness  were summarized in Fig. 7(f). The results show that RMS Rq  roughness varies from 225 nm to 295 nm, indicating a rough  surface of heater spreader. Compared with the OK sample (S3)  and the control samples (S4-S5), the surface roughness of the  failed samples (S1-S2) are slightly smaller, which may  suggest the presence of siloxane related residual on the heat  spreader surface. More in-depth study is needed to reveal the  relationship between surface morphology and delamination  failure.

## IV.   CONCLUSIONS 

XPS combined with TOFSIMS technique is proven to be  an effective method for surface contamination issue on  FCBGA packages. TOFSIMS results are consistent with XPS  analytical results for the elements with the detection limit for  both technique. The results revealed that there is indeed a thin

siloxane related contamination on top surface of the failed heat  spreader, causing the delamination failure after HTOL test.  Additionally, it is suggested that this failure may be  reworkable by Ar/O2 plasma cleaning, and further verification  is needed.
