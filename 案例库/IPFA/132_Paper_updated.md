## Characterization analysis of organophosphate  contamination on Aluminum pad  

Frank Su, W.F. Hsieh, Ray Tu, Henry Lin, Vincent Chen, Irene Ou & Y.S. Lou  Ardentec Cooperation, No. 3, Gongye 3rd Rd., Hsin-Chu Industrial Park, Hukou Township,  Hsinchu County 303036, Taiwan, R.O.C.  Phone: +886-989-206293 ,  Email:  frank.su@ardentec.com

Abstract — This   study was a special case of contamination  deposited on the surface of aluminum pad. In the beginning,  dried water droplet-like deposits were found on aluminum pad  surface, and these deposits were distributed around the  periphery of the aluminum pad frame. The Optical Microscope  (OM) and Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) were first used  to inspect these contamination/corrosion products, then the  interface between the contamination/deposit and aluminum  metal was checked by cross-sectional Transmission Electron  Microscope (TEM). The TEM analysis indicated this case was a  contamination deposited on Al pad surface, not a corrosion  case.   At the same time, the composition of the contamination  deposit   was   examined   by   Energy   Dispersive   X-ray  Spectrometer (EDS) and Auger Electron Spectroscope (AES)  analysis. It was found that the deposits contain Phosphorus (P),  Potassium (K), Sodium (Na), Carbon (C) and Oxygen (O)  elements, and no corrosive fluorine (F) element was found.  Raman spectroscopy was used to identify the chemical bonding  of deposit. Moreover, the deposit was also analyzed by  TOF-SIMS, and the results also indicated the deposit contained  potassium, phosphorus, oxygen and carbon elements as EDS  results shown, and these results concluded this case was a novel  case of organophosphate contamination of aluminum pad. BG  tape that directly contacted with the wafers surface in BGBM  (Backside Grinding and Backside Metallization) process would  not induce this contamination event; the organic Airborne  Molecular Contamination (oAMC) did that. The reaction  mechanism of this event should be related to the hydrogen  bonding on the surface of the aluminum pad. The active  mechanism was proposed.

Keywords  –  Organophosphate contamination, oAMC, Organic  Airbone Molecular Contamination

##   I. I NTRODUCTION 

Aluminum pad play a special role in the semiconductor  electrical test and package process. Aluminum pad was the  entry point for measuring current, voltage, and resistance in  the electrical test process. And in the package process, the  aluminum pad is also the location of the connection wires,  both of them have very high requirements on the surface  properties of the aluminum pad, otherwise the corrosion  product and contamination deposit on aluminum pad would  have a great impact on the reliability and failure of the device.

Previous studies [1] have clearly shown that when  non-stick on pad (NSOP) occurs, it can cause the  component's leads to fail to bond to the aluminum pad.  However, this problem can lead to die fail whenever it occurs  on a component. Therefore, this problem has received more  attention from packaging and testing houses.

The main causes of corrosion and contamination of  aluminum pad are presented in the literature [2], which are  basically due to the process of forming aluminum pads or the  relative environment of wafer storage conditions. For

example, fluoride corrosion event on the surface of  aluminum pad were often due to the fluorine ions remaining  after the window opening process of the pads, or on the other  hands, the slow released fluoride ion into the cassette from  the latter part of the BPSG process to form corrosion. In  general, most of the abnormal events of aluminum pads,  corrosion is caused by the reaction between fluorine element  and aluminum element on the surface of the pad to produce  corrosion products. With the help of cross-sectional analysis  to observe the pad interface, it is clear that corrosive  materials are drilled down into the pads, while deposits are  organic materials that accumulate on the top surface of the  pads and tend to form bumps that develop above the pads.

In the past, the surface behavior of aluminum pad was  mainly analyzed by OM/SEM/AES etc. In recent years, the  studies   [3-4] have applied TEM/EDS and TOF-SIMS to  analyze the surface behavior of aluminum pads, which makes  the study of the deposition and corrosion of aluminum pads  more comprehensive.

In this study, since the deposition phenomenon is relatively  special, we would first inspect the special deposition pattern  by OM/SEM, then analyze the composition and possible  structure of the deposits by EDS/Auger/TEM//Raman  spectroscopy/TOF-SIMS, and finally deduce the possible  contamination mechanism and point out the possible cause of  the special deposits.

## II.   E XPERIMENTS AND  R ESULTS  

In this failure analysis case, the shape and distribution of  these dried water droplet-like defects on contaminated Al pad  were examined by Optical Microscope (OM, Leica  DM2700M) and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM,  Hitachi SU3500). And the chemical element composition of  defect   was   obtained   by   Energy   Dispersive   X-ray  Spectrometer (EDS, HORIBA EMAX x-act), which was  operated at 15 KeV electron energy. Auger Electron  Spectroscopy (AES, JEOL JAMP-9500F) was also applied  for chemical composition analysis of Al pad surface less than  10 nm. In addition, the Transmission Electron Microscopy  (TEM, FEI Talos F200X) operated at   $200\;\mathrm{KeV}$   could get the  interfacial information of defect and the corresponding  TEM-EDS line scan and mapping analysis were applied. In  order to identify the chemical bonding of organic defects, the  micro Raman spectroscopy (UniDRON) with 457 nm laser  source was performed. Time-of-Flight Secondary Ion Mass  Spectrometer (TOF-SIMS, ION-TOF M6 Plus) was applied  for molecular fragment information of defect.

## A.   OM and SEM/EDS Results   

Fig. 1 showed the OM and SEM image of contaminated  Al pad. Fig. 1(a), (b) clearly indicated that there were many  dried water droplet-like defects on top surface of Al pad,

which were remarked by black arrow. These defects were  circular shape and distributed around the periphery of the  aluminum pad frame. However, no defects were found near  the circuit probing (CP) mark. The OM images also revealed  that defects were only found in Al pad region but not in other  passivation layer region. Fig. 1(c) and (d) showed the EDS  analysis results of defect (Point-A) and clean pad region  (Point-B). The phosphorus (P), potassium (K) and sodium  (Na) elements were detected at defect region. The dominated  elements of defect were carbon and oxygen, the percentage  were 44.1 and   $35.6\ \%$   respectively, which represented that  this defect was organic material. On the other hand, no P, K  and Na elements were detected at clean pad region as shown  by Fig. 1(d). Therefore, the region near probing mark wasn’t  contaminated.

## C.   TEM/EDS Analysis Results   

In order to identify the interface status between defect and  Al pad surface, the cross-sectional TEM analysis was  applied. The results were shown in Fig. 3. The rough height  of this water droplet-like defect was about 711.0 nm. Fig. 3(b)  showed the high resolution TEM image of interface between  defect and Al metal. The interface was clear and there was a  thin layer with a thickness of about   $5.7\ \mathrm{nm}$   between defect  and Al metal. Previous article showed that the thickness of  aluminum oxide layer on a normal Al bond pad was about 3-5  nm [6]. Therefore, this thin layer between droplet-like defect  and Al metal was aluminum oxide, which represented that  there was no interfacial chemical reaction between Al metal  and defect but simply physical deposition.

In addition, Fig. 4 showed the TEM/EDS mapping results  of defect. The EDS mapping data clearly showed the  elements distribution inside the defect bulk. According to the  EDS mapping results, the P, K and Na elements were  detected inside the defect core. The corresponding EDS line  scan results were shown in Fig. 5. The EDS signal profile of  P, K, Na and O elements were stable with analysis depth,  which meant that the element distribution of this defect was  uniform. And the distribution was not concentrated or  stratified.

## D.   Raman Spectroscopy Analysis Results  

The EDS data reveled that this droplet-like defect was  organic species. A common analysis technique suitable for  organic matter was Raman spectroscopy, which useful for the  identification of chemical bonding of unknown organic  species. The Raman spectroscopy results were shown in Fig.  6. The Raman spectrum of defect and the corresponding  similar standard Raman spectrum in library and Fab’s  database were shown in Fig. 6 (a), (b). Table II summarized  the search results of Raman spectrum of defect. Because the  defect contained phosphorus element based on EDS results,  the row in Table II only displayed the phosphorus-containing  substances. This library search results meant that the defect  was organophosphate material.

In Fab’s database, the BG tape (Back-side grinding tape)  and Tyvek paper substances would directly contact with the  wafer surface in BGBM (Backside Grinding and Backside  Metallization)   process   and   packaging.   The   Raman  spectroscopy analysis of BG tape was to analyze the adhesive  layer. In Fig. 6 (b), the band around   $1700\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$   was assigned  to the   $\mathrm{C=O}$   symmetric stretching vibration [7], which was  related UV reaction during the de-taping process. There was  no   $1700\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{\textrm{cm}^{\!-\!1}}}$   band in Raman spectrum of defect, which  represented that the defect didn’t come from the BG tape.  Moreover, it was found   $17.70\ \ \%$   similarity of Raman  spectrum between defect and Tyvek paper.

## E.   TOF-SIMS Analysis Results  

TOF-SIMS was the most effective material analysis  technique for pad surface contamination, especially for the  extra thin contaminated layer. This organic defect was  detected in the backside grinding process, and the organic  substances that would contact with the wafer surface during  the entire process was BG tape. Therefore, it was important to  confirm whether the defect was BG tape residue. In this case,  the TOF-SIMS analysis were performed on normal,  abnormal pad and BG tape, the results were shown in Fig. 7.  Table III summarized the positive fragment intensity of  normal pad, abnormal pad and BG tape. The characteristic  positive fragments of defect were  $\mathrm{Na^{+}}$  ,  $\bar{\bf K}^{+}$   and  $\mathrm{K}_{2}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{O}_{2}{}^{+}$  , which  were different from the positive fragment of BG tape,   $\mathrm{CHS^{+}}$  and  ${\mathrm{C}}_{3}{\mathrm{H}}_{3}^{+}$  . This result meant that the defect didn’t come from  the BG tape residue.

## III.   D ISCUSSIONS 

The EDS and AES results clearly indicated that the defect  was organophosphate material. In BGBM process and

packaging of this abnormal sample, BG tape and Tyvek paper  were the organic materials that would directly contact with  the wafer surface. Because de-taping process was directional,  tape residues were generally fiber shape and mainly  distributed in grooves corner as shown in the Fig. 8, which  was different from this organic contamination case.

Fig. 8.  The cartoon diagram of de-taping residue formation.

Based on cross sectional TEM image, this defect didn’t  drilled down into the Al pad,   so there was no interfacial  chemical reaction between Al metal and defect but simply  physical deposition. The terminal group of clean aluminum  pad surface was Al-OH functional group which was  hydrophilic [2]. However, the main elements of defect were  carbon and oxygen, so the defect was regarded as  hydrophobic organic material that accumulated on the top  surface of the pads and tend to form dried water droplet-like  bumps as shown in OM and SEM image. In addition, these  defects were only found in Al pad region but not in other  passivation layer region. The outer valence electrons of  oxygen atom of organophosphate molecule could form  hydrogen bond with functional hydroxyl group (OH) of Al  pad surface. The bond energy of hydrogen bond was higher  than the bond energy of van der Waals force. Therefore  organophosphates tended to deposit on Al pad surface rather  than the surface of the passivation layer.

Previous research indicated the Al pad surface around the  probing mark had a higher fluorine concentration which  presented a donut/circle shape distribution [8]. Fluorine atom  could react with hydroxyl group (OH) of Al pad surface,  which caused the terminal group of Al pad surface to change  from Al-OH group to Al-F group that could prevent  hydrogen   bond   formation   between   organophosphate  molecules and Al pad. Therefore the organophosphate  molecules would not deposit on Al pad near the probing mark.  These defects were distributed around the periphery of the Al  pad frame. Because the shape and distribution of these  defects were different from the tape residue. Therefore, it was  believed that the reaction mechanism of defect formation was  the deposition of organic molecules rather than organic  residues from direct contact of BG tape.

The issue of air molecular contamination (AMC) was  widely discussed in cleanroom management. According to  AMC classification proposed in SEMI F21-95, AMC could  be divided into three categories: Molecular acid (MA),  Molecular base (MB), Molecular condensable (MC) and  Molecular dopant (MD). And the organophosphate belong to  MC category. Previous studies had shown that the volatile  organic compounds (VOCs) with concentrations as high as a  few parts-per-million could deposit on wafer [9], which was  regarded as Organic AMC (oAMC). The reaction mechanism

of this organic contamination event was deposition of  organophosphate material on Al pad. Previous research  indicated that the organic phosphate could be introduced into  polymer resin, which could provide the polymer matrix with  excellent antistatic properties [10]. These organic phosphate  would exist in mono-, di- or tri- forms and were transformed  into the corresponding salts, such as potassium   $\left(\mathbf{K}^{+}\right)$   or  sodium  $\mathrm{(Na^{+})}$  . And the EDX results of this study also showed  that these organic defects contained K and Na element. The  suspected root cause of this organic AMC case might be the  release of antistatic substances into the FAB environment and  deposition on the wafer surface. Therefore, for fab  management, we will inventory the antistatic materials in the  FAB and establish a corresponding Raman database and  finally evaluate the replacement of other antistatic materials.

In this study, a special organic contamination deposited  on aluminum pad was reported. The OM, SEM, AES, TEM,  EDS, Raman spectroscopy and TOF-SIMS were applied to  analyze this event. Based on the analysis results, this novel  pad contamination was due to the condensation of  organophosphate molecules. A failure mechanism was  proposed for this contamination event, the forming of  hydrogen bond of Al pad surface was supposed to be relative  to this contamination. The source of organic materials might  be the release of antistatic substances used in wafer storage.
