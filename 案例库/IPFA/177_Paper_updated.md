## Insights into Overcurrent-Induced Gate Breakdown  and Failure Analysis of 1200 V SiC MOSFETs   

Ruolan Wang, Mingsheng Fang, Li Song, Xuanyu Hu, Anli Yang, Yupu Wang, Kaixuan Li, Ting Liu, Cao Meng, Dandan Wang\*, Bing  Yang, Qichao Ding and Zhihong Mai   Hubei Jiufengshan Laboratory, 9 Jiulonghu Street, Wuhan, Hubei Province 430206, P. R. China  Phone: +86   027-59301999; \*Email :  wangdandan@jfslab.com.cn

Abstract —Silicon carbide (SiC) metal-oxide-semiconductor  field-effect transistors (MOSFETs) have been widely studied  due to their high switching speed and high voltage resistance.  However, its weak short current withstand capability makes SiC  MOSFET face many failure problems. This paper presents the  experimental results obtained from investigating the impact of  overcurrent in SiC MOSFETs. The results indicate that a gate  oxide burnout occurs by loading excessive current between  source and drain. The failure mechanism is gate breakdown,  which can be detected in-situ by monitoring the change in the  electrical characteristic curves of SiC MOSFETs. To further  analyze the irreversible damage caused by excessive current,  thermally induced voltage alteration was initially utilized to  locate the failure point. Then, a Focused Ion Beam (FIB) cut is  performed, revealing severe burns at the gate area. According  to the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and TEM  energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy results, it can be observed  that the source metal diffuses into the trench of the SiC  MOSFET, which is the origin physical failure point of the  device.

Keywords—1200V   SiC   MOSFET,   Failure   analysis,  Overcurrent failure mechanism

## I.    I NSTRUCTIONS   

Silicon carbide (SiC) metal-oxide-semiconductor fieldeffect transistors (MOSFETs) have emerged as promising  devices for power converters, motor drives, and hybrid  systems due to their exceptional features, including high  operating temperatures, fast switching speed, and low  internal resistance losses [1-9]. However, failures are  inevitable during the development, production, and  utilization of SiC MOSFETs. Common failure causes  encompass overvoltage, overcurrent, short circuit, open  circuit, static electricity, overheating, and mechanical  damage [10-17]. In view of the escalating demand for product  quality and reliability, failure analysis plays a crucial role in  identifying design defects, process parameter mismatches,  and operational issues. This study presents a comprehensive  failure analysis of SiC MOSFETs, focusing on investigating  their reliability, material characteristics, and structural  properties.

To comprehensively explore the failure mechanisms of  SiC MOSFETs, a synergistic approach combining electrical  testing, package analysis, hotspot identification, transmission  electron microscopy (TEM) imaging at failure locations, and  energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping is  employed.  The study intentionally induces a failure mode by  exposing  SiC MOSFETs to increase current between the  source and drain, allowing for an effective evaluation of the  impact of failure modes on device structures and materials.  Furthermore, a comparative analysis of electrical, structural,  and material properties between failed and non-failed SiC  MOSFETs is conducted. The findings contribute to  developing robust fabrication processes and enhancing  performance in SiC-based power electronics.

The research specifically investigates the overcurrent  failure mechanism of SiC MOSFETs through multi-faceted  examination techniques of failed and non-failed devices from  electrical, structural, and material perspectives. SiC  MOSFETs experiencing overcurrent failure exhibit a  significant increase in gate leakage (I GSS ) and drain-source  leakage current (I DSS ). Additionally, visible damage to the  gate structure is observed, leading to alterations in element  distribution. These findings provide a comprehensive  overview of failure analysis and offer valuable insights for  improving the performance of SiC MOSFET devices.

## II.  SAMPLE CHARACTERIZATION

SiC MOSFET is widely used in high-voltage fields, such  as boost inverters, charging stations, on-board chargers, and  motor control due to its advantages of high frequency, high  withstand voltage, and high efficiency. Therefore, it is  imperative to conduct failure analysis on high-voltage SiC  MOSFETs. In this work, a commercial SiC MOSFET chip  with a breakdown voltage of 1200 V was used as a  representative example to conduct a comprehensive analysis. ## III.    R ESULTS  A ND  D ISCUSSION 

SiC MOSFET faces complex and diverse failure modes  in the process of practical application. Researchers need to  analyze the exposed phenomena according to specific cases,  and find out the failure mechanism, propose solutions,  optimize and improve device performance. In this section, a  specific SiC MOSFET failure case will be used to  demonstrate the methods and processes of failure analysis. A  failure sample was obtained by sweeping a pulse (duty cycle   $0.1\%)$   I DS  of SiC MOSFET from 0 A to 100 A while the V GS   was set at 20 V. During the measurement, the maximum pulse  current (duty cycle   $0.1\%$   reached 72.5 A, and the SiC  MOSFET failed.

To accurately identify the specific failed location, a  scanning optical microscope (SOM) equipped with an EMMI  (Emission Microscopy), optical beam induced resistance  (OBIRCH), and thermally induced voltage alteration (TIVA)  modules are utilized synergistically. The SOM system  employed in this study offers a high current detection  resolution of less than 10 pA and achieves a spatial resolution  at the micrometer level. By employing TIVA, the hot spot  responsible for the failure is precisely located at the gate edge  of the failed SiC MOSFET, as depicted in Fig. 5(a) and (b).  Electrical analysis confirms the findings by revealing a  significant increase in the gate-source leakage current in the  failed sample, with the hot spot specifically observed at the

gate edge. Based on these observations, the failure mode is  likely attributed to short circuit damage between the gate and  the source of the SiC MOSFET.

The hot spot marked by the red circle in Fig. 5(b) is  selected for cross-sectioning by FIB slice by slice until a  damaged layer appears. Fig. 6(a) shows the schematic  diagram of SiC trench MOSFET, the channel exists in the Pdoped region. Fig. 6(b) represents the FIB cross section at the  hot spot area, a burn location was found between source metal  and gate polysilicon at the left area, reflecting that the trench  structure has undergone serious deformation. Besides, the  normal device is shown in the right area.

To validate the previous inference, a precise  measurement of the hot spot location on the TIVA image  shown in Fig. 5(a) was conducted, and the TEM lamella was  extracted at this position. Subsequently, a TEM image was  obtained to provide further insights. The TEM image reveals  complete damage to the gate of the failed SiC MOSFET cell,  with the gate and source being electrically connected, as  depicted in Fig. 7(b). Additionally, it is observed that certain  upper elements, such as N and Ti, extend toward the gate in  the failed cell. As can be seen from the first picture in Fig.  7(b), obvious gate breakdown occurs in the failed SiC  MOSFET. After the gate oxide is broken down, the device  temperature at the breakdown location rises rapidly caused by  the excessive current. When the temperature reaches the  melting point of the source metal, the molten source metal  will diffuse to the gate breakdown position, causing the SiC  MOSFET gate and source to be short-circuited. Collectively,  these findings support the conclusion that, upon powering on  the failed SiC MOSFET, current flows from the gate to the  source, which is consistent with the obtained electrical  results.

With the increasing adoption of SiC MOSFETs in  various applications, it is anticipated that new and previously  unknown failure modes and mechanisms will arise. These  failure modes can result from a combination of factors such  as device design, fabrication processes, operating conditions,  and external stresses. So the identification and understanding  of these failure modes are essential for ensuring the reliability  and performance of SiC MOSFETs [18-23].

Once a failure mode is determined, it is imperative to  conduct a comprehensive analysis that encompasses the  electrical, structural, and material properties of the failed  device. The electrical analysis involves characterizing  parameters such as leakage currents, breakdown voltages, onstate resistance, and switching characteristics. This analysis  helps to discern any anomalies or deviations from the  expected behavior, providing insights into the root causes of  the failure. In addition to electrical analysis, structural  examination plays a crucial role in failure analysis.  Techniques such as scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and  TEM allow for the visualization and characterization of  physical defects, such as cracks, voids, delamination, or  metal deposition issues  etc . These structural investigations  provide invaluable information about the failure mechanisms  and the regions of interest within the device.

Furthermore, material analysis is vital for understanding  the failure mechanisms and their impact on the device's  performance. Techniques such as EDS and secondary ion  mass spectrometry (SIMS) enable the identification and  mapping of elemental composition and impurities within the  device structure. These material analyses can reveal material  degradation, contamination, or deviations from the desired

composition, shedding light on potential causes of failure. By  integrating electrical, structural, and material analyses, a  comprehensive understanding of the failure mode and its  underlying mechanisms can be achieved. This knowledge  serves as a valuable reference for enhancing the design,  fabrication processes, and operational conditions of SiC  MOSFETs. It enables the development of more reliable and  robust devices, ensuring their longevity and optimal  performance in demanding applications.

In light of SiC MOSFETs' susceptibility to failure due  to weak short current withstand capability, this research paper  presents experimental findings that investigate the impact of  overcurrent on these devices. The results demonstrate that  excessive current between the source and drain leads to gate  oxide burnout, resulting in gate breakdown. Timely detection  of this failure mechanism can be achieved by monitoring  changes in the electrical characteristic curves of SiC  MOSFETs. To gain further insights into the irreversible  damage caused by excessive current, thermally induced  voltage alteration was initially employed to locate the failure  point, followed by a FIB cut that unveiled severe burns in the  gate area. Detailed examination using TEM and TEM EDS  spectroscopy revealed the diffusion of source metal into the  trench of the SiC MOSFET, pinpointing the physical origin  of the device's failure.

In summary, the growing adoption of SiC MOSFETs  highlights the need for comprehensive exploration and  understanding of emerging failure modes. Through  meticulous examination and analysis of failed devices  encompassing electrical, structural, and material aspects,  valuable insights into the fundamental causes of failure have  been obtained. These insights form the basis for enhancing  the reliability and performance of SiC MOSFETs, thereby  facilitating their broader utilization across diverse domains.
