## Revisiting 3D X-ray for Board Level FA to                In-line Metrology of Wafer Level Integration   

S.H. Lau, Jeff Gelb, Sheraz Gul, Tianzu Qi, Vikram Singh, Sylvia Lewis, Wenbing Yun    Application Lab, Sigray Inc, Concord, CA USA  Phone: (1) 408 5052139,  Email: shlau@sigray.com

Abstract —While 3D X-ray Tools or X-ray Microscopes  (XRM) have been the traditional nondestructive imaging tools  in Failure Analysis (FA) labs, they normally involve smaller  packages or small sections cut out of a package or PCB if high  resolution better than 1 um is desired. They are seldom used for  intact board level FA nor is a tool of choice for inline metrology  for defect inspection in wafer level integration. As sample size  increases to several cm, such as 2.5D/3D Packages, Multi Chiplet  Modules (MCM), PCBs or a  $\mathbf{300}\;\mathbf{mm}$   wafer, high resolution   $(<\!1$    um) imaging is not practical nor in many cases achievable. The  acquisition time takes too long (typically hours); images are  noisy and are filled with beam hardening artifacts (with bands  and streaks). In this paper we will revisit the use of X-rays for  high resolution (sub-micron) board level FA and in line  metrology for next generation wafer level integration using a  novel 3D X-ray system that can achieve sub-micron resolution  on large PCBs to   $\mathbf{300}\mathbf{\:mm}$   wafers in as little as a few minutes to  evaluate failures or defects in 3D.

Keywords—3D X-ray, Board level FA, Wafer Level  Integration, Hybrid Bonds, PCBs, Reliability, Metrology

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

To support high-speed and large-capacity communication  such as 5G, Internet of Things (IoT) and AI require High  Performance Computing (HPC) utilizing high performance  processors   and   high-bandwidth   memory.   Therefore,  semiconductor devices and packages are increasingly  migrating towards 3D stacking with new approaches in  connectivity and bonding. Pitch scaling of vertical  interconnection is a crucial development aspect for advanced  chiplet integration and wafer level integration. Micro- bump  has fulfilled the demand for vertical interconnection in 3D  integration, however, it is facing the limitation of scaling  below   $10~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   pitch due to reliability concerns and the  alignment limitation with thermal compression bonding. CuCu Hybrid bonding can alternate the vertical interconnection,  where the technology is now getting matured for Wafer-toWafer(W2W) integration . This introduces significant  challenges for inline defect inspection and offline failure  analysis techniques. Primarily, 3D stacking and wafer  bonding result in optically opaque systems that require  approaches such as X-rays to see through multiple layers of  buried structures for defect detection. However, with the  continual shrinkage in device features in 3D Heterogenous  Integration (3DHI) (e.g., as microbumps are scheduled to  transition to Cu-Cu hybrid bonds and TSVs interconnects are  scaling to single digit micrometers), non-destructive  techniques are facing a technological brick wall. While 3D  X-ray Tools or X-ray Microscopes (XRM) have been the  traditional nondestructive imaging tools in Failure Analysis  labs, they typically can only achieve   $1~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   or coarser  resolution with smaller packages. [1,2] While sub-micron  resolution has been demonstrated, these are only possible

with very small and thin packages or samples which have  been sectioned into   $<\,1\,\mathrm{\mm}$   diameter in size. [3] But as  sample size increases to several cm, such as 2.5D/3D  packages, multi-chiplet modules (MCM), PCBs or 300 mm  wafers, high resolution imaging is normally not attempted.  This is because acquisition time takes too long (typically  hours); images are noisy and are filled with beam hardening  artifacts (with bands and streaks) or are simply beyond the  capabilities of the instrument. It is also for these reasons we  do not find 3D X-ray tools with sub-micron resolution being  deployed for in-line metrology of wafer level packaging of  ICs. In this paper we describe a novel approach in 3D X-ray  imaging for large planar samples such as large package  modules, PCBs and 300 mm Wafers where defects can be  detected at resolution down to   $0.5\;\upmu\mathrm{m}$   spatial resolution in as  little as a few minutes. Furthermore, results are void of the  typical beam hardening artifacts [4]. This feature makes it an  ideal candidate for non-destructive Board Level FA and in  line Metrology of Wafer Level Integration.

## II.    M ETHOD   

Real-time 2D X-ray imaging is preferred for initial quality  control and allows inspection of solder joints for voids, major  cracks, dimensions and shape of components such as BGAs  or QFN packages or PCBs. However, this method has  limitations when it comes to analyzing fine structures and  complex geometries associated with 3DHI. The latter would  benefit from 3D X-ray or computed tomography (CT) which  allows for unrestricted viewing angles and virtual slices and  provides a greater depth of information. Unfortunately for  high resolution imaging to  $<1~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   spatial resolution required  the use of a much smaller sample geometry making most  conventional 3D X-rays or XRM not practical for Board  Level FA or for in line metrology of   $300~\mathrm{{mm}}$   wafer Level  integration.  To overcome the issues of lack of resolution with  large samples sizes and long acquisition time, we developed  a novel 3D X-ray tool combining traditional tomography with  laminography, in a patented architecture that is capable of  imaging defects in large boards and wafers, including voids,  delamination, solder joints anomalies, microbumps defects,  RDLs and organic substrate defects such as underfills [5].  An  illustration of the difference in architecture of traditional 3D  X-ray or XRM versus the novel tool, Apex XCT (Sigray Inc,  USA) is shown in Fig. 1.

The novel architecture with its rotation axis allows a dramatic increase  in X-ray flux to reach the detector because the x-ray source can be placed at  an oblique angle very near the sample being rotated lying flat (image on  right). Throughput   for   all   x-ray   imaging   systems   is  predominantly limited by the number of x-rays photons  passing through the object. Because x-rays are produced  isotopically, acquisition time follows the inverse square rule  in which throughput is proportional to   $1/\mathrm{r}^{2}$  , where r is the  source-sample distance [4]. Stated otherwise, the larger the  sample is, the further the sample must be placed from the  source to ensure rotation without hitting it. Therefore,  exposure time required will increase exponentially. With the  novel architecture, the X-ray source can be placed very near  to the sample surface, and the sample is rotated without  changing the source-sample distance at the region of  illumination.

## III.    R ESULTS 

To illustrate the suitability of the novel 3D X-ray tool to be  included into the workflow of current Board Level FA and  for in-line Metrology of next generation Wafer Level  Integration, the following results are presented.

## A.   B OARD  L EVEL  FA   &   R ELIABILITY 

Board-level failure analysis in MCM or PCBs is a crucial  process in identifying and resolving issues in electronic  circuits. Non-destructive techniques are preferred since they  allow for analysis without damaging the components. Some  non-destructive techniques commonly used in board-level  failure analysis includes electrical testing, TDR (time domain  reflectometry), Infrared Thermography, SAM (Scanning  Acoustic Microscopy), vibration analysis and 2D X-ray,  which are used to isolate and the location of faults, opens,  shorts, voids and delamination. After localizing the  suspicious regions of faults, 3D X-ray may be used to see if  gross defects, (generally a few microns to tens of microns  resolution) can be detected. In the event where defect sizes  are beyond the resolution of the X-ray imaging tool or where  the suspected region of interest may be obscured by large  metallic structures and/or radiation hardening artifacts, a  physical destructive cross section has to be performed  followed by inspection with Optical Microscopes or SEM.

This modus operandi is often undesirable since the sample  has to be destroyed preventing it from being reused for further  testing and evaluation. However, with the novel 3D X-ray  system we have demonstrated that large boards or PCBs can  be imaged down to 0.5 um spatial resolution in as little as a  few minutes per region of interest, making this novel tool to  be desirable and a valuable part of the non-destructive  workflow for board level FA. We will provide three examples  below.

## 2.    Image Quality of Board Level FA vs Small Cut out  section of the defective component from the PCB  

It would be difficult to use conventional XRM or 3D X-ray  to scan a large and thick PCB down to 1 um or less with good  clarity. This is because as the sample size increases,  resolution degrades, while acquisition time increases  exponentially. High resolution scanning on PCB samples,  with the presence of multiple components with solder balls  mounted on several layers of metallization in the presence of  low Z materials will also create a lot of beam hardening  artifacts, which is evident by streaks and noise in the 3D  image.  That is the reason why Board Level FA is normally  not attempted with conventional 3D X-rays. In most FA  workflows, it will be necessary to cut out small sections of  the PCB where the fault has been localized before imaging  with 3D X-ray.

To demonstrate that it is possible to scan the whole board  without losing resolution nor acquisition time, two identical  PCBs with similar defects were used for the following tests.  The failures were localized to a particular electronic  component on the board. The PCB measured   $170\mathrm{~mm~x~}130$    mm. On one of the PCBs, the defective component was cut

out from the main board and scanned at 1 um resolution voxel  while the same region of interest on the defective component  was scanned on the intact PCB. Fig. 5 and Fig. 6.

## 2.    Intermetallic Interface Evaluation after Drop Tests

For example, it can be dropped repeatedly at different  heights and evaluated for failure or reliability. Evaluation has  to be done at the board level. Methods used are often  destructive, which includes dye and pull (DnP) test to

evaluate selective interface at solder joint interface or at the  wire bond pad for intermetallic voids.

## B.   I N - LINE  M ETROLOGY FOR  300  MM WAFER LEVEL  INTEGRATION   

Wafer-to-wafer (W2W) hybrid bonding is a key technology  in the 3D stacking integration process. However, high density  Cu-Cu hybrid bonding interconnections between W2W can  give rise to structural defects, such as misalignment and  delamination during the bonding process. The testing and  characterization of such defects can be challenging. In wafer  level integration, the current size of the microbumps is   $30\,\upmu\mathrm{m},$  ,  with a roadmap to sub   $10~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   diameter in the near future. To  keep pace with the roadmap, defect detection will require  high-resolution (submicron) imaging and precise defect  detection. In line Metrology of wafer level integration has  several challenges which come with scaling. While current  solutions exist in the fab, with finer RDL pitches and smaller  bump sizes, especially as the technology transitions to hybrid  bonding, the need to utilize high-resolution techniques is  inevitable.  In general, providing micron-level resolution  using X-rays in wafer inspection is not that much of a  challenge. But some of the key challenges are to do that with  sub-micron resolution with a high sampling rate. The  following are examples which will illustrate how this novel  3D X-ray tool has addressed these issues.

## IV.          C ONCLUSION 

In this paper we demonstrated the application of a novel 3D  X-ray system to address a number of existing limitations of  X-ray imaging systems for board level failure analysis and in  line metrology for next generation wafer level integration.  The system is designed to acquire high resolution 3D images  rapidly without limitations to sample size, making it an ideal  tool for imaging MCM packages, PCB and 300 mm wafers.   Several challenging examples were demonstrated at high  resolutions down to   $0.5\,\mathrm{\upmum}$  , and at high speeds and high  contrast. These included cracks in the organic layer in a PCB,  intermetallic voids of RMA parts at the board level. It was  also demonstrated that the novel tool can address the next  generation in-line metrology of Cu-Cu hybrid bonds, and  TSVs at the resolution and sampling size needed for  production monitoring. All images were clear of the beam  hardening artifacts that currently plague both failure analysis  (FA) and in-line metrology. We have also developed a  roadmap for this tool to keep pace with the resolution, sample  and speed requirements for future anticipated challenges that  will emerge in heterogeneous integration and wafer-level  (wafer-to-die and wafer-to-wafer) packaging.
