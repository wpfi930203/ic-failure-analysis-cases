## Degradation Study of GaN Cascode Devices Under High-Temperature Gate Bias Stress 

Shizheng Yang 1 , Jingyu Yang 1 , Xinghuan Chen 1 , Liang He 1 , Yijun  $\mathrm{{Sh}i^{1}}$  , Zhixin Zhang 1 , Yuan Chen 1 , Zhiyuan He 2 , Yiqiang Chen 1 , Guoguang Lu 1 1 China Electronic Product Reliability and Environmental Testing Research Institute (CEPREI), Guangzhou, China 2 School of Integrated Circuits, Guangdong University of Technology, Guangzhou, China Email : heliang@ceprei.com, zhangzhixin@ceprei.com, luguog@126.com

Abstract —This work investigates the degradation behaviors and  mechanisms  of  GaN  cascode  devices  under  hightemperature off-state gate stress with different temperature, focusing on the analysis of threshold voltage   $(\mathbf{V_{th}})$  , onresistance, and third quadrant output characteristics. High temperatures and electric fields stimulate the formation of more interface defects, leading to an increase in the negative shift of  $\mathbf{V_{th}}$  , which is caused by hole traps with an activation energy of 0.31 eV. This work provides insights for the reliability assessment and high-temperature application of GaN cascode devices.

Keywords—GaN cascode, HTGB, threshold voltage, defects

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Gallium Nitride (GaN) is a wide-bandgap semiconductor material that possesses high breakdown electric fields, high saturation electron velocity, and high electron mobility [1-3]. These  properties  afford  it  significant  advantages  in applications requiring high frequencies, high temperatures, and high power, such as power electronics, communications, automotive,  and  aerospace.  At  present,  although  the performance  parameters  of  GaN-based  high  electron mobility  transistor  (HEMT)  devices  largely  meet  the demands of practical applications [4-9]. The key issue that needs addressing at this stage is the long-term reliability of devices under high temperature   $(>150~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$   and high electric field stress [9-13]. A negative off-state gate voltage is often employed to suppress false triggering caused by switch noise in some harsh application environments, ensuring the device is completely turned off. Therefore, researches on the longterm reliability and understanding degradation mechanisms of GaN devices under high-temperature off-state gate stress are important and indispensable.

This paper primarily investigates the degradation of GaN cascode devices under high-temperature gate bias stress (HTGB) with varied temperature, characterizing threshold voltage shifts   $(\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}})$  , on-resistance changes, and fluctuation of  other  electrical  parameters.  Then  the  degradation mechanisms of the devices under high-temperature off-state stress are explored.

In this study, three HTGB experiments were carried out at different temperatures with   $150\mathrm{\mathrm{\bar{o}}C}$  ,  $165^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , and   $180^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  . The stress conditions are shown in Table 1, with a gate stress of -  $18~\mathrm{V}$   and a total stress duration of 1000 hours. The specific process of these stress experiments are as follows: Firstly, initial electrical parameters of selected devices are measured to exclude the devices that fail early, have poor parameters, or show significant deviations. Then, the devices with relatively stable performance parameters are subjected to constant off-state stress experiments. The experimental process adopts a measure-stress-measure testing sequence, as shown in Figure 3. At specific intervals during the stress process, electrical parameters measurements of the devices are taken, and this cycle is repeated until the total stress time meets the experimental requirements or the devices show significant degradation.

## III. E XPERIMENTAL  R ESULTS 

The degradation of transfer characteristics of GaN cascode devices before and after the experiments with different temperature stresses is shown in Figure 4. It can be seen that after HTGB stress, all devices exhibited some degree of degradation in transfer characteristics. We selected

the gate drive voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{gs})}$   corresponding to   $(\mathrm{V_{ds}=0.5\ V})$  and   $\mathrm{(I_{ds}=1\ m A)}$   as threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{th})}$   of the devices, and  extracted  the shift  in Vth   $(\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}) $  with  different temperatures HTGB experiments. The   $\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}}$   of the device increased from 0.07 V @   $150^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   condition to 0.11 V   $@$   $165^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   condition and reached 0.15 V   $@$     $180^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   condition. In addition, compared to the initial state, the   $\mathrm{{g}_{\mathrm{{m,max}}}}$   of the device decreased by 0.146 S, 0.169 S, and 0.385 S under the   $150^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  ,  $165^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , and   $180^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   temperature stress conditions, respectively. It is evident that with the increase in temperature stress, the negative shift in Vth became more pronounced, and the switching control capability weakened. Moreover, through the transfer characteristic curves, we also extracted and compared the degradation of subthreshold swing (SS) the devices. For fresh device, the SS is 175 mV/decade (  $(\underline{{\boldsymbol{a}}})\;\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{gs}}=$  2 V), and the SS values of the devices increased to 197 mV/decade, 201 mV/decade, and 216 mV/decade after HTGB stress experiments at   $150^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  ,   $165^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , and   $180^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , respectively. These phenomena all indicate that related performance parameters of GaN cascode devices degraded after HTGB stress experiments, and temperature stress has an accelerating effect on their degradation.

To explore whether the gate region degraded during the experiments, we tested the gate leakage current   $\left(\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{gss}}\right)$   of the devices, as shown in Figure 5. Figure 5 (a) compares the gate leakage current of the devices before and after 1000-h HTGB stress with different temperature group, with the gate voltage testing range from -18 V to   $+18~\mathrm{V}$  . The results show that  $\mathrm{I_{gss}}$  of all devices being less than 1 nA. We extracted the gate leakage current of the devices under the test condition at   $\mathrm{V_{gs}}$  of  $+18\mathrm{~V~}$  , and the relationship between   $\mathrm{I_{gss}}$   and stress time is shown in Figure 5 (b). Under the same temperature stress, I gss did not show significant degradation with the increase of stress time. Meanwhile, comparing different temperature stresses, I gss  did not show obvious differences. It indicates that the gate region of the devices did not experience breakdown damage, and the gates still maintain good insulation characteristics.

Figure 6 shows the drain leakage current (  $(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{~}}\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{ds}}=650\mathrm{~V})$  ) of the devices after 1000-h HTGB stress with different temperature. During the test of drain leakage current, the gate voltage of the devices was set to   $_{0\mathrm{~V~}}$  , at which both the internal Si MOSFET and GaN HEMT were in off state. We found that the all   $\mathrm{I_{dss}}$   of devices after 1000-h HTGB stress with different temperature slightly decreased compared to the fresh devices and the I dss  decreases slightly with the stress time, indicating that the stress had a minimal impact on the

drain leakage current, and the devices still maintained lowleakage high-voltage characteristics.

Figure 7 shows the change in on-resistance   $\mathrm{(R_{on})}$   of GaN cascode devices over stress time under different gate test voltages (low gate voltage:   $\mathrm{Vgs}=+3.5\;\mathrm{V}$  , high gate voltage:  $\mathrm{Vgs}~=~+8~\mathrm{~V})$  . We found that under high gate voltage conditions,  the  Ron  of  the  devices  did  not  change significantly, always remaining around   $125~\mathrm{m}\Omega$  ; but for low gate voltage conditions, the Ron of all three groups of devices showed some degree of increase. For example, under the   $180^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   condition, the Ron of the device increased from  $151.6~\mathrm{m}\Omega$   to   $162.4~\mathrm{m}\Omega$  , an increase of   $7.12\%$  . Figure 7 (b) shows the change in ΔRon (  $(@\mathrm{Vgs}=+3.5\;\mathrm{V})$   of the devices under different temperature stresses over time, and it can be seen that ΔRon increases with the increase in temperature and stress time, indicating that the degradation of Ron is positively correlated with temperature. Importantly, the stress time and temperature dependence also indicate that the increase of Ron may originating from the trapping effects.

## IV. D EGRADATION  M ECHANISM  A NALYSIS 

Since GaN cascode devices consist of Si MOSFET and GaN HEMT parts [11, 14, 15], to pinpoint the internal degradation location, we further tested the reverse output characteristics of the devices, i.e., the body diode output characteristics, as shown in Figure 8. Figure 8 (b) shows the internal current path diagram in reverse output. When the gate voltage is 0 V, the Si MOSFET is turn-off, and the GaN HEMT is turn-on. At this time, the reverse output current flows through the body diode inside the Si MOSFET and the 2DEG channel of the GaN HEMT, so the reverse output resistance of the device can be approximated as the series addition of the on-resistance of the GaN HEMT and the onresistance of the body diode of the Si MOSFET. Given the relative maturity of Si-based MOSFET technology and the high reliability of the devices, and considering that the HTGB stress mainly affects the gate of the Si MOSFET without significantly impacting its body diode, we believe that the body diode inside the Si MOSFET did not degrade significantly, i.e., the resistance of the body diode remained constant. Therefore, by comparing the reverse on-resistance (R reverse-on ) of the GaN cascode devices before and after the HTGB experiments, we can determine whether the GaN HEMT part has degraded, and pinpointing the internal degradation location of the devices. As shown in Figure 8 (a), the reverse output characteristics of the devices after HTGB stress experiments at three different temperatures almost overlap with those of fresh devices, indicating that the R reverse-on  remained essentially unchanged, and the GaN HEMT did not undergo degradation. And the degradation of the GaN cascode devices is primarily concentrated in the gate region of the Si MOSFET.

By analyzing the shift of threshold voltage, change of onresistance, and third quadrant output characteristics of GaN cascode devices under HTGB stress, we deduce that the internal degradation of the devices is mainly concentrated in the gate region of the Si MOSFET. Regarding the negative shift  in   $\mathrm{V_{th}} $  after  stress,  the  corresponding  physical mechanism can be explained using the energy band structure diagram of gate region, as illustrated in Figure 9. When the device is subjected to negative gate stress, holes can be captured by traps located at or near the gate oxide interface under the joint action of the electric field and high temperature.

These captured holes cause further band bending in the Si region during the device turn-on process, attracting more electrons to accumulate near the interface and promoting channel inversion. As results, it leads to the negative shift of  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  . As the temperature stress increases, high temperatures stimulate the formation of more interface defects, and carriers at lower energy levels can gain more energy at higher temperatures, making them more likely to reach the vicinity of the interface and be captured by traps. It further increases the hole density at the gate oxide interface and thereby further increasing the negative shift of   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  . The formation of internal defects can be explained using the Reaction-Diffusion (R-D) model [16]. According to the R-D model, Si-H bonds at the  $\mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface break under high electric fields, releasing hydrogen atoms  and forming interface traps. Meanwhile, the released hydrogen atoms, being quite reactive, lead to the breakdown of Si-H bonds in the nearby oxide layer, thus generating more defects.

Furthermore, we fit the change in  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   of the GaN cascode devices under different temperature HTGB stresses over time based  on  the  Arrhenius  empirical  formula,  which characterizes the impact of temperature on device parameter degradation. As shown in Figure 10, we obtained an activation energy of 0.31 eV for the traps associated with hole capture in these experiments. It indicates that the degradation of   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   in GaN cascode devices under HTGB stress is caused by hole traps with an activation energy of  ${\sim}0.31~\mathrm{eV}$  . This further confirms the physical mechanism of interface trap capturing holes.

## V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper researches the degradation phenomena and mechanisms of GaN cascode devices under high-temperature off-state stress with different temperature   $(>150~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$  . The threshold voltage shifts   $(\Delta\mathrm{V_{th}})$  , on-resistance changes, and fluctuation of other electrical parameters of the devices are characterized. Based on the energy band structure diagram, the physical degradation mechanism induced by high temperature and electric field stress are explained. This work provides  some  useful  information  for  the  reliability assessment and high-temperature application of GaN cascode devices.
