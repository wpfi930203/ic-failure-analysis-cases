## Analysis of the Influence of Pin Position on CDM Peak Current Based on 2.5D and 2D Packaging 

Menghua Wang 1,2\* , Liyi  $\mathrm{He}^{2}$  , Hui Zheng 2 , Shuanshe Chao 2 , Dan Yang 2 , Xiao He 2 , Na Mei 2 1 State Key Laboratory of Mobile Network and Mobile Multimedia Technology, Shenzhen, P.R.China 2 Department of Reliability Engineering, Sanechips Technology Co., Ltd, Shenzhen, P.R.China Phone : (+86)18829290168, Email: wang.menghua@sanechips.com.cn

Abstract — The trend and principle for the variation of Charged Device Model(CDM) peak current of IO and PG pins with pin position in 2.5D CoWoS(50  ×  50mm) and 2D FCBGA  $({\bf45}\times{\bf45mm})$   packaging are analyzed in this work. Pin position has an important effect on the CDM peak current, the closer to the packaging center, the higher peak current of CDM. Moreover, the peak current of the CDM depends on the total charge in the first CDM pulse(Q) and full width at half maximum time of the first CDM pulse(Td), and the trend of CDM peak current with pin position is primarily influenced by parasitic resistance for IO(InputOutput) pins and capacitance for PG(Power and Ground) pins. Furthermore, the CDM peak current of the PG pin is generally higher than that of the IO pin, as the discharge time of the IO pin is much longer than that of the PG pin, which further indicates that the parasitic resistance of the IO pin is larger than that of the PG pin. Therefore,  the  influence  of  the  pin  position  should  be considered when designing ESD protection circuits to avoid CDM-related failures caused by ESD underdesign.

Keywords—Electrostatic discharge (ESD), charged-device model (CDM), peak current, pin position, 2.5D CoWoS and 2D FCBGA packaging

## I.  I NTRODUCTION 

With the development of advanced technology, chip failure caused by electrostatic discharge (ESD) has become one of the main factors restricting  the reliability of integrated circuit (IC) products [1-4]. The Charge Device Model (CDM) is one of the most important models in the ESD family. Nowadays, the increasing number of CDM failure events is not only related to circuit design, but also to package design, which has led to a lot of efforts to study the impact of package on CDM results [5]. For CDM testing, the entire chip is first charged to a certain voltage and then discharged through a given pin. Therefore, the discharge current depends on how much charge is absorbed by the device, so the CDM peak current is related to the size of the package [6-8]. However, according to CDM waveform analysis, pin type and position also have important effects on CDM peak current, but there are few studies, especially for 2.5D package technology.

In this work  ， the influence of pin position on CDM peak current is analyzed under 2.5D and 2D packages. By classifying the pin types, the difference of CDM peak current between IO and PG pin types is compared. In order to investigate the effect of pin position and pin type on CDM peak current, the variation of total charge in the first CDM pulse(Q), total capacitance(C), full width at half maximum time of the first CDM pulse(Td) and rise time of the first CDM pulse(Tr) with pin position is further analyzed. It is found that the closer to the package center, the higher peak current of CDM, and the CDM peak current value depends on the ratio of Q and Td. The results indicate that the center pin is more sensitive to voltage stress, which should be concerned in ESD design.

## III. R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSION 

The CDM peak current of the IO and PG pins is plotted as a function of the pin positions, as shown in Figs. 4 (a) and (b), respectively. It can be observed that the CDM peak current of PG pins is generally higher than IO in 2.5D and 2D packages, which may be because the metal wire width of VDD and VSS is wider than that of the signal wire, so the resistance on the metal is smaller, making the current flowing through the PG metal wire larger. Therefore, for different pin types, the corresponding CDM peak current is also different. What's more, it is clear that pin position has a significant effect on the CDM peak current, and the CDM peak current increases as the pin position approaches the center of the package, and the same trend is observed for both 2.5D and 2D packages. This trend indicates that under the same test stress, the inner pin is more sensitive to voltage stress than the edge pin, which should be more concerned, especially for automotive grade projects, the influence of pin position on CDM peak current should be taken into account. Therefore, the influence of the pin position  should  be  considered  when  designing  ESD protection circuits to avoid CDM-related failures caused by ESD underdesign.

In order to further understand the reason for the trend of total charge variation with pin position, we further investigated the function of the test total capacitance with pin position for IO and PG pins in 2.5D and 2D packages, as shown in Figs. 6 (a) and (b), respectively. It can be seen that the trend of capacitance variation with pin position is the same as the total charge. Since the voltage stress test conditions are the same, the trend of capacitance determines the trend of total charge. It is speculated that the trend of capacitance variation with pin position may be related to the design of IO and PG networks, since IO signal lines are thin and long, while PG networks are thick and large. In addition, due to the large size of the chip, some pins do not enter the electric field formed by the grounding plate and charging board of the testing machine, and the area of the chip in the electric field decreases with the increase of distance. Therefore, the capacitance of the IO pin is relatively insensitive to changes in field area, while the capacitance of the PG pin is sensitive to changes in field area, so the capacitance of the PG pin decreases as the pin position approaches the edge of the package. In addition, for PG pins, the larger the packaging area, the more obvious the trend of capacitance decrease. Since the area of 2.5D package is larger than the 2D package, the capacitance decrease trend under 2.5D package is more obvious. Furthermore,  the reduced  capacitance  of the large 2.5D  package  also might result from a missing total overlap of the Tester GND plane with part of the package in corner positions of the package.

Fig. 7 shows the relationship between Td and package distance in 2.5D and 2D packages for IO pins and PG pins, respectively. It can be observed that for IO pins, Td increases as the pin position moves away from the center, while for PG pins, Td is insensitive to pin position. This may be related to the design of IO and PG networks, because basically IO signal lines are thin and long, while PG networks are thick and wide, so the parasitic resistance of PG networks is smaller than that of IO. Thus, the parasitic resistance of the IO pins increases when moving away from the center of the package, resulting in longer discharge times [9]  ， and for the PG pins, the discharge time of different pin positions is basically the same due to the small parasitic resistance. Therefore, the trend of CDM peak current with pin position is primarily influenced by parasitic resistance for IO pins and capacitance for PG pins.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we analyze the trend and principle of CDM peak current for IO and PG pins in 2.5D and 2D packages as the pin position changes. Based on the results, it is demonstrated that closer to the package center results in higher CDM peak currents. Moreover, the peak current of the CDM depends on the total charge and Td. In both 2.5D and 2D packages, as the PG pin moves away from the center of the package, the total charge decreases and Td remains essentially the same, while as the IO pin moves away from the package center, the total charge trend remains relatively unchanged and Td increases. Thus, the trend of CDM peak current with pin position is primarily influenced by parasitic resistance for  IO pins  and  capacitance for  PG  pins. Furthermore, the CDM peak current of the PG pin is generally higher than that of the IO pin, because the discharge time of the IO pin is much longer than that of the PG pin, which further indicates that the parasitic resistance of the IO pin is larger than that of the PG pin. Therefore, the influence of pin position should be fully considered in the

ESD design process  ， especially for some pins that are sensitive to CDM peak current.
