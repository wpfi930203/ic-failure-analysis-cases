## GaN Epitaxial Layer Dislocation Characterization  Using ECCI and TEM  

Jiashi Zhou 1 , Jiamei Song 1 , Hongyuan Wen 1 , Xiaodong Yang 1  1 Shanghai   Nanoport, Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. 2517 Jinke Road, Shanghai, China  Phone : (+86)15221628508, Email :  Jiashi.zhou@thermofisher.com

Abstract —Gallium nitride (GaN) is a representative material  of the third-generation semiconductor. The growth of GaN  heteroepitaxial layers on substrates often leads to dislocations  due to lattice mismatch. This paper presents a comprehensive  characterization method of these defects using two kinds of  electron microscopes, which are commonly available in  analytical laboratories. Specifically, we used the electron  channeling contrast imaging (ECCI) method based on a dual  beam electron microscope system to observe dislocations from  the sample surface. We discussed key parameters for acquiring  optimal ECCI images and subsequently prepared planar and  cross-section lamella samples for analysis at the nano-scale level  using transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Our study  provides valuable insights into GaN dislocation characterization  utilizing two types of electron microscopes, while also discussing  image conditions that can serve as a useful reference for  semiconductor researchers and engineers conducting similar  investigations.

## Keywords—GaN, Dislocation, ECCI, TEM  

## I.    I NSTRUCTIONS 

GaN is a crucial compound semiconductor material  extensively utilized in industrial sectors such as light emitting  diode (LED), laser diode, RF chip, and power device. In  comparison to Si, the wide bandgap of GaN devices provides  higher breakthrough voltage and working temperature. The  formation of two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) due to  spontaneous polarization results in high electron mobility and  low on-resistance for GaN devices (Table I). Based on these  properties, GaN high electron mobility transistor (HEMT) and  MOSFET devices have found mature applications in power  management,   automotive   electronics,   and   wireless  communications.

## II.   E XPERIMENTS 

In this study, a GaN film sample heteroepitaxially grown  on a silicon substrate was selected for experimentation. The  Thermo Fisher Scientific Helios Series FIB electron  microscope with embedded ECCI function was utilized to  analyze the dislocation profile and distribution on the sample  surface. Two TEM samples were then prepared using the  Helios FIB, one being a planar sample oriented in the [0001]  direction and the other being a cross-section sample oriented  in the [1-100] direction. The Thermo Fisher Scientific TEM  Spectra 300 was employed for TEM analysis. The dislocation  distribution, type, and density of the samples were analyzed  using STEM bright field images under two-beam conditions.  Subsequently, dislocations were observed at an atomic scale  using high-resolution high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF)  images.

Electron channeling contrast arises from the interaction  between incident electrons and crystals with different  orientations. When the incident angle is altered, if the  electrons are parallel to the lattice, more of them will penetrate  deeper into the sample; otherwise, they will be scattered and  can be detected as a backscattered signal. This phenomenon is  known as the channeling effect (see Fig. 2).

## III.   R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSIONS 

## A.   ECCI Best Imaging Conditions  

This chapter will discuss the optimal imaging conditions  for ECCI. Several critical factors influencing ECCI image  quality include sample tilt and rotation, electron beam voltage  and current, and choice of detector.

The electron beam incident angle, which is the most  important parameter affecting dislocations contrast in ECCI  mode, is determined by sample tilt and rotation. The selected  area channeling pattern (SACP) method will be employed to  identify the optimal sample tilt and rotation angles. SACP is a  technique developed for crystal orientation determination  through backscattered electron collection. In contrast to  electron channeling pattern (ECP), SACP reduces the  collecting area of patterns by using two separately driven coils  to rock the beam onto a specific area of interest [4].

The SACP pattern of the GaN epitaxial layer grown on a  Si substrate sample, utilized for dislocation characterization in  this experiment, is depicted in Fig. 3. The Kikuchi line is  generated by backscattered electrons (BSE), with the central  position representing the BSE signal peak. Conversely, the  dark region exhibits minimal BSE signal and maximum  channeling contrast. Therefore, the optimal position for ECCI  contrast lies between the BSE signal peak and channeling  effect, which corresponds to the Kikuchi line edge as indicated

The electron beam current and voltage are two crucial  parameters that influence the ECCI image. A higher current  can provide a better signal-to-noise ratio, but it also leads to  decreased image resolution as the probe size increases.  Therefore, there is a delicate balance between the current and  image resolution. In this experiment, a current of 5.5 nA was  used based on experience to achieve an optimal balance  between   signal-to-noise   ratio   and   image   resolution.  Additionally, the voltage plays a role in determining the

electron interaction depth with the sample; lower voltage  reveals more surface profile while higher voltage with higher  electron energy is preferable for observing dislocations in  GaN layer due to their vertical threads penetrating deeply into  the whole epitaxial layer (see Fig. 5).

## B.   TEM practice of GaN dislocation characterization  

Traditionally, dislocation types in various materials have  been characterized through conventional transmission  electron microscopy (TEM) under two-beam conditions.  However, TEM images are complicated because of thickness  fringes and bend contours, as demonstrated in the left image  in Fig. 6. A corresponding scanning transmission electron  microscopy (STEM) image from the same plane view sample  is shown on the right side. In comparison to TEM, STEM  image provides images of dislocations with greatly simplified  contrast, enabling rapid observation of all dislocation types  simultaneously.

As is well-known, the presence of imperfect crystals can  cause atoms in a specific region of the crystal to deviate from  their original positions, leading to lattice distortion. This  distortion alters the relative orientation of the crystal plane at  the defect and the incoming electron beam, resulting in  variations in diffraction intensity and contrast. Based on this  contrast effect, it becomes possible to identify the types of  defects present in the crystal. As depicted in Fig. 7,  R   represents the lattice displacement (distortion) vector, which  is generally a function of position, that is, different positions  near dislocations have varying effects on the complete  crystal's lattice.

According to the kinematics of electron diffraction, it can  be inferred that dislocations become imperceptible when  g·b    $\mathbf{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{\varepsilon}0$  , which can serve as a criterion for the vanishing of  dislocation contrast [5].

The investigated GaN layers have a wurtzite structure  which is hexagonal close packed structure (HCP). Three types  of perfect Burger’s vector can occur in GaN layers: (i)   $\mathbf{a_{\alpha}=}$     $1/3{<}11{-}20{>}$  , (ii)   $\mathtt{c}=\mathtt{<}0001\mathtt{>}$   and (iii)   ${\sf a}+{\sf c}=1/3\dot{<}11{-}23\dot{>}$  [6].  As the dislocations observed in GaN layers are threading  dislocations, i.e. have a line parallel to the growing c-axis,  three main types of dislocation are observed: (i) edge adislocation, (ii) screw c-dislocation and (iii) mixed type   $\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{c}$    dislocation. The Burger’s vectors have been measured by an  efficient LACBED method [7]. The dislocation types can be  characterized in either cross section (XS) or planar view (PV)  samples.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION   

In summary, by using ECCI function based on a SEM or a  dual beam electron microscope, an overview of dislocations  distribution and density from the top surface can be seen.  SACP method is necessary for acquiring best contrast  dislocation image. Higher electron beam voltage is better for  dislocations observation. TEM can observe more details of  dislocations because of its high resolution. Zone axia tilt  condition can be used to characterize dislocation types in  cross-section and planar lamella samples. Cross-section  STEM has the advantage of providing dislocation behavior in  each layer, while Planar view STEM has better statistics on  dislocation density of each type due to its larger sampling area.
