## Impact Analysis of  Negative Gate Voltage on  SiCMOS Reliability  under OFF-State  Avalanche Stress  

Hang Xu, Jianbin Guo, Tianyang Feng, Yafen Yang, Qing-Qing Sun  and David Wei Zhang   State Key Laboratory of ASIC and System, School of  Microelectronics, Fudan University, Shanghai 200433, China   Email : yafen_yang@fudan.edu.cn

Abstract —This paper fully researches and analyzes the effect of  negative OFF-state gate-source bias voltage (V GS ,off) on the device  degradation under OFF-state avalanche stress. It is found that the  degradation associated with negative V GS ,off is much greater than  that of 0V V GS ,off and negative bias temperature instability  (NBTI). While the shift in threshold voltage   $(\mathbf{V_{T}})$   and on-resistance   $(\mathbf{R_{on}})$   are relatively small, and the biggest change is reverse leakage  current   $(\mathbf{Dss})$   corresponds to the generation of interface state. This  phenomenon is analyzed in conjunction with Technology  Computer Aided Design (TCAD) analysis, which demonstrates  that under the avalanche stress condition, the electric field at the  bottom of the gate oxide attains substantial magnitudes. Moreover,  the introduction of negative V GS ,off significantly amplifies the  electric field in this region, which has a significant impact on the  long-term reliability of the device.

Keywords—SiCMOS, OFF-State Avalanche, Negative Gate  Voltage, Degradation

## I.    I NTRODUCTION  

Compared with traditional silicon material, Silicon Carbide  (SiC) offers three distinct advantages: large bandgap, high  thermal conductivity, it allows the design of smaller and more  efficient devices, with better heat dissipation to improve device  reliability at high temperature [1]-[3]. Furthermore, the design  and processing of SiC-based Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor  (SiCMOS) are similar to that of silicon-based MOS, making it  viable alternatives for silicon-based power devices. Notably, planar SiCMOS has been widely used in power systems,  including new energy vehicles, and continues to exhibit  promising technical potential [4]-[6].

However, there are currently two main obstacles limiting the  widespread adoption of SiCMOS. First, SiC devices are  relatively expensive. Second, many reliability issues of  SiCMOS remain unresolved. The reliability of SiCMOS has not  been verified through long-term and large-scale applications.  Power devices, as core components in power electronic  systems, directly impact the safe and stable operation of the  system. Therefore, studying the failure mechanisms and  degradation mechanisms of SiCMOS is of significant  importance.   For SiCMOS, the gate oxygen layer is the weakest

part of reliability [1]. The barrier height at the gate oxide  interface of SiCMOS devices is relatively low, making it easier  for charge carriers in the channel to tunnel through the barrier  into the oxide layer. On the other hand, the residual carbon  atoms during the oxidation process in SiC can lead to a higher  interface state density at the SiC/SiO 2  interface [7]-[9].

Given the extreme operating environments of SiCMOS,  reliability is a main concern. Inductive load switch in flyback  converters is a typical application, where voltage spikes during  the turn-off process are common [10]-[11]. It is well-known  that driving the Device Under Test (DUT) with a negative OFFstate gate-source bias voltage (V GS ,off) can suppress parasitic  latch-up effect of Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)  and  improve the reliability in this scenario [12]-[15]. However,  limited research has been conducted on the detrimental effects  associated with negative V GS ,off. Currently, we are making  efforts to delve deeper into the impact of negative V GS ,off on  the characteristics and performance of SiCMOS devices, as  well as their long-term stability in extreme operating  environments. This research provides a more in-depth  understanding of the reliability of SiCMOS in various  application scenarios and helps further optimize its design and  manufacturing processes, thereby driving the development and  application of SiCMOS technology.

To solve these problems, we delved into the impact of  negative V GS ,off on SiCMOS device degradation under OFFstate avalanche stress. The study identified that negative  V GS ,off conditions lead to more pronounced degradation than  0V V GS ,off and negative bias temperature instability (NBTI).  While small shifts in threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{T})}$   and on-resistance   $\mathrm{(R_{on})}$   were observed, the most notable alteration is the  corresponding reverse leakage current (I Dss ) generated by the  interface state. Technology Computer Aided Design (TCAD)  analysis further supports these findings by highlighting changes  in the electric field at the gate oxide interface. Our work  addresses an important aspect of SiCMOS device reliability  under stress, which is a valuable contribution to the field.

## III.    C ONCLUSION 

This paper extensively investigates and evaluates the  impact of negative V GS ,off on device degradation during OFFstate avalanche stress. Our findings reveal that the degradation  induced by negative V GS ,off surpasses that observed under 0V  V GS ,off and NBTI. While shifts in   $\mathrm{V_{T}}$    and   $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{on}}$  are relatively  minor, the most notable alteration manifests in I Dss , attributed to  the generation of interface state. This phenomenon is further  analyzed by TCAD, which underscores the substantial electric  field attained at the bottom of the gate oxide under avalanche  stress conditions. Additionally, the introduction of negative  V GS ,off markedly intensifies the electric field in this region,  elucidating the underlying mechanisms driving device  degradation. Through the reliability study of SiCMOS, this  paper helps to further optimize the design and manufacturing  process, thereby promoting the development and application of  SiCMOS technology.
