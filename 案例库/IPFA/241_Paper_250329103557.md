## Investigation on Gate Etching and Stability of GaN  p-FETs  

Chuying Tang 1,2 , Fangzhou  $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{l}}$  , Chun  $\mathrm{{Nu}}^{1}$  , Chenkai Deng 1,2 , Yi Zhang 1 , Yang Jiang 1 , Wenchuan Tao 1 , Wenyue   $\mathrm{Y}\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{I}}$  , Qing Wang 1  and  Hongyu Yu 1  1   School of Microelectronics, Southern University of Science and Technology, Shenzhen 518055, China  2   Harbin Institute of Technology, Harbin 150006, China  Email : yuhy@sustech.edu.cn( HY. Yu); wangq7@sustech.edu.cn(Q.W)

Abstract — In this work, an etching recipe whose reactive gas  is BCl 3  and RF power is 15W, achieved ultra-low etching rates  (600s, 45nm) and excellent surface morphology. An enhanced  GaN p-FETs was successfully developed, and the impact of defects  introduced by p-GaN etching on the gate reliability is reverseengineered. The results indicate that the gate/dielectric interface  exists interface states with a density of   ${\cal I}\!\!\!\!\theta^{I2}\!\!\sim\!\!{\cal I}\!\!\!\!\theta^{I3}\;\;c m\!\!\!\!^{-2}\!e V^{I I}\!\!\!$  ,  resulting in poor gate reliability and the high-temperature  operational characteristics .

Keywords—GaN p-FETs, etching, interface states, gate  reliability

## I.      I NTRODUCTION 

Gate etching is a critical technology for enhanced GaN pFETs. The excessive etching damage can directly lead to gate  failure. Rapid etching rates will result in uncontrollable etch  depths and uneven intra-wafer etching depths [1-3]. Therefore,  a low-damage, slow-etching-rate p-GaN etching recipe is a  research point. Additionally, there is also a significant lack of  qualitative analysis on the etched p-GaN surface.

$\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   and   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   are commonly used reaction gases for GaN  etching.  $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   plasma has a strong bombardment effect on GaN,  resulting in high etching rates and poor etching surface [4]. In  contrast,   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   plasma has a weaker bombardment effect on  GaN, exhibiting slower etching rates and better etching  morphology [5]. Therefore,   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   and   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   mixed reaction gas  is used for gate etching of GaN p-FETs. In addition, by  introducing  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  , it is possible to achieve atomic layer etching  (ALE), which can reduce etching rate and enhance etching  uniformity. Furthermore, introducing   $\mathbf{N}_{2}$   to the reaction gas  can be expected to compensate for nitrogen vacancies  generated during the etching process.

In this work, a low-damage, slow-rate gate etching process  was developed based on Cl 2 , BCl 3 , O 2 , N 2 . By preparing  enhanced GaN p-FET devices, the impact of interface defects  induced by etching on the gate reliability is explored.

## II.    D EVICE  S TRUCTURE AND  B ASIC  P ERFORMANCE 

A p-GaN/AlGaN/GaN on Si epitaxial wafer grown by  MOCVD was used in this work, as shown in Fig. 1(a). The  structure consisted of a 55 nm p-GaN with an Mg  concentration of  $3\times10^{19}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$  , a  $20\ \mathrm{nm\Al_{0.2}G a_{0.8}N}$   barrier, a  300 nm unintentionally doped GaN channel, and a GaN buffer  layer. The 2DHG formed at the p-GaN/AlGaN interface  served as the p-type channel. The hole sheet density was 1.73   $\times\:\:\:10^{13}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$  , and the hole mobility was   $10.1\ \ {\dot{\mathrm{cm}}}^{2}/{\mathrm{V}}{\cdot}{\mathrm{s}}$   as  measured by Hall.

Fig.1(a) shows the schematic of GaN p-FETs. Fig. 1(b)  shows the key processing steps for GaN p-FETs. Firstly, mesa  isolation was achieved by inductively coupled plasma (ICP)  dry etching. Secondly, Ni   $(20\ \mathrm{nm})/\dot{\mathrm{Au}}(30\ \mathrm{nm})$   ohmic metal  stack is prepared. After lift-off, it was annealed by rapid  thermal annealing in   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   atmosphere at   $500\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 5 min.

Thirdly, gate recess etching is performed by ICP. Here, the   $\mathrm{SiN}_{x}$   deposited by PECVD serves as the etch mask and  protects the ohmic contact from the potential etch damage.  The etch depth is controlled by the etch time. After that, 20  nm   ${\mathrm{Al}}_{2}{\mathrm{O}}_{3}$   is deposited by atomic layer deposition as the gate  dielectric. A 20/50 nm Pt/Au gate metal stack is deposited by  magnetron sputtering. Finally, BOE is used to open via on S/D  region for subsequent device testing. A Keithley 4200-SCS  source-meter was used to test electrical properties of GaN pFETs as well as perform  C-V  testing.

## III.  ANALYSIS OF ETCHING RATE AND SURFACE MORPHOLOGY 

Fig.2 shows the etching uniformity for three etching  recipes. It can be seen that recipe 2 has the relatively best  etching uniformity, which can be controlled within  $\pm\,2{\sim}3\;\mathrm{nm}$  .  However, the etching uniformity of recipes 1 and 3 is poor, at   $\pm\ 4{\sim}5\ \mathrm{nm}$  . The   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   plasma in recipes 2 oxidizes p-GaN and  modifies the p-GaN surface. Then, the modified layer is  removed by  $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   plasma. Therefore, recipes 2 achieves a highly  uniform etching effect similar to atomic layer etching.

Fig.3 shows that the slope of the etching time-depth lines  for recipe 1 and 2 is around 0.3. For recipe 3 with pure   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$  reaction gas,   the etching time-depth line is only 0.0365, which

is nearly 100 times less than that of recipe 1 and 2. After a long  etching time of 600 s, the etching depth of the sample is about   $45\ \mathrm{nm}$  , indicating that recipe 3 has achieved an ultra-slow  etching rate. This is because BCl 3  ICP etching has extremely  low p-GaN etching selectivity, while Cl 2  plasma has high GaN  etching selectivity [5]. It is concluded that recipe 2 achieves  good etching uniformity, while recipe 3 achieves an ultra-slow  etching rate.

Fig.4 shows the roughness of the p-GaN after etching. It is  found that the surface roughness of p-GaN without etching is  0.56 nm. For recipe 1, after 45 s and 60 s of   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}/\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   plasma  bombardment, the surface roughness of p-GaN slightly  increased, reaching   $0.67\,\mathrm{\nm}$   and   $0.69\,\mathrm{\nm}$  , respectively. It  indicates that the short-term plasma bombardment of recipe 1  causes slight damage to p-GaN, and the surface roughness  after etching has a weak dependence on etching time. For  recipe 2, it is showed that after 120 s and 180 s of  $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}/\mathrm{O}_{2}/\mathrm{N}_{2}$    plasma bombardment, the surface roughness of p-GaN  becomes 0.73 nm and 1.14 nm, respectively. As the etching  time increases, the surface roughness of p-GaN after etching  significantly increases. It indicates that the plasma  bombardment of recipe 2 caused severe damage to the p-GaN,  and the damage accumulates with etching time. This is  because the  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   plasma in the reaction gas oxidizes p-GaN. The  p-GaN surface is prone to residual oxides generated during the  etching process, resulting in a significant increase in surface  roughness. As the etching time increases, the oxidation  products accumulate more and more, resulting in a larger  surface roughness of the p-GaN. Therefore, the surface  roughness shows a dependence on etching time. For recipe 3,

it is showed that after prolonged   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   plasma bombardment  for 600 s and 800 s, the surface roughness of p-GaN   becomes  0.56 nm and 0.81 nm, respectively, which slightly increases  compared to the unetched p-GaN. The change in surface  roughness with etching time was not significant, indicating  that recipe 3 has lower etching damage. In addition, compared  to the samples etching 60 s (etching depth\~30 nm) in recipe 1,  the samples etching 600 s (etching depth\~45 nm) in recipe 3  have lower roughness. Under similar etching depths, recipe 3  has a smaller surface roughness. Indicates that compared to  recipes 1 and 2, recipe 3 has the smallest etching surface  damage.

Fig. 5(a) shows that recipe 1 achieves much burrs and  uneven edges, but the etching angle close to   $90~^{\circ}$  . This is  because the   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   plasma in recipe 1 has a strong GaN  bombardment effect, making it easy to produce more burrs at  the edges. In addition,   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   and   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   plasma in recipe 1 belong  to physical bombardment, so a more vertical etching edge can  be obtained. However, the edge burrs generated during the  etching process can affect contact quality between the gate  dielectric and p-GaN. Recipe 2 has fewer burrs and a flat edge.  But the etching angle is large, close to   $120~^{\circ}$  , as shown in Fig.  5(b). This is because the  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   plasma oxidizes p-GaN, thereby  weakening the impact intensity of   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   plasma on GaN.  However, the large etching angle of recipe 2 will increase gate  leakage. Recipe 3 has fewer burrs on the etching edge, and a  flat edge. The etching angle is close to   $90~^{\circ}$  , as shown in  Fig.5(c). This is because recipe 3 only has   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   plasma.  Compared to   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$   plasma,   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$   plasma has a relatively lower  impact intensity on GaN, resulting in less damage to the  etching interface. And   $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}$  plasma also belongs to physical  bombardment, so it can obtain a more vertical etching angle.  Therefore, it can be concluded that compared to recipe 1 and  2, recipe 3 has the optimal etching surface morphology and  etching angle. It is concluded that recipe 3 (pure BCl 3 ) has  advantages such as ultra-low etching rate   $(4.5~\mathrm{nm/min})$  ), low  surface roughness, excellent step morphology, and near  vertical etching angle. It very suitable for the preparation of  enhanced GaN p-FETs.

## IV.   A NALYSIS OF  G A N  P -FET S  G ATE  R ELIABILITY  

The enhanced-mode GaN p-FETs was prepared based on  the three gate etching recipes mentioned above. Fig. 6 shows

that the devices fabricated based on recipes 1 and 2 exhibit no  switching behavior. The devices   $(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{GS}}–\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{G}}–\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{GD}}$   is   $3{\mathrm{-}}2{\mathrm{-}}4\upmu\mathrm{m})$  )  prepared with recipe 3 exhibit excellent switching  characteristics, with I ON /I OFF  of   $10^{5}$   and threshold voltage of -  $1.5~\mathrm{V}$  . It is concluded that low-damage gate etching is one of  the key processes for enhanced GaN p-FETs.

Fig. 7 illustrates a significant threshold voltage hysteresis.  As the V GS  scan range increases,   $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{TH}}$   shifts to a more negative  bias. It indicates that as the conduction channel gradually  opens, a significant number of holes are trapped at the   $\mathrm{{Al}_{2}\mathrm{{O}_{3}/p}}$  -GaN interface.

Fig. 8 show that in the temperature testing range of   $25{\sim}100\ \ {}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , the threshold voltage of the device shows a  positive shift with the increase of the testing temperature. The  positive shift of the threshold voltage decreases with the  increase of temperature. This is because the hole electron pairs  present at the gate interface are ionized under high  temperature, resulting in an increase in the hole concentration  of p-GaN below the gate and a positive shift for threshold  voltage. The positive shift of the threshold voltage indicates  that a high density of defect states exist at the etched gate  interface. In addition, when the testing temperature rises to   $125{\sim}150\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , the transfer characteristic curve of the device  deteriorates severely, and the device exhibits no switching  performance. It indicates that the device has experienced  thermal failure, and the defects introduced by gate etching  seriously affect the high-temperature operating characteristics  of the device.

Finally, the interface density of states at   $\mathrm{{Al}_{2}\mathrm{{O}_{3}/p}}$  -GaN  below the gate was extract. Fig. 10 (a) shows the frequency  conversion (from  $100\,\mathrm{kHz}$   to   $900\:\mathrm{kHz}$  ) test results of the gate  C-V  characteristics of the device. It can be seen that the gate  C-V  curve of   $\mathrm{{Al}_{2}\mathrm{{O}_{3}/p}}$  -GaN/AlGaN structure has two rising  edges. The first rising edge corresponds to the formation of  2DHG channels, while the second rising edge refers to the  charge accumulation at the   $\mathrm{Al_{2}O_{3}/p{-}G a N}$   interface. As the  testing frequency changes, the  C-V  curve shifts. It indicate that  different energy trap densities (Dit) exist at the   $\mathrm{{Al}_{2}\mathrm{{O}_{3}/p}}$   GaN  interface [7]. Subsequently, the energy with traps at the  interface was extracted, as shown in the illustration in Fig.  10(b). It can be concluded that the trap energy introduced after  etching is mainly distributed in the range of   $0.24{\sim}0.29~\mathrm{eV}.$  .  Further, the interface trap density of states was extracted by  using formulas [7, 8]:  Dit   $\mathrm{\overline{{\overline{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}}}}=\mathrm{\DeltaC_{ox}}/\mathrm{q}\Delta\mathrm{\bar{E}}\mathrm{-}(\mathrm{\bar{C_{ox}}}\mathrm{+}\mathrm{C_{B}})/\mathrm{q}^{2}$  , where   $\mathrm{C_{ox}}$   and   $\mathrm{C_{B}} $  are the  capacitance of gate dielectric and barrier, ΔE is the interface  trap frequency-dependent energy difference. As shown in  Fig10 (b), the extracted interface state density at the gate is   $10^{12}{\sim}10^{13}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-2}\ \mathrm{eV}^{-1}$  , indicating that a significant number of  defects are introduced at the p-GaN surface after etching.

## V.    C ONCLUSION  

A gate etching recipe is developed, with ultra-low etching  rate   $(4.5\ \mathrm{nm/min})$  , low etched surface roughness, excellent  step morphology, and an almost vertical etch angle. It highly  suitable for the fabrication of recessed gate enhanced GaN pFETs. At the same time, the impact mechanism of etchinduced interface states on gate reliability is investigated. The  interface state defects introduced by gate etching lead to a  decrease in gate reliability and high-temperature operation  capability of the device. It provides crucial guidance for  further optimization of the GaN p-FETs device process.
