## Identification of Crack-Detect Failure Analysis in  16nm Finfet Technology  

Noor Iza Atiqah Supaat 1 , Imran Arif Abdul Jamil 1 , Liew Chik Hooi 1 , Tan Hui Hui 1 , Au Yin Fun 1 , Siow Cheng Liang 1

1

NXP Semiconductor Shd Bhd, 2 Jalan SS 8/2, Free Industrial Zone Sungei Way, 47300 Petaling Jaya, Selangor, Malaysia.  Phone : (+60)3 7669 2000, Email:  noor.iza.atiqah.supaat@nxp.com

Abstract — The first purpose of crack detect test is to detect  any crack detect failure. The crack detect test helps to identify  the possible die failure along the die perimeter. Based on the  Failure Analysis (FA) flow, the test was initially run during the  verification phase to detect any failure. If the test failed, the  analysis was continued with the inspection including high  optical   microscope   inspection   and   backside   Infra-Red  inspection (IR). If the crack/die chip can be observed during  the inspection, the analysis should have a route to the root  cause of the failure. While if not, then FA needs to analyze the  failure further. This paper will further focus on identifying  crack detect FA in 16nm Finfet Technology. Crack detect  circuitry not only detects the metal lines that get cracked, but it  also utilizes inverter buffers to propagate a signal around the  die periphery.  The buffers along the die periphery could be  easily probed with an Electro-Optical Probe (EOP) or Laser  Voltage Probe (LVP) to isolate the failing area due to simple  and bigger transistor geometry size. Inverter buffers were used  to acquire EOP/LVP signals. Each device was provided with a  die map with a crack-detect chain section. The purpose is to  identify a die chip/crack at the edge of the die. This technical  paper will focus on the challenges of failure analysis techniques  used to locate the failing nets precisely. The result of Physical  Failure Analysis (PFA) revealed the cause of the failure which  can be designated to different root causes by signature  analysis. Few case studies presented in this paper will  demonstrate how the nanoprobing analysis during the PFA  stage helps to isolate the defect region.

Keywords—Crack detect, EOP, LVP, inverter, FA, physical  defect, gate, drain, metal, patterning defect, open, 16nm, Finfet,  nanoprobing.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Crack ring test implementation is a representative to  show the connection for crack ring testing. The driver on the  crack ring is the Test Data Register (TDR) or Test Data  Output (TDO). The crack ring cannot be forced to a value  directly on the pin or observed directly on the pin. Only the  TDR/TDO register can be used to assess the crack ring.  Functional patterns were used to evaluate the crack ring by  driving through TDR/TDO and observing the value in  another register. For example, the crack detect ring pattern  drives the test pin 1, the crack ring TDR reads with expected  data 1s, and vice versa. Other than data on TDR, data on the  other pins will be insignificant and can be ignored. The sense  points in the TDR were identified based on the device.  Different devices have different sense points, propagation,  and even configurations to verify.

This paper describes multiple techniques used to directly  identify the fault location through Electrical Failure Analysis  (EFA) such as Photon Emission Microscopy (PEM), ElectroOptical Frequency Mapping (EOFM), and Electro-Optical  Probing (EOP). Moreover, a localized probing technique,

nanoprobing was used to reveal the indirect defects at the  transistor level. Electrical characterization at the transistor  level also offers a suitable guide for the basic physical  analysis needed to "visualize" defects.

## II.   P RINCIPLE BACKGROUND   

Photon Emission Microscopy (PEM) is an optical fault  isolation technique commonly used for fault isolation and  circuit debugging. However, a failure mode with challenging  behavior like jitter signal and propagation delay is quite  difficult to isolate. While EOFM and EOP could measure the  transistor switching and visualize it as an image by operating  the transistors at a specific frequency which could be  observed in the circuit. Signal toggling at switching  transistors can be measured during operation in real-time [3]  – [4]. The electro-optical technique is a laser-based optical  tool with non-destructiveness and contactless through the  backside silicon surface. In the EOP/EOFM techniques, a   $1340\,\mathrm{\nm}$   infrared incoherent laser probes the waveforms  nondestructively in an integrated circuit internally. The laser  is focused directly at the level of the active areas of the  device through the backside. The voltage at a particular node  of the device influences the reflected light intensity in the  parts-per-million range. With the sensitive detection system,  this signal can be extracted, and semi-quantitative voltage  information can be caught [5].

## III.   M ETHODOLOGY 

Crack detect failure analysis begins with failure  characterization on the sample unit. This is to confirm that  the unit failed crack detect by using the FA environment  tools. The unit was run with a crack detect test program  which helps to identify the possible initial die cracks or die  chips along the die perimeter. The methodology of  investigating crack detect failure is by using the crack detect  ring pattern and setting it in a looping pattern to optically  probe the signal transition. After completing the verification  phase, the next stage will be the EFA phase. Prior to the EFA  step, the analysis is initiate with high optical microscope  inspection and backside Infra-Red inspection (IR). If the  crack can be observed during the inspection, the analysis  should have a route to the root cause of the failure. Whereas  if not, then the FA needs to analyze the failure again. EFA is  a phase of non-destructively defect localization from the  backside. EOP was used to analyze the signal toggling at the  switching transistors. This helps to identify the detailed  switching information of the faulty transistors. EOFM  analysis was carried out to confirm and localize the failure  by providing a visual map of active components toggling at a  particular frequency [1]-[2]. Based on the EOP/EOFM result,  the defect location can be isolated precisely. Additional  Photon Emission Microscopy (PEM) study was conducted to  compare the behavior of the good and bad units. Then the

analysis continued with the circuitry studies with the crack  detect points or crack detect tap out path to identify the  suspected failing path. This step is crucial before proceeding  to the destructive PFA step. During the PFA step, the sample  unit was extracted from the package. This is to enable the  delayering process to the respective metal layer to facilitate  nanoprobing analysis.

The challenges in PFA are driven primarily by smaller  device feature sizes to nanometers or less. In addition to the  technical challenges, infrastructure changes are also likely to  occur. Even though, the I-V curve of a single transistor is the  most effortless task in the electronics world. If the size of the  transistor is reduced to tens of nanometers or less, this task  becomes incredibly challenging. Probing on small structures  requires a nano prober with high positioning accuracy and  very stable positioners. Nanoprobing is crucial to provide a  fast and simple system to effectively increase the probing  throughput [6] – [7]. The defects may appear to be a crack or  die chip defect. Also, can be standard process-related issues  that are commonly found using the industry-standard  techniques. In this paper, two case studies will be presented  for the identification of crack-detect failure analysis in 16nm  Finfet Technology.

## V.   D ISCUSSION  &   C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we have presented two different types of  crack detect cases with two different devices. Both are from  the same 16nm Finfet technology. Functional patterns were  used to evaluate the crack ring by driving through TDR/TDO  and observing the value in another register. EOP was used to  probe the buffers along the die periphery to isolate the failing  area. Each device was provided with a die map with a crackdetect chain section to identify a die chip/crack at the edge of  the die. Thus, a custom test setup was developed to meet the  required period and frequency for EOFM and EOP analysis.  The looping setup differs from device to device due to the  testing methodology in ATE. The looping frequency is  preferable above 20Khz for better EOFM acquisition due to  the frequency bandwidth limitation of the tools. The  modification of the looping can be evaluated by performing  EOP to confirm at least one toggling signal occurred within  the looping cycle. Trial and error can be performed until the  toggling signal is observed within the expected frequency.  The minimum requirement of loop length during the EOP  analysis should be below 120us. With periodical signal  toggling after the pattern modification, EOP and EOFM will  be easy to perform for fast electrical fault isolation.

Due to Finfet technology, the defect finding is challenging  since the TEM approach and direction need to consider the  3D profile of the fin. Plus, the crack detect is mostly located  at the wafer edge. Hence nanoprobing is needed to further  isolate the possible fault location. To probe on small

structures, require a nano prober with high positioning  accuracy. Through multi-metal layers and scaling down, the  PFA team encountered difficulties in FA. Failure isolation  for PFA has more challenges with smaller defects and  process changes. The responses to process conditions  changed due to each failure analysis technique giving  challenges to nanoprobing. Hence, nanoprobing is crucial to  provide a fast and efficient system to effectively increase the  probing output that was used to locate the failing nets  precisely. The result of Physical Failure Analysis (PFA)  revealed the cause of the failure which can be assigned to  the different root causes by signature analysis. In  conclusion, the crack detect failures is not only about the  cracked metal lines, but also there is possibility of several  types of defects that affects the crack detect itself.
