## Analysis and Characterization Techniques for Wide  Band Gap Semiconductor Materials  

Yan Liu, Yupu Wang\*, Siming Wang, Kaixuan Li, Ting Zhang, Qiong Zhang, Anli Yang, Bing Yang, Qichao Ding, Zhihong Mai  1 Hubei Jiufengshan Laboratory, 9 Jiulonghu Street, Wuhan, Hubei Province 430206, P. R. China  \*Phone: +86 27-59301999, Email :  wangyupu@jfslab.com.cn

Abstract —In this paper, based on the challenges of analysis  and characterization of wide band gap semiconductor materials,  the analysis techniques and characterization techniques were  evaluated with SiC and GaN epitaxy wafers. Tool configuration  and analysis results are discussed. A proposal of wafer  characterization plan is given for the development of the  fabrication technology of a wide band gap semiconductor  device.

Keywords—Wide band gap semiconductor, SiC, GaN,  fabrication technology, material characterization.

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

The rapid growth of the compound semiconductor market  is mainly attributed to the wide applications of compound  semiconductors such as GaAs, InP, SiC, and GaN in light  emitting diodes (LEDs), optoelectronic devices, radio  frequency devices, and power devices in communications,  lighting, new energy vehicles, and power supply. GaAs and  InP represent the second generation of semiconductor  technology, while SiC and GaN represent the third generation  of semiconductor technologies. They are attracting great  attention due to their excellent high-frequency and hightemperature performance. However, due to the complexity of  its manufacturing process, the manufacturing cost also  increases accordingly.

From the industry's forward-looking perspective, the  third-generation compound semiconductors have attracted  extensive attention in the field of power semiconductors with  the applications of high temperature, high voltage, high  frequency, high power, and high radiation resistance, because  of their excellent electrical and thermal properties, as well as  chemical stability [1-7]. With the development of material  growth technology and device manufacturing technology,  especially the growth of demand in new energy vehicles, 5G  communications, rail transit, and UHV power transmission,  the third-generation compound semiconductor power devices  have entered a rapid growth period. The analysis and  characterization play a critical role in identifying defects and  process issues to improve the material quality for device  fabrication.

It   is   important   to   establish   a   comprehensive  characterization of compound semiconductor materials and  devices. In this paper, we focus on the material  characterization techniques of the wide bandgap (WBG)  epitaxial wafers, and characterization and analysis data were  obtained to have a more detailed understanding of the quality  of WBG epitaxial wafers [8-10].

## A.   Crystallography and physical characterization  

Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) is the most  critical tool to determine the atomic composition and lattice  structure. Other technologies such as X-ray diffraction  (XRD), Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), and Electron  Backscatter   Diffraction   Spectroscopy   (EBDS)   are  indispensable in analyzing wafer lattice structure, quality,  and defect. WBG semiconductor devices are normally  fabricated on epitaxial wafers. Compound semiconductor  epitaxial wafers have dedicated epitaxial compositions for  different applications. Besides the conventional high  crystallography characterization, more attention needs to be  paid to the layer interface and its roughness. Fig. 1 shows a  high-resolution TEM image of the SiC epitaxial layer with   $S i O_{2}$   layer by dry oxidation. It proved that SiC was  crystallized. Depending on the epitaxial wafer process, other  crystallography analyses, like XRD, XRR, and EBSD, can

also be added to the crystallography and physical  characterization specification list.

## B.   Composition and trace element analysis  

The use of Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry (SIMS), Xray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS), Auger Electron  Spectroscopy (AES), and Energy Dispersion X-ray  spectroscopy (EDX) can be used to analyze the composition  elements and depth profile of elements. In particular,  Dynamic Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry (D-SIMS) can  provide qualitative, quantitative, and depth profile analysis of  semiconductor doping elements. Fig. 3 is the ToF-SIMS  composition depth profile of SiC and GaN HEMT epitaxial  wafers. It is shown that the layer structure of the GaN  epitaxial layer is found to be AlGaN/GaN/AlGaN/GaN, and  Na/K/Si/Ca are surface contaminants. While for SiC, it is

shown that there is no layer structure found in SiC epitaxial  layer, except the surface contamination of F and Si oxide. The  ratio of Al to Ga of the first AlGaN layer is found to be higher  than the second layer. Also, the ratio of Al to Ga varies with  depth in the first AlGaN layer. D-SIMS can also be used for  composition analysis, shown in Fig. 4. The GaN HEMT layer  structure   is   found   to   be   an  AlGaN/GaN/AlGaN/GaN/AlGaN/Sapphire substrate. The  sharp interface indicates epitaxial growth instead of diffusion  or implant with gradual change. However, due to the  sensitivity of the D-SIMS system, composition analysis could  introduce some contamination which could affect the  detection limits of some of the elements. Other techniques,  like XPS, AES, and EDX can also provide the composition  information, with a lower sensitivity, compared to SIMS  analysis. Fig. 5 is the XPS spectrum on a SiC epitaxial wafer  which proved that it contained Si, C, O.

## C.   Chemical bonds and binding energy  

The molecular bonds and corresponding molecular  binding energies of compound semiconductors can be  analyzed using XPS, FTIR, and Raman technologies. XPS  can detect the component elements and binding energies of  the materials, and obtain the element depth profile of epitaxial  thin film materials. Fig. 7 shows a Raman spectrum on a SiC  epitaxial wafer. Together with Fig. 5, abundant information  on chemical bonding and other information on SiC binding  energy can be used to monitor and control the fabrication  process.

## E.   Physical properties  

In the application of electronic devices and optoelectronic  devices, analysis and characterization of the electrical  properties, optical properties, thermal properties, and  mechanical properties of materials are needed. Electrical  properties including conductivity, carrier concentration, and

mobility can be measured using contact and non-contact  methods, such as Hall effect, four-probe, and mercury probes.  Optical properties can be tested by PL, FTIR, and Raman. PL  is a non-destructive way of determining the electronic band  gap, providing a quantitative method for the analysis of the  elemental composition of compound semiconductors. In the  meantime, the high sensitivity of this technology provides the  possibility of identifying extremely low-concentration  impurities. Thermal characteristics can be measured using a  thermal imaging microscope. Its thermal mapping function  can measure the temperature distribution on the surface of an  object and is used to measure the thermal resistance of  semiconductor materials. In addition, the technologies of  Nano-Indentation (NI) and Atomic Force Microscope (AFM)  can also be used to analyze and measure the mechanical  properties of materials. Among them, the AFM is equipped  with a nanomechanics module to measure the mechanical  properties of nanoscale structures.

Fig. 13 is the PL spectrum on GaN. The laser is at a  wavelength of 325 nm. The scan range is from 326 nm to  1000 nm. A strong local exciton was observed at 360 nm and  yellow light emitting around 550 nm.

## F.   Material characterization on WBG devices.   

The manufacturing technology of semiconductor devices  is as important as the material growth technology. In terms of  device analysis, the dimensions of the manufactured  microstructure need to meet the design parameters. Design  parameter requirements include physical manufacturing  technology specification (pMTS) and many other indicators.  Hence SEM and TEM carry out a lot of critical dimension  (CD) measurements. SEM and TEM EDX are powerful in  microstructure analysis and material identification. SEM is  usually used to analyze defects in microstructure patterns,  wiring connections, and circuit reconstruction. It is also

widely used in reverse engineering work for intellectual  property maintenance and reference of circuits and layouts.  Based on obtaining the microstructure of semiconductor  devices and their materials, we can judge the process flow  and the problems caused during the manufacturing process.  In addition to manufacturing and material growth technology,  reliability testing of semiconductor devices is also an  important. Reliability testing is used to evaluate device  performances and stability over extended periods of usage  and under various environmental conditions. Common  reliability tests include temperature cycle testing, damp heat  testing, voltage stress testing, degradation testing, etc. These  tests can help determine the device's longevity and stability,  as well as its performance under specific operating  conditions.

## III.    C ONCLUSION 

A comprehensive wafer characterization and analysis  technique was assessed on WBG epitaxial wafers. For WBG  semiconductor device fabrication, more attention needs to be  paid to the WBG materials, comparing the Si-based  fabrication process. In addition, material properties in  mechanical, thermal, and optical properties need to be  considered in the material characterization and analysis.
