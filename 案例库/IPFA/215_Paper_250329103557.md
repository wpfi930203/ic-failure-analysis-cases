## Deep-Level Trap States Characterization of 650V   p-GaN Schottky Gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs  

Xiaohu Wang 1 , Xuefeng Zheng, 1  Danmei Lin, 1  Xiaohua Ma, 1  and Yue Hao 1  1  State Key Laboratory of Wide-Bandgap Semiconductor Devices and Integrated Technology, the school of microelectronics, Xidian  University, Xi’an, Shaanxi, China  Phone : (0086)29-88201759, Email: xfzheng@mail.xidian.edu.cn

Abstract —In this work, the trap states of three nitride  regions (GaN layer, AlGaN layer, and p-GaN layer) of   ${\bf650\;V\;p\cdot}$  - GaN Schottky gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs were characterized  using capacitance mode deep-level transient spectroscopy (CDLTS) to detect the trap information in each region. The  capacitance-voltage characteristics at a frequency of 1MHz of pGaN Schottky gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs were employed to  distinguish three nitride regions at different voltage ranges.  Moreover, the C-DLTS technique with different pulses is used  to characterize the trap states of different nitride regions. The  electron trap   $\mathbf{E1(E c-0.546~eV)}$   was characterized in the GaN  layer, and hole traps   $\mathbf{H1(Ev+0.175~eV)}$  ), H  $\mathbf{\left[2(E v+0.246\ e V)\right.}$  , and   $\mathbf{H3(E_{V}+0.197\;eV)}$   were detected in GaN layer, AlGaN layer, and  p-GaN layer, respectively. Electron trap E1 may be a deep-level  acceptor trap related to Fe, while hole traps H1, H2, and H3 are  considered as Mg acceptor traps. The characterization of trap  states in different layers can provide a solid foundation for  reliability evaluation and process improvement. To sum up, the  reliability of the device can be effectively improved by  controlling the residue and diffusion of Mg in the process and  inhibiting the defect level in the device.

Keywords—C-DLTS, GaN, p-GaN, AlGaN, trap states

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Due to the advantages of wide bandgap energy, high  electron saturation velocity, high critical electric field, and  high thermal conductivity, GaN power devices have excellent  application potential in aerospace, communication, data  centers, and other power switching fields [1-4]. The necessity  of normally-off GaN HEMTs in power electronic circuits lies  in ensuring simple driving and safe operation of power  electronic circuits.

Compared to other types of normally-off GaN power  transistors, p-GaN gate AlGaN/GaN HEMT has the  advantages of a simple manufacturing process and low cost. It  has become one of the most mature technologies for normallyoff GaN HEMT implementation solutions. By growing a pGaN layer above the AlGaN barrier layer in the gate region,  the conduction band at the interface of AlGaN/GaN  heterojunction is effectively raised to above Fermi level, so as  to realize enhancement-mode GaN HEMTs [5].

Nevertheless, p-GaN gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs still  possess many reliability problems, such as threshold voltage  instability [6],  $R_{\mathrm{ON}}$   degradation [7], radiation effect [8-9], etc.  The instability of threshold voltage may seriously affect the  stability and safety of the circuit. Among them, traps play an  important role in reliability problems such as threshold  voltage instability. The defect characterization of p-GaN  HEMTs has become particularly important in process  improvement [10-12].

In this work, capacitance mode deep-level transient  spectroscopy (C-DLTS) was used to characterize the trap  states within three semiconductor regions (GaN layer, AlGaN

layer, and p-GaN layer) of 650 V p-GaN Schottky gate  AlGaN/GaN HEMTs.

## II.   D EVICE AND CHARACTERIZATION 

The p-GaN Schottky Gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs structure  used in this work is shown in Fig. 1. The structure of gate  longitudinal region is composed of Si substrate, AlN nuclear  layer (releasing lattice stress),   $4~\ensuremath{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  Fe-doped GaN buffer  layer, 100 nm unintentionally doped GaN channel layer, 15  nm AlGaN barrier layer, 100 nm p-GaN layer and gate metal  (Ti/Au) from bottom to top. The epitaxial layer is completed  by metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD). After  the GaN/AlGaN/p-GaN epitaxial structure is realized, the pgan layer outside the gate region is etched based on   $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}/\mathrm{BCl}_{3}/\mathrm{O}_{2}$   plasma. Subsequently, a Ti/Al/Ni/Au (  $30\,\mathrm{\nm}$     $/180\;\mathrm{nm}\,/50\;\mathrm{nm}\,/\,60\;\mathrm{nm})$   metal stack was formed by electron  beam evaporation, and then it was rapidly annealed for 30 s at   $850\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   in the N2 atmosphere, and finally, an ohmic contact  was formed. The passivation layer of   $\mathrm{Si}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$   is realized by  plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD).  Finally, the gate metal formed with   $\mathrm{Ti}/\mathrm{Au}(35\mathrm{nm}/200\mathrm{nm})$   was  obtained by the sputtering process. The gate length is   $1~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  ,  the gate-source spacing is  $2~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  , the gate-drain spacing is 11   $\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , and the source field plate (SFP) spacing is   $5.3~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . The  threshold voltage of this device is 1 V.

Considering a series of reliability degradation of p-GaN  Schottky gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs, such as dispersion,  current collapse, threshold voltage instability, and dynamic  on-resistance degradation, effectively identifying traps in  devices is of great significance for understanding the causes  of reliability degradation. In particular, the very deep energy  level of traps with a long time constant may affect the DC  parameters of devices, involving the variation of the DC bias  point. As a powerful and effective characterization method,  the C-DLTS electrical characterization technique can  effectively clarify the nature and spatial location of traps in  semiconductor devices. The detection range of C-DLTS is a  deep-level trap about 1 eV away from the band edges[13]. The

trapped carriers in the depletion region are emitted under  thermal excitation, which leads to the transient change of  capacitance.

In order to illustrate the principle of C-DLTS, p-GaN  Schottky Gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs (the measurement  voltage of the gate is   $-5\mathrm{~V~}$  , and the drain voltage and source  voltage are 0 V.) under deep depletion conditions are  considered, and the pulse voltage of 1 V is applied with a 1 ms  pulse width, which is shown in Fig 2 (a). That is,   $V_{\mathrm{m}}$   is   $-5\mathrm{~V~}$  ,  and   $V_{\mathfrak{p}}$   is 1 V.

The trapping and emission process of electron trap   $E_{\mathrm{T}}$   in  n-GaN is used to analyze the DLTS principle. Figure 3(a)  shows the state of electron trap   $\ensuremath{{E_{\mathrm{T}}}}$   in a deep depletion state,  where the depletion region is widened and the electron trap  $E_{\mathrm{T}}$  is empty. When the pulse filling voltage of 1V is applied, the

depletion region of n-GaN shrinks, which corresponds to the  process of electron trapping by electron trap  $E_{\mathrm{T}}$   and the energy  band result of Fig 3(b).

When the pulse voltage    $V_{\mathfrak{p}}$   ends and returns to the initial  static bias    $V_{\mathrm{m}}$   of -5V, the depletion region widens. However,  the emission process of electron traps takes some time, which  is the trap time constant(τ). In order to maintain the electrical  neutrality of the depletion region, more positive ions in the  space charge region are needed to compensate for the trapping  effect of the electron trap, which leads to the instantaneous  reduction of the capacitance, as shown in Fig 2(b). With the  increase of time, the electrons trapped by the electron trap are  gradually emitted to the conduction band, and its capacitance  gradually returns to the initial value of   $C_{0}$  . The above process  is the process of electron trapping and electron emission in the  n-GaN depletion region. For the hole trap in the n-GaN layer,  the capacitance response is shown in Fig 2(c). The formula of  the capacitance is shown as follows:

$$\begin{array}{r}{C=\frac{\varepsilon S}{d}}\end{array}$$
(1)

where C is capacitance, ε is the dielectric constant, S is the  area of the junction, and d is the width of the depletion region.

Considering the detailed balance and ignoring the  degeneracy, the thermal emission rate   $(e_{\mathrm{th}})$   of the deep-level   $E_{\mathrm{T}}$   can be expressed as[13]

$$\begin{array}{r}{e_{t h}(T)=\frac{1}{\tau}=\sigma\vartheta N_{C}e x p\left(\frac{E_{T}-E_{C}}{k T}\right)}\end{array}$$
(2)

where  $T$   is the absolute temperature,  $\upsigma$   is the trap cross-section,  υ is the average thermal velocity,  $N_{\mathrm{C}}$   is the conduction band  density of states,  $E_{\mathrm{C}}$   is the conduction band energy minimum,  and  $k$   is the Boltzmann constant.

For  $N_{\mathrm{T}}\ll N_{\mathrm{D}}$  , the transient change of n-GaN capacitance  in the rate window range from   $t_{1}$   to  $t_{2}$   of the doped donor   $(N_{\mathrm{D}})$  can be expressed as[14]

$$\begin{array}{r}{\frac{\Delta C(t)}{C_{0}}=\frac{N_{T}}{2N_{D}}\biggl(e x p\left(-\frac{t_{2}}{\tau}\right)-e x p\left(-\frac{t_{1}}{\tau}\right)\biggr)}\end{array}$$
where   $C_{0}$   is the steady-state value of the capacitance and   $\Delta C$    is the amplitude of the capacitance transient. The maximum  value of τ can be obtained by differentiating formula (3), and  the result is shown in formula (4).

$$\begin{array}{r}{\tau_{m a x}=\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{\ln(t_{2}/t_{1})}}\end{array}$$
(4)

Combining formulas (2), (3), and (4), and carrying out  logarithmic operation, the Arrhenius curve can be obtained as  follows.

$$\begin{array}{r}{\ln(\tau T^{2})=\ln\left(\frac{T^{2}}{q\sigma\vartheta N_{C}}\right)+\frac{E_{T}}{k T}}\end{array}$$
(5)

The concentration of traps can be obtained by the formula  (3), and the result is shown in the following formula[14].

$$\begin{array}{r}{N_{T}=\frac{\Delta C_{m a x}}{C_{0}}\frac{2r^{r/(r-1)}}{1-r}N_{D}}\end{array}$$
where  $r\,{=}\,\,t_{2}/t_{1},\,N_{\mathrm{T}}$   is the concentration of traps. To sum up, the  transient DLTS with variable temperature capacitance can  effectively extract information such as trap concentration, trap  time constant, and trap energy level.

In order to accurately locate the defect response region of  p-GaN Schottky gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs, different voltage  steady-state bias and pulse voltage were applied to  characterize the trap states within different nitride regions.  Fig. 4 shows capacitance-voltage characteristics of p-GaN  Schottky   Gate   AlGaN/GaN   HEMTs   with   1   MHz  measurement frequency. The gate structure of p-GaN  Schottky gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs is considered as a backto-back series-connected metal/p-GaN Schottky diode and pGaN/AlGaN/GaN diode[10], as shown in Fig. 4. When the  gate voltage   $V_{\mathrm{G}}$   is biased in the range of   $-5\;\mathrm{~V~}{\sim}1\;\mathrm{~V~}$  , the

capacitance response positions are the GaN buffer layer and  the GaN channel layer. When   $V_{\mathrm{G}}$   increases to 1 V (  $(V_{\mathrm{TH}}{=}1\mathrm{V})$  ,  2DEG begins to form, which indicates that the capacitance  response is the AlGaN layer in the voltage range of   $1\ \mathrm{V}{\sim}1.6$    V. When   $V_{\mathrm{G}}$   increases to   $1.6~\mathrm{V}$  , the capacitance reaches the  maximum, and after that, the voltage bias is all p-GaN layer.

Firstly, the filling pulse (  $\mathrm{\Delta}V_{\mathrm{m}}\,{=}\,{-}5\;\mathrm{V}$  ,   $V_{\mathfrak{p}}\!=\!1\;\mathrm{V}$  ) was applied  to probe the trap states of the GaN layer, and the electron trap  E1   $(\mathrm{E_{C}}.0.546~\mathrm{eV})$   and hole trap H1   $(\mathrm{{E_{V}}{+}0.175\,\,\,e V)}$   were  detected, as shown in Fig 3. The concentrations of electron  trap E1 and hole trap H1 are   $1.24{\times}10^{14}\,\mathrm{cm}^{3}$   and   $1.47{\times}10^{14}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-}$  3 , respectively. The capture cross-sections of E1 and H1 are   $3.6{\times}\bar{10^{-18}}\ \ \mathrm{cm}^{2} $  and   $\bar{3.4{\times}10^{-21}}~\mathrm{~cm}^{2}$  , respectively. Previous  studies have shown that the electron trap E1 may be a deeplevel acceptor trap of the buffer layer, such as the Fe-related  trap[15-16], which completely corresponds to the Fe doping  of the buffer layer in this paper. Generally, acceptor trap  doping, such as C and Fe doping, in deep energy levels of the  buffer layer can effectively reduce the background carriers of  the buffer layer, thus improving its resistivity and obtaining  higher working voltage. The hole trap H1 in the GaN layer  may be Mg residue or   $\mathrm{Mg}$   diffusion introduced in the  process[17-20].

## III.   C ONCLUSIONS 

In this work, the C-DLTS technique was used to  distinguish the trap states within different nitride regions in pGaN Schottky Gate AlGaN/GaN HEMTs. The traps located  at different positions of p-GaN HEMTs are systematically  characterized based on the DLTS technique, and the  corresponding trap concentrations are extracted. For the most  mature p-GaN HEMTs, it still face many reliability problems,  such as threshold voltage shift, irradiation effect, etc.  Therefore, it is of great practical significance to characterize  its defect and locate the defect position by DLTS to further  improve the quality of the process. Controlling the Mg  diffusion in the process and suppressing the defect level in the  device can effectively improve the reliability of the device.
