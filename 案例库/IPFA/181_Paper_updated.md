## Analysis of Distinct Degradation Behavior in   LTPS  TFTs under Hot Carrier Condition with Varying  Gate Voltage  

Hong-Yi Tu 1 , Ting-Chang Chang 2 , Tsung-Ming Tsai 1 , Li-Chan Sun 1 , Chen-Wei Kuo 1 , Chia-Chuan Wu 3 , Ya-Ting Chien 1

1 Department of Materials and Optoelectronic Science, National Sun Yat-Sen University, Kaohsiung, 80424, Taiwan.  2 Department of Physics, National Sun Yat-Sen University, Kaohsiung, 80424, Taiwan.  3 Department of Electrical Engineering, National Sun Yat-Sen University, Kaohsiung, 80424, Taiwan.  Phone : +886 956-161-216, Email : tutuyi1216@gmail.com

Abstract  — In this research, a distinct degradation  behavior is observed in the low-temperature polycrystallinesilicon thin-film transistor under hot carrier condition with  varying driving gate voltages. The factor causing this distinct  degradation behavior can be generalized into two terms.

The first term is the gate-to-drain voltage, which  dominates the degree of impact ionization, controlling the  generation of electron-hole pairs. Furthermore, the gate-todrain voltage also influences the distribution of electric field  direction and strength at the drain edge, governing the  trapping of electrons into the gate insulator at the drain edge  and at the buffer. The second factor contributing to the  distinct degradation behavior is the stress current induced  thermal in the channel. The current flowing in the channel  induces Joule heating, leading to the occurrence of negative  bias temperature instability in the channel. Consequently,  higher driving currents exacerbate the degradation associated  with negative bias temperature instability.

In summary, the degradation behavior in the lowtemperature polycrystalline-silicon thin-film transistor under  hot carrier conditions with varying driving gate voltages is  influenced simultaneously by the gate-to-drain voltage and  the driving current.

Keywords—Low-temperature   Polycrystalline   silicon  (LTPS), Thin Film Transistor (TFT), Hot Carrier (HC),  Negative Bias Temperature Instability (NBTI)

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

In recent years, display technology has progressed  significantly, showcasing improvements in mobility and  device structure. Currently, various materials are utilized as  the active layer in thin-film transistor (TFT) devices,  including metal oxide, organic semiconductor, amorphous  silicon, and polycrystalline silicon. Among the mentioned  materials, low-temperature polycrystalline silicon (LTPS)  stands out as the most promising candidate for advancing  display technology. LTPS offers several advantages, such as  higher carrier mobility (ranging from 50 to   $25\mathrm{\bar{0}~c m}^{2}/\mathrm{V}{\cdot}\mathrm{s})$  )  and higher driving capability. Furthermore, LTPS is a stable  material, contributing to outstanding reliability performance  in LTPS TFTs, enabling the realization of novel display  technologies. Recently, LTPS TFTs are massive applied as  driving TFTs for active-matrix organic light-emitting diodes  (AM-OLED)  [ 1 ]-[ 3 ] . Furthermore, due to its high driving  current, LTPS TFTs are widely employed in active matrix  micro LED displays for driving and controlling the  brightness of micro LEDs [4]-[6]. Additionally, given its  elevated driving capability, LTPS TFTs are extensively  used in system-on-panel (SOP) development contributing to  the achievement of super-narrow border displays [7]-[9].

Moreover, LTPS can be applied in flexible displays on  plastic substrates, facilitated by its low process temperature  [10]-[12]. In recent year, LTPS TFT has been integrated  with   InGaZnO   TFT   to   realize   Low-Temperature  Polycrystalline Oxide (LTPO) technology, with the driving  capability of LTPS TFT and the low off-state current  advantage of InGaZnO. The development of LTPO  technology enables the realization of high-performance  displays with low power consumption [13]-[15].

LTPS TFTs, with the aforementioned advantages, are  extensively used in the display industry, integrated circuit  industry and research. However, despite their widespread  application, there are still some reliability issues that need  clarification and resolution. As previously mentioned,  LTPS TFTs often serve as driving transistors in various  applications which make LTPS TFTs frequently operating  under hot carrier condition. Moreover, these devices may  function under diverse driving conditions to meet different  application requirements, where variations in gate voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{g})}$   are applied. The impact of the performance under  these distinct driving conditions is crucial for predicting  device degradation. However, this aspect has not been  discussed or clarified to date.

In this research, a distinct degradation behavior is  observed under varying  $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions in the presence of hot  carrier stress. ## II.   E XPERIMENT 

The devices utilized in the research are p-type LTPS  TFTs grown on a polyimide (PI) substrate with rigid glass.  Initially, PI is spin-coated onto the rigid glass.  Subsequently, a buffer layer containing   $\mathrm{Si}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$   and   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  ,  with a total thickness of 800nm, is deposited onto the PI  substrate   using   plasma-enhanced   chemical   vapor  deposition (PECVD). Following the deposition of the  buffer layer, a 50nm amorphous silicon layer is deposited  via PECVD, followed by a dehydrogenation process  involving furnace annealing for one hour at   $300^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  . In the  next step, a 308nm XeCl excimer laser with an energy  density of   $420\mathrm{mJ}/\mathrm{cm}^{2}$   is applied in the excimer laser  annealing (ELA) process to recrystallize the amorphous  silicon into polycrystalline silicon. Following the ELA  process,   $90\mathrm{nm}\;\;\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and   $20\mathrm{nm}\;\;\mathrm{Si}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$   are sequentially  deposited by PECVD to serve as the gate insulator (GI).  Subsequently, a 300nm molybdenum layer is sputterdeposited as the gate metal, and the gate region is patterned.  The self-align boron ion implantation process is then  applied to create the source/drain region with a  concentration of   $1\!\times\!10^{20}/\mathrm{cm}^{3}$   and the light-doped drain  (LDD) region with a concentration of   $1\!\times\!10^{18}/\mathrm{cm}^{3}$  . After the  formation of the source/drain and LDD regions, a five-hour  thermal annealing process is conducted at   $400^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   by  furnace annealing. Following furnace annealing,   $300\mathrm{nm}$     $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and   $300\mathrm{nm}\;\;\mathrm{Si}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$   are sequentially deposited by  PECVD as the interlayer dielectric (ILD). Lastly, Ti/Al/Ti  metallization is applied to form the source and drain  contacts, and the structure is patterned accordingly. The  width and length of the devices used in this research is  $3\upmu\mathrm{m}$    and   $6\upmu\mathrm{m}$  , respectively. The cross-sectional illustration of  the device is depicted in Fig. 1 (a).

In this research, measurements were conducted using the  Agilent B1500A semiconductor parameter analyzer on a  Lakeshore probe station at room temperature. The device  operated under hot carrier conditions with different   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$  conditions during the stress. Specifically, the gate was  applied at   $\mathrm{V_{g}=-5\ V}$  , -7.5 V, -10 V, -12.5 V, and   $\cdot15\mathrm{~V~}$  ,  while the drain voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{d})}$   was set at   $\mathrm{V_{d}}=-30\;\mathrm{V}$   with the  source grounded. The total stress time was 2000 seconds.  The threshold voltage   $\mathrm{(V_{th})}$   was defined using the constant  current method, wherein   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   was determined when I d   reached 1nA with  $\mathrm{V_{d}}={\underline{{0.1}}}\,\mathrm{V}$  .

## III.   R ESULT AND  DISCUSSION  

Fig. 1(b) illustrates the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift at different stress times  under hot carrier conditions with varying  $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions. The  results reveal three distinct degradation behaviors. For   $\mathrm{V_{g}=}$  -5 V and   $-7.5\,\textrm{V}$  , a direct positive   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift is observed.  However, for   $\mathrm{V_{g}\,=\,\lrcorner10V}$  , an initial positive   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift is  followed by a shift in the negative direction. Additionally,  for   $\mathrm{V_{g}=-12.5\ V}$   and -15 V, there is a direct negative   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$  shift. These results indicate a degradation tendency in the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift direction with an increase in   $\mathrm{V_{g}},$  . Specifically, as  $\mathrm{V_{g}}$  increases under hot carrier conditions, the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift changes  from positive to negative. Next, the  $\mathrm{I_{d}}{-}\mathrm{V_{g}}$  transfer curves at  different stress times under hot carrier conditions with   $\mathrm{V_{g}=}$  -5 V and -15 V are shown in Fig. 1(c) and (d), respectively.  The results demonstrate that when the device is stressed  under low   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions, there is a positive  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift with an  increase in on current   $(\mathrm{{lceil}})$  . In contrast, the curves for the  device stressed under high   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions show a clear  negative   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift along with subthreshold slope (S.S.)  collapse and a decrease in I on .

Next, the C-V transfer curves and the transconductance   $\left(\mathrm{{g}_{\mathrm{{m}}}}\right)$   were also measured initially and after the stress. The  results of C-V and   $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   for  $\mathrm{V_{g}}=\mathrm{-5V}$   are shown in Fig .  2(a)  and (c), respectively. Initially, the C-V curve after the stress  shifts in the positive direction under low   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$  conditions,  consistent with the   $\mathrm{I_{d-}V_{g}}$   results. Moreover, there is an  increase in overlap capacitance at the off state. According to  previous literature, when the device operates under hot  carrier conditions, electron-hole pairs are generated due to  impact ionization (I.I.). Furthermore, the electrons induced  by I.I. inject into the gate insulator (GI) at the drain edge,  creating an effective  $\mathrm{P}^{\cdot}$    region in the channel that results in  an increase in overlap capacitance. Additionally, with the  formation of the  $\mathrm{P}^{\cdot}$   region, the effective length of the device  decreases, leading to an increase in   $\mathrm{\bfg}_{\mathrm{m}}$   after the stress [16]- [21]. The difference in C-V and   $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   results before and after  the stress confirms that the main degradation mechanism

After confirming the mechanism that causes the  difference in g m  increase and the change in overlap  capacitance between the low and high   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   condition,  furthermore, the mechanism cause   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift in positive  direction, it is essential to further clarify the opposing  degradation mechanism under high   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions. In the  results of the   $\mathrm{I_{d}}{-}\mathrm{V_{g}}$   curve at different stress times shown in  Fig. 1 (d), it can be observed that there is either a   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift in  the negative direction and S.S. collapse, which are classic  degradation   behaviors   indicative   of   negative   bias  temperature instability (NBTI) [29]-[31]. Additionally, the  decrease in   $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{m}}$   after stress, as shown in Fig. 2 (d), is also a  degradation behavior in NBTI conditions. Consequently,  Joule heating-induced NBTI might be the main reason for  the observed degradation. To confirm whether NBTI is the  primary cause of the difference in degradation, power law  fitting is applied. The power law fitting equation is  presented below:

$$\Delta V_{t h}=A\;t^{n}$$
By fitting the relationship between   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift and stress time,  the power n can be extracted from the fitting equation. Each  n value extracted from the experiment can correspond to an  NBTI reaction in previous literature [32]-[35]. The results  of the power law fitting are shown in Fig. 6. The extracted  n values from the fitting results of the device stressed at   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$  = -10 V, -12.5 V, -15 V are 0.38, 0.32, and 0.31, respectively.  All three n values correspond to the same NBTI reaction in  previous literature. The fitting results confirm that Joule  heating induced NBTI is the mechanism causing the  degradation. Given that the stress current is very high under  higher  $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions during stress, furthermore, the device  is fabricated on a PI plastic substrate, which makes it  challenging for the device to dissipate the heat generated by  the current and make NBTI occurs during the stress [36]- [38]. The degradation is attributed to the breaking of Si-H  bonds at the interface and in the grain boundaries by the  negative bias temperature instability reaction during stress.  This leads to the generation of fixed oxide charges in the GI.  Additionally, the collapse of S.S. occurs due to the  generation of interface traps. As the stress time increases,  the severity of S.S. collapse worsens due to the increased  formation of interface traps. Moreover, the generation of  more current under hot carrier conditions with a higher   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$  results in elevated device temperatures, exacerbating the  NBTI degradation.

The degradation mechanism under different    $\mathrm{V_{g}}$    conditions is clarified, and the illustrations of the  degradation mechanism are shown in Fig. 7 (a) and (b).  Degradation is primarily dominated by the electrons  induced by I.I., which inject into the gate insulator (GI) at  the drain edge and into the buffer under low  $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   conditions,  as illustrated in Fig. 7(a). In contrast, under the hot carrier  conditions with higher  $\mathrm{V_{g}},$  , degradation is mainly dominated  by Joule heating-induced NBTI degradation. This is  because the driving current is high under high   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$    conditions which leading to an increase in the device  temperature. Furthermore, among the two degradation  factors,   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$   and driving current,   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$   predominates in  leading to degradation. This is because   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$   triggers impact  ionization and electron injection at the drain edge, both of  which can occur relatively quickly. Conversely, thermal  effects induced by Joule heating during operation with high  current require more time to manifest. Additionally, the  NBTI reaction also necessitates a longer duration to trigger  and occur. Hence,   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$   emerges as the predominant factor  in the degradation mechanism.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

In this research, a distinct degradation behavior in LTPS  TFT under the hot carrier condition with different   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$   is  observed. When the device operates under the hot carrier  condition with lower   $\mathrm{V_{g,}}$  , the degradation is primarily  dominated by I.I.. However, when the device operates under  hot carrier conditions with higher   $\mathrm{V_{g}}$  , the degradation  mechanism is dominated by Joule heating-induced NBTI.  Furthermore, since the   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$   is lower in this condition, the  pinch-off point is nearly located at the drain edge,  preventing serious I.I. occurrences in the channel. The  degradation mechanism under hot carrier conditions with  different driving conditions can be predicted by  summarizing the two factors below:

1. Firstly, the  $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift in the positive direction and the  transconductance increase are primarily influenced by   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$  .  Higher   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$   intensifies I.I., and furthermore, higher   $\mathrm{V_{gd}}$  causes the electric field direction to reverse at the drain edge,  allowing electrons to trap into the GI at the drain edge.

2. Secondly, the   $\mathrm{V_{th}}$   shift in the negative direction is  predominantly influenced by the driving current. Higher  driving current during operation increases the temperature  of the device further, exacerbating NBTI degradation.
