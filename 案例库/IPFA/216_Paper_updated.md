## Degradation Comparison of P-type Thin Film  Transistor under Dynamic Gate Pulsed Stress  

Xiangxu Tan, Huaisheng Wang\*, Mingxiang Wang, Dongli Zhang, and Nannan Lv  School of Electronic and Information Engineering, Soochow University, Suzhou, China  Phone : (+86)13913556523, Email :  wanghuaisheng@suda.edu.cn

Abstract —The degradation of p-type poly-Si TFTs under  various dynamic gate pulse stresses was investigated. We clarify  the impacts of pulse rising time ( t r ) and pulse falling time ( t f ) on  device degradation, respectively. Under dynamic PBTI stress or  dynamic gate voltage stresses swinging between the on-state and  off-state, both the electrons trapping into gate oxide and the  traps generation near the source/drain are responsible for the  two-stage degradation, which can be explained by the hot  carrier (HC) effect using the nonequilibrium pn junction  degradation model. To suppress the dynamic degradation, a  new four-terminal TFT is designed with a carrier injection  structure. A relieved degradation with an additional hump  phenomenon can be observed for the first time under the same  pulsed gate stress since the channel state can follow the pulse  transition, eliminating the HC effect. More electrons trapping at  the channel side is proposed to explain the hump phenomenon  and is proven by the TCAD simulation.

Keywords—low-temperature polycrystalline silicon (LTPS),  thin-film transistors (TFTs), degradation model, the fourthterminal, hot carrier (HC) effect, hump phenomenon

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Recently, thin film transistors (TFTs) produced by lowtemperature polycrystalline-silicon (LTPS) on glass or  flexible substrates have been studied extensively for use in  AMLCDs and AMOLEDs due to their irreplaceable role in  display technology [1]. In practical pixel circuits, TFTs need  to operate at high-speed pulsed gate frequencies with faster  pulse rising-time ( t r ) and falling-time ( t f ) because of the  requirements of higher resolution and higher refresh rate.  Thus, TFTs easily suffer from various dc and ac electrical  stresses such as NBTI, PBTI and hot carrier (HC) effect [2– 5]. In display industry, p-type poly-Si TFTs are more widely  used in active matrix displays since p-type poly-Si TFTs have  better stability against HC effect than n-type poly-Si TFTs [5– 6]. As a result, the improved reliability of p-type TFTs under  dynamic stresses has become a great concern for pixel circuit  design.

As reported in previous work, the degradation of n-type  TFTs under ac stress is more severe than that under dc stresses,  [3–8]. However, for p-type polysilicon TFTs, degradation  phenomena under dc and ac stress become more complex due  to the combination of PBTI/NBTI stress and carrier injection  [9–10]. On the other hand, the performance of p-type TFTs is  also easily influenced by the HC effect, which largely  deteriorates the reliability of TFTs [11–13]. Recently, a new  way to suppress the pulse induced degradation of TFTs was  proposed and defined as four-terminal TFTs with a counterdoped body terminal connected to the floating channel [14– 16]. It has been reported that the four-terminal TFTs can  indeed suppress the dynamic HC effect, and are even more  effective than lightly-doped-drain TFTs [16].

In previous work, the performance and reliability of n-type  four-terminal TFTs under long-term dynamic stresses have

been comprehensively discussed [16]. However, few studies  have investigated the reliability of p-type four-terminal TFTs  under different dynamic stresses [6]. Therefore, this work  focuses on comparing the p-type device degradation between  a conventional TFT and a four-terminal TFT before and after  dynamic gate stress. The reliability of the two types TFTs  under ac gate bias stress was analyzed. The hump  phenomenon on the four-terminal TFT after dynamic gate  stress is observed for the first time, which is different from the  degradation behaviour of n-type conventional TFTs and fourterminal TFTs. Non-uniform electron trapping at the gate  oxide is proposed to be responsible for the hump phenomenon  based on the experimental and TCAD simulations.

## II.   E XPERIMENTAL 

The TFT devices used in this work were fabricated using  the CMOS LTPS mass production process. Conventional and  four-terminal p-type TFTs were prepared on the same glass  substrate, as shown in Fig. 1. The process steps are as follows:  first, a   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   film is formed as a buffer layer on the glass plate,  and then a 500 Å thick amorphous silicon film is deposited  and then crystallized into poly-Si by ELA. After patterning the  active region, the carrier injection structure (red region) away  from channel   $3~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   distance is first implanted using an n-type  dopant to form the substrate terminal.  Next, 800 Å SiO 2  and  600 Å SiN X  bilayer films were sequentially deposited as the  gate insulator layer, and Mo films were deposited and  patterned as the gate. Then the source/drain regions of the ptype TFTs were formed by self-aligned boron implantation.  Finally, Ti/Al/Ti metal layers are deposited and etched as  electrodes.

In this paper, we analyzed the degradation of p-type TFTs  under dynamic gate stresses with grounded source and drain  electrodes. The pulse period ( T ) was fixed at   $2\,\upmu\mathrm{s}$   or  $4\,\upmu\mathrm{s}$   with

a duty ratio of   $50\%$  . The pulse peak voltage and base voltage  were defined as   $V_{g p}$   and   $V_{g b,}$  , respectively. Three kinds of  dynamic gate stress tests, i.e., pulsed gate stress swings of  25   $\mathrm{V}\sim0~\mathrm{V},$  ,  $-15\mathrm{~V}\sim25\mathrm{~V~}$  , and  $0~\mathrm{V}\sim25~\mathrm{V}$  , were conducted at  room temperature using an Agilent 4156C to measure the  transfer curves before and after each stress. The degradation  of the stressed TFTs was characterized by the variation in  I on    $(\Delta I_{o n})$   with respect to its initial value defined at   $V_{g}\!\!=\!-10~\mathrm{V}$   in  the transfer curves measured at   $V_{d}\!\!=-0.1\,\textrm{V}$  . The flat band  voltage   $(V_{F B})$   of p-type TFTs is approximately  $-1.2\;\mathrm{V}$  .

## III.   R ESULTS AND  D ISCUSSION 

Conventional TFTs were first subjected to the three  abovementioned kinds of pulsed gate stresses with the  T  of 2  μs, both   $t_{r}$   and   $t_{f}$   were   $0.1~{\upmu\mathrm{s}}_{1}$  , respectively. Two typical TFT  degradation phenomena of transfer curves versus stress time  were observed. Fig. 2(a) shows a two-stage degradation before  and after dynamic PBTI stress (  $V_{g}$   swings between  $0\:\mathrm{V}{\sim}\,25\:\mathrm{V})$  )  plotted in both linear and logarithmic scales. That is,   $\Delta I_{o n}$    increases approximately  $10\%$   initially, while the subthreshold  characteristic remains unchanged. Then, it decreases abruptly  after one thousand seconds stress time, leading to the secondstage degradation. Similar degradation behaviour was  observed for the bipolar pulsed stress (  $V_{g}$   swings between  15  V and 25 V) except for the larger   $\Delta I_{o n}$   in both stages. In the  first stage,  I on  increase can be attributed to the electrons  trapping into gate oxide near the source/drain, which shortens  the effective channel length [5,11–12,17]. However, the  second-stage degradation is caused by dynamic HC-induced  trap state generation at the interface and grain boundaries  (GBs) [18–20]. It is important to note that the effective  channel length shortening effect and trap state generation are  the two main competing degradation factors, and they occur  simultaneously during the overall degradation duration. As the   $V_{g}$   stress time continues,  I on  decreases significantly since   $\Delta I_{o n}$    in the second-stage would overwhelm the limited  $I_{o n}$   increase  in the first stage. In contrast, as shown in Fig. 2(b), the TFT  degradation does not occur under the dynamic NBTI stress  swinging  $-25\;\mathrm{V}\sim0\;\mathrm{V}$  .

Regarding to the degradation mechanism of p-type TFTs  under dynamic gate stress, the nonequilibrium pn junction  degradation model can be used to explain the degradation [6– 7,21]. When the pulse   $V_{g}$   increases from 0 to 25 V, the p-type  TFT should switch from near the flat-band condition to  electron accumulation; however, electrons accumulated in  channel cannot follow the pulse switching because electrons  are generated only through trap emission and/or thermal  excitation from the active layer including the channel and  source/drain. For example, electrons emitted from the deeplevel traps can reach milliseconds [22], far more than the pulse  period of  $2\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$  . Therefore, one can infer that, during the entire  pulse cycle, the TFT channel should remain in a depleted state,  forming the two non-equilibrium junctions between the  channel/source and the channel/drain. During   $t_{r}$  , the potential  of the channel is coupled to a positive potential due to the  coupling effect [23]. As a result, the two junctions near the  S/D become reversely biased, and the depletion region of the  two pn junctions rapidly extends to the channel through traps  ionization, where a large lateral electric field is established.  Then, electrons emitted from deep-level defects are exposed  to the high field across the junctions and become HCs, and  some HCs can overcome the barrier of gate oxide and then be  injected into the oxide near the S/D with the aid of vertical  electric field, resulting in effective channel shortening effect.

With the applied   $V_{g}$   pulse stresses continue, more HCs can  generate more traps located at interface and GBs near the S/D,  leading to the rapid decrease of the   $I_{o n}$   in the second stage.  Furthermore, as the pulse   $V_{g}$   decreases from   $25~\mathrm{V}$   to 0 V, the  potential of the channel is coupled to negative, making the two  pn junctions forward biased. Thus, the depletion region of pn  junctions first shrink during  $t_{f}$   transition and then expand again  because the channel should remain in depleted state after the   $V_{g p}$   drops to zero. It is obvious that   $t_{f}$   also plays a critical role  to the dynamic PBTI stress. Traps generation can also be  induced by the HCs even if pulsed   $V_{g}$   returns to zero.  Therefore, the mixed electrons trapping in oxide and traps  generation near the S/D under dynamic PBTI can be  responsible for the two stages degradation of p-type TFTs,  which is different from the monotonous   $I_{o n}$   decrease in n-type  TFTs since both electron trapping and traps generation will  lead to the decrease of  $I_{o n}$  .

In the case of dynamic NBTI of   $V_{g}$   swinging  $-25\;\mathrm{V}\sim0\;\mathrm{V}$  ,  once the    $V_{g}$   pulse switch below   $V_{t h}$   about   $-3\mathrm{~V~}$  , holes can be  swept quickly from S/D into the channel to form the hole  inversion layer. Thus, source and drain are conductive through  the channel, preventing dynamic HCs degradations. In fact, it  has been proved that dynamic HCs degradation occurs only  under the condition that the   $V_{g}$   drops to below   $V_{F B}$  [6]. So,  strictly speaking, there is negligible HCs degradation under  the dynamic NBTI as the gate pulse voltage swings in the  range of   V FB    zero, and then, no HCs degradation can be  observed as shown in Fig. 2(b).

To better understand the dynamic HCs degradation, ∆ I on   degradation is further compared under various pulse stress  conditions as well as different  $t_{r}$   and   $t_{f}$  as shown in Fig. 3. It is  found that the both two-stage degradation depends on  $t_{r}$   and   $t_{f}$  under the dynamic PBTI stress, which is consistent with  previous observations [5–6,18]. Obviously, degradations in  both stages are enhanced by steeper  $t_{r}$   and  $t_{f,}$   that is, more   $\Delta I_{o n}$    increase in the first stage and more   $\Delta I_{o n}$   drop in the second  stage under the dynamic PBTI stresses as shown in Fig 3 (a)  and (b). However, as to the bipolar gate stresses that   $V_{g}$   pulses  swing between   $-15\;\mathrm{V}$   and 25 V, ∆ I on  degradation depends on   $t_{r}$   but shows no dependence on   $t_{f.}$   The steeper   $t_{r}$   results in  severe  $\Delta I_{o n}$   degradation than that of under dynamic PBTI, it is  reasonable that it has a shorter pulse transition interval from   $V_{F B}$   to 25 V. Meanwhile, the curves of   $\Delta I_{o n}$   degradation under  various  $t_{f}$   almost overlapped because the dynamic degradation  does not occur due to the conductive channel when   $V_{g}$    drops  below   $V_{F B}$  .  Therefore, it proves that the nonequilibrium pn  junction degradation model is also applicable for the  degradation of the p-type TFTs under various dynamic gate  stresses.

In order to prolong the TFTs lifetime, a new four-terminal  TFT is designed with a carrier injection structure, forming a  substrate   terminal.   Experiments   show   that   transfer  characteristics of the four-terminal TFT always coincide in  logarithmic and linear coordinates with the voltage of  substrate terminal   $(V_{s u b})$   varied from  $-0.5\mathrm{~V~}$   to 5V or floated  as demonstrated in Fig. 5. It is found that the existence of  substrate terminal would not affect the performance of the  four-terminal p-type TFTs. Next, the same   $V_{g}$   pulse stresses  were applied to both the conventional TFT and the fourterminal TFT under the   $V_{g}$   pulse stress swinging in   $0\sim30\;\mathrm{V}$  ,  both   $t_{r}$   and   $t_{f}$  were fixed at   $0.1~{\upmu\mathrm{s}}$  . After   $30~\mathrm{K}$   seconds pulse  stress, a typical two-stage degradation can be easily observed  for the conventional TFT as shown in Fig. 6(a). However, for  the four-terminal TFT with grounded   $V_{s u b}$  , one can observe a  totally different degradation in which the increase of   $\varDelta I_{o n}$  companying a hump phenomenon in subthreshold region.  The dramatical decrease of   $\varDelta I_{o n}$   in the second stage  degradation becomes disappear completely as shown in Fig.  6(b), which means the four-terminal TFT can effectively  suppressed the HCs-induced degradation without affecting  the normal operation of the device.

With regard to the degradation mechanism of the fourterminal TFT under dynamic PBTI stress, it is noting that the  substrate terminal can produce a marked effect to eliminate  the nonequilibrium state in the channel. As the   $V_{g}$   increases  from zero to   $30\ \mathrm{V}$  , the channel can be coupled to a certain  positive voltage due to the coupling effect, making the  channel and substrate forward biased, and then, a large  number of electrons can surmount the potential barrier of the  channel/substrate and diffuse to the whole active layer. In  other words, electron accumulation can follow the   $V_{g}$   pulse  transition. Therefore, the channel remains in an equilibrium  state with accumulated electrons, thus, such dynamic HC  degradation does not occur. Meanwhile, some electrons could  be injected or tunnelled into oxide under the force of high  vertical electric field pointing from gate to channel, which  makes the  $I_{o n}$   of TFT increases. As to the hump phenomenon,  we propose that more electrons concentrate on the edge of  oxide and form a parasitic TFT, resulting to the hump  phenomenon.

The hypothesis has been proved by the distribution of  nonuniform electric field and the trapped electrons  concentration in oxide with the assistance of TCAD  simulations. First, 3D TCAD simulated structures of both the  conventional TFT and the four-terminal TFT are established  as shown in Fig. 7, the simulated transfer characteristics were  fitted with experimental data to extract TFT’s material  parameters [24]. Next, the same   $V_{g}$   pulse stress (  $\mathit{V g}{=}\ 0\sim30$  V,   $t_{r}=t_{f}\,{=}0.1~\upmu\mathrm{s})$   was input to the simulator, the electrons  concentration at the end of   $t_{r}$   was extracted and reached   $1.93\!\times\!10^{20}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$   distributed uniformly while the counterpart of  the conventional TFT was less than   $3.12{\times}10^{14}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$   in the  entire poly-Si layer as shown in Fig. 8. Meanwhile, the  maximum electric field across the four-terminal TFT was   $2.41{\times}10^{6}\,\mathrm{V/cm}$   and located both side of the channel as shown  in Fig 9. So, some carries could be injected or tunnelled into  the oxide, forming the  $I_{o n}$   increase and the hump phenomenon.  However, for the conventional TFT, HCs could be generated  near the source/drain during the pulse   $t_{r}$    because the total  electric field including the transient lateral field and vertical  electric field at the end of   $t_{r}$   could reach to   $8.17{\times}10^{5}\ \mathrm{V/cm}$  ,  which resulted to electron trapping and traps generation near  the source/drain region. Therefore, the new p-type fourterminal TFT is found to provide an effective way to suppress  dynamic HCs degradation for the display industry and can be

served as a crucial reference for the higher reliability of polySi TFTs in the practical applications operating at high  frequency.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

Dynamic   $V_{g}$  -induced two-stage degradation in p-type  poly-Si TFTs has been systematically investigated. It is  shown that the two-stages degradation of p-type TFTs can be  attributed to both the effective channel shortening effect and  the dynamic HCs effect. Dynamic PBTI stress-induced  I on degradation strongly depends on both   $t_{r}$   and   $t_{f}$  whereas only  the   $t_{r}$   plays the critical pole to the degradation if   $V_{g}$   pulse  stress swinges between on-state and off-state. Based on the

nonequilibrium PN junction model, a four-terminal TFT is  designed to suppress the dynamic HCs degradation. The  consequent hump phenomenon can be due to the parasitic  TFT caused by the more electrons trapping at the channel side.
