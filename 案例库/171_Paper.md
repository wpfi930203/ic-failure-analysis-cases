## Improved LDMOS for ESD Protection of High Voltage  BCD Process  

SHEN Hong-yu, DONG Shu-rong\*,XU Ze-kun, HU Tao, Guo Wei, Huang Wei   1 ESD Lab, College of Information Science and Electronic Engineering, Zhejiang University ， Hang Zhou , Zhe Jiang 310027,China

Abstract- LDMOS is widely used as an ESD protection device. In high  voltage BCD technology. However, due to the use of  low  concentration medium voltage well in HV process, the LDMOS is  easily damaged by the Kirk effect under ESD stress, and the  robustness is very low. A novel LDMOS is proposed in this work by  using a high concentration well to surround the drain intraditional   LDMOS, which, achieves a high ESD robustness with a current level  of 0.76A,and the Ron is reduced from the original 25Ω to 6.25Ω. In  other hand ,in order to save the area, the conventional LDMOS-SCR  has been improved by the drain terminal segment, which make the  improved LDMOS-SCR maintain a high robustness while the device  area is smaller than that of the conventional LDMOS-SCR, thereby  improving the area efficiency.

Keywords-ESD ， LDMOS ， LDMOS-SCR ， High Voltage BCD  Process ， robustness.

## I ． Introduction  

High-voltage (HV) integrated circuit (IC) in Bipolar-CMOS  DMOS (BCD) technology are extensively used for power  management applications, automotive electronics, display driver  circuits and so on [1-3]. The electrostatic discharge (ESD) is a  tough issue for HV IC [4]. Because HV ICs generally operate in  high current and high voltage environments, the reliability of the  device is very high, which requires full consideration of on-chip  ESD protection. Laterally-diffused metal–oxide semiconductor  (LDMOS) transistors are often used as output drivers. What is  more, LDMOS transistors are also widely used as ESD protection  devices [1-2]. However, the efficiency of LDMOS used as ESD  protection device is vulnerable to ESD stresses, which are often  due to the inhomogeneous triggering of the parasitic bipolar  junction transistor (BJT) and base push out effect. [5]

The n-LDMOS structure which commonly used for high  voltage I/O is shown in Fig.1, The difference with normal NMOS  is that a medium voltage N-Well (MVNW) and a medium voltage  P-Well (MVPW) are used in n-LDMOS .Compared with  conventional NW and PW, MVNW and MVPW have lower  doping concentrations, so the withstand voltage capability under  DC is improved. [6-8]High-voltage BCD process can have a  variety of operating voltage devices according to actual needs,  such as 12V, 24V, 40V. The higher the operating voltage, the  lower the concentration of the well used.[9] Under different  processes, the STI part may be replaced by field oxygen isolation.  It is also because of the high withstand voltage of the BCD process  that it is widely used as the main process for the fabrication of high  voltage devices.

However, due to the use of low-concentration medium-voltage  wells in the LDMOS structure, LDMOS is easily damaged by the  Kirk effect [10] under ESD stress, and the robustness is very low.  The Kirk effect means that under transient high current, the  boundary of the depletion region in the low concentration MVNW  will advance to below the  $\Nu+$   region of the drain, forming a strong  electric field region and causing breakdown damage. Fig.2 shows  a typical TLP test curve of 12V LDMOS. An obvious feature is  that the device is broken after a hysteresis, and the secondary  breakdown current is only 0.26A. A common way to enhance  conventional LDMOS is to increase the width of the device. Due  to the comprehensive consideration of current drive capability and  ESD protection capability, the width of LDMOS sometimes needs  thousands of  $\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . A novel scheme that can improve the robustness  of LDMOS  without increasing device area is proposed in this  paper.

From the analysis of the cause of LDMOS failure, the low  robustness of the basic LDMOS structure under ESD stress is  mainly due to the propulsion of the depletion region under high  current, and the root cause of this failure is the low doping of the  medium voltage N-well Therefore, it is conceivable to cover the   $\Nu+$   region of the drain terminal with NW having a concentration  higher than MVNW, so that when the boundary of the depletion  region in the MVNW is advanced to the MVNW/NW boundary,  the propulsion speed is slowed, thereby delaying the occurrence of  breakdown damage. In turn, the robustness of LDMOS under ESD  stress is increased.Fig.3 is a schematic diagram of the improved  LDMOS structure.

Fig.4 shows the TLP test results for 12V conventional LDMOS  and LDMOS with NW at the drain terminal. It can be seen that the   trigger voltages of the two structures are all about 17.5V.  However, the It2 increased significantly from the 0.26A to 0.76A,  which is nearly three times that of the original. In addition, the Ron  has been reduced from the original  $25\Omega$   to 6.25Ω, which is only a  quarter of the original. At the same time, in order to confirm the  DC voltage resistance of the LDMOS containing the NW at the  drain terminal, we performed a DC voltage scan. As shown in Fig.  5, it can be seen that the breakdown voltage under DC voltage is  about 17.5V. In summary, the LDMOS with NW at the drain  terminal does not deteriorate the DC characteristics, can safely  withstand the operating voltage of 12V, and at the same time can  improve the robustness by nearly 3 times compared with the  conventional LDMOS.

It can be seen from the results of the TLP test that the LDMOS  with NW at the drain terminal is positively enhanced in both  robustness and Ron, and this is attributed to the fact that the Kirk  effect of the original LDMOS is weakened by the addition of NW.  The most worrying aspect in the process of device improvement is  the effect on the DC characteristics of the device after the addition  of NW. Since the doping concentrations of MVNW and MVPW in  the LDMOS are lower than those of the conventional NW and PW,  the withstand voltage capability under direct current is improved.  As mentioned before, the high-voltage BCD process can have  multiple operating voltage devices, depending on actual needs.  The higher the operating voltage, the lower the concentration of  the well used. Therefore, the introduction of NW may weaken the  DC withstand voltage capability of the device, resulting in its  inability to operate at normal operating voltages. Fortunately, DC  voltage scanning the 12V LDMOS with NW at the drain terminal  has a breakdown voltage of 17.5V. This shows that adding NW to  LDMOS can improve the ESD performance of the device while  ensuring DC compression capability. Whether this improved idea  can be more widely applied to improve the ESD performance of  medium voltage well devices is worthy of further investigation.

## III ． Drain-terminal Segmented LDMOS-SCR  

Another improvement is derived from LDMOS-SCR, which  inserts a  $\mathrm{P}+$  implant between the drain and gate of the LDMOS to  form a SCR path, thereby significantly reducing the holding  voltage. The layouts of LDMOS and LDMOS-SCR are shown in  Fig. 6.An extra   $\mathrm{P}+$   stripe is inserted in the left of   $\Nu+$   region at the  drain side of LDMOS-SCR in contrast to LDMOS, thus the  LDMOS-SCR has a special current discharge path through the  parasitic SCR and its Vh can be significantly reduced.[11]

However, due to the robustness problem, the conventional  LDMOS-SCR adopts a multi-finger structure to improve the  robustness of the device, which results in a greatly increased  layout area of the LDMOS-SCR device compared to the original  LDMOS. In order to minimize the device area while ensuring that  the robustness of the device is not impaired, a new LDMOS-SCR  structure is needed

As shown in Fig. 7(a), the double-finger LDMOS-SCR in the  form of a regular layout. Compared to the conventional LDMOS  structure, the layout area is increased due to the addition of two  $\mathrm{P}+$  implants. In order to save the area, the LDMOS structure of the  source-drain segment shown in Figure 7(b) is proposed.

In this paper, based on the double-finger LDMOS-SCR, a layout  optimization method for segmenting the drain-terminal is  proposed, so that the LDMOS-SCR structure can be formed  without increasing the area of the conventional LDMOS structure.  The design of this new structure is to make full use of the vertical  area of the layout, and to change the originally horizontally  arranged drain to a longitudinal segmented structure. This not only  ensures that the parasitic SCR structure can be formed to improve  the robustness, but also reduces the layout area. Here is an example  of analyzing a single interdigital finger.

When the device is subjected to a strong ESD pulse, it will  experience three different regions of operation. （ 1 ） Reverse PN  breakdown region: the PN junction formed by P-Body/DNW has  breakdown first, the ESD induced transient current goes through  DNW and P-Body. (2) Parasitic bipolar region: as the voltage  drops on the P-Body resistance is about  $0.8\;\mathrm{V}$  , the  $\Nu+$   implantation  in source terminal begins to inject electrons into P-Body and the  electrons that transit the P-Body are collected by DNW. Then the  bipolar transistor formed by  $\Nu+$   implant region, P-Body and DNW  begins to conduct. (3) SCR region: As the ESD induced current  increases further, the electrons drift in the N-Body will cause  severe impact ionization which leads to the avalanche injection  near the drain terminal   $\Nu+$   implantation. The holes generated by  avalanche impact ionization will drift to the   $\mathrm{P}+$   implant region  from its left side. Then the  $\mathrm{P}+$   implant region can inject holes into  the N-Body, from its right side, which leads to the parasitic SCR  formed by   $\mathrm{P}+$   implant region, N-Body, P-Body and source   $\mathrm{N}+$  implant region conducting. [12]It can be seen that the device can  still form a complete LDMOS-SCR device structure after  segmenting the drain terminal.

Fig.8 shows the TLP testing results. It can be seen that the  conventional LDMOS-SCR and the drain-terminal segmented  LDMOS-SCR have similar performances, and can form a deep

hysteresis SCR path with a secondary failure current of about  1.6A.Therefore, the drain-terminal segmented LDMOS-SCR can  reduce the single device area while maintaining the strong  robustness of the conventional LDMOS-SCR. In addition, the  trigger voltage of both device structures is about 25V, and the  device has a significant snap-back after it is turned on. Compared  with   the   conventional   LDMOS-SCR,   the   drain-terminal  segmented LDMOS-SCR is more stable, and the Ron of the two is   $5.5\Omega$   and  $6.1\Omega$  .

However, the holding voltage of two types of LDMOS-SCR are  around 5V, which is generally lower than the operating voltage of  the device under the high-voltage BCD process, which may cause  latch-up problems. How to maintain the high robustness of  LDMOS-SCR while effectively improving the latch-up resistance  remains to be further studied.

## IV ． Conclusions of the Study  

In this paper, based on the shortcomings of the weak robustness  of the LDMOS under the high voltage BCD process, the Kirk  effect of n-LDMOS is weakened by using NW to surround the  $\Nu+$    region of the drain terminal, which enhances the robustness of the  device. From the TLP test results, The robustness of LDMOS with  NW is increased by 3 times, and the Ron is only a quarter of the  original. By improving the LDMOS-SCR by the drain terminal  segment, it maintains the robustness of the conventional  LDMOS-SCR while reducing the individual device area.  However, since the holding voltage of the two types of  LDMOS-SCR is about 5V, it is still possible to cause latch-up  problems.   Therefore, how to improve the low holding voltage of  LDMOS-SCR requires further research.
