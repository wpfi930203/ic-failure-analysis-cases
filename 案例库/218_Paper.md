## Defect Characterization by Differential Phase  Contrast Imaging Technique in Scanning  Transmission Electron Microscope  

Ching-Chun Lin \*  and Kim Hs u  Integrated Service Technology Inc.     No. 19, Pu-ding Rd., Hsinchu 30072, Taiwan Tel:  +886-3-579-9909 Ext. 6162   E-mail : chingchun_lin@istgroup.com .

Abstract-  A novel approach to detect the crystal defects extended  along particular orientation using differential phase contrast  (DPC) images with segmented type detector for wide range of  devices and materials in scanning transmission electron  microscope (STEM) is provided. In addition, the signals from  different segments could be further processed to enhance the  contrast of light elements specifically, which also called enhanced  annular bright field (eABF) mode in segmented annular all field  (SAAF) system.

Keywords –Differential Phase Contrast (DPC), STEM, contrast,  segmented annular all field (SAAF).

## I. INTRODUCTION  

The crystal defects, such as dislocation  generated during the  fabrication process of modern semiconductor devices have  been known as the possible root causes of leakage current, poor  device reliability especially when the defects are close to the  active area [1-3]. The way to control the density, length and  types of defects becomes crucial for device and material  performances [4]. As the device keep scaling down, the  influence of defects become significant, which leads to the  increasing demand of defect characterization in transmission  electron microscope (TEM) [5-7].

Not only morphology, but also the structural and  compositional information of the material could be acquired  via TEM, which has been applied as an important tool in failure  analysis of IC industry for a long period of time. In recent year,  differential phase contrast (DPC) in scanning transmission  electron microscope (STEM) has drew a great attention to  microscopist community due to the fact that it could be used to  directly visualized the built in electric and magnetic field of  materials at atomic resolution using Cs corrected microscope  [8-10]. According to the early literature [11-12], the  mechanism behind DPC relies on the elimination of the  intensity signals on phase contrast detectors relative to the  optical axis, which then turns the electron deflection caused by  the interaction between incident beam and specimen into image  contrast.

The DPC imaging using X-ray and light has been utilized in  the medical and biological area to improve the contrast of the  weakly scattering objects, such as the light elements, and  polymers [13-14]. Moreover, based on the previous work done  by Findlay [15] and Shibata [16], the enhanced annular bright  field (eABF) imaging technique can be utilized to selectively

improve the light element contrast by subtracting the middle  angle bright field (MaBF) signal from that of the annular bright  field (ABF). To realize this idea, the signal of these two  detectors has to be normalized, which makes the above  calculation meaningful. Thus, the segmented detector collects  signal from MaBF to ABF simultaneously was used here.

In the present study, the segmented type detector was used to  create the DPC images for defect analysis on IC and light  emitted diode (LED) samples. Furthermore, improved  interpretability for light elements such as silicone oxide and  nitride stacking structure in DRAM was demonstrated using  eABF mode comparing with ABF, MaBF and TEM high  resolution (HR) images.

## II. EXPERIMENTS  

As shown in Fig. 1(a), the SAAF Octant detector with eight  channels, which could be read out simultaneously was  equipped on the JEM-F200 with cold field emission gun. The  inner and outer portion of the detector, i.e. the channel 1-4 and  channel 5-8 stands for the MaBF and ABF image as shown in  figure 1(b), respectively. Moreover, signal from each  individual channel could be further processed, which allows us  to collect electron scattering signal from various detection  angles, and thus provides great flexibility on the adjustment of  STEM image contrast.

The STEM energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) data was  collected in JEOL JEM-F200 TEM (200kV) equipped with  dual silicon drift detectors (SDDs). The STEM bright field (BF)  images were also conducted using JEOL JEM-F200. The probe  forming aperture angle was around 20 mrad and the detection  angle was 1.1 mrad.

The focus ion beam (FIB, FEI Helios 660) was used to  prepare all the TEM samples at 30kV and low kV for U cut and  final clean to remove the possible ion beam damage layer,  respectively.

All the TEM lamellas were clean before TEM observation  using the mixture of argon and oxygen plasma (Fischione,  Nanoclean Model 1070) to prevent the sample surface from  carbon contamination during electron beam illumination.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION  

## A.   Defect analysis   

A dynamic random access memory (DRAM) sample was  used in this study. We aim to investigate the possible stress or  crystal defects near the corner of Si substrate and shallow  trench isolation (STI) during the STI corner rounding  engineering [17].

After carefully alignment between sample crystallography  and detector rotation, an abnormal contrast highlighted with  black arrow, was found at the corner of STI using the integrated  contrast of channel 1 and 7 of the SAAF detector as shown in  Fig. 2(b). The high angle annular dark field (HAADF) mode  with detector’s inner collection semi angle of 62.8 mrad was  then used to examine the anomalous contrast. From figure 3(c),  no significant contrast variation was found in the same area,  which implies that the anomalous contrast may result from  some crystal defects with diffraction contrast, such as  dislocation or vacancy, etc.

Besides IC sample, we also applied the same concept on  light emitted diode (LED) sample to examine the dislocation  density across the GaN layer. Furthermore, an interesting  comparison was conducted between TEM two-beam-condition  and the X-Y scanning components of DPC images.

Similar results can be obtained in DPC images, which show  that the simultaneously captured DPC images possess the  potential to replace the traditional two beam condition with  large amount of sample tilting effort as shown in Fig. 3.

## B.   Light element detection  

For light element detection, annular bright field (ABF)  STEM technique has been extensively applied to directly  capture the light element image inside materials [18].  However,  ABF image for ultralight elements is still challenging. Less  dosage has been adopted to minimize the radiation damage to  these elements, which results in the poor signal to noise ratio of  ABF images [16]. In the present study, we are trying to explore  the distinction between ABF, MaBF and the enhanced ABF  (eABF) image which is the resultant signal after ABF minus  MaBF.

The same DRAM sample was used to verify the sensitivity of  DPC to the thin silicon nitride also known as decoupled plasma  nitridation (DPN) and oxide dielectric layers beneath poly-Si  gate. The nitride and oxide are not only light elements but beam  sensitive materials, which tend to be damaged after scanning by  a fine electron probe with atomic size.

Three major bright field modes in segmented detector  including ABF, MaBF and eABF images of NON layers are

compared in Fig. 4. The ABF image in Fig. 4(a) shows strong  image intensity but blurred nitride and oxide interface contrast,  which can be verified using the intensity line profile in Fig. 4(b).  Unlike ABF image, the MaBF image in Fig. 4(c) and the  corresponding intensity profile in Fig. 4(d) show clear interface  between nitride and oxide.  Moreover, we found an ultrathin  nitride layer between oxide and Si substrate, which was  suspected to be the process defect. Nevertheless, MaBF was  demonstrated to visualize nitride/oxide/nitride (NON) stacking  layers successfully.

We aim to preserve the clear interface contrast of nitride and  oxide while enhance the overall image intensity. Therefore,  eABF in Fig. 4(e) was created to enhance the light elements  contrast i.e. NON without losing too much image intensity by  subtracting MaBF signal from ABF signal. From the intensity  profile of eABF image in Fig. 4(f), eABF shows significant  improvement in NON boundary interpretability comparing  with MaBF and ABF images.  However, the contrast of nitride  and oxide in eABF image is invert, which is confusing. We  think this issue may relate to the specimen thickness and the  spatial incoherence [15].

To confirm the elemental distribution, STEM EDS spectral  imaging (SI) map, line profile and TEM HR images were  conducted as shown in Fig. 4(g) and (h), respectively. The EDS  map and superimposed nitride line profile in Fig. 4(g) shows  two thin nitride signals between oxide dielectric. However, no  significant contrast in TEM HR image, which may result from  diffraction contrast dominated in HR image rather than  amplitude contrast.

## IV. CONCLUSION  

To sum up, we demonstrate here the defect analysis using  DPC images in STEM for DRAM and LED samples. The  simultaneously captured DPC images from different segments  can be further processed, and hence we can obtain electron  scattering signals by different azimuth. Moreover, these  electrons from different detector portions can bring different  contrast from annular bright field to middle angle bright field  images, which allows us to have more flexibility to deal with  various kinds of analytical requirements.

In addition, eABF mode in SAAF detector was utilized to  greatly improve interpretability of the DPN nitride and oxide  dielectric under the polysilicon gate in a DRAM structure  comparing with ABF and MaBF images. From EDS SI map  and line profile, NON stacking layers was confirmed without  damage after performing DPC imaging, which implies that the  SAAF system can have great sensitivity and signal to noise  ratio toward light elements or beam sensitive materials in a  normal electron dose.

In the future, we will explore more applications for SAAF  detector in semiconductor field. The current results show that  DPC technique will be a promising tool in the FA area.
