## Benchmarking BTI Reliability in Scaled FETs with TMD Channels via Predictive Modeling 

Y. J. Chai, Y. Z. Lv, Yu. Yu. Illarionov\* \*Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Southern University of Science and Technology, 1088 Xueyuan Blvd, Shenzhen, China Phone: +86-18929340141, Email : illarionov@sustech.edu.cn

Abstract —Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) offer excellent opportunities to continue scaling of modern electronics following Moore’s law. However, the use of TMD channels together with high-k 3D oxides which was selected by the industry as the main path for FAB integration of these new devices may result in severe reliability limitations. To get more insight into this problem, here we perform predictive modeling of biastemperature instabilities (BTI) in FETs with different TMD channels. Our findings suggest that in the ideal case MoSe 2  nFETs as well as   $\mathbf{MoS}_{2}$   and   $\mathbf{MoSe_{2}}$   p-FETs could fully satisfy  $\mathbf{30}\,\mathbf{mV}$   per 10 years BTI failure criteria. Nevertheless, in reality, the use of thin highly amorphous, and thus defective   $\mathbf{SiO}_{2}$   seed layers to grow high-k oxides like   $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{f}\mathbf{O}_{2}$   on TMDs may still severely degrade the reliability of TMD FETs. Still our results show that the use of MoSe 2  may be attractive for both n- and p-FETs as considerably improved bias stability can be achieved at positive and negative gate voltages.

Index Terms —TMD FETs, reliability, charge trapping, defect bands

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recent research advances at fabricating FETs with twodimensional TMD semiconductors [1]–[3] have inspired the industry to begin with integration of these new technologies into FAB process flows [4]–[6]. In all these studies the socalled TMD/3D path is selected, which suggests the use of TMD channels together with conventional high-k oxides known from Si technologies (e.g.   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   or   ${\mathrm{Al}}_{2}{\mathrm{O}}_{3}$  ) as gate insulators. However, the use of oxides may result in severe reliability limitations, as all they typically contain fundamental defect bands which cannot be avoided even if the processing is well optimized [7]. Charge trapping by border defects [8] energetically aligned inside these defect bands causes sizable BTI drifts of the gate transfer characteristics [9] and thus can severely perturb stable device operation. Nevertheless, the number of active defects may be reduced by selecting optimum channel/oxide combinations to enlarge the energy barrier for charge trapping via proper engineering of the band diagram [10]. Here we for the first time perform predictive modeling of negative BTI (NBTI) and positive BTI (PBTI) reliability in FETs with  $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$  ,  $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$  ,   ${\mathrm{W}}{\mathrm{S}}_{2}$   and  ${\mathrm{W}}{\mathrm{S}}{\mathrm{e}}_{2}$   channels with   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   gate oxide stack scaled down to equivalent oxide thickness (EOT) of 1 nm. By using well-established compact physics framework Comphy [7], we simulate BTI drifts assuming 10 years of device operation and benchmark them against  $30\,\mathrm{mV}$   failure criteria [11]. The obtained results allow us to identify that   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   could be the best material

## II. M ODELING TECHNIQUE AND PARAMETERS 

We employ open source compact physics framework Comphy [7] which allows comprehensive simulation of reliability issues by using a non-radiative (NMP) model [12] to describe charge trapping. In Comphy, we can compute the mean degradation in a deterministic fashion as well as the variability by Monte Carlo Sampling of individual defects. The simulation of the threshold voltage    $V_{\mathrm{th}}$   for arbitrary time-dependent gate voltages    $V_{\mathrm{g}}$   with Comphy is based on a description of the gate stack and channel properties. The charges of oxide defects can be considered self-consistently in the Poisson equation which allows for accurate simulation at high charge densities. In our analysis, we consider top-gated mono-layer (1ML) TMD FETs with Ti gate electrodes (Fig.1). In line with modern scaling requirements, the total EOT of the   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   gate stack is equal to 1 nm in all cases. The parameters of oxides and TMD channels used in our simulations are summarized in Table 1. We also assume that both   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   have two distinct defect bands, namely deep and shallow. Their default energetic alignments (Et), half-width (Et-sigma) and defect densities (Nt), as well as relaxation energy (Er) and its standard deviation (Er-sigma) are known from Si technologies [7] as given in Table 2.

Our reliability-based design approach focuses on the optimization of material combination and the band diagram of the channel cross-section, including energetic alignments of defect bands in the insulator with respect to the channel conduction and valence bands. Fig. 2 shows the band diagrams of FETs with 4 selected TMD channels and   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}(1\,\mathrm{nm})/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}(0.8\,\mathrm{nm})$  gate oxide stack under CMOS compatible voltages generated using default parameters from Table 2. In Figs. 2 (left) we show the band diagrams of TMD FETs with gate voltage    $V_{\mathrm{g}}$  equal to flat band voltage    $V_{\mathrm{FB}}$  . The middle and right of Figs. 2 show the band diagram under PBTI   $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}+2\,\mathrm{V})$   and NBTI  $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2.5\,\mathrm{V})$   stress conditions, respectively. We can clearly see that while the gate oxide stack is the same in all cases, the number of active defects in shallow and deep defect bands of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   which can be charged/discharged via the Fermi level    $E_{\mathrm{F}}$   shift depends on the channel material which sets the energetic barrier for charge trapping. As the applied positive/negative gate voltage changes, the active defects in shallow/deep defect bands change their charge states if the applied stress time exceeds their time constants. During PBTI stress those defects which are charged at the initial equilibrium can capture electrons if their energy level is moving above  $E_{\mathrm{{F}}}$   over time, which causes a positive shift on the threshold voltage. After the end of the stress the defects situated above  $E_{\mathrm{{F}}}$   can emit electrons, and thus return to their equilibrium charge states which results in recovery of the threshold voltage shift. Conversely, during NBTI stress  $E_{\mathrm{F}}$   is close to the valence band of the oxide emits electrons, which leads to negative shifts. After the stress, the capture of electrons by those defects that have become charged leads to recovery. Therefore, understanding the energetic location of defect bands allows for targeted mitigation strategies to improve the device reliability by reducing the number of active defects.

The corresponding PBTI and NBTI stress/recovery traces simulated with Comphy using the setup of Fig. 2 are compared in Fig. 3. Based on these results we can predict that the degradation gained after 10 years   $(3{\times}10^{8}\,\mathrm{s})$   of device operation satisfies   $30\,\mathrm{mV}$   failure criteria for all selected TMDs when considering PBTI and NBTI drifts. Fig. 3(a) shows that MOSFETs with TMD   ${\mathrm{W}}{\mathrm{S}}{\mathrm{e}}_{2}$   channel have the lowest PBTI drift under positive gate bias   $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}+2\,\mathrm{V})$   due to a small number of active defects (Fig. 2(d)).   ${\mathrm{MoSe}}_{2}$   FET also exhibit one of the lowest PBTI drifts in our results, this could be very beneficial for  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   n-FETs which are currently considered for FAB integration [13], [14]. The main contribution on PBTI drifts is from  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   shallow defect band in those FET devices. However, Fig. 3(b) shows that the impact of NBTI drifts on FETs with TMD channels under negative bias stress of  $V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2\,\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{I}}$     $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   FET has the lowest NBTI drift. However, in Fig. 3(c) it is shown that when using a stronger stress bias    $V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2.5\,\mathrm{V}$   NBTI degradation in   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   FETs remains the smallest among all devices only in the milliseconds stress range. Nevertheless, stronger band bending at more negative gate biases increases the number of active defects in the deep

$\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   defect band which come into play for longer stress times, thus resulting in considerably larger NBTI drifts in  $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   FETs. As a result,   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   devices appear to be the best in NBTI reliability when using stronger bias stresses over prolonged periods of time. Considering recent studies on FAB integration of   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   pMOSFET devices [13], [14] and even the use of this material in the first gate-all-around (GAA) p-FETs [14], this observation is very useful. Therefore, if the defect densities and widths of defect bands in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  and   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   were the same as known from Si technologies, the present FAB efforts on complementary integration of   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$  n- and p-FETs [13]–[15] could already have high potential to produce stable circuits. Low NBTI drifts in   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   could be hypothetically beneficial considering that there are some trial studies on   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   p-FETs. However, in reality, we have to consider that seed layers and high-k oxides deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD) directly onto flat surfaces of TMDs can be more defective as compared to industry-grade oxides in Si FETs.

## IV. I MPACT OF    $\mathrm{{SiO}}_{2}$   THICKNESS 

Maintaining sub-1 nm EOT in top-gated TMD FETs may be rather challenging since direct growth of   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   by ALD typically requires seed layers [16] like   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   considered in this study. Thus, in future, it may be highly desired to make these seed layers as thin as possible to fully exploit scaling benefits of high-k oxides. Then next we examine possible impact of  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   thickness on PBTI drifts   $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}+2\,\mathrm{V})$   in  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs and NBTI in   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   (  $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2\,\mathrm{V})$   and  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs   $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2.5\,\mathrm{V})$  . Fig. 4(a-b) depicts band diagrams in   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs with flat band conditions and under PBTI stress with thin   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   thickness of 0.2 nm. The corresponding stress traces are provided in Fig. 4(c). We can see that even with default parameters of defect bands, scaling of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   seed layer results in sizable increase of PBTI drifts in  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs, likely because shallow defects in   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   start to be activated as these defects come closer to the interface. In that case the shallow defect band of   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   centered at about   $0.44\,\mathrm{eV}$   above the conduction band of   ${\mathrm{MoSe}}_{2}$   should have the dominant contribution to the PBTI degradation. However, the contribution of the shallow defect band in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   to NBTI is still sizable in   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   devices. Fig. 4(d-e) depict band diagrams in   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   FET with flat band condition and under NBTI   $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2\,\mathrm{V})$   stress with   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  thickness of 0.2 nm. Nevertheless, for NBTI in  $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   FETs, in spite of the  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   deep defect band playing the dominant role, the increase in the thickness of  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   also result in activation of more defects over time in   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   layer. The corresponding NBTI stress traces are shown in Fig. 4(f), where an abrupt increase of degradation for thinner   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   seed layers is visible. Fig. 4(g-h) depicts band diagrams in   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FET with flat band condition and under NBTI stress   $(V_{\mathrm{FB}}-2.5\,\mathrm{V})$   with relatively small   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   thickness of 0.6 nm. Moderate thinning of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   may even reduce degradation, likely because different energetic alignments with respect to valence band edge of  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   of both bands   ${\mathrm{(HfO}}_{2}$  : 0.73 eV and   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  : 0.31 eV). Therefore, an interplay between deep defect bands in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$

and   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   may depend on the thickness ratios between the two layers. Understanding the conditions under which each defect band becomes activated and their respective contributions to device behavior are crucial for predicting and controlling the threshold voltage shift in those FETs. This knowledge can help us to optimize device designs and thus improve reliability.

## V. I MPACT OF DEFECT BAND WIDTH 

Finally, we consider that the thinning of seed   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layers may come together with their excessive amorphization. This should result in considerable broadening of defect bands and increased defect density Nt, even though fundamental energetic alignment has to be conserved. Thus, in Fig. 5 we analyze the possible impact of Et-sigma and Nt of the shallow bands in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   which should be responsible for PBTI in   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs. Already from the band diagrams of the flat band and under PBTI stress shown in Fig. 5(ab, d-e) it is obvious that broader defect bands with higher defect densities result in larger number of active defects. The corresponding stress traces (Fig. 5(c, f)) suggest that while broadening of shallow defect band in   $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   has a dramatic impact on PBTI in  ${\mathrm{MoSe}}_{2}$   FETs (Fig. 5(c)), only an inferior increase of degradation is observed even if shallow defect band in  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   becomes 3 times broader with 10 times larger Nt (Fig. 5(f)).

Fig. 6 shows the band diagram under different stress voltages of   $\cdot\,2\,\mathrm{V}$   and - 2.5 V with respect to    $V_{\mathrm{FB}}$   in   $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$  and   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs, and its corresponding stress traces. In  $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   FETs at  $\cdot\,2\,\mathrm{V}$   considerable time is required to activate defects in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   deep defect band even if the width and Nt are large. This is because this defect band which has the dominant role in NBTI is considerable above   $0.31\,\mathrm{eV}$   the valence band of the channel. Then say that in   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs at   $\cdot\,2.5\,\mathrm{V}$   a considerable impact of defect band broadening and increase of Nt is pronounced already at very short stress times because deep defect band in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   is much closer to  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   valence band edge (only   $0.28\,\mathrm{eV}$   above). Furthermore, owing to stronger band bending at - 2.5 V deep defect band in  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   also come into play. Therefore, either careful control of the quality of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   seed layers or the use of alternative seed layers could be required to improve the stability of  $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$   and   $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   FETs with respect to NBTI. Thus, we confirmed that PBTI is governed by shallow defects in the  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   and that NBTI is mostly dominated by deep defect band in   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  . This problem may be especially sound for  $\mathrm{MoSe}_{2}$   n-FETs in which PBTI degradation coming from the shallow  $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$   defect band is more important, but the effect of  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   seed layer is non-negligible. Understanding how different materials and operating conditions affect device reliability is crucial for optimizing semiconductor performance, especially as we continue to push the boundaries of miniaturization and efficiency in electronic devices.
