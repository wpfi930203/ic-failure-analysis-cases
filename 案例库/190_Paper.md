## An Improved Analysis Method on Si-Photonics  Waveguide Sidewall Roughness   

Hao TAN\*, Xintong ZHU, Poh Chuan ANG, Yuzhe ZHAO, Krishnanunni MENON, Yanlin PAN,  Changqing CHEN and Pik Kee TAN  GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte. Ltd., 60 Woodlands Industrial Park D Street 2, Singapore, 738406, Singapore.  \* Phone: +65-66702865   Fax: +65-66700425   Email:  hao.tan@globalfoundries.com

Abstract-   Low propagation loss waveguides are always preferred  when making Si-photonics waveguide devices. However, light  scattering due to sidewall roughness of waveguide contributes to  the majority of signal losses. To reduce the loss, a reliable  approach to measure the sidewall roughness is critical to fine-tune  the fabrication process. In this paper, we report a direct sidewall  roughness measurement method by using Atomic Force  Microscopy (AFM) incorporated with a special scanning mode  (Peakforce Tapping mode) and a point-terminated probe. This  method has been successfully applied to measure the sidewall  roughness of Si-Photonics waveguides for production process  monitoring. The present method can also be applied to  characterize the sidewall roughness of other nanostructures with  similar requirements or situations.

Keywords  –  Si-photonics, Waveguide, Sidewall roughness, AFM .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Silicon photonics has attracted more and more attention as an  emerging technology for optical telecommunications and for  optical interconnects in microelectronics in recent years. In Si  photonics technology, data is transferred by photons (i. e. light)  [1] - [3]. On a Photonic Integrated Circuit (PIC), light is  processed and routed on a chip by means of optical waveguides.  The Si core–  ${\mathrm{-SiO}}_{2}$   cladding waveguide is a critical building  block for the realization of a large variety of on-chip photonic  components. Fig. 1 illustrates the cross-section view of a  waveguide device on a Si wafer substrate. The main drawback  of extreme light confinement in small cross-section Si  waveguides is the high sensitivity of light losses due to   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Si}$    interface imperfections associated with the fabrication process.  In particular, one of the most important sources of non-ideality  is the waveguide sidewalls roughness or line edge roughness  (LER), which has a direct impact on the propagation losses by  light scattering. Transmission losses are detrimental to the  power-loss budget that must be minimized for most optical  applications [1] – [3]. Thus, controlling the sidewall roughness  plays an important role in Si waveguide fabrication. As a result,  a reliable characterization method must be established in the  first place and consequently, the improvements can be made  and verified.

All these methods have been proven to be workable  under certain conditions, but there are different drawbacks.  Tilting sample or AFM scanner needs a specially designed  holder with adjustable tilt angles, which are not always  available for different AFM users. Specially modified probe  combined with lateral scanning mode to check the changes in  torque seems to have fewer requirements, but the geometry of  the special probe also limits its access range, especially those  area in-between waveguides (usually a few microns). The  lift-up method is very simple for sample preparation; however,  it is highly sample-dependent as not all such structures can be  lifted up easily, and sometimes impossible.

However, most AFMs operate in a top-down configuration  which limits their access to the vertical sidewalls. ## II. EXPERIMENTS  

Si waveguide samples were fabricated with a normal wafer  process in GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte Ltd. A SELA  precision cleave system was used for the cleaving process. A  Bruker ICON AFM was used for the roughness measurement.

Fig. 2 shows the sample preparation flow: A waveguide  wafer sample is first cleaved along the waveguide and  in-between two waveguides. Then, the cleaved sample is put in  a specially designed holder to keep the sidewall surface upward.  After that, AFM inspection can be performed on the  cross-section edge to reveal the waveguide sidewall surface  features.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

An improved sidewall roughness measurement method has  been proposed in this study. Compared with conventional  methods, the current solution integrated a new designed  magnetic sample holder and Peakforce Tapping mode. The new  magnetic sample holder provides a great convenience for  sample loading/unloading, while the Peakforce Tapping mode  controls the amount of force applied during probe tip  engagement and scanning, which can protect both the probe tip  and the sample. It has been successfully applied to Si-photonics  waveguide sidewall roughness monitoring for our process  improvement and monitoring. This method can be also applied  to characterize the sidewall roughness of other nanostructures  with similar requirements or situations.
