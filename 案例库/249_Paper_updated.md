## Characterization of Stress Voltage/Time-Dependent  Dynamic R DS,ON  in 600 V-rated GaN Power Devices  

Liang He 1 , Wanting Bai 1 , Yao Li 1 , Shuqiang Liu 1 , Yijun Shi 1 , Yiqiang Ni 1 , Yuan Chen 1 , Zhiyuan  $\mathrm{He}^{2}$  , Yiqiang Chen 1 , Guoguang Lu 1  1 China Electronic Product Reliability and Environmental Testing Research Institute (CEPREI), Guangzhou, China  2 School of Integrated Circuits, Guangdong University of Technology, Guangzhou, China  Email: sq_liu@163.com, niyiqiang@ceprei.com, yiqiang-chen@hotmail.com

Abstract —The effects of off-state stress voltage ( V DS,OFF ) and  time ( t stress ) on dynamic on-resistance ( R DS,ON ) are investigated in  600 V-rated GaN power devices. A modified double-pulse test  (DPT) circuit and the multigroups DPT method are used to  evaluate the dynamic  R DS,ON  degradation under hard-switching  conditions. The findings highlight  t stress  as a crucial factor  leading to either monotonic increases or non-monotonic  variations in Schottky-type p-GaN gate HEMT dynamic  R DS,ON   with  V DS,OFF . Moreover it is found that the p-drain structure of  hybrid drain-gate injection transistor effectively mitigates  dynamic  R DS,ON  degradation at higher  V DS,OFF . Consequently,  t stress  should be carefully considered, guided by the mechanisms  identified in relation to dynamic  R DS,ON  degradation.

Keywords—GaN power devices, dynamic on-resistance, offstate stress time, double-pulse test

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

Compared to Silicon (Si)-based counterparts, Gallium  Nitride (GaN)-based electrical power converters possess  higher flexibility and energy efficiency, thanks to the  excellent performance of GaN power devices [1-2]. However,  when a AlGaN/GaN high electron mobility transistor  (HEMT) blocks a high-voltage bias in the off-state and then  turns on, the dynamic on-resistance  R DS,ON  exhibits a  recoverable increase. The degradation of dynamic  R DS,ON   owing to electron trapping is still faced with reliability issues  for GaN-on-Si power devices that limit their wider  application [3].

Given that many operating parameters can affect GaN  HEMT dynamic on-resistance under application conditions,  previous works have extensively evaluated dynamic  R DS,ON degradation of commercial discrete GaN power devices with  the consideration of the factors including off-state stress  voltage  V DS,OFF , drain current   $I_{D}$  , temperature, gate driving  condition ,and switching condition [4-9]. Systematic  researches on dependence of dynamic  R DS,ON  on  V DS,OFF  is still  lacking. Moreover, few works have comprehensively  discussed how off-state stress time  t stress  influences dynamic  R DS,ON . Therefore, this work seeks to deepen the  understanding of dynamic  $R_{D S,O N}$   by investigating the impact  of  t stress  in two typical types of 600 V-rated commercial  discrete GaN power devices, including Schottky-type p-GaN  gate HEMT (SG-HEMT) and hybrid-drain gate injection  transistor (HD-GIT), using a multigroups double-pulse tests  (DPTs) methods.

A modified DPT circuit in Fig. 3 (a) is utilized to evaluate  the dynamic  R DS,ON  of the DUTs. To enable accurately  t stress   control, an additional half bridge with two 1200 V-rated SiC  MOSFETs is incorporated into a traditional DPT circuit. Two  printed circuit boards (PCBs) with only difference in gate  driving circuit are fabricated for dynamic  R DS,ON  evaluation  of both device A and device B. Fig. 3 (b) shows the PCB  photograph of voltage-driving DPT circuit for device A. As  datasheet recommends, driving voltage  V GS,ON / V GS,OFF  is set  to   $+6\;\mathrm{V}/0\;\mathrm{V}$   for device A, and driving current  I GS,ON  is set to  10 mA for device B. A clamping circuit presented in [10] is

used to accurately capture the on-state voltage  V DS,ON  by  clamping the high  V DS,OFF  to a low clamping voltage that  prevents saturating the amplifier of the oscilloscope channel.  The   $I_{D}$   is measured with a   $0.1\ \Omega$   coaxial shunt. Dynamic  R DS,ON  can be calculated using Ohm's law based on the  measured   $V_{D S,O N}$   and  $I_{D}$   during each DPT.

The multigroups DPTs method we previously proposed in  [11] is adopted to investigate the impact of  t stress  on the  dynamic  R DS,ON . The timing diagram for this test method is  depicted in Fig. 4. After   $\mathbf{Q}_{2}$   turns off and a short dead-time  period, Q 1  turns on at t 0  and remains in on-state, during which  the DUT begins to block the  V DS,OFF . Subsequently, the first  DPT is conducted from t 1  to t 2 . After completing the first test,  the DUT blocks the  V DS,OFF  again at moment t 3  before  undergoing the second DPT, continuing in this cycle until the  predetermined off-state stress duration concludes. In this  experimental setup, since the DUT is in the off-state for the  most stress time rather than continuously switching, the  dynamic  R DS,ON  test results can exclude the influence of selfheating. In our experiments, DPTs are set at each time order  of magnitude within  t stress  less than 10 s, and DPTs are  conducted every 10 or 20 s after  t stress  more than 10 s. Each  on-state pulse is set to  $2\,\upmu\mathrm{s}$  , and the off-state interval between  on-state pulses is also set to   $2\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$  .

Normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  of device A and device B,  which is the ratio of measured dynamic  R DS,ON  and  static  $R o N$  at   $1.95~\upmu\mathrm{s}$   after devices turn on, are plotted in Fig. 7 and Fig.  8 respectively to comprehensively study the mechanism for  stress voltage/time-dependent dynamic  R DS,ON  degradation.  Obviously, normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  for both DUTs  increase when increasing the  t stress  at first, but eventually  reaches a saturated state where it remains unchanged despite  further increases in  t stress .

For device A shown in Fig. 7(b), the normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  increase with increasing  V DS,OFF  when  t stress  is less than  0.5 s. Then, the normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  exhibits nonmonotonic behavior with  V DS,OFF , and the turning point of  peak dynamic  R DS,ON  occurs at a lower  V DS,OFF  as  t stress   increases.   Commercial   SG-HEMTs   from   the   same  manufacturer have been observed a relationship between  dynamic  R DS,ON  and  V DS,OFF  follows a monotonic increasing  pattern in [4, 5, 8], but a non-monotonic variation in [6, 7, 9].  Experimental results suggests that  t stress , which do not have a  common standard and accurate control in previous studies,  can be a significant factor of the characterization of dynamic  R DS,ON .

Fig. 8(b) illustrates the variation between normalized  dynamic  R DS,ON  and  V DS,OFF  for device B. For shorter  t stress   (less than 1 s), the normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  are around 1.15,  with little correlation with  V DS,OFF . As  t stress  extends beyond  10 s, device B exhibits non-monotonic behavior of  normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  with  V DS,OFF . This result is

consistent with a general understanding of HD-GIT from  previous studies [4, 9]. Especially when  V DS,OFF  is  $400\;\mathrm{V}$  , the  normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  is only around 1.19. Compared to  peak dynamic  R DS,ON  under   $150~\mathrm{V}$   off-state stress, HD-GIT  dynamic  R DS,ON  degradation has a greater degree of recovery  at higher voltages. Notably, at a  V DS,OFF  of   $400\;\mathrm{~V~}$  , the  normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  is only 1.19, compared to the  peak normalized dynamic  R DS,ON  of 2.10 under   $150\,\mathrm{~V~}$   offstate stress. This indicates a significantly greater degree of  recovery for HD-GIT dynamic  R DS,ON  at higher voltages.

The varying stress voltage/time-dependent dynamic  R DS,ON  behaviors observed in the two types of GaN power  devices can be attributed to several mechanisms, which are  summarized as follows.

1) At relatively low  V DS,OFF , the primary reason for the  increase in dynamic on-resistance is predominantly driven by  electron trapping caused by the electric field. In the structure  of GaN HEMT devices, traps are an unavoidable presence.  During hard switching, the simultaneous presence of voltage  and current to the device leads to injection of electrons and  accelerates the trapping processes related to hot-electrons  [12]. Consequently, the acceptor-type traps in the Carbon(C)- doped buffer layer of device A and B ionize, partially  depleting the 2-DEG and thus aggravating the degradation of  dynamic  R DS,ON  as  V DS,OFF  increases.

2) At relatively high  V DS,OFF , charge redistribution within  the device structure due to the drain to substrate increasing  leakage is described to suppress the dynamic   R DS,ON degradation [13, 14]. Impact ionization is also considered as  a contributing factor to the partial recovery of dynamic onR DS,ON  at higher  V DS,OFF  [15]. Additionally, injected holes

from the p-drain structure of device B at high voltage stress  can release the trapped electrons [9], suggesting that dynamic  R DS,ON  can be suppressed more effectively than device A.

3) In GaN HEMT devices, the degradation behavior of  dynamic  R DS,ON  is associated with a critical voltage related to  t stress  and device structure [16]. When  V DS,OFF  is below this  critical voltage, the electric field is insufficient to facilitate  electron trapped in the buffer layer, resulting in a lower  dynamic  R DS,ON  degradation of device B under 50 V stress and  relatively short  t stress . Due to the differences in device  structure, device A possesses a lower critical voltage,  resulting in a more severe degradation of dynamic  R DS,ON  in  the same situation.

## IV.   C ONCLUSION 

This work comprehensively investigate  V DS,OFF / t stress - dependent   dynamic   R DS,ON    under   hard-switching   of  commercial ohmic/schottky-type contact p-GaN gate HEMT.  Through the multigroups DPTs method, this research  delineates how  t stress  affect dynamic  R DS,ON  in two mainstream  types of commercial GaN HEMTs: SG-HEMT and HD-GIT.  Key findings indicate that   $t_{s t r e s s}$   is a pivotal factor influencing  R DS,ON  variation, showing both monotonic and non-monotonic  behaviors dependent on  V DS,OFF . This research contributes to  the broader field of GaN HEMT technology by providing a  detailed analysis of dynamic  R DS,ON  behaviors, which are  crucial for the design and optimization of more efficient and  reliable GaN-based power converters.
