## Distribution of HCD-induced Interface Trap in  nano-scale Si n-FinFET due to Corner Effect  

Mingyang Sun 1,2 , Ruixi   $\mathrm{Y}\mathbf{u}^{1,2}$  , Yunfei  $\mathrm{{Sh}i^{1,2}}$  , Hong Yang 1,2\* , Qianqian Liu 1 , Bo Tang 1 , Xiaobin He 1 , Jianfeng Gao 1,2 , Junfeng Li 1,2 ,  Huaxiang Yin 1,2 , Jun Luo 1,2  and Wenwu Wang 1,2     1  Key Laboratory of Microelectronics Device & Integrated Technology, Institute of Microelectronics of the Chinese Academy of  Sciences, Beijing 100029, China  2  Microelectronics Institute, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China   Phone: (86)10-82995916, Email:  yanghong@ime.ac.cn

Abstract — In this paper, in order to understand the  interface trap distribution along channel induced by Hot  Carrier Degradation (HCD) of nano-scale Si n-FinFETs, both  Bias Temperature Instability (BTI) and HCD of n-FinFETs are  compared. Under BTI stress, the short-channel n-FinFET is  influenced by both corner passivation effects and a larger  corner electric field. Interface traps exhibit a lower density in  the short-channel device at lower stress voltages, but surpass it  at higher stress voltages. During HCD stress, the degradation  process in short-channel devices is predominantly dominated by  a larger lateral electric field, resulting in significantly fewer  interface traps. Moreover, interface traps in short-channel  devices no longer accumulate near the drain side, as observed in  long-channel devices, but extend towards the source, distributed  across the entire channel.

Keywords—reliability, hot carrier degradation, n-FinFET,  interface trap

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

The Hot Carrier Degradation (HCD) has remained crucial  in the exploration of device reliability. As device parameters  continue to shrink, the effects of HCD [1-5] on trap generation  in short-channel devices differ from their long-channel  counterparts. The distribution of interface traps after HCD  stress has been a subject of varied interpretations [6-10].

Recently, It shows that the interface traps generate under  HCD from the source side by comparing the ratio of the  threshold voltage variation between the saturation region and  the linear region   $(\Delta\mathrm{V_{t,sat}}\ /\Delta\mathrm{V_{t,lin}})|$  [11-12]. However, there is  another conclusion, and the interface traps generate in the  direction away from the drain when reverse  $\mathrm{Ib}$   using the same  method[13]. Furthermore, other researchers[14-16]  have  utilized the forward and reverse Id-Vg sweep method to  confirm that interface and oxide trap 1 in FinFETs are near  the source region, while oxide trap 2 is distributed near the  drain side. In order to make clearly the interface trap  generation of Fin field-effect transistor (FinFET) with short-and long- channel, we employes both of these methods and  ultimately confirmed, through the forward and reverse I d -  $\mathrm{\nabla{V_{g}}}$    sweep method.

In this study, we aim to dissect the influences of Bias  Temperature Instability (BTI) stress on HCD stress by  comparing short-channel and long-channel devices after BTI  and HCD stresses[17]. Considering corner effects, corner  longitudinal electric fields, and lateral electric fields, we  analyze various factors affecting the generation of interface  traps and summarize the patterns of interface trap generation.  Our findings indicate that the interface traps in short- channel  devices originate from the drain end, extends towards the  source end, and ultimately uniformly distributes across the  entire channel.

## III.   Results and Discussion  

Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 illustrates the threshold voltage shifts of  devices after 1000 s of BTI and HCD stress tests on devices  with short and long channel lengths. It is evident that, across  various stress voltages, the threshold voltage shift after BTI  stress is consistently greater than that after HCD stress for  both gate lengths. This observation suggests that the  degradation process in these devices is primarily dominated  by BTI.

This phenomenon is attributed to two factors: on one hand,  oxygen vacancy defects in the oxide layer contribute  significantly to BTI degradation. On the other hand, the fins  fabricated using the sidewall transfer technology have larger  spacing, resulting in negligible self-heating and consequently  less HCD degradation.

It is  observed that the Vit degradation in the 60-nm device is  lower than that in the 500-nm device under stress voltages of  1.6V and 1.8V. This phenomenon may be attributed to the competitive  relationship between corner passivation effects and corner  electric fields. The previous process would also result in the  passivation of the interface, particularly at corners where  passivation is more prevalent. Simultaneously, the electric  field at corners is stronger, leading to intensified carrier  collisions and transitions, exacerbating the degradation  process (as depicted in Fig.8). Since corner effects are more  severe in short-channel devices, under the competition of  these two mechanisms, the 60-nm device exhibits fewer  initial interface defects due to corner passivation effects,  resulting in lower  $\mathrm{V_{it}}$   compared to the 500-nm device at lower  BTI stress voltages. However, when the stress voltage  reaches 2.0V, the dominant role of the stronger electric field  at corners causes V it  to surpass that in the 500-nm device.

In Fig.9, a comparative analysis of the   $\mathrm{V_{it}}$   in devices with  different gate lengths after HCD stress is presented. Notably,  the   $\mathrm{V_{it}}$   of the 500-nm devices surpasses that of the 60-nm  devices. Additionally, this discrepancy becomes more  pronounced with increasing stress voltages.

This phenomenon is closely associated with the lateral  electric fields introduced by HCD stress. In the context of the  BTI-dominated degradation process (as depicted in Fig.7),  after 2V stress, the   $\mathrm{V_{it}}$  of the 60-nm devices has already  exceeded that of the 500-nm devices under the influence of  the longitudinal electric field. However, after 1000s of 2V  HCD stress, the  $\mathrm{V_{it}}$   of the 500-nm devices is  $245\%$   higher than  that of the 60-nm devices, indicating the predominant role of  the lateral electric field at this stage. Subsequent simulations  have revealed that the lateral electric field in the 60-nm  devices is significantly greater than that in the 500-nm devices,  further substantiating this observation.

As is shown in Fig.10, The intense lateral electric field in  the short-channel devices directs electrons more directly  toward the drain, reducing the probability of defect-inducing  collisions. Consequently, the   $\mathrm{V_{it}}$   in short-channel devices is  significantly lower than that in long-channel devices, and  with increasing stress voltage, the larger lateral electric field  exacerbates the disparity in   $\mathrm{V_{it}}$   between the two channel  lengths.

## IV.    C ONCLUSION 

This study investigates the distinct patterns of interface  trap generation in devices with different gate lengths by  contrasting the outcomes of   $500\mathrm{nm}$   and 60nm devices  subjected to BTI and HCD stresses. Under the sole influence  of BTI stress, the short-channel device is concurrently  affected by a stronger corner passivation effect and a larger  corner electric field. Consequently, interface traps exhibit a  lower density in the short-channel device at lower stress  voltages compared to the long-channel device, but surpass it  at higher stress voltages. During HCD stress, the devices are  influenced by both longitudinal and lateral electric fields. Due  to the larger lateral electric field in the short-channel device,  interface traps are fewer in number. Furthermore, unlike  traditional   long-channel   devices   where   traps   are  predominantly distributed near the drain, in short-channel  devices, these traps extend towards the source.
