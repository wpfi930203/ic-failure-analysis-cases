## Correlation Analysis and Characterization of  Micromorphology and Optoelectronic Properties of  SiO 2 /SiC in Pressure Sensor  

Limei Rong1 \* , Jinze He1, Jiangfeng Du1\*, Tiancheng Luo1, Rongsen Yang1,    Kun Gao1, Qi Yu1, Jiao Xu2, Guanghong Zhao2, Yugang Yin2  1 School of Electronic Science and Engineering, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, Chengdu, China.  2  Beijing Research Institute of Telemetry, Beijing, China.   \*Email:  lmrong@uestc.edu.cn   \* jfdu@uestc.edu.cn

Abstract-  This paper discusses the local optical properties and the  morphologies of interface and oxide layer of   $\mathbf{SiO}_{2}/\mathbf{SiO}$  . The  cross-section morphology of the   $\mathbf{SiO}_{2}/\mathbf{SiO}$   after polishing and  etching was illustrated by scanning electron microscopy, and the  refractive index was characterized by ellipsometry. The   ellipsometry based on multilayer interface model is better than  that based on single-interface model in reflecting the gap layer and  the different typical interface layers across the interface. A  correlation is found between morphology and refractive index  under the temperature range from   ${\bf600}\,\,\,^{\circ}\!{\bf C}$  to   $\mathbf{900}\ \ {}^{\circ}\mathbf{C}.$  . The  capacitance model including multilayer interface is established for  sensitivity component in pressure sensor, and the maximum rate  of capacitance drift is  $\mathbf{3.56\%}$  .

Keywords  –   Local optical properties, SEM, SiO 2 /SiC,  Ellipsometry, Multilayer interface model, Morphology.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

SiC-based pressure sensors for applications in aerospace and  other harsh environments are increasingly in demand due to the  outstanding   electrical,   high-temperature   mechanical,  anti-irradiation, and chemical properties of SiC materials [1-3].  Silicon oxide is used as gate dielectric and surface passivation  in SiC devices. Despite continuous improvement in the SiC  MOS interface, the exact reactions during thermal oxidation of  SiC at a microscopic level have not yet been clearly described  and discovered; the quality and   the controlling factors of this  quality are still far from a satisfactory level [4]. Theoretical  studies [5-7], such as those of Knaup and Okuno, have  established a simple, abrupt model of   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface to  explore the electrical properties under the contribution of  interface defects. However, some test results reported on the  interface structure are inconsistent. Önneby [8] observed the  existence of a transition layer by XPS. Seki [9] suggested that  the interface is rather abrupt and has a very thin   $(<\,2\,\mathrm{\nm})$    transition layer at the SiC side of the interface by ellipsometry.  The conclusions differ even if the same test method of  TEM/EELS is used, that is, the interface is either transition  layer [10, 11], or abrupt [12].   In addition, the physical and  electrical characterization techniques have limitations [13-17].  Therefore, accurate characterization of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   interface has  always been a challenge. This work proposes a method for  visualizing the microscopic morphology of the insulating and

interfacial layers of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   and an ellipsometric optical  method by establishing an appropriate model for quantitatively  characterizing the properties of these materials. Accordingly,  the correlation between these properties is revealed. The  capacitance drift of the   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   capacitive pressure sensor is  studied by applying the test data of the multilayer interface  model to explore the mechanism of device performance  variation with process and microstructure.

## II. SAMPLE AND TEST CHARACTERIZATION METHODS 

The   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   structure used n-type 4H-SiC (0001) as  substrate with a thickness of   $350~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  for the pressure sensor.  The   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer with a thickness of   $2~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$   was grown on SiC by  PECVD. The   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   samples were divided into four groups,  which were subjected to temperature treatments at   $600\ \mathrm{~}^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$  ,   $700\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$  ,   $800\ \ {}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , and   $900\ \ {}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for   $1~\mathrm{~h~}$  , respectively. The  cross-sectional morphology of the samples was observed by   scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Inspect F50) after  samples were polished and were etched with buffered oxide  etcher (BOE). The refractive index of the samples was  described using ellipsometry (Sentech SE850), and its test  model consisted of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  , interface layer, and SiC. Single-layer  and multilayer interface models were used. The multilayer  interface model was used to demonstrate the local optical  properties across the interface that changed with the variations  in interface depth and treatment temperature. The single-layer  interface model was used to study the relationship between the  average value of the interface properties and temperature.

The oxide layer data by the multilayer interface model are  similar to those by the single-layer one. However, the interface  layer information of the former is considerably more than that  of the latter. We regard the boundary of silica as the reference  surface and assume similar structure of areas with close values  of refractive index (Fig. 3). On this basis, we divide the  interface into six regions: the gap layer, the loose layer, and the  typical interface layers 1–4 (I1-I4 with the corresponding  values of refractive index of approximately 1.65, 2, 2.3, and 2.8,  respectively).

At   $600\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$  , a layer (approximately 0.1 nm) is observed with a  refractive index of 1.04 near   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   in the   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface.  This layer should be a gap layer because its refractive index is  close to that of vacuum. Such structure with a thickness and a  refractive index of 1.11 nm and 1.3, respectively, is also found.  This layer is loose because its refractive index is considerably  smaller than those of SiC and   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  , and is slightly larger than  the air layer, thereby naming it as loose layer. The loose layer  may be formed by insufficient reaction between the   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$    interfaces. Moreover, the typical interface I2 with a thickness of  0.19 nm appears near SiC. At   $700\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , the gap layer disappears,  and the thickness of the loose layer increases to approximately  1.3 nm. Fig. 3 shows that the thickness of the typical interface is  increased to 0.65 nm and the number of the typical layers is  changed from one layer at   $600\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   to two layers (I1 of 0.34 nm  and I2 of 0.31 nm). At  $800\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , the thickness of the loose layer is  decreased to 0.7 nm, and the typical interface has three layers  (I1, I2, and I3 with a total thickness increase of   $2.63\ \mathrm{nm}$  ). At   $900\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , the loose layer disappears, and the typical interface has  four layers (I1, I2, I3, and I4 with maximum thickness  compared with all samples). The refractive index of the typical  interface I4 near SiC is 2.77, which is greater than those of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$    and SiC. This finding is consistent with result in [9].

## IV.  DISCUSSION 

## 4.1 Correlation between microstructure and refractive  index of SiO 2 /SiC using single-layer interface model  

Some correlations are found between the refractive index by  ellipsometry and the morphology by SEM. Fig. 1 shows that the  cross-section morphology behaves differently with the change  in temperature. The trend corresponds to the curve in Fig. 2(a),  in which the refractive index of the   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer increases first  and then decreases with the increase in treatment temperature.  At  $700\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   and  $800\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , the refractive index of the  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   layer is at  the highest points. This finding indicates that the microscopic  structure with quasi-crystal morphology at  $600\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$  , as shown in  Fig. 1(a), has lower refractive index and the microscopic  structure with amorphous morphology has higher refractive  index at higher temperatures, as shown in Figs. 1(b) and 1(c).  However, the refractive index slightly decreases when particle  aggregation occurs in the   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$    layer with amorphous structure,  as shown in Fig. 1(d).

The thickness and brightness of the interface layer of  SiO 2 /SiC by visualization, as shown in Fig. 1, are highly  correlated to the refractive index and thickness by the  single-layer interface model, as shown in Fig. 2(b). Specifically,  as the temperature increases, the texture of interface by  visualization thickens and brightens. Correspondingly, the  values of refractive index and thickness by data increase.

## 4.2 Correlation between microstructure and refractive  index of SiO 2 /SiC using multilayer interface model  

The SEM results in Fig. 1(a) indicate the formation of a gap  layer between   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and SiC. The gap layer correlates to the  refractive index by the multilayer interface model (near 1 at 600   $^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$  ) in Fig. 3. A loose layer forms on the side near   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  , as  shown in Fig. 3. In the figure, the values of the refractive index  are close to 1.35 for   $600\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  ,   $700\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$  , and   $800\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   when the  reaction between   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and SiC at the interface is insufficient.  The loose layer can still be clearly displayed using ellipsometry  based   on   multilayer   interface   model.   However,   the  microstructure of this layer is not clear by using SEM. At a high  temperature, the transition zone between SiC and  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   thickens,  and the number of typical interface layers increases. This  condition is clearly indicated by I1–I4 in Fig. 3. The SEM  results in Fig. 1(d) show that the thickness of the interface layer  significantly increases, and its brightness is significantly high. The SEM morphology   at a magnification of 80,000 times can  only provide qualitative information, whereas the ellipsometry  can provide more quantitative details that reach a resolution of  0.1 nm.

## 4.3 Analysis of single- and multilayer interface models  

The thickness of the single-layer interface model is compared  with the total thickness of the multilayer interface model, as  shown in Fig. 4, to verify the consistency of the fitting results.  The interface thicknesses of the two models are nearly the same  regardless of the temperature. The discrepancy in thickness  between the two models is observed at   $800\ \ {}^{\circ}\mathbf{C},$  , but the  maximum difference is only   $0.06~\mathrm{nm}$  . This finding proves the

correctness of the multilayer interface model and provides a  method for studying the local optical properties of the SiO 2 /SiC  interface.

## 4.4 Influence on electrical property  

The drift of the capacitance is a key parameter to evaluate the  SiC capacitive pressure sensor. To this end, a revised  capacitance model is established for the   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   structure  including multilayer interface based on the ellipsometric and  SEM test results. In accordance with the definition of the direct  transition probability and the Kramers–Kronig dispersion  relationship within the linear response range, the solid  macroscopic optical response function can generally be  described by the complex dielectric function ε(ω) or the  complex refractive index  $\scriptstyle{N(\omega)=n(\omega)+i k(\omega)}$  . In this index,  $n$   is  the refractive index and   $k$   is the extinction coefficient.  Therefore, we can calculate the corresponding dielectric  constant by the refractive index and the extinction coefficient  obtained by ellipsometry.

We build a capacitor model [18], as shown in Fig. 5(a). In  accordance with the ellipsometry based on multilayer interface  model, we can regard each layer of the   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   interface  transition region as a parallel plate capacitor. Thus, the  transition region becomes a stacked capacitor structure, as  shown in Fig. 5(b). The total capacitance is parallel connection  of the cavity capacitance  $(C_{c})$   and the ring capacitance   $(C_{b})$  , and  it is expressed as follows:

$$\begin{array}{r}{C=\frac{\pi\varepsilon_{0}a^{2}}{2\sqrt{A w_{0}}}\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{w_{0}}{A}}}{1-\sqrt{\frac{w_{0}}{A}}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\pi(L^{2}-a^{2})}{\sum_{i=1}^{z}\frac{d_{i}(T)}{\varepsilon_{r_{i}}(T)}}}\end{array}$$
(1)

$$\begin{array}{r}{A=g+\frac{d_{o x}(T)}{\varepsilon_{o x}(T)}+\sum_{i=1}^{z}\frac{d_{i}(T)}{\varepsilon_{r_{i}}(T)}}\end{array}$$
(2)

where   $T$   is temperature.  ε 0 ,  ε ox , and   $\varepsilon_{r i}$   are a vacuum dielectric  constant, the relative dielectric constant of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  , and i-th  interfacial layer, respectively.   $d_{o x}$   and  $d_{i}$   are the thicknesses of  the oxide layer and the interface of the i-th layer, respectively.  a   is the radius of the sensitive film,   $w_{\theta} $  is the maximum  disturbance of the sensitive film,   $L$   is the radius of the sensor  sensitive component, and  $g$   is the height of the cavity.

We calculate the rate of capacitance drift of sensitivity  component   under the effect of temperature and the multilayer  interface compared with that without interface layer. The  calculation results are shown in Fig. 6. The figure shows that  the trend of capacitance drift is nearly consistent with that of  oxide layer, and the maximum rate of total capacitance drift is   $3.56\%$  . This finding shows that the capacitance drift is sensitive  to temperature, which should be strictly controlled in the  process.

## V. C ONCLUSION   

## R EFERENCES 

This study demonstrated the cross-sectional morphology of  etched  $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   structure with the temperature varied from 600   $^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   to  $900~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   and revealed the correlation between morphology  intuitively and the refractive index quantitatively. The  variations in interface gap, interface layer, and    $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$    morphology and the trends of refractive index and its thickness  of interface under four different temperatures were clearly  described. The refractive index obtained by ellipsometry based  on multilayer interface model can better characterize the  interface structure because it can simultaneously reflect the  local properties and the properties across the entire interface.  This study provides a characterization method that correlates its  microstructure of insulating and interface layers with the  refractive index of macro parameter. The results of ellipsometry  and SEM are very close and correspond to each other. This  consistency indicates that the proposed model and test method  are valid. The   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$    capacitance model with multilayer  interface was established,   and the rate of capacitance drift of  sensitivity component for   $\mathrm{SiO}/\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   pressure sensor structure  was calculated using the test data of ellipsometry based on  multilayer interface model. The results show that the maximum  rate of capacitance drift is  $3.56\%$  . This work is important for the  characterization, failure analysis, and performance optimization  of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{SiO}$   structure.
