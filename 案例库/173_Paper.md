## Si Nano-Crystal Size and Structural Defect  Characterization  

Using Electron Microscopes   Elizabeth Sebastian\*, Jie Zhu and Zhi Qiang Mo    GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore, 60 Woodlands Industrial Park D Street 2, Singapore 738406    \* Phone: +65 66701545   Fax: +65 63622938   Email:  elizabeth.sebastian@globalfoundries.com

Abstract-   Si nano-crystals (NCs) based flash memory is less  susceptible to charge loss in tunnel oxide, allowing tunnel oxide  reduction from   ${\sim}100\mathbf{A}$   to   $\mathbf{\tilde{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{0A}}$   and therefore lower operating  voltage and improved cycling endurance performance. Most  critical parameters controlling Si NCs performance include size,  size distribution and optimal area density   $(\#/\mathfrak{c m}2)$  . Objective of  this study includes developing an image processing method for  large population of Si NCs characterization, evaluating and  comparing different electron microscope imaging techniques and  studying Si NCs structural defects.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Nano crystal-based NVM devices have received much  attention due to their potential to overcome the limitations of  conventional polysilicon-based flash memory since 1990.   Using NCs as charge storage media offers several advantages,  the main one being the potential to use thinner tunnel oxide  without sacrificing non-volatility. This reduction in the tunnel  oxide thickness helps to lower operating voltages and to  increase operating speeds of such devices [1]-[4]. Fabrication  of NC in non-volatile memory cells requires a perfect control of  its critical parameters such as area density, size and size  distribution [5]. It is not practical to do manual measurement of  such parameters when dealing with large number of NCs. This  led us to develop a more automatic and practical method. This  study intends to develop an image processing method for a  large number of Si NC characterizations. This work also  evaluates and compares different imaging techniques including  Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) and Scanning  Electron Microscope (SEM) and shows advantages and  limitations of each technique. Detailed study on the structural  defects of Si NCs is also demonstrated.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

In total 2 samples with different conditions are tested with  the newly developed image processing method. Samples used

for this study are non-production wafer and are test wafers used  only for evaluation purpose. Sample A is after process A and  sample B is after process B. In both the samples, NCs are grown  on a 6nm tunnel oxide. For SEM imaging, Helios 450  Dual-beam machine is used with 1-5kV acceleration voltage  and 25-200pA electron beam current with thru-lens detector.  For TEM analysis, a 300-kV TEM (Titan) equipped with  spherical aberration corrector and Gatan energy filter is used to  acquire high-resolution and Si Plasmon images. “Image J” open  software is used to process both SEM and TEM images.  “Analyze particles” function of the software is used for Si NCs  size, size distribution and area density analysis.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

In conclusion, we applied software image processing method  and compared Si nano-crystal size, size distribution and areal  density using different electron microscope techniques. ImageJ  “particle analysis” function is found to be very promising for  large population Si NCs size analysis. SEM tends to give  slightly larger Si NCs size comparing with TEM, most likely  because SEM analysis includes Si + native oxide thickness.  Optimum SEM imaging condition is required in obtaining  high/uniform contrast between Si NCs and substrate, which is  important for accurate size analysis using ImageJ. TEM is not  suitable for “routine” Si NCs size analysis due to challenges in  sample preparation and much smaller sampling area as

compared with SEM technique, but TEM technique provides  unique capability useful for studying atomic level defects in Si  NCs, such as twin structures, stacking faults, grain boundary  and etc. Pros and cons of SEM and TEM techniques when used  in this study are shown in table 2.
