## Light-Illumination-Induced Degradation and Its  Long-Term Recovery in Indium-Tin-Zinc Oxide  Thin-Film Transistors  

Meng Zhang 1 , Xiaotong   $\mathrm{Ma}^{1}$  , Zhendong Jiang 1 , Sunbin Deng 2 , Guijun Li 1 , Rongsheng Chen 3 ,  \* Yan Yan 1 , Man Wong 2 , Hoi-Sing Kwok 2   1 Institute of Microscale Optoelectronics (IMO), Shenzhen University, Shenzhen 518060, China  2 State Key Laboratory of Advanced Displays and Optoelectronics Technologies, The Hong Kong University of Science and  Technology, Clear Water Bay, Kowloon, Hong Kong  3 School of Electronic and Information Engineering, South China University of Technology, Guangzhou 510640, China  \* Phone: +86-755-26533630   Fax: +86-755-26534831   Email:  yanyan@szu.edu.cn

Abstract-    Light-illumination-induced   degradation   and   its  long-term recovery in indium-tin-zinc oxide (ITZO) thin-film  transistors (TFTs) are investigated. Negative threshold voltage  shift and subthreshold degradation are observed under light of  546.1 nm with   $\mathbf{14.92\mW/cm^{2}}$  . Short-term recovery and long-term  recovery of ITZO TFT exhibit two different characteristics.  Incorporated   with   TCAD   simulations,   the   degradation  mechanism and recovery mechanism are tentatively discussed.

Keywords  –  Light illumination, Recovery, Indium-tin-zinc oxide,  ITZO, Thin film transistors .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Metal-oxide (MO) thin-film transistors [1] are  promising devices to achieve high-resolution active matrix  displays with low fabrication cost. Among MO TFTs,  indium-gallium-zinc oxide (IGZO) TFTs [2] are the first to  be commercialized. However, the mobility   $(\mu)$   of IGZO  TFTs is only about  $10\,\mathrm{cm}^{2}/\mathrm{Vs}$   [3], which directly limits their  applications to ultra-high-definition (UHD) displays, such as   $8\mathrm{K}\times4\mathrm{K}$   display with a frame of   $120~\mathrm{Hz}$  . Indium-tin-zinc  oxide (ITZO) TFTs with a  $\mu$    of  ${\sim}25~\mathrm{cm}^{2}/\mathrm{Vs}$   [4] can provide  sufficient driving capability to readily realize UHD displays.  Reliability issue is always a big concern before the  commercialization. Since the TFT array suffers from  long-time light illimitation, light-stress induced instability in  TFTs can not be ignored.

In this work, light-illumination-induced degradation in  ITZO TFTs is investigated. After a   $546.1\ \mathrm{nm}$   light with   $14.92\;\mathrm{mW}/\mathrm{cm}^{2}$   illumination, a persistent negative shift with  decreased subthreshold swing ( SS ) is observed. An  immediate recovery of the  SS  after light stress removal is  also observed. Besides the degradation, the behaviors of  ITZO TFT with a long-term recovery   $(>36$   days) is also  studied. Based on the experimental results and TCAD  simulations, the degradation and recovery mechanisms are  tentatively discussed.

## II.   EXPERIMENTAL  

The schematic diagram of ITZO TFT is shown in Fig. 1.  The process started with 4-inch silicon wafers, which is covered  by 500-nm thick thermal oxide. First, a layer of indium tin  oxide (ITO) was sputtered and patterned as source and drain.  Then a layer of ITZO film was deposited and patterned as active  channel. Next, a layer of   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   generated by plasma-enhanced  chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) method was deposited as  gate insulator (GI), followed by a layer of sputtered ITO. After  gate patterning, a passivation layer was deposited using  PECVD method. Then, the contact holes were opened and a  layer of ITO was sputtered, followed by the definition of  source/drain/gate electrodes. Finally, the wafer was annealing  at  $300\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   for 4 hours.

The channel width ( W ) and channel length ( L ) are  respectively fixed at   $100~\upmu\mathrm{m}$  and   $10~\upmu\mathrm{m}$   for ITZO TFTs under  test. The light stress has 546.1 nm wavelength and the power  density is   $14.92\:\mathrm{mW}/\mathrm{cm}^{2}$  . The ITZO TFTs are measured before  and after light stress by using the Keysight B1500A  semiconductor parameter analyzer.

To analyze the degradation mechanism, a TCAD tool is  employed. Show in Fig. 7 is the experimental curves and  fitting curves of ITZO for the initial stage (black), 7000 s  light illumination (red), 0 s recovery (blue) and 36.88 days’  recovery (green). It can be observed that the simulation data  fits the experimental data well. Shown in Fig. 8 is density  state distribution used in transfer curve fitting. It can be  observed that after 7000 s light stress, the defect density in  both tail states and deep energy level increases. The positive  fixed charge generation and metastable defect state  generation [5] may be responsible for positive threshold  voltage   $V_{t h}$   shift and  SS  degradation under light stress. The  immediate recovery of the   SS  may be attributed to  immediate reduction of the donor-like states in the tail  distribution after light stress removal. The long-time  recovery may be due to the trapped-hole de-trap process.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Light-illumination-induced degradation behaviors and its  long-term recovery in ITZO TFTs are studied. The 546.1 nm  light stress results in  SS  degeneration, as well as negative   $V_{t h}$    shift. Short-term recovery and long-term recovery exhibit  different characteristics. Based on TCAD simulations, the  mechanisms are tentatively discussed.
