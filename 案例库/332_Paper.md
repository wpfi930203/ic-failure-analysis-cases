## Investigations on the Short-Circuit Degradation and its  Mechanism of 1.2-KV 19-A SiC power MOSFETs  

J. L. Wang 1 , Y. Q. Chen 2, \*) , Z. Y. He 2 , Y. F.  $\mathrm{En}^{2}$  , X. B.   $\mathrm{{Nu}}^{1}$  , Y. Huang 2 , and K. W. Geng 1, \*)   1 School of Electronic and Information Engineering, South China University of Technology, Guangzhou, Guangdong, China,  510000  2 Science and Technology on Reliability Physics and Application of Electronic Component Laboratory No.5 Electronics Research  Institute of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology Guangzhou, Guangdong, China, 510610  \* Phone: 13286899608     Email:  yiqiang-chen@hotmail.com,  gengkw@scut.edu.cn

Abstract-   To provide a guideline for converter design and fault  protection, the failure mechanism and reliability of silicon carbide  (SiC) power MOSFETs needs to be further investigated. In this  paper, the failure mechanism during short-circuit (SC) of  commercial 1.2-KV/19-A SiC power MOSFETs was investigated.  After the SC tests, the  I ds  of the device decreased significantly.  Moreover, it was found that the  V th  and   $I_{\mathbf{gs}}$   increased obviously.  The results demonstrated that negative charges were captured by  the gate oxide and accumulated during the SC tests, which is  eventually causes degradation of the gate oxide. The results of this  study may be useful in the design and application of SiC power  MOSFETs.

Keywords  –  SiC power MOSFET, reliability, gate oxide failure,  Repetitive Short-Circuit tests.

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

As a third-generation wide bandgap semiconductor material,  silicon carbide (SiC) has become one of the most advantageous  semiconductor materials in power electronics applications due  to its superior properties [1]. Continuous improvement in  material fabrication technology has allowed SiC power  MOSFETs to become one of the most promising candidates for  high-voltage power devices [2]. Recently, high voltage SiC  power MOSFETs rated at 1.2 KV has been commercially  available and the commercial marketing applications were  accelerated.

Despite the reliability and ruggedness of SiC power  MOSFETs has greatly improved during the past years, devices  employed in high-frequency and high-speed fields were  expected to have an enhanced mode operation and to be able to  function for short amount of times during transients for safety  consideration [3]. Some papers have been dedicated to the  short-circuit (SC) behavior of 1.2 KV SiC power MOSFETs.  For instance, the parasitic transistor latch-up in a single SC test  may lead to significant degradation of the devices [4-7].  Another main failure mechanism during short circuit was found  to be the gate oxide failure which observed under the condition  of a high gate voltage bias [5], [8], [9]. At the same time, in the  repetitive SC tests, the main failure mode was found to be  related to the increased temperature. This could lead to the  melting or reconstruction of the source/gate metal [10-13].  However, to the author's knowledge, very limited research  efforts have been focused on the failure analysis associated with  the short circuit of 1.2-KV 19-A SiC power MOSFETs.

In this paper, experiments have been performed on a set of  commercial 1.2-KV 19-A SiC power MOSFETs. The  degradation of DC characteristics of devices was observed and  the failure mechanism was analysed. In order to further explore  the degradation mechanisms, the TCAD simulations will done  in the next work. The results could provide useful guidance on  the design of the gate driver and fault protection circuits.

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION   

## A.    Effect of Repetitive SC on Electrical Characteristics  

Repetitive SC tests have been performed under the  gate-source voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{gs}})$   of   $15/{-5}\mathrm{~V~}$   and drain-source voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{ds}})$   of 250 V. The gate pulsewidth   $(T_{\mathrm{sc}})$   of   $25~\upmu\mathrm{s}$   and the  interval time of 3 s between repetitive cycles was chosen in  order to prevent the source/gate metal from deterioration and  avoid the overheating of the device, respectively.

To determine the effect of SC on the electrical properties of  SiC power MOSFETs, the output characteristics   $(I_{\mathrm{ds}}-V_{\mathrm{ds}})$   were  measured in respect of SC cycles, as shown in Fig. 3. From the  output characteristics with   $V_{\mathrm{gs}}$    ranging from 6 to   $10\mathrm{~V~}$   at 1 V  step, the  $I_{\mathrm{ds}}$   of the device decreases   significantly after repetitive  SC tests. Transfer characteristics of the device are shown in Fig.  4, and the  $I_{\mathrm{ds}}$   also decrease significantly after repetitive SC tests,  which are in agreement with our output characteristics test  results.

The threshold voltage   $(V_{\mathrm{th}})$   extracted under the conditions of   $V_{\mathrm{gs}}=V_{\mathrm{ds}}$   and  $I_{\mathrm{{ds}}}=2.5~\mathrm{{mA}}$   are shown in Fig. 5. In general,   $V_{\mathrm{th}}$  increases after repetitive SC tests. The results indicated that the  gate oxide layer of the device is degraded. The on-state  resistance   $\left(R_{\mathrm{ds\(on)}}\right)$   of the device in respect of SC cycles are

shown in Fig. 6. The device   $R_{\mathrm{{ds}\,(\mathrm{{on})}}}$   can be described as the sum  of the channel resistance  $R_{\mathrm{ch}}$   and the remaining part of the total  resistance, commonly referred to as the residual resistance  $R_{\mathrm{s}}$  .  The latter includes the contact resistance, JFET region  resistance, drift region resistance, and substrate resistance [14].  For this device, ignoring the initial fluctuations, the overall   $R_{\mathrm{ds}}$  (on)  of the device increased after repetitive SC tests. It’s due to  degradation occurs in the channel region and then spreads to the  drift/JFET region as the heat generated by the SC tests is  concentrated in the top side of the SiC chip [15].

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

The repetitive SC ruggedness of commercial   $1.2\mathrm{-kV}$   SiC  power MOSFETs was investigated in this paper. The  $V_{\mathrm{th}}$   and  $I_{\mathrm{gs}}$  obviously increased can be observed after repetitive SC tests.  The failure mechanism was discovered and confirmed  according to the experiments, that’s negative charges were  captured by the gate oxide and accumulated during the SC tests,  which is eventually causes drastically degradation of the gate  oxide. This is a preliminary study and the failure analysis will  be researched in the future. The results of this paper may  provide useful guidance on the design of the gate driver and  fault protection circuits.
