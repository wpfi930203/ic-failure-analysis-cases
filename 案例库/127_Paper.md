## Degradation behaviour of electrical properties of inverted metamorphic four-junction  (IMM4J) solar cells under 1 MeV electron irradiation  

Zhang Yanqing 1, Ma Guoliang 1, Wang Tianqi 1, Liu Chaoming 2, Huo Mingxue 1, Qi Chunhua 1 \*

1 Research Center of Basic Space Science, Harbin Institute of Technology, Harbin 150001, China  2 School of Materials Science & Engineering, Harbin Institute of Technology, Harbin 150001, China  \* Corresponding author Tel.:   $+86$   15304806698; \*Email: qchunh@126.com

## Abstract 

In this study, the degradation effects of inverted metamorphic four-junction (IMM4J) solar cells were investigated after 1 MeV  electron irradiation using spectral response and electrical property measurements. The results show that, similar to traditional  tri-junction (TJ) GaInP/GaAs/Ge solar cells, the electrical properties (such as  $\mathrm{{I_{sc}}}$  ,   $\mathrm{V_{oc}}$  , and   $\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{max}}$  ) degrade as a function of   $\log\!\varphi$  ,  where  $\varphi$   represents the electron fluence. In particular, the degradation of  $\mathrm{V_{oc}}$   is more serious than that of  $\mathrm{{I_{sc}}}$   because of the sum rule  for  $\mathrm{V_{oc}}$   and the limit rule for  $\mathrm{I_{sc}}$   in IMM4J cells. Analysis of the spectral response curves indicates that, unlike traditional TJ cells,  the   $\mathrm{In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7}\mathrm{As}$   (1.0 eV) sub-cell exhibits the most severe damage in the irradiated IMM4J cells and the   $\mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As}$   sub-cell  becomes a limits part for the electrical properties of the IMM4J solar cells.

## 1.    Introduction 

After multi-junction solar cells (GaInP/GaAs/Ge) were  used in space, scientists began to find a suitable   $1.0~\mathrm{eV}$    sub-cell material to reduce the thermalization loss and  optimize the output power through spectral matching [1].  In recent years, solar cells with an inverted metamorphic  (IMM) configuration have been investigated for space  applications. This IMM design is extremely spectrally  flexible because the band-gap of every junction can be  adjusted independently [2, 3]. In particular, the  composition and epitaxial structures of the In  $_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Ga}_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{As}$    junctions can be controlled using metamorphic buffers,  thereby allowing band-gaps to range, in principle, from   $0.33~\mathrm{eV}$   for InAs to 1.42 eV for GaAs [4].

Based on this principle, researchers at National  Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) introduced the  IMM solar cell in 2005 [5]. IMM3J solar cells with a  band-gap arrangement of GaInP (1.89 eV)/GaAs (1.41   $\mathrm{eV)/In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7As}$   (1.0 eV) have achieved an efficiency of  over  $33\%$   under 1 Sun AM0 or greater illumination, while  the efficiency of conventional tri-junction (TJ) devices is  limited to just around   $30\%$   [6]. By adding a fourth  $0.7\;\mathrm{eV}$    sub-cell   $(\mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As})$   to increase the bandwidth of the  conversion spectrum, Emcore and Spectrolab developed  an IMM4J device, which was characterized by a  conversion efficiency of   $33.9\%$   at 1 Sun AM0 [8, 9].

Despite these achievements, there is still important  work to be performed on new solar cells in order to  achieve much higher efficiencies. The IMM solar cell has  different fabrication procedures and configurations  compared to the conventional TJ solar cell. For this  reason, achieving a proper understanding of the  deterioration of its electrical properties (and deriving a  degradation model) and has become the precondition of  space applications. However, in spite of the fact that the  IMM solar cell is the most important candidate for  next-generation space solar cells, its irradiation damage  behaviors have not been adequately reported. forward a  possible theory, though they provided no systematic  explanation of the damage mechanism [4].

In this paper, 1 MeV electron irradiation tests are  carried out on the IMM4J solar cell, and the degradation  behavior of its electrical properties is investigated. Finally,  through the analysis of the spectral response, the damage  mechanisms of the IMM cells are also qualitatively  discussed.

## 2.    Experiment 

Samples of the IMM4J solar cells (GaInP / GaAs /   $\mathrm{In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7}\mathrm{As}\ /\ \mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As})$   had an area of   $3\ \mathrm{cm}\times4$   cm  and were supplied by Shanghai Institute of Space Power  Sources (SISP). A metal-organic chemical vapor  deposition (MOCVD) technique was used to grow the  epitaxial layers in the IMM4J solar cell. Fig. 1 shows the  configuration schematic of the IMM4J solar cell tested in  this study, where the P-type base doping and the N-type  emitter doping of the GaInP, GaAs,   $\mathrm{In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7}\mathrm{As}$   and   $\mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As}$   sub-cells were controlled at   $1\!\times\!10^{17}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$  and  $2{\times}10^{18}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$  , respectively. Six step-graded  $\mathrm{{Al}_{x}\mathrm{{In}_{1:x}\mathrm{{As}}}}$  buffer layers with total thickness of 3000 nm bridged the  GaAs and   $\mathrm{In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7}\mathrm{As}$   sub-cells, as well as the   $\mathrm{In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7}\mathrm{As}$   and   $\mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As}$   sub-cells.

Electron irradiation was performed at room temperature  and in DD12-type Van der Graaf electron accelerators at  the Heilongjiang Institute of Applied Physics. The  samples were irradiated using 1 MeV electron beams with  fluences of   $3\!\times\!10^{14}$  ,   $5\!\times\!10^{14}$  ,   $\mathbf{\tilde{l}}_{1\times10^{15}}$  ,   $2\!\times\!10^{15}$  , and   $3\!\times\!10^{15}$   $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$  . The irradiation flux was held constant at   $1\!\times\!10^{11}$   $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$  .

The electrical properties of the IMM4J solar cells  before and after irradiation were measured using an HSC1  solar cell tester from Hi-Show PV Science & Technology.  Fig. 2 shows the I-V curve for the pristine IMM4J cell  under AM1.5G illumination. The short-circuit current   $\left(\mathrm{{I_{sc}}}\right)$  , open-circuit voltage   $(\mathrm{V_{oc})}$  , and conversion efficiency  of the pristine cells were measured to be 144.9 mA, 3.131  V, and   $33.1\%,$  , respectively. In this study, the spectral  response curves in the wavelength range of 300 to 1700  nm were also measured using a solar cell quantum  efficiency measurement system (model QEX10) from PV  Measurements, Inc. (USA).

## 3.    Results and discussion 

The spectral response spectra are shown in Fig. 4 for  the IMM4J solar cells after 1 MeV electron irradiation.  The sub-cells’ short-circuit current density   $\left(\mathrm{{J_{sc}}}\right)$   could also  be measured using the spectral response test; the  corresponding  $\mathrm{{J_{sc}}}$   degradation factors are presented in Fig.  5. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) of all four  sub-cells decreases as the 1 MeV electron fluence  increases; in other words, these sub-cells could be  damaged owing to irradiation. The results demonstrate  that the In 0.3 Ga 0.7 As sub-cell shows the weakest radiation  resistance in the IMM4J cell, but the  In 0.58 Ga 0.42 As sub-cell  exhibits the lowest initial  $\mathrm{{J_{sc.}}}$  . , which maintains a minimum  during the whole irradiation tests. Thus, the  In 0.58 Ga 0.42 As   sub-cell becomes a limits part for the electrical properties  of the IMM4J solar cells, and the  In 0.3 Ga 0.7 As  sub-cell  shows the most severe damage as a result of 1 MeV  electron irradiation.

## Conclusion 

Our results show that 1 MeV electrons could lead to  homogeneous damage to the four sub-cells of the IMM4J  cell. Furthermore, the electrical properties degenerate as a  function of log φ .   $\mathrm{{P_{max}}}$   exhibits the largest decrease  because it is proportional to the product of the current and  the voltage. Furthermore, the spectral response indicates  that the   $\mathrm{In}_{0.3}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.7}\mathrm{As}$   sub-cell exhibits the most severe  damage as a result of 1 MeV electron irradiation and the   $\mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As}$   sub-cell exhibits a minimum of   $\mathrm{{J_{sc}}}$   during  the whole irradiation tests. Thus, the   $\mathrm{In}_{0.58}\mathrm{Ga}_{0.42}\mathrm{As}$  sub-cell becomes a limits part for the electrical properties

of the IMM4J solar cells.
