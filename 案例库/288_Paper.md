## Failure Analysis on TiAl Metallization Process for  Ohmic Contact on 4H-SiC pMOSFET  

Chia-Lung Hung \* , Jung-Chien Cheng, and Bing-Yue Tsui  Institute of Electronics, National Chiao Tung University, Hsinchu, Taiwan, R.O.C.  \* Phone: 886-3-579-9958   Email: kevencl.hung@msa.hinet.net

Abstract-   SiC is suitable for high-power and high-temperature  applications due to its’ wide energy bandgap and high thermal  conductivity. Most literature focus on SiC nMOSFET due to  higher electron mobility than hole. In this work, we fabricated 4HSiC pMOSFET using TiAl alloy as contact metal to reduce the  contact resistivity. However, ultrahigh leakage current was  measured among all terminals of the pMOSFET. By comparing  with different contact schemes, the failure mechanism is attributed  to Al spiking into the underneath poly-Si and SiO 2  during the  metallization process. Using suitable blocking layer such as  LPCVD Si 3 N 4  (300 nm) or PECVD Si 3 N 4  (100 nm) on   $\mathbf{SiO}_{2}$   (200  nm) can avoid Al spiking so that conventional pattern topology and  TiAl metallization process can be used on device fabrication.

Keywords  –  4H-SiC pMOSFET, ohmic contact, Al spiking,  leakage current, Blocking layer .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Silicon devices can only operate at maximum temperature   $125\sim\!150\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  , which is limited by its small bandgap, only 1.12  eV. For high-temperature applications, 4H-SiC is a more  suitable substrate due to its wider energy bandgap, 3.25 eV, and  higher thermal conductivity, 4.9 W/cm-K [1]. For discrete SiC  power MOSFETs, due to the electron mobility is higher than  the hole mobility, most studies focus on the nMOSFET [2].  However, several SiC CMOS ICs have been demonstrated [3- 5]. To build logic and gate drive circuits of CMOS ICs,  pMOSFETs are as important as nMOSFETs [6]. Because of the  low activation rate of p-type dopant in SiC, the contact  resistivity (ρ C ) of the source (S)/drain (D) contact is still need to  be solved. It has been reported that TiAl alloy can form good  ohmic contact on p-type SiC [7-9]. Thus, we have fabricated  planar 4H-SiC pMOSFET with this metallization to reduce the  series resistance. However, ultrahigh gate (G) and S/D junction  leakage current were measured among all terminals of the  pMOSFET.   In this work, three kinds of contact scheme are  designed to figure out the leakage path. After that, electron  beam induced current (EBIC) hot spot analysis is performed to  locate leakage paths. According to the cross-sectional scanning  electron microscope (SEM) inspection on the hot spot locations,  Al spiking is identified to be responsible for the ultrahigh  leakage current. Finally, we propose several kinds of blocking  layer to avoid Al spiking for future process integration on the  4H-SiC MOSFET fabrication.

Fig. 4 shows the planar view of the device inspected by  optical microscope and the cross-sectional sketches of the  suspected leakage paths. During the   $950~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   PMDA, Al would  become molten state and easily spike into the underlying polySi and/or oxide layers. To avoid the Al spiking effect, we  devised a new contact scheme (CS-B) compared with original  contact scheme (CS-A) with reduced metal area such that the  metal stack is enclosed in the contact window. Although the  gate leakage current is suppressed in CS-B, the leakage current  is still too high from device point of view. This result also  confirms that Al spikes through both poly-Si and field oxide  layers.

In order to solve the Al spiking problem, we devised a 2-step  contact scheme (CS-C): step-1 forms TiAl metallization on  S/D/B with   $500~\sim~950~\mathrm{~\textdegree C~}$   PMDA and step-2 forms Al  metallization on poly-Si gate with only   $400~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$   sintering. However, in this way, we need to  deposit and pattern metal twice, which will increase process  complexity and cost. ## IV. C ONCLUSIONS 

TiAl alloy can form good ohmic contact on p-type SiC.  However, the high-temperature PDMA results in Al spiking  through poly-Si and   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and thus very high gate-to-body and  drain-to-body leakage current. Using TiAl alloy on SiC S/D/B  contacts and Al on poly-Si gate contact separately is beneficial  to prevent the leakage phenomena. Furthermore, replacing the  FOX by a LPCVD   $\mathrm{Si}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$   (300 nm) can be expected to basically  solve the Al spiking issue up to   $950~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$  . The bi-layer dielectric  stack, PECVD  $\mathrm{Si}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$   (100 nm) on PECVD   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   (200 nm), also  shows good blocking capability against Al spiking. Therefore,  conventional pattern topology and metallization process can  still be integrated into 4H-SiC MOSFETs with these blocking  layers.
