## The Applications of Simulation and Artificial  Intelligence in Advanced Packaging  

Yan Li, WooPoung Kim   Advanced Packaging Business Team R&D, Samsung Semiconductor Inc., 3655 N. First St., San Jose, USA  Phone : (01)4804557921, Email :  yan.a.li@samsung.com

Abstract —Advanced Packaging enables the heterogenous  integration of chiplets with die embedding and die stacking  techniques, and provides maximum device performance with  much faster product development cycle. However, as the 2.5D  and 3D packages are getting more and more complicated, it is  very challenging to ramp the yield and assure the reliability of  new products within the short timeline, using conventional  methods.  Artificial Intelligence (AI) assisted design cooptimization, manufactory process simulation and monitoring,  yield enhancement, reliability assurance, and failure mode  prediction, which are developed for Si fabrication, could be  employed to shorten the advanced packaging development  cycle. This paper summarizes the current status for the various  applications of simulation and AI in advanced packaging.  Further development trends are highlighted with the goal of  providing a holistic and robust virtual representation, or digital  twin, for advanced packaging.

Keywords—Advanced Packaging, AI, ML, simulation,  process, yield, quality, reliability, FA

## I.    I NTRODUCTION 

As illustrated in Fig. 1, advanced packaging technologies,  which enable heterogeneous integration of chiplets by dense  Die to Die (D2D) interconnection, 2.5D interposer, 3D die  stacking, and 3.5D die stacking on interposer, are developed  to stretch Moore’s law of Integrated Circuit (ICs) with much  lower cost, while the traditional method of decreasing  transistor size by lithography is getting more and more  expensive [1][2]. Because it can provide maximum device  performance with much faster product development cycle,  lower cost, and higher yield by leveraging the best achievable  chiplet having smaller die size [3]. Chiplet heterogenous  integration brings in tremendous opportunities as well as  significant challenges in IC packaging. Die on Wafer (DoW)  or Wafer on Wafer (WoW) Hybrid Copper Bonding (HCB),  which can provide direct Cu-Cu connection, is substituting  solder-based micro bump Thermal Compressive Bonding  (TCB) for die stacking, when the interconnect pitch scales  down to less than   $10\upmu\mathrm{m}$   or   $1\upmu\mathrm{m}$  . While the interconnects are  getting smaller and denser, the overall package size is  becoming bigger, because more chiplets and High Bandwidth  Memory (HBM) are assembled on the same package due to  the High-Performance Computing (HPC) and Artificial  Intelligence (AI) market demands of ever higher performance,  lower power consumption, wider memory bandwidth with  reduced latency.

The boundary between Si and advanced packaging is  blurring, due to the dramatic scaling down of interconnect size  and pitch for wafer and die stacking [4]. Design optimization,  assembly technology development, process control, yield  enhancement, quality monitoring, reliability risk assessment,  and failure analysis are getting more and more complicated in  advanced packaging having sophisticated assembly steps and  submicron   interconnects.   Adopting   well-developed  methodologies currently used in Si Fabs to advanced  packaging area is imperative to meet the short product  development cycle demands [4].

The applications of Machine Learning (ML), a subfield of  AI, in chip design, in Si Fab process simulation and control,  as well as yield and reliability prediction have been  demonstrated as efficient methodologies to tackle complex  design and process challenges [5]. Deep Learning (DL), a  subset of ML, having multi-layered neural networks and the  ability to automatically learn complex and high-dimensional  patterns in the big data, has successful applications in defect  detection and classification in semiconductor manufactory  [6]. The Adoption of similar methodologies proven to be  effective in Si Fabs to Advanced Packaging area is one of the  efficient approaches to help address challenges and reduce  product development cycle time. This paper summarizes the  applications of simulation and AI based methodologies in  Advanced Packaging area, and provides insights for future  developments.

## II.    AI  APPLICATONS IN ADVANCED PACKAGING CO - DESIGN    

ML based methodologies have been used in electronic  design automation (EDA) since 1990. The recent advances in  ML and DL techniques, as well as the increasing design  complexity of very large-scale ICs have fostered the  implementation of ML and DL in EDA for chip design to  improve design efficiency, automation, and co-optimization in  multi-core chips [7] [8] [9]. EDA tool vendors, such as  Synopsys, Cadence design systems, and Siemens have  provided ML and DL based EDA platforms for IC designers.  Additionally, EDA tool vendors and many hyperscale chip  designers are now adopting generative AI capabilities to  facilitate chip design [10].

Complex advance packages with 2.5D and 3D  configurations need die-package-Printed Circuit Board (PCB)  co-design and co-optimization. Because the interdependence  among multi-domain, such as die, package, and PCB, and  multi-physics, for instance thermal, optical, electrical, and  mechanical, requires a parallel and integrated flow for design,  analysis, and optimization [11]. Robust modeling and  simulation tools and methodologies are needed to accurately  predict the electro-thermal and thermo-mechanical coupling  across multi-domains within a co-design optimization  environment for effective system optimization at design stage.  Additionally, accurate simulation methodologies are desired

to forecast the thermo-mechanical stress across multi-domain  as well as die and substrates warpage during assembly process  and reliability tests, for efficient process and performance  optimization at design stage [11].

Derived from the 2D chip design methodologies, Various  ML and DL techniques have been applied in 3D IC chip  design to help tackle the challenges during the 3D integration,  such as die-to-die variation and design optimization [7].   Similar ML and DL based methodology concepts could be  adopted from the chip co-design to die-package-system multidomain, multi-scale, and multi-physics codesign and cooptimization.

## III.    AI  APPLICATIONS IN ADVANCED PACKAGING PROCESS  SIMULATION AND MONITORING   

Machine learning based infrastructure, for example, the  Calibre  $^\mathrm{\textregistered}$   Fab Insights platform provided by Siemens has been  used in Si Fab for process simulation and monitoring [5].  Applications in front-end lithography [12] [13] and High  Aspect Ratio Process (HARP) [5] demonstrate the  effectiveness and efficiency of the ML based methodologies  in predicting process parameters. Feature engineering, which  facilitates the conversion of relevant physical information into  a format that can be understood by machine learning models,  is the key for a successful ML model. Additionally, ML based  methodologies have been used as inline Virtual Metrology  (VM), which can predict real time process parameters by  analyzing data from inline sensors for process and tool  monitoring [14] [15].

Similar ML based infrastructures and concepts can be used  in the process simulation and monitoring of advanced  packaging. Die sidewall chipping depth during the wafer  dicing process is one of the critical process parameters to  monitor during advanced packaging to ensure downstream  yield and reliability. To optimize the wafer dicing, all  parameters, such as blade rotational speed and wafer feed rate,  need to be tuned to the use case, and constantly monitored  during dicing.  Design of Experiments (DOE) are typically  performed for each product for wafer dicing, and the best  conditions are selected from the DOEs.

A jointed study of IMEC, KOA, and Siemens on ML  applications in the wafer dicing process is performed. A ML  model is established based on very limited data to help  understand the key process parameters which lead to die side  wall cracks. The ML model is used to predict die sidewall  crack size, which was compared with the actual  measurements. The results suggest that the ML model is  sufficiently accurate for first order prediction [16]. As shown  in Table 1, by varying the spindle speed of the blade and the  wafer feed rate, nine wafer processing conditions are  performed in this study. Only two wafers are used in this  study, which generates very limited data set. Fig. 2 illustrates  the die sidewall chipping depth measurements using KLAICOS F160XP die sorting and inspection system. The raw  data, including dicing conditions and maximum chipping  depth per die is decomposed as the ML model inputs.  Additionally, the input data is enriched by die spatial data,  which is the radial distance from wafer center point, as well as  the die edge orientation (horizontal vs vertical). The input data  is also split to horizontal and vertical die edge maximum crack  depth.

Statistical analysis of the input data could result in a simple  inference, showing condition 1, which showing the least mean

value of chipping depth would be the preferred process  condition. A ML based feature importance ranking approach  is established to provide detailed information, such as “which  individual process parameter is more effective, and how it is  ranked compared to other design or process parameters”,  which could not be achieved by simple statistical analysis  Gradient Boosted Forests ML algorithm having flexibility and  adaptability is selected to build up the ML model based on the  input data. The ML wafer dicing model is used to predict the  die sidewall chipping depth and comparing with the actual  measurement data. As shown in Fig. 3. The Root Mean Square  Error (RMSE), which is defined as

$$\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i})^{2}}$$
is   $11.581~\ensuremath{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$  . As the ML model is built based on very  limited input data, the model is considered accurate for first  order predictions. The study demonstrates the effectiveness of  ML based methodologies in the advanced packaging process  simulation and monitoring. It could be adopted in any other  assembly steps and could be improved by more input data.  Once a robust process simulation model is established, virtual  experiments can be conducted in the model for future  products,   which   will   dramatically   shorten   product  development cycle time [16].

## IV.    AI  APPLICATIONS IN YIELD ENHANCEMENT AND  RELIABILITY RISKASSESSMENT OF ADVANCED PACKAGES 

ML and DL based methodologies have been employed in  Si Fab. for data management and data analytics to enhance  yield and reliability in semiconductor manufactory. The

methodologies can provide valuable and predictive insights  for processes optimization, yield improvement, and reliability  assurance, from the existing vast amount of data collected  throughout the semiconductor manufacturing process by  identifying patterns and anomalies that contribute to yield loss  and reliability issues in real time [17] [18] [19] [20].

If similar AI based methodologies can be applied to  advance packaging high volume manufactory, the extensive  process monitoring data generated in a daily base could  provide predictive insights on yield enhancement and  reliability assurance. Deep Topological Data Analysis  (DTDA), an unsupervised machine learning method that  clusters and models the data in the form of geometric objects,  has been used in manufactory floor for data visualization and  data analytics, which helps engineers identify the features  contributing to yield and quality issues, enabling corrective  actions [21]. The combination of conventional finite-element  analysis-based   simulation   technology   for   reliability  assessment and the advanced AI based data-driven  methodologies can provide a fusion prognostic for device  reliability [22]. Additionally, the calibrated finite-elementanalysis simulation model can be used to generate numerous  virtual training data for a more efficient AI model that can  effectively and accurately predict the reliability life cycle of  various geometries of Wafer Level Packages (WLP) based on  the input geometric data of the new WLP products [23] [24].

## V.   AI  APPLICATIONS IN FAILURE ANALYSIS OF ADVANCED  PACKAGES   

Failure Analysis (FA) can identify defects causing  electrical failures in advanced packages, either at End of Line  or post reliability tests, investigate root cause of failures, and  provide valuable data feedbacks and solution paths for  problem solving. The high level of functional integration and  the complex package architecture in advanced  packages pose  significant challenges for conventional fault isolation (FI) and  failure analysis (FA) methods. Innovative FA flow, which can  provide high volume FA support with low cost and short  Through-Put Time (TPT) is highly desired in high-volume  manufactory. Integrating AI into FA flow can promote  automation and big data analysis, save human resources and  costs, shorten TPT, and enables high volume FA support on  very complicated advanced packages [25].

AI has been adopted in daily FA work recently with  promising results achieved in the following categories: 1)  defect identification and classification; 2) noise mitigation in  scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron  microscopy (TEM), scanning acoustic microscopy (SAM),  and 3D X-ray microscopy images; 3) failure mode recognition  by clustering and pattern detection; and 4) failure report  classification and failure mode prediction [25]-[28].

Fig. 4 illustrates optical microscopy images  automatically  taken on the surface of printed circuit board (PCB) boards for  the presence of defects such as “missing hole”, “mouse bite”,  and “open circuit”. Around 600 images per defect type are  utilized to train the AI model with Convolutional Neural  Network (CNN), which achieves a precision rate of above   $98.8\%$  , and a TPT less than 1 second per sample post training.  The well-developed AI model can be extended for additional  defect types and PCB types [26]. Similar concepts can be  applied to auto-detect and auto-classify other package defects  by optical microscopy, infrared (IR) microscopy, SAM, X-ray  Microscopy, SEM, and TEM [25].

Fig. 5 presents the application of AI techniques for noise  reduction in SEM images. A CNN is trained to denoise SEM  images for better image edge detection. Similar approaches  can be used to denoise optical, IR, TEM, SAM, and X-ray  images [26].

Fig. 6 demonstrates the efficient failure pattern recognition  assisted by AI [27]. The circles indicate wafers, and the dark  dots represent failing die locations on wafers. Wafer failure  pattern recognition is very important during the investigations  of failure modes and mechanisms. For instance, “center”,  “donut”, and “edge” patterns typically suggest certain wafer  level process issues. While a “scratch” pattern indicates  handling related issues. A deep convolutional neural network  (DCNN), was trained with 54,355 images, containing more  than 400 datasets for each pattern type. The trained model can  successfully recognize failing patterns, having \~   $98\%$    accuracy when tested by 118,595 datasets [27]. AI assisted  failure mode identification by clustering and pattern  recognition could be extensively implemented in product  yield analysis, manufacturing defect monitoring, device  failure analysis, and failure root cause investigation [25].

FA reports, containing an extensive amount of knowledge  about device electrical failure signatures, test conditions, FIFA steps, findings, physical defects, and root cause of the  failures, can be classified according to electrical and physical  faults by various AI-based text classification algorithms [28].  As shown in Fig. 7, failure mode prediction based on the well  classified historical FA report datasets is possible.  A vector is  generated for each term of every FA report and is utilized for  comparison between different FA reports. The Euclidean

distance between each pair of FA reports is then calculated  and can provide a measure of how close the two reports are.  The electrical signature or the physical defects can be  estimated by finding the smallest Euclidean distance between  the new problem statement and all historical reports [28].

Future developments of AI applications in FA could  include: 1) assisting FA data collection and performing full  FA; 2) training new FA engineers; 3) generation or  modification of detailed measurement data from rare failure  modes of different samples; and 4) automatic FA report  generation and evaluation [25]. The availability of hundreds  or thousands of standardized training datasets for each failure  mode is the key to successful AI execution in FA. The  restrictions of AI application in FA are in the fields where only  small training datasets are available. How to normalize and  share training data for generic failure modes in semiconductor  devices, and how to develop AI algorithms without hundreds  or thousands of training datasets, are imperative and  challenging topics [25].

## VI.    CONCLUSIONS 

Advanced   Packaging   enables   the   heterogeneous  integration of chiplets by providing 2.5D, 3D, and 3.5D  packaging   architectures   having   enormously   dense  interconnects. As the interconnects in advanced packaging get  smaller and denser, achieved by DoW or WoW technologies,  the lines between packaging and Si Fab process are blurring.  Adopting the well-developed Fab Front-end methodologies,  especially AI based methods, to advanced packaging area can  dramatically shorten the product development cycle, improve  yield, and assure device reliability.

The applications of ML and DL based methodologies in  advanced packaging have been reported in the literature, for  example, AI based die-package-PCB co-design platforms,  ML based process simulation and monitoring infrastructures,  ML and DL assisted data management and analytics for yield  enhancement and reliability assurance, AI techniques for  automation and prediction in FA etc. However further  developments in the AI based methodologies are needed to  provide a holistic and robust virtual replica, or digital twin, of  the entire physical process of advanced packaging, from  design, process, yield, reliability, to FA. Good correlations  between the digital twin and the physical world need to be  established and calibrated in real time. The product  development cycle could be dramatically reduced with a much  lower cost by the digital twin technique.
