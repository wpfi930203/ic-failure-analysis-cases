## Reduction of Current Collapse in GaN (MIS)-HEMTs  Using Dual Material Gate  

Binit Syamal, Siau Ben Chiah, Xing Zhou \*   School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Nanyang Technological University, 50 Nanyang Avenue, Singapore 639798  \* Email:  exzhou@ntu.edu.sg

Abstract-  GaN high electron-mobility transistors suffer from  various defects or trap states present either in AlGaN barrier or  GaN buffer layer. This work presents a different approach to  reducing the current-collapse effects in GaN-based transistors by  using a dual material gate technology, whereby two different  materials having different work functions are merged together to  form a single gate, resulting in an improvement of current collapse  of around   $\bf{50\%}$  . The current transport efficiency also improves,  thus offering improved transconductance. The presence of two  gate materials of different work-function modifies the peak  electric-field at the drain end, reducing current-collapse and  dynamic- R ds,ON  degradation.

Keywords – GaN HEMTs, Buffer traps, Current-collapse, Gatelag, Drain-lag .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

GaN-based high electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) are  very promising devices for power conversion systems, due to  their high breakdown field, high electron mobility, and ability  of forming a 2-Dimensional Electron Gas (2-DEG) [1]–[4]. In  addition, the emerging CMOS-compactible GaN-on-Si and IIIV/Si co-integration technologies have made GaN HEMTs an  attractive choice for commercialization [5]. However, current  collapse is still the most critical issue for GaN HEMTs whereby  the presence of traps located either under the gate or in the gateto-drain access region or GaN buffer results in drain current  reduction when exposed to high negative gate voltages (gatelag), or to high drain voltage levels (drain-lag) [6], [7]. The DC  characteristics significantly deviate from high-frequency  operation because of the electron capture at the surface, bulk,  and interface traps [8]. Various reports are available on how to  reduce the current-collapse effect, mostly focusing on the  substrate growth or on silicon nitride passivation layer  deposition techniques [9], [10]. This work, based on a 2-D  numerical simulation (TCAD), presents a dual material gate  (DMG) engineering technique, whereby it is possible to reduce  the peak electric field at the drain-end, thus reducing the current  collapse significantly.

## III. CURRENT COLLAPSE

In GaN-HEMTs, traps can affect the device   I – V   characteristics by a) directly trapping electrons that would  otherwise be part of the mobile channel electrons, i.e., depleting  the 2-DEG density in the channel, b) trapping charges  elsewhere, thereby creating a potential barrier that impedes the  flow of channel electrons [14], [15]. The possible mechanisms  of gate lag in GaN HEMTs were reported in [16], attributing it  to the surface and barrier traps.  On the contrary, drain-lag or  both-lag effects are attributed mostly to buffer or substrate traps  [17]. As GaN-HEMTs are used mostly for power-switching or  RF applications, the latter dominates in the form of current  collapse. Even though, DMG technology demonstrates better currentcollapse performance, one key challenge in terms of switching  speed is gate-to-drain capacitance   $C_{\mathrm{gd}}$  . For hard switching  applications,   $C_{\mathrm{gd}}$   is the most important capacitance in a power  applications as it dominates the output switching waveforms  through the “Miller” effect, especially in a high impedance  clamped inductive load circuit [18]. As seen from Fig. 6, with  reducing   $\phi_{2}$    in DMG devices,   $C_{\mathrm{gd}} $  increases significantly  compared to SMG device. As per circuit design specifications,  there exists a clear trade-off between the current-collapse and  the switching performance.

Another key challenge exists in terms of fabrication of the  DMG technology. For normally-off p-HEMT devices, in  specific p-GaN/AlGaN/GaN structure, whereby the gate is  Magnesium (Mg)-doped to tune the threshold voltage, the  doping on the drain side can be optimized by implementing one  extra lithography step to get the desired work-function  difference and reduced current-collapse performance. Similar  technique with extra lithographic steps could also be used for  “(metal) gate-first   $E$  -mode GaN HEMTs” where sputtered   $\mathrm{W/Cr/SiN_{x}}$   gate stacks were defined, as reported in [19].

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Current collapse in GaN-HEMTs is seen to be reduced by  using two materials of different work-function merged together  to form a single gate. Due to lower peak electric field at the  drain side, electron trapping reduces significantly, both in linear  and saturation regions. Key design parameters include the  partition-length and work-function of the gate material, and  detailed optimization techniques can be explored to obtain  reduced   current-collapse   as   well   as   good   switching  performance.
