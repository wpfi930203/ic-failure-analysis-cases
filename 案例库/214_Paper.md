## 4-Point-Bending Characterization of Interfacial  Adhesion Strength of Co-Zr-Ta and Co-Zr-Ta Variant  Thin-Film Stacks    

Xintong Zhu 1\* , Xiaoxuan Li 1 , Ramesh Rao Nistala 1 , Zishan Ali 2 , Lulu Peng 3 , Lawrence Selvaraj 2 , Chor Shu Cheng 2  and  Zhi Qiang Mo 1    1 QRA-MFA Dept, GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte Ltd /60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, Singapore 738406  2 Technology Development, GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte Ltd /60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, Singapore  738406  3 NVM & Specialty Projects, GLOBALFOUNDRIES Singapore Pte Ltd /60 Woodlands Industrial Park D, Street 2, Singapore  738406  Phone: (+65) 66701533 Fax: (+65) 63604370 \*Email:  xintong.zhu@globalfoundries.com .

Abstract-  In this study, soft magnetic thin films including  Co-Zr-Ta (CZT) and its variant are deposited on TEOS   $\mathbf{SiO}_{2}$   and  polyimide to characterize the interfacial adhesion strength of the  full magnetic stack using the 4-Point-Bending (4PB) technique.  Variation in critical load value  Gc , an indicator of the interfacial  adhesion strength, is observed. Auger Electron Spectroscopy  (AES) is performed for elemental analysis to confirm the interface  of de-lamination.

Keywords  – Adhesion, CZT, 4-Point-Bending .

## I. I NTRODUCTION 

Applications in Internet of Things (IoT) and mobile  communication devices such as mobile phones have propelled  miniaturization of various functionalities and circuitry on chips,  including normally off-chip and bulky elements such as  inductors [1]. Most on-chip inductors in use utilize a spiral  geometry fabricated without magnetic material and exhibit  inductances ranging from   $1{-}10\ \mathrm{nH}$   [2]. This low inductance  density design occupies a large substrate area in an era where  size reduction of integrated circuits (IC) is a hot issue. It is  therefore necessary to increase inductance density when  manufacturing such devices and using magnetic material in the  process is one feasible and practical solution. With the addition  of magnetic material, increased inductance  $L$   and quality factor   $\mathcal{Q}$   and/or reduction in capacitance, resistance, and area can be  achieved. For a magnetically enhanced on-chip inductor, a  number of coil and winding geometries have been investigated  including spiral, stripe, toroidal, solenoid, and meander  structures   [2].   Manufacturing   monolithic   devices   in  applications such as wireless communications, RF integrated  circuits, radar, power delivery, and EMI noise reduction  requires integration of on-chip inductors with magnetic  materials into silicon technology which is faced with various  challenges depending on the type of magnetic material in use.

Processing temperature limitations have restricted usage of  soft ferrite magnetic cores such as NiZn and MnZn, which are  widely used in discrete inductors and transformers for RF  devices, in integrated inductors despite desirable properties  such as extremely high resistivity and low eddy current loss [1].  High saturation magnetization and high permeability combined

with compatibility with existing CMOS process, i. e. deposition  by sputtering or electro-plating, make ferromagnetic metallic  materials good candidates. Combinations of ferromagnetic  materials have been explored to deposit efficient magnetic  materials with highly soft nature and high uniaxial anisotropy.  CZT and its variant are examples of such material. Fabrication  process, magnetic performance and structural properties of  CZT and its variant have been well researched [3] but not the  mechanical strength.

Interfacial adhesion of a multi-layer system has been  considered important criteria of device reliability. The 4PB test  is a broadly used method to evaluate adhesion strength of such  stacks. During the 4PB test, thin films are sandwiched between  two elastically massive substrates (such as Si wafer), and the  relaxation of residual stress is confined as compared to other  commonly employed techniques such as scotch tape test,  scratch test, indentation test and peel test [4]. During the test,  the initiated crack propagates along the interface with the  weakest adhesion strength in a multi-material stack. This study  uses the 4PB method to characterize the interfacial adhesion  strength of CZT and CZT variant thin-films deposited on TEOS   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   and polyimide. Confirmation of the de-laminated interface  during the 4PB test thus validating the 4PB test results are done  by elemental analysis using Auger Electron Spectroscopy.

## II.   EXPERIMENTS  

The starting materials of all samples are   $300\mathrm{{mm}}$   bare silicon  (100) wafers which went through deposition of TEOS   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$   as  the first layer. Thickness of this layer is the same for all the  wafers. The wafers are then split and different stacks of  thin-films are fabricated on top of the   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  :

1)    Wafer #1 is deposited directly with a layer of Ti as  barrier metal which is followed by multiple  alternating layers of CZT variant and Dielectric A;     2)    Compared to wafer #1, wafer  $\#2$   is spin-coated with  a layer of polyimide before Ti barrier metal, and  instead of CZT variant, CZT is used as the magnetic  layer in the repeating film stack;

3)    On wafer #3, the dielectric film is changed to  Dielectric B, while other films remain unchanged  compared to wafer #2;

4)    Wafer #4 has the same film stack structure as wafer  #2, the only difference is that the polyimide film on  this wafer is exposed.

The fully processed blanket film wafers are diced into pieces  of   $40\mathrm{mm}\!\times\!7\mathrm{mm}$   dimensions for the 4PB test [5]. Subsequent  sample preparation procedure was performed following these  steps:

1)    Bond the sample pieces (active beam) to bare silicon  beams (passive beam) of the same size using epoxy  glue: the side with magnetic film facing the epoxy.     2)    Subject the bonded samples to curing of epoxy at  elevated temperature for certain duration.     3)    Cut a pre-notch on the back side of active beam (the  side with Si substrate) on each sample [6] using a  diamond saw. Depth of the pre-notch is controlled  by the dicing saw and remains constant for all  samples. Moreover, as a measure of initiation for a  crack through the Si substrate of active beam, the  depth of the pre-notch will not affect the result of  measured  Gc .

## III. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

For the 4PB test, 10 samples are prepared from the wafers of  each split condition.  Gc  values of successfully delaminated  samples are averaged and shown in Figure 4. Success rate is  defined as the number of successful de-laminations  (de-lamination at the desired interfaces) divided by the total  number of samples prepared which, in this case, is 10. Zero  successful delamination is observed on wafer #4. For the  successfully delaminated samples, AES analysis on the two  surfaces resulted from the de-lamination, visual example of  which is shown in Figure. 5, are performed   to analyse elemental  composition. Four conclusions can be drawn from the  Gc  values results:

(i)    Adhesion strength of thin-film stacks of wafer #1  and wafer #2 are close: an average   $G c$   of approx. 5   $\mathrm{J}/\mathrm{cm}^{2}$   indicating little impact on the adhesive  integrity as the layers are changed;     (ii)   Comparing the results from wafer #2 and wafer #3, a  significant increase in the adhesion strength is  observed, showing that Dielectric B is more helpful  in improving interfacial adhesion integrity than  Dielectric A;     (iii)   No successful de-lamination is observed on wafer  #4 is proof that exposed polyimide provides the  strongest interfacial adhesion of the 4 splits, so  strong that the  Gc  value is beyond what the 4PB  technique can measure (samples break before  de-lamination or de-laminates at epoxy to film  interface before de-lamination of interface of  interest happens).    (iv)   A reverse correlation is observed between  Gc  value  and rate of successful delamination.

The significant difference in  Gc  value results is to be  attributed to material difference.

A comparison of example measurement data from  successfully delaminated sample of each condition is shown in  Figure 6. The loading curve “dips” when the load reaches  around 15\~30N for wafer #3 and #4. This is when the pre-notch  penetrates through the substrate and reaches the film. But no  deflection along any film interface happens, so the stress  continues to build up as load increases, until either an interface  gives in (wafer #3)  or the epoxy to passive Si beam gives in  (wafer #4) under such high load. In the case of wafers #1 and #2,  there is no such behaviour because one of the interfaces  de-laminates after the crack reaches it. Load value at  steady-state growth region varies from wafer to wafer. But  wafers #1 and #2 are comparable, and both are lower than wafer  #3. The same trend is observed for  Gc  value of these conditions.

AES analysis result on the two sides of one successfully  de-laminated sample is shown in Figure. 7 as an example. In  Figure.5, visual inspection suggests that metal film is on Side B  only and the purple colour on Side A is   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$  . AES analysis on  Side A (Figure. 7(a)) shows C, Ti, O and Si. Meanwhile, on

Side B, AES (Figure. 7(b)) shows only C, Ti, and O, there is no  Si, confirming that the   $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Ti}$   interface was delaminated, but  there was a small percentage of Ti residue left on Side A.

Traditional scratch and tape tests were done on the same set  of samples which presents results that tally with 4PB test  results.

## IV. C ONCLUSION 

Co-Zr-Ta and Co-Zr-Ta variant thin-film stacks are  experimented to characterize the interfacial adhesion between  the films using 4PB test. Process with exposed polyimide is  proved to have the best adhesive integrity.

By using 4PB method the adhesion between CZT and the  underlying substrate can be qualified so that the possibility of  de-lamination during subsequent post CZT processing is  reduced.   This   will   help   achieve   contamination-free  manufacturing of CZT based devices in CMOS manufacturing  line.
